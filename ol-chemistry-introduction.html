🧪 Introduction to Chemistry

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Build a strong foundation in Chemistry by understanding matter, substances, mixtures, physical and chemical changes, elements, compounds, laboratory safety and the importance of Chemistry.

1. 🧪 What Is Chemistry?

Chemistry is the branch of science that studies matter, its composition, structure, properties and the changes it undergoes.

In simple terms, Chemistry helps us understand what substances are made of, how they behave and how they can change into other substances.

💡 Examples of Chemistry Around Us

  • 🍳 Cooking food involves chemical changes.
  • 🧼 Soap and detergents are products of Chemistry.
  • 💊 Medicines are developed using Chemistry.
  • ⛽ Fuels release energy through chemical reactions.
  • 🌱 Fertilisers provide nutrients needed by plants.
  • 🧱 Cement, glass and many building materials depend on Chemistry.
  • 🧴 Cosmetics, plastics and paints involve chemical substances.

2. 🌍 Importance of Chemistry

Chemistry is important because substances and chemical changes are involved in almost every part of everyday life.

Area Importance of Chemistry
🏥 Medicine Used to produce medicines, disinfectants and other healthcare products.
🌾 Agriculture Helps in the production of fertilisers, pesticides and soil treatments.
🍎 Food Helps us understand food composition, preservation and processing.
🏭 Industry Used in the manufacture of plastics, cement, glass, paints and many other products.
⚡ Energy Helps us understand fuels, batteries and other energy-related processes.
🌍 Environment Helps us understand pollution, water treatment and environmental chemical processes.
🧵 Materials Used to develop fibres, polymers, metals and other useful materials.

3. 🌎 Chemistry and Other Sciences

Chemistry is closely connected to many other branches of science.

  • ⚛️ Physics: Chemistry uses ideas about energy, forces and particles.
  • 🧬 Biology: Living organisms depend on many chemical reactions.
  • 🌍 Geology: Chemistry helps explain rocks, minerals and Earth's materials.
  • 🌱 Agricultural Science: Chemistry is important in soil science and fertiliser use.
  • 🌳 Environmental Science: Chemistry helps explain pollutants and their effects.
  • 💊 Medicine: Understanding chemical substances is essential in pharmaceutical science.

🧠 Key Idea

Chemistry is often described as a central science because it connects with many other scientific disciplines.

4. 🌐 Matter

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space.

Examples include:

  • 💧 Water
  • 🌬️ Air
  • 🪨 Rocks
  • 🪵 Wood
  • 🧂 Salt
  • 🫙 Glass
  • 👤 The human body

📌 Remember

If something has mass and occupies space, it is considered matter.

5. 🧊 States of Matter

At O/L level, the three common states of matter are solid, liquid and gas.

State Shape Volume Particle Arrangement Particle Movement
🧊 Solid Fixed Fixed Closely packed Vibrate about fixed positions
💧 Liquid Not fixed Fixed Close together but less ordered Move around each other
💨 Gas Not fixed Not fixed Far apart Move rapidly in all directions

🔬 Particle Theory

Matter is made up of tiny particles. The arrangement, spacing and movement of these particles help explain the properties of solids, liquids and gases.

6. 🔄 Changes of State

A substance can change from one state to another when energy is transferred to or from it.

Change From To
🔥 Melting Solid Liquid
❄️ Freezing Liquid Solid
♨️ Boiling / Vaporisation Liquid Gas
💨 Condensation Gas Liquid
🌫️ Sublimation Solid Gas
❄️ Deposition Gas Solid

📌 Important

A change of state is a physical change. The substance itself remains chemically the same.

For example, water changes from liquid water to water vapour, but it remains H₂O.

7. 🧱 Physical Properties

A physical property is a characteristic of a substance that can be observed or measured without changing the substance into a new substance.

Examples of Physical Properties

  • 🎨 Colour
  • 👃 Odour
  • 💎 Hardness
  • 🌡️ Melting point
  • 🔥 Boiling point
  • ⚖️ Density
  • 💧 Solubility
  • ⚡ Electrical conductivity
  • 🧲 Magnetic properties
  • 🪨 State at room temperature

8. ⚗️ Chemical Properties

A chemical property describes how a substance behaves when it undergoes a chemical change.

Examples

  • 🔥 Ability to burn in oxygen
  • 🧪 Ability to react with acids
  • 💧 Ability to react with water
  • 🌬️ Ability to react with oxygen
  • ⚡ Ability to undergo electrolysis
  • 🧲 Ability to take part in oxidation or reduction reactions

🧠 Physical vs Chemical Property

Physical Property Chemical Property
Can be observed without forming a new substance. Describes behaviour during a chemical change.
Example: melting point. Example: ability to burn.
Example: density. Example: reaction with an acid.

9. 🔄 Physical Changes

A physical change is a change in which no new substance is formed.

Examples

  • 🧊 Melting ice
  • 💧 Freezing water
  • 💨 Evaporation of water
  • ✂️ Cutting paper
  • 🪨 Breaking a rock
  • 🧂 Dissolving salt in water

📌 Key Point

In a physical change, the chemical identity of the substance is maintained.

10. ⚗️ Chemical Changes

A chemical change occurs when one or more new substances are formed.

Examples

  • 🔥 Burning a fuel
  • 🧲 Rusting of iron
  • 🍎 Rotting of food
  • 🧪 Acid reacting with a metal
  • 🍳 Cooking an egg
  • ⚡ Electrolysis producing new substances

🔎 Signs That May Indicate a Chemical Change

  • 🌡️ Temperature change
  • 💨 Gas being produced
  • 🎨 Colour change
  • 🧱 Formation of a precipitate
  • 💡 Production of light
  • 👃 Formation of a new odour

These observations can provide evidence of a chemical reaction, although no single observation is sufficient in every situation.

11. 🧪 Chemical Reactions

A chemical reaction is a process in which substances are converted into new substances.

The substances that take part in a reaction are called reactants, while the substances formed are called products.

⚗️ Example: Magnesium Burning in Oxygen

Word equation:

Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide

Symbol equation:

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Here, magnesium and oxygen are the reactants, while magnesium oxide is the product.

📌 Reaction Rule

Reactants → Products

The arrow shows the direction in which the reaction is represented.

12. ⚖️ Conservation of Mass

In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged to form new substances. Atoms are not created or destroyed in an ordinary chemical reaction.

This is why chemical equations must be balanced.

🧪 Example

Word equation:

Hydrogen + Oxygen → Water

Symbol equation:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

There are four hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms on each side of the balanced equation.

⚠️ Exam Tip

When balancing an equation, change the coefficients in front of formulas. Do not change the small numbers inside chemical formulas, because that would change the substance itself.

13. 🧩 Pure Substances

A pure substance contains only one chemical substance and has a fixed composition.

Pure substances include elements and compounds.

Type Description Example
⚛️ Element Contains only one type of atom. Iron, Fe
🧪 Compound Contains two or more different elements chemically combined. Water, H₂O

14. ⚛️ Elements

An element is a pure substance made up of only one type of atom.

Elements are represented using chemical symbols.

Element Symbol
Hydrogen H
Oxygen O
Carbon C
Nitrogen N
Sodium Na
Magnesium Mg
Aluminium Al
Chlorine Cl
Iron Fe
Copper Cu

📌 Symbol Rules

  • The first letter of an element symbol is always capitalised.
  • If there is a second letter, it is lowercase.
  • Some symbols come from the English name, while others come from older or Latin names.

Example: Na represents sodium and Fe represents iron.

15. 🧪 Compounds

A compound is a pure substance formed when two or more different elements are chemically combined in fixed proportions.

Examples

Compound Formula Elements Present
Water H₂O Hydrogen and oxygen
Carbon dioxide CO₂ Carbon and oxygen
Sodium chloride NaCl Sodium and chlorine
Calcium carbonate CaCO₃ Calcium, carbon and oxygen

🧠 Compound vs Element

An element contains one type of atom, while a compound contains atoms of different elements chemically combined.

16. 🥤 Mixtures

A mixture contains two or more substances that are physically combined rather than chemically combined.

Examples

  • 🌬️ Air
  • 🌊 Seawater
  • 🪨 Soil
  • 🥣 Sand and water
  • 🧂 Salt and sand
Mixture Components
Air Nitrogen, oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide and other gases
Seawater Water, dissolved salts and other substances
Soil Mineral particles, organic matter, air, water and organisms

17. 🧪 Mixtures vs Compounds

Mixture Compound
Substances are physically combined. Elements are chemically combined.
Composition can vary. Elements are present in fixed proportions.
Components generally retain their individual properties. The compound has properties different from its constituent elements.
Components can usually be separated by physical methods. Elements require chemical processes to be separated.
Example: iron and sulfur mixed together. Example: iron sulfide, FeS.

18. 🧲 Separating Mixtures

Since the substances in a mixture are not chemically combined, they can often be separated using physical methods.

Method Used For
🧺 Filtration Separating an insoluble solid from a liquid.
🔥 Evaporation Recovering a dissolved solid from a solution.
💧 Simple distillation Obtaining a solvent from a solution.
🧪 Fractional distillation Separating miscible liquids with different boiling points.
🎨 Chromatography Separating components of a mixture, especially dissolved substances.
🧲 Magnetic separation Separating magnetic materials from non-magnetic materials.
🪨 Decantation Separating a liquid from a settled solid or from another immiscible liquid.

19. 🔬 Chemistry Laboratory

Chemistry is an experimental science. Chemists use laboratory equipment to carry out experiments, make observations and collect measurements.

Common Laboratory Apparatus

Apparatus Main Use
🧪 Test tube Holding small quantities of substances and carrying out small-scale reactions.
⚗️ Beaker Holding, mixing and heating liquids.
🧪 Conical flask Mixing liquids and carrying out reactions with reduced risk of splashing.
📏 Measuring cylinder Measuring a volume of liquid.
💧 Pipette Measuring and transferring a fixed volume of liquid accurately.
⚖️ Balance Measuring mass.
🌡️ Thermometer Measuring temperature.
🔥 Bunsen burner Providing heat.

20. 🦺 Chemistry Laboratory Safety

Safety is extremely important when working in a Chemistry laboratory.

Important Safety Rules

  • 🥽 Wear appropriate eye protection when required.
  • 🧥 Wear suitable protective clothing.
  • 🚫 Never taste chemicals.
  • 👃 Never directly inhale chemicals.
  • 🔥 Keep flammable substances away from naked flames.
  • 🧪 Follow instructions before carrying out an experiment.
  • 🧹 Keep the working area clean.
  • ⚠️ Report accidents and spills immediately.
  • 🧴 Label chemicals and containers correctly.
  • 🗑️ Dispose of chemicals using the appropriate procedure.

⚠️ Important Laboratory Rule

Never carry out an unfamiliar experiment without proper instructions and supervision.

21. 🧠 Scientific Method in Chemistry

Chemistry experiments follow a logical process that helps scientists investigate questions and obtain reliable results.

  1. ❓ Identify a problem or ask a question.
  2. 💭 Suggest a hypothesis or prediction.
  3. 🧪 Plan an experiment.
  4. 📊 Collect observations and measurements.
  5. 🔎 Analyse the results.
  6. 📝 Draw a conclusion.
  7. 🔁 Repeat or improve the investigation when necessary.

22. 📊 Observations and Measurements

A good Chemistry student must be able to distinguish between observations and measurements.

Type Meaning Example
👀 Observation Something noticed during an experiment. A colourless solution becomes blue.
📏 Measurement A quantity determined using an instrument. The temperature is 75 °C.

📌 Exam Tip

In practical questions, record what you actually observe. Do not write an explanation as though it were the observation.

23. 🧪 Chemical Equations: A First Look

Chemical equations are a shorter way of representing chemical reactions.

Example 1: Zinc and Hydrochloric Acid

Word equation:

Zinc + Hydrochloric acid → Zinc chloride + Hydrogen

Symbol equation:

Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

Example 2: Calcium Carbonate and Hydrochloric Acid

Word equation:

Calcium carbonate + Hydrochloric acid → Calcium chloride + Water + Carbon dioxide

Symbol equation:

CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂

📌 Our Chemistry Rule

When learning reactions, always understand the word equation first, then connect it to the symbol equation.

24. 🧾 Chemical Formulae

A chemical formula shows the elements present in a substance and the relative number of atoms of each element.

Substance Formula Meaning
Water H₂O 2 hydrogen atoms for every 1 oxygen atom.
Carbon dioxide CO₂ 1 carbon atom for every 2 oxygen atoms.
Oxygen gas O₂ Two oxygen atoms bonded together.
Hydrogen gas H₂ Two hydrogen atoms bonded together.
Sodium chloride NaCl Sodium and chlorine in a 1:1 ratio of ions.

25. 🔢 Subscripts and Coefficients

Two important numbers appear frequently in chemical equations: subscripts and coefficients.

Number Meaning Example
Subscript Shows the number of atoms of an element within one formula unit or molecule. H₂O contains two H atoms.
Coefficient Shows the number of particles or formula units represented. 2H₂O represents two H₂O molecules.

⚠️ Very Important

Never change a subscript when balancing an equation. Change the coefficient instead.

For example, the correct balanced equation is:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

26. 🧪 Chemical Reactions You Should Recognise

At O/L level, you will meet many common types of chemical reactions. You should gradually learn to recognise their reactants, products and observations.

Reaction Word Equation
🔥 Combustion Fuel + Oxygen → Products
🧪 Acid + Metal Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen
🧪 Acid + Base Acid + Base → Salt + Water
🧪 Acid + Carbonate Acid + Carbonate → Salt + Water + Carbon dioxide
⚗️ Precipitation Soluble substances react to form an insoluble solid.

📌 Example: Acid + Metal

Word equation:

Magnesium + Hydrochloric acid → Magnesium chloride + Hydrogen

Symbol equation:

Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂

27. 🧠 Key Chemistry Terms

Term Meaning

29. ⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Do not call a mixture a compound.
  • ❌ Do not say that a physical change creates a new substance.
  • ❌ Do not change subscripts when balancing equations.
  • ❌ Do not forget that the first letter of an element symbol is capitalised.
  • ❌ Do not confuse reactants with products.
  • ❌ Do not give an explanation when a practical question asks only for an observation.
  • ❌ Do not assume every colour or temperature change automatically proves a chemical reaction.
  • ❌ Do not ignore laboratory safety instructions.

31. 📚 Final Takeaway

Chemistry begins with understanding matter and substances. Before moving into atomic structure, the periodic table, bonding and chemical calculations, you should be comfortable with the difference between elements, compounds and mixtures, physical and chemical changes, and the basic language of chemical reactions.

🧪 Keep one principle in mind as you continue through Chemistry: understand the substance, understand the change, then understand how the change is represented.
