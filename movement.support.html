🦴 Movement and Support

How animals and plants are supported, how the skeleton enables movement, and how muscles and joints work together.

🧠 1. Introduction to Movement and Support

Movement is the change in position of the whole organism or part of an organism. Movement allows organisms to respond to their surroundings, obtain food, escape danger and carry out other essential activities.

Support is the ability of an organism or part of an organism to maintain its shape and provide a firm framework against which forces can act.

💡 Why are movement and support important?

  • 🏃 Movement allows animals to move from one place to another.
  • 🍎 Animals move to obtain food and water.
  • 🛡️ Movement can help organisms escape predators or danger.
  • ❤️ Internal movement helps substances move around the body.
  • 🦴 Support maintains body shape.
  • 🛡️ The skeleton protects important organs.
  • 💪 The skeleton provides attachment points for muscles.

🦴 2. Support in Animals

Animals require a supporting structure because most animals have bodies that cannot maintain their shape without some form of support.

In humans and other vertebrates, the main supporting structure is the internal skeleton (endoskeleton).

🦴 Functions of the Human Skeleton

  1. Support: The skeleton gives the body its shape and supports soft tissues.
  2. Protection: Bones protect delicate organs. For example, the skull protects the brain and the rib cage protects the heart and lungs.
  3. Movement: Bones provide attachment points and act as levers when muscles contract.
  4. Blood cell production: Bone marrow produces blood cells.
  5. Mineral storage: Bones store important minerals such as calcium and phosphorus.

🦴 3. The Human Skeleton

The human skeleton is the framework of bones that supports and protects the body and works with muscles and joints to produce movement.

🖼️ Human Skeleton — Major Bones

Important bones to know: skull, vertebral column, rib cage, sternum, shoulder girdle, humerus, radius, ulna, pelvic girdle, femur, patella, tibia and fibula.

🧍 5. Axial and Appendicular Skeleton

The human skeleton can be divided into two major parts: the axial skeleton and the appendicular skeleton.

Forms the main central axis of the body. Includes the limbs and the structures attaching them to the body.
Includes the skull, vertebral column and rib cage. Includes the arms, legs, shoulder girdle and pelvic girdle.
Mainly involved in support and protection. Mainly associated with movement of the limbs.

🦿 6. Joints

A joint is a place where two or more bones meet. Joints allow bones to move relative to one another and can also hold bones together.

🔩 Why are joints important?

  • Allow movement between bones.
  • Hold bones together.
  • Provide flexibility to the skeleton.
  • Help the body perform coordinated movements.

🦿 7. Types of Joints

Joints can be classified according to the amount and type of movement they allow.

🔒 A. Fixed / Immovable Joints

These joints allow little or no movement.

Example: joints between the bones of the skull.

Their main importance is protection.

↔️ B. Slightly Movable Joints

These joints allow only limited movement.

Example: joints between some vertebrae of the vertebral column.

🔄 C. Freely Movable / Synovial Joints

These joints allow a wide range of movement.

Examples include the elbow, knee, shoulder and hip joints.

Synovial joints contain structures that reduce friction and hold the bones together.

🖼️ Types and Structure of Joints

🔩 9. Structure of a Synovial Joint

A synovial joint is a freely movable joint. It contains several structures that allow smooth movement while keeping the bones together.

Structure Function
🦴 Bone Provides support and acts as a lever during movement.
🛡️ Cartilage Covers the ends of bones and reduces friction and impact.
💧 Synovial fluid Lubricates the joint and reduces friction.
🔒 Ligament Connects bone to bone and helps stabilise the joint.
💪 Tendon Connects muscle to bone and transmits the force of muscle contraction.
🧴 Synovial membrane Produces synovial fluid.
🛡️ Joint capsule Surrounds and helps protect the joint.

🔗 10. Ligaments and Tendons

Ligament Tendon
Connects bone to bone. Connects muscle to bone.
Helps stabilise joints. Transfers the force produced by muscle contraction to bones.
Important for preventing excessive movement. Important for producing movement of bones.

⚠️ Common Mistake

Do not confuse these two:

Ligament = bone → bone 🔗

Tendon = muscle → bone 💪

🦴 11. Structure of a Long Bone

Long bones are bones that are longer than they are wide. Examples include the femur and humerus.

🖼️ Structure of a Long Bone

Part Function / Description
🦴 Epiphysis The enlarged end of a long bone.
🦴 Diaphysis The long central shaft of the bone.
💪 Compact bone Dense, strong bone forming much of the outer part of the shaft.
🧽 Spongy bone Less dense bone found mainly toward the ends of the bone.
🩸 Bone marrow Found inside bones; red marrow is involved in blood cell production.
🛡️ Periosteum A tough connective tissue covering on the outer surface of bone.
🛡️ Cartilage Covers joint surfaces and reduces friction between bones.
🩸 Blood vessels Supply bone cells with oxygen and nutrients and remove wastes.

💪 12. Muscles and Movement

Muscles produce movement by contracting. A muscle can become shorter and thicker when it contracts, producing a pulling force.

💡 Important Point

Muscles can pull on bones when they contract, but they cannot push bones.

Therefore, muscles that move a joint are often arranged in antagonistic pairs.

💪 13. Antagonistic Muscle Pairs

An antagonistic pair is a pair of muscles that work against each other to produce opposite movements.

A common example is the biceps and triceps of the upper arm.

🖼️ Biceps and Triceps — Antagonistic Pair

💪 14. How the Biceps and Triceps Move the Arm

⬆️ Bending the Elbow — Flexion

When the elbow is bent:

  1. The biceps contracts.
  2. The biceps becomes shorter and thicker.
  3. The biceps pulls on the forearm through its tendon.
  4. The forearm moves upward.
  5. The triceps relaxes.

⬇️ Straightening the Elbow — Extension

When the elbow is straightened:

  1. The triceps contracts.
  2. The triceps becomes shorter and thicker.
  3. The triceps pulls on the forearm.
  4. The forearm moves downward and the elbow straightens.
  5. The biceps relaxes.
Movement Contracting Muscle Relaxing Muscle
⬆️ Flexion / bending Biceps Triceps
⬇️ Extension / straightening Triceps Biceps

🧠 15. How Bones, Joints and Muscles Work Together

Movement of a limb requires the coordinated action of bones, joints and muscles.

🔄 The sequence

  1. 🧠 A nerve impulse stimulates a muscle.
  2. 💪 The muscle contracts.
  3. 🔗 The tendon transmits the force to a bone.
  4. 🦴 The bone moves around a joint.
  5. 🦿 The joint allows controlled movement between the bones.

⚙️ 16. Bones as Levers

During movement, bones act as levers. Joints act as points around which the bones move, while muscles provide the force needed to move them.

🎯 Remember

Bone = lever 🦴

Joint = pivot / fulcrum 🦿

Muscle = force 💪

🌱 17. Support in Plants

Plants do not have a skeleton like vertebrate animals. Their support comes from their cells, tissues and the pressure inside their cells.

🌱 How plants are supported

  • Cell walls: Cellulose cell walls provide strength and help maintain cell shape.
  • Turgor pressure: Water entering plant cells makes them firm. This helps support non-woody parts such as young stems and leaves.
  • Vascular tissues: Xylem provides mechanical support as well as transporting water and mineral salts.
  • Lignified tissues: Lignin strengthens certain plant tissues and provides additional support, especially in woody plants.

🌱 18. Movement Responses in Plants

Plants cannot move from one place to another like animals, but parts of plants can grow in response to environmental stimuli.

A tropism is a growth response of a plant part to a directional stimulus.

☀️ Phototropism

Phototropism is the growth response of a plant to light.

The shoot of a plant usually shows positive phototropism because it grows towards light.

This helps the plant obtain more light for photosynthesis.

🌍 Gravitropism / Geotropism

Gravitropism is the growth response of a plant to gravity.

Plant Part Response to Gravity Type
🌱 Roots Grow towards gravity. Positive gravitropism
🌿 Shoots Grow away from gravity. Negative gravitropism

🏃 19. Importance of Movement in Animals

  • 🍎 Finding and obtaining food.
  • 💧 Finding water.
  • 🛡️ Escaping predators and danger.
  • 🏠 Finding suitable habitats.
  • ❤️ Reproduction and finding mates.
  • 🌡️ Moving to favourable environmental conditions.
  • 🧭 Responding to changes in the environment.

⚠️ 20. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. ❌ Saying muscles push bones.
    ✅ Muscles contract and pull on bones.
  2. ❌ Confusing tendons and ligaments.
    ✅ Tendon = muscle to bone; ligament = bone to bone.
  3. ❌ Saying both muscles in an antagonistic pair contract at the same time for normal movement.
    ✅ Usually one contracts while the other relaxes for the opposite movement.
  4. ❌ Saying the biceps straightens the arm.
    ✅ Biceps mainly bends the elbow; triceps mainly straightens it.
  5. ❌ Confusing cartilage with bone.
    ✅ Cartilage is a flexible connective tissue that covers joint surfaces and reduces friction.
  6. ❌ Forgetting that joints are where bones meet.
  7. ❌ Confusing phototropism with gravitropism.
    ✅ Phototropism = response to light.
    ✅ Gravitropism = response to gravity.
