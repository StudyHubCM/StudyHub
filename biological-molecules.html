🧪 Biological Molecules & Food

Learn about carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, DNA, nutrients, food tests, their functions, sources and the key facts needed for your O/L Biology examination.

🧬 1. Introduction to Biological Molecules

Biological molecules are chemical substances found in living organisms. They are essential for building cells, releasing energy, carrying out life processes and controlling activities in the body.

The major biological molecules studied at O/L level are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and DNA.

⭐ Key Idea

Most biological molecules contain the element carbon (C). Many carbon-containing molecules found in living organisms are called organic molecules.

Important Terms

Monomer: A small molecule that can join with other similar molecules to form a larger molecule.

Polymer: A large molecule made from many smaller repeating units called monomers.

Biological molecule: A molecule produced by or found in living organisms.

Nutrient: A substance obtained from food that is needed by the body for energy, growth, repair and proper functioning.

🍞 2. Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are biological molecules made mainly from the elements carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.

They are an important source of energy for living organisms.

🧠 Types of Carbohydrates

  • Glucose — a simple sugar used in respiration.
  • Maltose — a sugar formed from two glucose molecules.
  • Starch — a storage carbohydrate found mainly in plants.
  • Glycogen — a storage carbohydrate found mainly in animals.
  • Cellulose — a carbohydrate forming plant cell walls.

Functions of Carbohydrates

  • Provide energy for respiration.
  • Provide energy for movement and other activities.
  • Glucose is used by cells during respiration.
  • Starch stores energy in plants.
  • Glycogen stores energy in animals.
  • Cellulose provides strength to plant cell walls.
  • Carbohydrates can be converted into other useful substances.
  • Dietary fibre from plant carbohydrates helps food move through the digestive system.

Sources of Carbohydrates

  • Rice
  • Corn
  • Cassava
  • Plantain
  • Potatoes
  • Bread
  • Pasta
  • Fruits

🥑 3. Lipids — Fats and Oils

Lipids include fats and oils. They contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.

Unlike carbohydrates, lipids contain proportionally less oxygen.

A lipid molecule is formed from fatty acids and glycerol.

🔬 Structure of a Lipid

One glycerol molecule combines with three fatty acid molecules to form a triglyceride.

Glycerol + 3 fatty acids → lipid

Functions of Lipids

  • Provide a concentrated source of energy.
  • Store energy for long periods.
  • Provide insulation against heat loss.
  • Protect delicate organs from physical damage.
  • Form an important part of cell membranes.
  • Help the body absorb fat-soluble vitamins.
  • Provide essential fatty acids needed by the body.
  • Help form certain hormones and other important substances.

Sources of Lipids

  • Vegetable oils
  • Groundnuts and peanuts
  • Avocado
  • Butter
  • Milk and dairy products
  • Egg yolk
  • Fish
  • Meat

🥩 4. Proteins

Proteins are large biological molecules made from smaller molecules called amino acids.

Proteins contain mainly carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen. Some proteins also contain sulfur.

🧩 Amino Acids

Amino acids join together to form long chains. These chains fold into specific shapes to form proteins.

Amino acids → protein

Functions of Proteins

  • Growth of the body.
  • Repair of damaged tissues.
  • Formation of muscles.
  • Formation of enzymes.
  • Formation of antibodies for defence against disease.
  • Formation of some hormones.
  • Formation of important body structures such as skin and hair.
  • Can be used as an energy source when necessary.

Sources of Proteins

  • Beans
  • Groundnuts
  • Fish
  • Meat
  • Eggs
  • Milk
  • Soya beans
  • Lentils

🧬 5. DNA

DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid. It is a biological molecule found mainly in the chromosomes of cells.

DNA contains the genetic information that controls inherited characteristics and helps determine how an organism develops and functions.

🧬 Structure of DNA

DNA is made from smaller units called nucleotides.

Each nucleotide contains:

  • A sugar called deoxyribose.
  • A phosphate group.
  • A nitrogen-containing base.

The DNA molecule consists of two strands twisted together to form a double helix.

Importance of DNA

  • Stores genetic information.
  • Controls inherited characteristics.
  • Provides information needed for making proteins.
  • Allows genetic information to be passed from parents to offspring.
  • Helps control the activities of cells.
  • Allows cells to produce proteins required for growth and repair.

🧪 7. Food Tests

Food tests are chemical tests used to identify particular nutrients or biological molecules present in a food sample.

At O/L level, you should know the test, the reagent, the procedure and the positive result.

🟦 8. Test for Starch

🧪 Iodine Test

Reagent: Iodine solution.

Procedure: Add a few drops of iodine solution to the food sample.

Positive result: The colour changes from brown/orange to blue-black.

Conclusion: Starch is present.

🍬 9. Test for Reducing Sugar

🧪 Benedict's Test

Reagent: Benedict's solution.

Procedure: Add Benedict's solution to the food sample and heat the mixture in a hot water bath.

Positive result: The solution changes from blue through green, yellow and orange to brick-red, depending on the amount of reducing sugar present.

Conclusion: A colour change indicates the presence of reducing sugar.

🥛 10. Test for Protein

🧪 Biuret Test

Reagent: Biuret reagent.

Procedure: Add Biuret reagent to the food sample.

Positive result: The colour changes from blue to lilac/purple.

Conclusion: Protein is present.

🥜 11. Test for Lipids

🧪 Ethanol Emulsion Test

Reagent: Ethanol.

Procedure: Shake the food sample with ethanol. Then add water.

Positive result: A cloudy or milky-white emulsion forms.

Conclusion: Lipid is present.

📋 12. Food Tests — Master Table

Food Substance Reagent Important Procedure Positive Result

Reducing sugar Benedict's solution Heat in a water bath Green → yellow → orange → brick-red
Protein Biuret reagent Add Biuret reagent Lilac/purple
Lipid Ethanol + water Shake with ethanol, then add water Cloudy/milky-white emulsion

🥗 13. Other Important Nutrients in Food

A healthy diet contains more than carbohydrates, proteins and lipids. The body also needs vitamins, mineral salts, fibre and water.

Vitamins

Vitamins are required in small quantities to keep the body healthy and allow normal body processes.

🍊 Vitamin C

  • Helps maintain healthy tissues.
  • Important for wound healing.
  • Supports normal functioning of the immune system.
  • Helps maintain healthy skin.
  • Helps maintain healthy gums.
  • Found in citrus fruits and many fresh fruits and vegetables.

☀️ Vitamin D

  • Helps the body absorb calcium.
  • Important for healthy bones.
  • Important for healthy teeth.
  • Helps maintain normal calcium levels in the body.
  • Can be obtained through the action of sunlight on the skin.
  • Also found in some foods such as oily fish and eggs.

Mineral Salts

🦴 Calcium

  • Needed for strong bones.
  • Needed for strong teeth.
  • Important for normal muscle function.
  • Important in blood clotting.
  • Helps nerves function normally.
  • Works with vitamin D in maintaining healthy bones.

🩸 Iron

  • Needed to make haemoglobin.
  • Haemoglobin is found in red blood cells.
  • Helps transport oxygen around the body.
  • Supports aerobic respiration by enabling oxygen transport.
  • Important for normal growth and development.
  • Iron deficiency can cause anaemia.

💧 14. Fibre and Water

Fibre

Dietary fibre is mainly obtained from plant foods. Humans cannot digest cellulose, but fibre is still important for the digestive system.

  • Adds bulk to food in the digestive system.
  • Helps food move through the intestines.
  • Helps prevent constipation.
  • Supports normal bowel movement.
  • Helps maintain a healthy digestive system.
  • Can be obtained from fruits and vegetables.

Water

  • Acts as a solvent for many substances.
  • Provides a medium for chemical reactions.
  • Helps transport dissolved substances around the body.
  • Helps transport waste products.
  • Helps regulate body temperature.
  • Forms an important part of cells and body fluids.
  • Helps maintain normal body processes.
  • Is lost from the body through processes such as sweating and urine production.

🍽️ 15. Balanced Diet

A balanced diet is a diet that contains all the necessary nutrients in the correct proportions to maintain health.

🥗 Components of a Balanced Diet

  • Carbohydrates
  • Proteins
  • Lipids
  • Vitamins
  • Mineral salts
  • Fibre
  • Water

Importance of a Balanced Diet

  • Provides energy for daily activities.
  • Provides materials needed for growth.
  • Allows repair of damaged tissues.
  • Helps maintain healthy body systems.
  • Helps prevent nutrient-deficiency diseases.
  • Supports normal growth and development.
  • Helps maintain a healthy body weight.
  • Supports the immune system and general health.

⚠️ 16. Nutrient Deficiency

A deficiency occurs when the body does not obtain enough of a particular nutrient.

Nutrient Deficiency Possible Effect
Vitamin C Vitamin C deficiency Scurvy
Vitamin D Vitamin D deficiency Weak or poorly formed bones
Iron Iron deficiency Anaemia
Calcium Calcium deficiency Weak bones and teeth

🧪 17. How to Answer Food-Test Questions

When a question asks you to describe a food test, do not give only the name of the reagent. Include the important steps and the expected observation.

⭐ Exam Answer Pattern

1. Name the reagent

2. State what you do

3. State the colour change or observation

4. State what the result means

❌ 18. Common O/L Mistakes

  • Do not confuse starch with reducing sugar.
  • Remember that Benedict's test requires heating.
  • Do not write blue as the positive result for starch — the positive result is blue-black.
  • Do not confuse Biuret with Benedict's solution.
  • Remember that protein is made from amino acids.
  • Remember that lipids are formed from fatty acids and glycerol.
  • Remember that DNA is made from nucleotides.
  • Do not confuse glycogen with starch: glycogen is the main storage carbohydrate in animals, while starch is the main storage carbohydrate in plants.
