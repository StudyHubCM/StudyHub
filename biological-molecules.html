🧪 Biological Molecules & Food

Learn about carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, DNA, nutrients, food tests, their functions, sources and the key facts needed for your O/L Biology examination.

🧬 1. Introduction to Biological Molecules

Biological molecules are chemical substances found in living organisms. They are essential for building cells, releasing energy, carrying out life processes and controlling activities in the body.

The major biological molecules studied at O/L level are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and DNA.

⭐ Key Idea

Most biological molecules contain the element carbon (C). Many carbon-containing molecules found in living organisms are called organic molecules.

Important Terms

Monomer: A small molecule that can join with other similar molecules to form a larger molecule.

Polymer: A large molecule made from many smaller repeating units called monomers.

Biological molecule: A molecule produced by or found in living organisms.

Nutrient: A substance obtained from food that is needed by the body for energy, growth, repair and proper functioning.

🍞 2. Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are organic compounds made mainly from carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.

They are one of the main sources of energy for living organisms.

Types of Carbohydrates

The main carbohydrates studied at O/L level include glucose, starch, glycogen and cellulose.

Monosaccharides

Monosaccharides are simple sugars made from a single sugar unit.

Examples: glucose and fructose.

Glucose

Glucose is an important simple sugar used by cells during respiration to release energy.

Polysaccharides

Polysaccharides are large carbohydrates made from many glucose molecules joined together.

Examples: starch, glycogen and cellulose.

Starch

Starch is the main storage carbohydrate in plants.

Functions / Importance of Carbohydrates

  1. Provide energy for respiration.
  2. Supply glucose needed by cells.
  3. Store energy in plants as starch.
  4. Store energy in animals as glycogen.
  5. Cellulose provides structural support in plant cell walls.
  6. Carbohydrates are important components of many biological structures.
  7. They can be converted into other substances needed by the body.
  8. They provide an important source of energy in the diet.

Sources of Carbohydrates

  • Rice
  • Corn / maize
  • Potatoes
  • Yams
  • Cassava
  • Bread
  • Pasta
  • Fruits

⭐ O/L Key Point

Starch is stored mainly in plants, while glycogen is the main storage carbohydrate in animals.

🥑 3. Lipids – Fats and Oils

Lipids are organic compounds that include fats and oils.

They contain mainly carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.

A typical lipid is formed from fatty acids and glycerol.

Properties of Lipids

  • They do not dissolve in water.
  • They are generally soluble in organic solvents.
  • Fats are usually solid at room temperature.
  • Oils are usually liquid at room temperature.
  • They contain a large amount of stored chemical energy.
  • They are less dense than water.

Functions / Importance of Lipids

  1. Store energy for long periods.
  2. Provide a concentrated source of energy.
  3. Form part of cell membranes.
  4. Provide thermal insulation and reduce heat loss.
  5. Protect delicate organs from physical damage.
  6. Help the body absorb fat-soluble vitamins such as vitamins A, D, E and K.
  7. Provide essential fatty acids needed by the body.
  8. Can be used as an energy reserve when carbohydrate stores are limited.

Sources of Lipids

  • Vegetable oils
  • Groundnuts / peanuts
  • Avocado
  • Nuts and seeds
  • Butter
  • Egg yolk
  • Fish
  • Meat

🥚 4. Proteins

Proteins are large organic molecules made from smaller units called amino acids.

Proteins contain mainly carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen. Some proteins also contain sulfur.

Amino Acids

Amino acids are the building blocks, or monomers, of proteins. Many amino acids join together to form a protein molecule.

Functions / Importance of Proteins

  1. Growth of new cells and tissues.
  2. Repair of damaged tissues.
  3. Formation of enzymes.
  4. Formation of some hormones.
  5. Formation of antibodies for defence against disease.
  6. Formation of muscles and other body structures.
  7. Formation of important substances such as haemoglobin.
  8. Can be used as an energy source when necessary.

Sources of Proteins

  • Fish
  • Meat
  • Eggs
  • Milk
  • Beans
  • Groundnuts / peanuts
  • Soya beans
  • Lentils

⭐ O/L Key Point

Proteins are made from amino acids, while carbohydrates such as starch and glycogen are made from glucose units.

🧬 5. DNA

DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid.

DNA is the genetic material found in cells. It carries genetic information that controls inherited characteristics.

Structure of DNA

DNA is a large molecule made from repeating units called nucleotides.

Each nucleotide contains a sugar, a phosphate group and a nitrogen-containing base.

Importance of DNA

  1. Stores genetic information.
  2. Carries information controlling inherited characteristics.
  3. Provides instructions for making proteins.
  4. Allows genetic information to be passed from parents to offspring.
  5. Helps control the activities of cells through the proteins produced.
  6. Allows organisms to maintain their characteristic features.

🧪 7. Food Tests

Food tests are chemical tests used to identify particular nutrients or biological molecules in food.

At O/L level, you should know the food sample, reagent, method, positive result and conclusion for each test.

🟤 8. Test for Starch – Iodine Test

Reagent

Iodine solution.

Method

  1. Place a small amount of the food sample on a white tile or suitable container.
  2. Add a few drops of iodine solution.
  3. Observe the colour change.

Positive Result

If starch is present, the iodine changes from brown/orange to blue-black.

Conclusion

A blue-black colour shows that starch is present.

🍬 9. Test for Reducing Sugar – Benedict's Test

Reagent

Benedict's solution.

Method

  1. Add Benedict's solution to the food sample.
  2. Heat the mixture in a hot water bath.
  3. Observe the colour change.

Results

If reducing sugar is present, the solution may change through colours such as:

  • Blue – no reducing sugar detected.
  • Green – small amount.
  • Yellow – more reducing sugar.
  • Orange – larger amount.
  • Brick-red precipitate – high amount.

Conclusion

A colour change from blue towards orange or brick-red indicates the presence of a reducing sugar.

Important: Benedict's test requires heating.

🥩 10. Test for Protein – Biuret Test

Reagents

Biuret reagent.

Method

  1. Add the food sample to a test tube.
  2. Add Biuret reagent.
  3. Mix carefully and observe the colour.

Positive Result

A positive test produces a lilac or purple colour.

Conclusion

A lilac/purple colour shows that protein is present.

🫒 11. Test for Lipids – Ethanol Emulsion Test

Reagent

Ethanol.

Method

  1. Add ethanol to the food sample.
  2. Shake the mixture thoroughly.
  3. Add water.
  4. Observe the appearance of the mixture.

Positive Result

A positive test produces a cloudy or milky-white emulsion.

Conclusion

A milky-white emulsion indicates that lipid is present.

📋 12. Food Tests Master Table

Food Substance Reagent Method Positive Result
Starch Iodine solution Add iodine Blue-black
Reducing sugar Benedict's solution Add Benedict's and heat Green → yellow → orange → brick-red
Protein Biuret reagent Add Biuret reagent Lilac / purple
Lipid Ethanol + water Shake with ethanol, then add water Cloudy / milky-white emulsion

⭐ Remember

Iodine → starch

Benedict's → reducing sugar

Biuret → protein

Ethanol emulsion → lipid

🥦 13. Other Important Nutrients in Food

A healthy diet contains more than carbohydrates, proteins and lipids. The body also requires vitamins, mineral ions, fibre and water.

Vitamin C

  • Important for maintaining healthy tissues.
  • Helps wound healing.
  • Supports normal immune function.
  • Found in citrus fruits and many fresh fruits and vegetables.
  • Lack of vitamin C can cause scurvy.
  • Scurvy can cause bleeding gums and poor wound healing.

Vitamin D

  • Helps the body absorb calcium.
  • Important for healthy bones and teeth.
  • Helps maintain normal calcium levels.
  • Can be obtained from certain foods.
  • The body can produce vitamin D when skin is exposed to sunlight.
  • Severe deficiency can contribute to weak or poorly formed bones.

Calcium

  • Needed for strong bones.
  • Needed for strong teeth.
  • Important in muscle contraction.
  • Involved in blood clotting.
  • Helps maintain normal cell functions.
  • Found in milk and other dairy products.

Iron

  • Needed to make haemoglobin.
  • Haemoglobin is found in red blood cells.
  • Haemoglobin transports oxygen.
  • Iron is needed for normal energy release.
  • Good sources include meat and green leafy vegetables.
  • Iron deficiency can cause anaemia.

Fibre

  • Adds bulk to food.
  • Helps food move through the digestive system.
  • Helps prevent constipation.
  • Supports healthy bowel function.
  • Found mainly in plant foods.
  • Good sources include vegetables, fruits and whole grains.

Water

  • Acts as a solvent.
  • Provides a medium for chemical reactions.
  • Helps transport dissolved substances.
  • Helps remove waste products.
  • Helps regulate body temperature.
  • Helps maintain normal cell function.

🍽️ 14. Balanced Diet

A balanced diet is a diet that provides all the essential nutrients in the correct proportions and amounts needed to maintain good health.

Components of a Balanced Diet

  1. Carbohydrates
  2. Proteins
  3. Lipids
  4. Vitamins
  5. Mineral ions
  6. Fibre
  7. Water

Importance of a Balanced Diet

  1. Provides energy.
  2. Supports growth.
  3. Repairs damaged tissues.
  4. Maintains healthy body tissues.
  5. Supports the immune system.
  6. Prevents nutrient deficiency diseases.
  7. Helps maintain normal body functions.
  8. Helps maintain good overall health.

⚠️ 15. Nutrient Deficiency

A deficiency disease occurs when the body does not obtain enough of an essential nutrient for normal functioning.

Nutrient Main Function Deficiency Problem
Vitamin C Healthy tissues and wound healing Scurvy
Vitamin D Calcium absorption and healthy bones Poor bone development
Calcium Strong bones and teeth Weak bones
Iron Haemoglobin formation Anaemia
Protein Growth and tissue repair Poor growth and tissue repair

📝 16. How to Answer Food-Test Questions

In an O/L examination, do not give only the name of the reagent. Learn the complete test.

A Good Answer Should Include:

  1. The substance being tested.
  2. The reagent used.
  3. The procedure or important condition.
  4. The expected colour change.
  5. What the result means.
  6. Any important condition such as heating.

Example

Question: Describe a test for starch.

Answer: Add iodine solution to the food sample. If starch is present, the iodine changes from brown/orange to blue-black.

❌ 17. Common O/L Mistakes

  1. Saying Benedict's solution tests for all sugars.
  2. Forgetting to heat during Benedict's test.
  3. Giving "purple" for the starch test instead of blue-black.
  4. Confusing Biuret with Benedict's solution.
  5. Saying lipids dissolve in water.
  6. Confusing glycogen with starch.
  7. Saying proteins are made from glucose.
  8. Forgetting that proteins are made from amino acids.
  9. Giving only one function when a question asks for several.
  10. Giving a food source instead of the nutrient being tested.
