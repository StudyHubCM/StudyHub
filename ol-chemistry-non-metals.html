🌬️ Non-metals

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Properties, reactions, preparation, uses and important compounds of common non-metals.

1. 🌍 Introduction to Non-metals

Non-metals are elements that generally do not show the typical physical properties of metals. They are found on the right side of the periodic table, apart from hydrogen, which is located on the left.

⭐ Common Non-metals

Hydrogen (H), carbon (C), nitrogen (N), oxygen (O), fluorine (F), phosphorus (P), sulfur (S), chlorine (Cl), bromine (Br) and iodine (I).

2. 🔬 Physical Properties of Non-metals

Property Typical property of non-metals
Electrical conductivity Generally poor conductors of electricity.
Thermal conductivity Generally poor conductors of heat.
Appearance Often dull, although some have distinctive colours and lustre.
Malleability Solid non-metals are generally brittle rather than malleable.
Ductility Solid non-metals are generally not ductile.
State They may be solids, liquids or gases at room temperature.
Melting and boiling points Many have relatively low values, although there are exceptions.

3. ⚠️ Important Exceptions

The physical properties above are generalisations. For example, graphite, a form of carbon, conducts electricity, even though carbon is a non-metal.

💡 Exam point: Do not say that every non-metal is a poor conductor of electricity. Graphite is an important exception.

4. ⚡ Chemical Properties of Non-metals

Non-metals generally tend to gain or share electrons rather than lose electrons. They can form negative ions or covalent compounds.

5. 🧲 Formation of Negative Ions

A non-metal atom can gain electrons to form a negatively charged ion called an anion.

Word/electron representation: Chlorine atom + electron → chloride ion

Symbol representation: Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

6. 🔗 Covalent Bonding

Many non-metals form covalent bonds by sharing electrons with other atoms.

Covalent compounds commonly contain non-metal atoms joined by shared pairs of electrons.

7. 🧪 Non-metals and Oxygen

Many non-metals react with oxygen to form non-metal oxides.

Word equation: Non-metal + oxygen → non-metal oxide

8. 🔥 Carbon and Oxygen

Carbon burns in sufficient oxygen to form carbon dioxide.

Word equation: Carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: C + O₂ → CO₂

9. 🔥 Sulfur and Oxygen

Sulfur burns in oxygen to form sulfur dioxide.

Word equation: Sulfur + oxygen → sulfur dioxide

Symbol equation: S + O₂ → SO₂

10. 🔥 Phosphorus and Oxygen

Phosphorus reacts with oxygen to form phosphorus(V) oxide.

Word equation: Phosphorus + oxygen → phosphorus(V) oxide

Symbol equation: 4P + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀

11. 💧 Non-metal Oxides and Water

Many non-metal oxides react with water to form acids. Such oxides are often described as acidic oxides.

12. 🧪 Carbon Dioxide and Water

Word equation: Carbon dioxide + water ⇌ carbonic acid

Symbol equation: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃

13. 🧪 Sulfur Dioxide and Water

Word equation: Sulfur dioxide + water ⇌ sulfurous acid

Symbol equation: SO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂SO₃

14. 🧪 Acidic Oxides

An acidic oxide is an oxide that reacts with a base to form a salt and water, and many acidic oxides also react with water to form acids.

Examples: Carbon dioxide (CO₂), sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and sulfur trioxide (SO₃).

15. ⚗️ Acidic Oxides and Alkalis

Acidic oxides can react with alkalis to form salts and water.

Word equation: Carbon dioxide + sodium hydroxide → sodium carbonate + water

Symbol equation: CO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O

16. 🌫️ Carbon

Carbon is a non-metal that exists in several allotropes, including diamond, graphite and graphene.

17. 💎 Diamond

In diamond, each carbon atom is strongly covalently bonded to four other carbon atoms. This produces a giant covalent structure.

Properties of Diamond

  • Very hard.
  • High melting point.
  • Does not conduct electricity.
  • Used for cutting and drilling.
  • Used in jewellery.

18. ✏️ Graphite

Graphite contains layers of carbon atoms. Each carbon atom is bonded to three other carbon atoms, leaving electrons that can move through the structure.

Properties of Graphite

  • Conducts electricity.
  • Soft compared with diamond.
  • Has layers that can slide over one another.
  • Used in electrodes.
  • Used in pencils.

19. 🧬 Graphene

Graphene is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal structure. It has useful electrical and mechanical properties.

20. 🏭 Carbon Monoxide

Carbon monoxide is a poisonous gas produced during incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels.

Word equation: Carbon + limited oxygen → carbon monoxide

Symbol equation: 2C + O₂ → 2CO

21. ⚠️ Carbon Monoxide

Carbon monoxide is dangerous because it reduces the blood's ability to transport oxygen effectively.

🚨 Carbon monoxide is colourless and odourless, so it cannot be reliably detected using the senses. Proper ventilation and safe fuel-burning appliances are important.

22. 🌬️ Nitrogen

Nitrogen is a colourless, odourless gas that makes up about 78% of Earth's atmosphere.

23. 🧪 Properties of Nitrogen

  • Colourless gas.
  • Odourless.
  • Relatively unreactive at room temperature.
  • Does not support combustion.
  • Exists naturally as diatomic molecules, N₂.

24. 🌱 Importance of Nitrogen

Nitrogen is essential for living organisms because it is present in amino acids, proteins and nucleic acids.

25. 🏭 Uses of Nitrogen

  • 🌱 Manufacture of ammonia and fertilisers.
  • 🥫 Food packaging to reduce oxidation.
  • 🧊 Liquid nitrogen for cooling and freezing.
  • 🏭 Providing an inert atmosphere in some industrial processes.

26. ⚗️ Hydrogen

Hydrogen is the lightest element and exists as diatomic molecules, H₂. It is a colourless gas and is highly flammable.

27. 🔥 Hydrogen and Oxygen

Hydrogen burns in oxygen to produce water.

Word equation: Hydrogen + oxygen → water

Symbol equation: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

28. 🏭 Uses of Hydrogen

  • Manufacture of ammonia.
  • Hydrogenation of some oils.
  • Fuel applications.
  • Some industrial reduction processes.

29. 🌿 Sulfur

Sulfur is a yellow solid non-metal. It is an important raw material in the manufacture of sulfuric acid.

30. 🔥 Burning Sulfur

Sulfur burns in oxygen with a blue flame and produces sulfur dioxide.

Word equation: Sulfur + oxygen → sulfur dioxide

Symbol equation: S + O₂ → SO₂

31. 🏭 Sulfur Dioxide

Sulfur dioxide is a colourless gas with a strong, irritating smell. It is an important atmospheric pollutant.

32. 🌧️ Sulfur Dioxide and Acid Rain

Sulfur dioxide released into the atmosphere can be oxidised and can contribute to the formation of sulfuric acid and acid deposition.

Word equation: Sulfur dioxide + oxygen → sulfur trioxide

Symbol equation: 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃

Word equation: Sulfur trioxide + water → sulfuric acid

Symbol equation: SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄

33. 🏭 Uses of Sulfur

  • Manufacture of sulfuric acid.
  • Production of some fertilisers and chemicals.
  • Vulcanisation of rubber.
  • Manufacture of some fungicides and other products.

34. 🟢 Chlorine

Chlorine is a greenish-yellow gas at room temperature. It exists as diatomic molecules, Cl₂.

35. 🧪 Properties of Chlorine

  • Greenish-yellow gas.
  • Distinctive pungent smell.
  • Reactive non-metal.
  • Exists as Cl₂.
  • Can act as an oxidising agent.

36. 💧 Chlorine and Water

Chlorine reacts reversibly with water to form hydrochloric acid and hypochlorous acid.

Word equation: Chlorine + water ⇌ hydrochloric acid + hypochlorous acid

Symbol equation: Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO

37. 🚰 Chlorine and Water Treatment

Chlorine is used to disinfect water because chlorine-containing species can destroy many harmful microorganisms.

💡 Remember: Chlorination is mainly about disinfection, not removing every dissolved impurity from water.

38. 🧪 Chlorine Displacement Reaction

A more reactive halogen can displace a less reactive halogen from a solution containing its halide.

Word equation: Chlorine + sodium bromide → sodium chloride + bromine

Symbol equation: Cl₂ + 2NaBr → 2NaCl + Br₂

39. 🧪 Test for Chloride Ions

Chloride ions can be tested by adding acidified silver nitrate solution. A white precipitate of silver chloride forms.

Word equation: Silver nitrate + chloride ions → silver chloride + nitrate ions

Symbol equation: AgNO₃ + Cl⁻ → AgCl + NO₃⁻

40. 🟣 Bromine

Bromine is a reddish-brown liquid at room temperature and exists as Br₂.

41. 🟣 Properties of Bromine

  • Reddish-brown liquid.
  • Volatile.
  • Reactive non-metal.
  • Exists as Br₂.

42. 🟣 Bromine Displacement

Bromine can displace iodine from a solution containing iodide ions because bromine is more reactive than iodine.

Word equation: Bromine + potassium iodide → potassium bromide + iodine

Symbol equation: Br₂ + 2KI → 2KBr + I₂

43. 🟣 Iodine

Iodine is a dark grey or black solid at room temperature. It exists as diatomic molecules, I₂, and produces a violet vapour when heated.

44. 🧪 Test for Starch Using Iodine

Iodine solution is used as a test for starch. In the presence of starch, iodine produces a blue-black colour.

⭐ This is a test rather than a reaction equation that needs to be written as a balanced chemical equation.

45. 🧂 Halogens

Fluorine, chlorine, bromine and iodine are halogens in Group 17 of the periodic table. They commonly exist as diatomic molecules.

Halogen Formula State at room temperature General colour
Fluorine F₂ Gas Pale yellow
Chlorine Cl₂ Gas Greenish-yellow
Bromine Br₂ Liquid Reddish-brown
Iodine I₂ Solid Dark grey/black solid

46. 📉 Halogen Reactivity

The reactivity of the halogens generally decreases down Group 17.

Reactivity order: Fluorine > Chlorine > Bromine > Iodine

47. 🧪 Halogen Displacement Rule

A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halogen from a solution containing its halide.

Example: Chlorine can displace bromine and iodine, but bromine cannot displace chlorine.

48. 🌱 Phosphorus

Phosphorus is a reactive non-metal that occurs in several allotropes, including white phosphorus and red phosphorus.

49. 🔥 Phosphorus and Oxygen

Word equation: Phosphorus + oxygen → phosphorus(V) oxide

Symbol equation: 4P + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀

50. 🌱 Importance of Phosphorus

Phosphorus compounds are important in fertilisers and biological molecules. Phosphate ions are present in substances such as DNA and ATP.

51. 🌬️ Oxygen

Oxygen is a colourless, odourless gas that makes up about 21% of Earth's atmosphere. It exists naturally as O₂.

52. 🔥 Properties of Oxygen

  • Colourless gas.
  • Odourless.
  • Supports combustion.
  • Essential for aerobic respiration.
  • Exists as O₂.

53. 🧪 Test for Oxygen

Oxygen is tested using a glowing splint. A glowing splint relights in oxygen.

🔥 Important: Oxygen supports combustion, but oxygen itself is not the fuel being burned.

54. 🏭 Uses of Oxygen

  • Medical oxygen under appropriate controlled conditions.
  • Welding and metal cutting.
  • Steel manufacture.
  • Supporting combustion in industrial processes.

55. 🧪 Preparation of Oxygen in the Laboratory

Oxygen can be prepared by decomposing hydrogen peroxide using a catalyst such as manganese(IV) oxide.

Word equation: Hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen

Symbol equation: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂

Catalyst: Manganese(IV) oxide, MnO₂

56. 🌱 Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide is a colourless gas present naturally in the atmosphere. It is produced by respiration and combustion and is used by plants in photosynthesis.

57. 🧪 Test for Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide can be tested using limewater. Limewater turns milky when carbon dioxide is passed through it.

Word equation: Calcium hydroxide + carbon dioxide → calcium carbonate + water

Symbol equation: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O

58. 🌫️ Excess Carbon Dioxide and Limewater

Passing excess carbon dioxide through limewater can make the solution clear again because calcium carbonate reacts with additional carbon dioxide and water.

Word equation: Calcium carbonate + carbon dioxide + water → calcium hydrogencarbonate

Symbol equation: CaCO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O → Ca(HCO₃)₂

59. 🌱 Photosynthesis

Plants use carbon dioxide and water to make glucose in the presence of light and chlorophyll.

Word equation: Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Symbol equation: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

☀️ Light energy and chlorophyll are required.

60. 🧪 Acids from Non-metal Oxides

Some non-metal oxides form acids when they react with water.

Non-metal oxide Product with water
Carbon dioxide Carbonic acid
Sulfur dioxide Sulfurous acid
Sulfur trioxide Sulfuric acid

61. 🌧️ Acid Rain

Acid rain refers to precipitation made more acidic by atmospheric pollutants, especially sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.

62. 🏭 Sources of Sulfur Dioxide

  • Burning sulfur-containing fossil fuels.
  • Some industrial processes.
  • Volcanic activity is a natural source.

63. 🌧️ Effects of Acid Deposition

  • Can damage vegetation.
  • Can acidify lakes and streams.
  • Can damage limestone and marble structures.
  • Can contribute to corrosion of some materials.

64. 🛡️ Reducing Acid Rain

  • Use fuels with lower sulfur content.
  • Remove sulfur compounds from industrial gases.
  • Use cleaner energy sources.
  • Reduce unnecessary fossil-fuel combustion.

65. 🧪 Non-metals with Metals

Non-metals can react with metals to form ionic compounds. The metal generally loses electrons while the non-metal gains electrons.

66. 🧂 Sodium and Chlorine

Sodium transfers an electron to chlorine, producing sodium ions and chloride ions. These ions form sodium chloride.

Word equation: Sodium + chlorine → sodium chloride

Symbol equation: 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl

67. 🧂 Magnesium and Oxygen

Word equation: Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

68. 🧂 Iron and Sulfur

Iron reacts with sulfur when heated to form iron(II) sulfide.

Word equation: Iron + sulfur → iron(II) sulfide

Symbol equation: Fe + S → FeS

69. 🧪 Non-metals with Hydrogen

Hydrogen can combine with some non-metals to form covalent compounds.

Word equation: Hydrogen + chlorine → hydrogen chloride

Symbol equation: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl

70. 💧 Hydrogen Chloride

Hydrogen chloride is a covalent gas. When dissolved in water, it forms hydrochloric acid.

Word equation: Hydrogen chloride + water → hydrochloric acid solution

Symbol representation: HCl(g) → H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

71. 🧪 Preparation of Hydrogen Chloride

Hydrogen chloride can be prepared by reacting sodium chloride with concentrated sulfuric acid under suitable laboratory conditions.

Word equation: Sodium chloride + sulfuric acid → sodium hydrogensulfate + hydrogen chloride

Symbol equation: NaCl + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HCl

72. 🧪 Ammonia

Ammonia is a colourless gas with a pungent smell. It is an important nitrogen-containing compound.

73. 🏭 Formation of Ammonia

Industrially, ammonia is produced from nitrogen and hydrogen in the Haber process.

Word equation: Nitrogen + hydrogen ⇌ ammonia

Symbol equation: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

74. 🌱 Uses of Ammonia

  • Manufacture of fertilisers.
  • Production of nitric acid.
  • Some cleaning products.
  • Manufacture of various nitrogen-containing chemicals.

75. 🧪 Test for Ammonia

Ammonia is alkaline and can turn damp red litmus paper blue.

💡 Ammonia is a gas, while ammonia dissolved in water produces an alkaline solution.

76. 🧪 Nitrogen and Hydrogen

Nitrogen and hydrogen combine reversibly under suitable conditions to form ammonia.

Word equation: Nitrogen + hydrogen ⇌ ammonia

Symbol equation: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

77. 🔬 Diatomic Non-metals

Several non-metal elements exist naturally as molecules containing two atoms.

Element Formula
Hydrogen H₂
Nitrogen N₂
Oxygen O₂
Fluorine F₂
Chlorine Cl₂
Bromine Br₂
Iodine I₂

78. 🧠 Why Diatomic Formulae Matter

When writing chemical equations, remember that hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, fluorine, chlorine, bromine and iodine occur as diatomic molecules under ordinary elemental conditions.

⭐ Example: Do not write H + Cl → HCl for the reaction between the elements. Use H₂ and Cl₂.

Correct symbol equation: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl

79. 🧪 Preparation of Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide can be prepared in the laboratory by reacting a carbonate with a dilute acid.

Word equation: Calcium carbonate + hydrochloric acid → calcium chloride + carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation: CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + CO₂ + H₂O

80. 🧪 Preparation of Hydrogen

Hydrogen can be prepared by reacting a suitable reactive metal with a dilute acid.

Word equation: Zinc + hydrochloric acid → zinc chloride + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

81. 🧪 Preparation of Chlorine

Chlorine can be produced by oxidation of chloride ions, including during electrolysis of concentrated sodium chloride solution.

Word equation: Chloride ions → chlorine + electrons

Symbol representation: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻

82. ⚡ Non-metals in Electrolysis

Some non-metals are produced at the electrodes during electrolysis. For example, chlorine is produced at the anode during electrolysis of concentrated sodium chloride solution.

83. 🧪 Non-metal Oxides: Acidic or Neutral

Oxide Classification

SO₂ Acidic oxide
SO₃ Acidic oxide

Non-metals are essential in living organisms, industry, agriculture and environmental processes.

85. 🔥 Combustion of Non-metals

Many non-metals burn in oxygen and form oxides.

Carbon: C + O₂ → CO₂

Sulfur: S + O₂ → SO₂

Phosphorus: 4P + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀

86. 🧪 Common Non-metal Reactions

Reaction Word equation Symbol equation
Carbon combustion Carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide C + O₂ → CO₂
Sulfur combustion Sulfur + oxygen → sulfur dioxide S + O₂ → SO₂
Hydrogen combustion Hydrogen + oxygen → water 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Chlorine with water Chlorine + water ⇌ hydrochloric acid + hypochlorous acid Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO
Nitrogen and hydrogen Nitrogen + hydrogen ⇌ ammonia N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

87. 📝 Important Definitions

Non-metal: An element that generally does not show the typical physical properties of metals.

Allotrope: A different structural form of the same element in the same physical state.

Acidic oxide: An oxide that can react with a base to form a salt and water.

Diatomic molecule: A molecule containing two atoms.

Halogens: The Group 17 elements, including fluorine, chlorine, bromine and iodine.

Oxidising agent: A substance that causes oxidation of another substance and is itself reduced.

Combustion: A chemical reaction involving burning, commonly with oxygen and the release of energy.

88. ⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Forgetting that several elemental non-metals are diatomic.
  • ❌ Writing O instead of O₂ for elemental oxygen in an equation.
  • ❌ Writing Cl instead of Cl₂ for elemental chlorine.
  • ❌ Saying all non-metals are poor electrical conductors without mentioning graphite.
  • ❌ Confusing carbon dioxide with carbon monoxide.
  • ❌ Saying carbon monoxide is produced during complete combustion.
  • ❌ Forgetting that many non-metal oxides are acidic.
  • ❌ Mixing up chlorine, bromine and iodine colours.
  • ❌ Forgetting that halogen reactivity decreases down Group 17.
  • ❌ Giving only the symbol equation when a word equation is required.
  • ❌ Writing unbalanced equations.

94. 🏁 Final Takeaway

Non-metals are essential to both living systems and industry. Their chemistry is closely connected to electron gain, covalent bonding, acidic oxides, combustion, halogen reactivity and the formation of important compounds.

⭐ Remember

Non-metals → electron gain/sharing → covalent compounds → acidic oxides → important reactions and industrial uses.
