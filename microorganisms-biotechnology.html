BIOLOGY • O/L 0510

🧫 Microorganisms & Biotechnology

Learn about microorganisms, their structures, useful and harmful effects, disease, fermentation, food production, antibiotics and biotechnology.

1. 🧫 What are Microorganisms?

Microorganisms are organisms that are too small to be seen clearly with the unaided eye. Most can be observed using a microscope.

Microorganisms are found almost everywhere, including soil, water, air, food and inside living organisms.

📌 Important

Not all microorganisms are harmful. Many are useful and are essential for ecosystems, food production and biotechnology.

2. 🔬 Main Groups of Microorganisms

Important groups include:

  • 🦠 Bacteria
  • 🍄 Fungi
  • 🧬 Protozoa
  • 🌿 Microscopic algae
  • 🦠 Viruses — commonly studied with microorganisms, although they are not classified as cellular organisms.

3. 🦠 Bacteria

Bacteria are microscopic single-celled organisms.

They are found in soil, water, food and living organisms. Some bacteria are useful, while others cause disease.

Bacteria do not have a nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane.

5. 🧬 Bacterial DNA

The main bacterial chromosome consists of a circular DNA molecule.

Some bacteria also contain small circular DNA molecules called plasmids.

Plasmids can carry useful genes and can sometimes be transferred between bacteria.

6. 🦠 Bacterial Reproduction

Most bacteria reproduce asexually by binary fission.

The basic process is:

  1. The bacterial DNA is copied.
  2. The cell grows.
  3. The DNA copies move apart.
  4. The cell divides into two daughter cells.

Binary fission → one bacterial cell produces two cells.

7. 📈 Rapid Bacterial Growth

Under suitable conditions, bacteria can reproduce very rapidly.

Their growth is affected by factors such as:

  • 🌡️ Temperature
  • 💧 Water availability
  • 🍽️ Nutrients
  • 🧪 pH
  • 🌬️ Oxygen availability

8. 🍄 Fungi

Fungi are organisms that obtain nutrients from organic material.

Some fungi are microscopic, while others can be seen without a microscope.

Examples include:

  • 🍞 Yeast
  • 🍄 Moulds

9. 🍞 Yeast

Yeast is a single-celled fungus commonly used in biotechnology and food production.

Yeast obtains energy by respiration. When oxygen is limited, yeast can carry out anaerobic respiration, producing ethanol and carbon dioxide.

10. 🍄 Fungal Hyphae and Mycelium

Many multicellular fungi consist of thread-like structures called hyphae.

A network of hyphae is called a mycelium.

Hyphae increase the surface area available for absorbing nutrients.

11. 🧬 Protozoa

Protozoa are microscopic, single-celled eukaryotic organisms.

Some protozoa are free-living, while others live as parasites.

🦟 Example

Plasmodium is a protozoan parasite that causes malaria.

12. 🦠 Viruses

Viruses are extremely small infectious agents.

They are not made of cells and can reproduce only inside suitable living host cells.

A virus contains genetic material surrounded by a protein coat.

13. 🦠 Virus Structure

  • Genetic material — DNA or RNA
  • Protein coat called a capsid
  • Some viruses have an outer envelope
  • Some have surface proteins used to attach to host cells

15. 🌍 Where Microorganisms Live

Microorganisms are found in many environments.

  • 🌱 Soil
  • 💧 Freshwater
  • 🌊 Oceans
  • 🍎 Food
  • 🌬️ Air and dust
  • 🐄 Animals
  • 👨‍👩‍👧 Humans
  • 🌿 Plants

16. 👍 Useful Microorganisms

Microorganisms are important in many natural and human processes.

They are used in:

  • 🍞 Bread production
  • 🥛 Yoghurt production
  • 🧀 Cheese production
  • 🥤 Fermentation
  • 💊 Antibiotic production
  • ♻️ Decomposition
  • 🌱 Nutrient recycling
  • 🧬 Biotechnology

17. 👎 Harmful Microorganisms

Some microorganisms are pathogens.

A pathogen is a microorganism or infectious agent that causes disease.

Pathogens may cause disease in:

  • Humans
  • Animals
  • Plants

18. 🦠 Pathogens and Disease

Different pathogens cause different diseases.

Pathogen Example Disease
Bacterium Cholera
Virus Influenza
Protozoan Malaria
Fungus Athlete's foot

19. 🧫 Decomposition

Decomposition is the breakdown of dead organic material into simpler substances.

Bacteria and fungi are important decomposers.

Decomposition returns nutrients to the environment.

20. 🍂 Conditions Affecting Decomposition

The rate of decomposition is affected by environmental conditions.

  • 🌡️ Temperature
  • 💧 Moisture
  • 🌬️ Oxygen concentration
  • 🧪 pH
  • 🍂 Amount and type of organic material

21. 🌡️ Effect of Temperature on Decomposition

Increasing temperature can increase the rate of enzyme-controlled reactions involved in decomposition up to an optimum temperature.

Very high temperatures can damage enzymes and reduce microbial activity.

Very low temperatures slow enzyme activity and microbial growth.

22. 💧 Effect of Water on Microorganisms

Microorganisms generally require water for their metabolic activities.

Reducing available water can prevent or slow the growth of many microorganisms.

🥫 Food Preservation

Drying or adding large amounts of salt or sugar can reduce available water and help prevent microbial growth.

23. 🧪 Effect of pH

Microorganisms have preferred pH ranges for growth.

Changing the pH can slow or prevent the growth of some microorganisms.

24. 🍞 Fermentation

Fermentation is a process in which microorganisms convert organic substances into useful products.

Different microorganisms and conditions produce different fermentation products.

25. 🍞 Fermentation in Yeast

When oxygen is limited, yeast breaks down glucose anaerobically.

🧪 Word Equation

Glucose → Ethanol + Carbon dioxide

Energy is also released.

26. 🍞 Bread Making

Yeast is used in bread making.

Yeast respires using sugars in the dough and produces carbon dioxide.

Carbon dioxide bubbles become trapped in the dough and cause it to rise.

During baking, the yeast is killed and the dough structure becomes firm.

27. 🍺 Ethanol Production

Yeast can be used to produce ethanol from sugars by anaerobic respiration.

The process is called fermentation.

Sugar → Yeast fermentation → Ethanol + Carbon dioxide

28. 🥛 Yoghurt Production

Certain bacteria are used to produce yoghurt.

These bacteria ferment sugars in milk and produce lactic acid.

The lactic acid lowers the pH and causes changes in the milk proteins, producing the characteristic texture and taste of yoghurt.

29. 🧀 Cheese Production

Microorganisms are involved in the production of many types of cheese.

Bacteria and fungi can be used during different stages of cheese production.

They can affect the flavour, texture and other characteristics of the cheese.

30. 🧪 Conditions Needed for Fermentation

Fermentation requires suitable conditions for microorganism growth and activity.

  • Suitable temperature
  • Suitable pH
  • A suitable food source
  • Suitable microorganism
  • Suitable oxygen conditions

31. 🏭 Industrial Fermentation

Microorganisms can be grown in large containers called fermenters or bioreactors.

Industrial fermentation allows useful products to be manufactured on a large scale.

32. 🧪 Fermenter Conditions

Conditions in a fermenter must be carefully controlled.

  • 🌡️ Temperature
  • 🧪 pH
  • 🍽️ Nutrient supply
  • 🌬️ Oxygen supply when required
  • 💧 Water
  • 🧫 Sterility
  • ⏱️ Time

33. 🧼 Why Fermenters Must Be Sterile

Fermenters are sterilised to prevent unwanted microorganisms from contaminating the culture.

Contamination could:

  • Compete for nutrients.
  • Produce unwanted substances.
  • Reduce the amount of desired product.
  • Make the product unsafe.

34. 💊 Antibiotics

Antibiotics are substances that kill bacteria or prevent their growth.

Some antibiotics are produced naturally by microorganisms and others are manufactured or modified for medical use.

35. 🦠 Antibiotics and Viruses

⚠️ Important Exam Point

Antibiotics do not kill viruses.

Viruses reproduce inside host cells and do not have the bacterial structures targeted by many antibiotics.

36. 🧫 Penicillin

Penicillin is an antibiotic originally obtained from a fungus of the Penicillium group.

It interferes with bacterial cell-wall formation, making susceptible bacteria unable to grow normally.

37. 🧬 Antibiotic Resistance

Antibiotic resistance occurs when bacteria develop characteristics that allow them to survive treatment with an antibiotic that previously killed or inhibited them.

Resistance can arise through genetic changes and can spread within bacterial populations.

38. ⚠️ Causes of Antibiotic Resistance

Resistance becomes more common when antibiotics are used inappropriately.

Examples include:

  • Unnecessary use of antibiotics.
  • Incorrect use of antibiotics.
  • Using antibiotics when they are not effective against the infection.
  • Failure to follow medical instructions.

39. 🧬 Natural Selection and Resistance

In a bacterial population, some bacteria may already have a genetic characteristic that gives resistance.

Antibiotic treatment kills susceptible bacteria, while resistant bacteria are more likely to survive and reproduce.

The proportion of resistant bacteria can therefore increase.

40. 🧼 Preventing the Spread of Infection

Infections can be reduced by:

  • Washing hands regularly.
  • Using safe drinking water.
  • Proper food preparation.
  • Good sanitation.
  • Vaccination where available.
  • Safe disposal of waste.
  • Cleaning and disinfecting surfaces.
  • Appropriate treatment of infected people.

41. 🧫 Aseptic Technique

Aseptic technique refers to methods used to prevent unwanted microorganisms from entering a culture or contaminating equipment.

Examples include:

  • Sterilising equipment.
  • Keeping containers closed.
  • Using sterile instruments.
  • Working carefully to reduce contamination.

42. 🧪 Culturing Microorganisms

Microorganisms can be grown on suitable nutrient media under controlled conditions.

A nutrient medium provides substances needed for microbial growth.

⚠️ School Laboratory Safety

Unknown microorganisms should not be deliberately cultured or opened in an uncontrolled environment. School practical work must follow the teacher's safety instructions and approved laboratory procedures.

43. 🧫 Microbial Colonies

When microorganisms reproduce on solid growth medium, they may form visible groups called colonies.

A colony may have characteristic size, shape, colour and appearance.

44. 🧬 What is Biotechnology?

Biotechnology is the use of living organisms, cells or biological processes to produce useful products or solve problems.

Biotechnology can use microorganisms, enzymes, cells and genetic material.

45. 🧫 Traditional Biotechnology

Humans have used microorganisms for thousands of years without knowing the detailed biology behind the processes.

Examples include:

  • 🍞 Bread making
  • 🥛 Yoghurt making
  • 🧀 Cheese production
  • Fermentation of foods

46. 🧬 Modern Biotechnology

Modern biotechnology uses knowledge of cells, DNA, genes and enzymes to produce useful products.

Applications include:

  • Production of medicines.
  • Production of useful enzymes.
  • Genetic engineering.
  • Production of some hormones.
  • Improvement of crops.

Genetic engineering involves changing an organism's genetic material by introducing, removing or modifying genes.

A gene from one organism can sometimes be transferred into another organism.

Recombinant DNA is DNA formed by joining genetic material from different sources.

Plasmids can be used as vectors to carry genes into bacterial cells.

49. 💉 Production of Human Insulin

Genetic engineering can be used to enable microorganisms such as bacteria to produce human insulin.

A simplified process is:

  1. Identify the human gene needed to make insulin.
  2. Obtain the gene.
  3. Insert the gene into a suitable plasmid.
  4. Introduce the recombinant plasmid into bacteria.
  5. Grow the bacteria under controlled conditions.
  6. The bacteria produce insulin.
  7. The insulin is collected and purified.

50. 🧬 Plasmids as Vectors

A vector is a carrier used to transfer genetic material into a cell.

Bacterial plasmids can act as vectors in genetic engineering.

Gene → Plasmid vector → Bacterial cell → Useful protein

51. 🌱 Genetically Modified Organisms

A genetically modified organism (GMO) has had its genetic material deliberately altered using genetic engineering techniques.

Genetic modification can be used to introduce useful characteristics.

52. 🌾 Genetically Modified Crops

Genetic engineering can be used to produce crops with useful characteristics.

Possible characteristics include:

  • Resistance to certain pests.
  • Resistance to some diseases.
  • Improved nutritional content.
  • Improved tolerance to environmental conditions.
  • Improved crop quality.

53. 👍 Advantages of Biotechnology

Biotechnology can:

  • Produce medicines.
  • Improve food production.
  • Produce useful enzymes.
  • Help manufacture vaccines and other biological products.
  • Improve agricultural crops.
  • Help manage waste.
  • Reduce some industrial costs.

54. ⚠️ Possible Concerns About Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering can provide important benefits, but it also raises scientific, environmental and ethical questions.

Concerns may include:

  • Possible effects on ecosystems.
  • Transfer of genes to other organisms.
  • Effects on biodiversity.
  • Ethical questions about modifying organisms.
  • Long-term environmental considerations.

55. 🧪 Enzymes from Microorganisms

Microorganisms can be grown to produce useful enzymes.

Microbial enzymes can be used in:

  • Food processing.
  • Detergents.
  • Textile production.
  • Industrial processes.

56. ♻️ Microorganisms and Waste Treatment

Microorganisms play an important role in breaking down organic waste.

In sewage treatment and other waste-treatment processes, microorganisms help decompose organic matter.

57. 💧 Sewage Treatment and Microorganisms

Sewage contains organic material that microorganisms can break down.

Microbial activity helps reduce the amount of organic material in treated water.

Proper sewage treatment helps prevent disease and protects water ecosystems.

58. 🌱 Microorganisms and Agriculture

Microorganisms are important in soil ecosystems.

They decompose dead material and release mineral nutrients that plants can use.

Some bacteria form beneficial associations with plants and contribute to nitrogen cycling.

59. 🌱 Nitrogen-Fixing Bacteria

Some bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen into forms that can contribute to nitrogen availability in ecosystems.

Rhizobium bacteria live in root nodules of leguminous plants such as beans and peas.

These bacteria contribute to nitrogen fixation and benefit from the association with the plant.

60. 🤝 Mutualism

Mutualism is a relationship between organisms in which both organisms benefit.

🌱 Example

Rhizobium bacteria obtain suitable conditions and nutrients from the plant, while the plant benefits from nitrogen compounds made available through bacterial activity.

61. 🧫 Microorganisms in Food Preservation

Food preservation aims to slow or prevent the growth of microorganisms that cause food spoilage.

Methods include:

  • ❄️ Refrigeration
  • 🧊 Freezing
  • 🔥 Heating
  • 💧 Drying
  • 🧂 Salting
  • 🍬 Sugaring
  • 🥫 Canning
  • 🧪 Using approved preservatives

62. ❄️ Refrigeration

Refrigeration lowers temperature and slows the growth and reproduction of many microorganisms.

It does not necessarily kill all microorganisms.

63. 🧂 Salting and Sugaring

High concentrations of salt or sugar reduce the availability of water.

This makes conditions less suitable for the growth of many microorganisms.

This principle is used in preserving foods such as some meats, fish and jams.

64. 🔥 Pasteurisation

Pasteurisation involves heating a food or drink to a controlled temperature for a specified time and then cooling it.

It reduces the number of harmful microorganisms while helping to maintain the quality of the product.

65. 🥫 Sterilisation

Sterilisation is a process designed to destroy or remove all viable microorganisms from an object or material.

Sterilisation is important when complete microbial control is required.

66. 🧼 Disinfection vs Sterilisation

Disinfection Sterilisation
Reduces or destroys many harmful microorganisms. Destroys or removes all viable microorganisms.
Used on surfaces and equipment. Used when a completely sterile item is required.

67. 🧫 Microorganisms and Food Spoilage

Microorganisms can break down food substances and produce unwanted changes.

Spoilage may cause changes in:

  • Smell
  • Taste
  • Colour
  • Texture

68. 🦠 Disease Transmission by Microorganisms

Pathogens can spread between hosts in different ways.

Transmission may occur through:

  • Air droplets
  • Contaminated food
  • Contaminated water
  • Direct contact
  • Contaminated objects
  • Blood and body fluids
  • Vectors such as mosquitoes

69. 🦟 Vectors

A vector is an organism that carries a pathogen from one host to another.

🦟 Example

The female Anopheles mosquito transmits the Plasmodium parasite that causes malaria.

70. 🧫 Microorganisms and Malaria

Malaria is caused by Plasmodium, a protozoan parasite.

The parasite is transmitted between humans by infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.

Preventing mosquito bites and controlling mosquito populations can help reduce malaria transmission.

71. 🧼 Controlling Microorganisms

Microbial growth can be controlled by methods that alter conditions needed for survival or reproduction.

Examples include:

  • Heat
  • Cold
  • Drying
  • Salt
  • Acidic conditions
  • Disinfectants
  • Antibiotics against susceptible bacteria

72. 🧪 Investigating Microbial Growth

In an approved school practical, students may investigate how a chosen environmental condition affects microbial growth.

The investigation should include:

  • Independent variable
  • Dependent variable
  • Control variables
  • Control group where appropriate
  • Repeats
  • Safe handling procedures

73. 📊 Measuring Microbial Growth

Microbial growth can be estimated by measuring the size or number of colonies in an approved investigation.

Results can be recorded in tables and represented using graphs.

74. 📈 Growth Curves of Microorganisms

When microorganisms are grown in a closed system, their population may pass through different stages.

Stage What Happens
Lag phase Cells adapt to their new environment.
Log/exponential phase Cells reproduce rapidly.
Stationary phase Growth slows as resources become limited and waste accumulates.
Decline phase Death rate may exceed reproduction rate.

75. 🍽️ Why Microorganisms Need Nutrients

Microorganisms require nutrients for:

  • Growth
  • Respiration
  • Reproduction
  • Repair
  • Synthesis of cellular substances

76. 🧪 Enzymes and Microorganisms

Microorganisms produce enzymes that break down large molecules into smaller molecules that can be absorbed and used.

Enzymes are important in decomposition and industrial biotechnology.

77. 🧬 Biotechnology in Medicine

Biotechnology contributes to medicine through:

  • 💉 Production of some vaccines.
  • 💊 Production of antibiotics.
  • 🧬 Production of genetically engineered proteins.
  • 🧪 Production of diagnostic substances.
  • 🧫 Cell and molecular technologies.

78. 🌾 Biotechnology in Agriculture

Biotechnology can be used to improve crops and livestock.

Applications include:

  • Improved disease resistance.
  • Improved pest resistance.
  • Improved nutritional value.
  • Improved crop characteristics.

79. ♻️ Biotechnology and the Environment

Biotechnology can help with environmental management.

Examples include:

  • Waste treatment.
  • Biodegradation of pollutants.
  • Composting.
  • Production of useful materials using microorganisms.

80. 🧫 Biodegradation

Biodegradation is the breakdown of substances by living organisms, especially microorganisms.

Biodegradation is important in decomposition and waste management.

81. 🌱 Composting

Composting uses microorganisms to break down organic waste into nutrient-rich material.

Suitable conditions such as moisture, oxygen and temperature encourage microbial activity.

83. 🧪 Biotechnology vs Fermentation

Fermentation is a biological process in which microorganisms convert substances into useful products.

Biotechnology is the broader use of organisms, cells, enzymes or biological processes to produce useful products or solve problems.

84. 🧬 Biotechnology vs Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering is one part of modern biotechnology.

Biotechnology = broad field using biological systems.

Genetic engineering = deliberate alteration or transfer of genetic material.

86. 📚 Important Definitions

  • Microorganism: An organism too small to be seen clearly with the unaided eye.
  • Pathogen: An infectious agent that causes disease.
  • Bacterium: A microscopic single-celled prokaryotic organism.
  • Virus: An infectious agent containing genetic material and a protein coat that reproduces inside suitable host cells.
  • Fermentation: A process in which microorganisms convert organic substances into useful products.
  • Antibiotic: A substance that kills bacteria or prevents their growth.
  • Biotechnology: The use of living organisms, cells or biological processes to make useful products or solve problems.
  • Genetic engineering: Deliberate alteration or transfer of genetic material.
  • Plasmid: A small circular DNA molecule found in bacteria.
  • Vector: A carrier used to transfer genetic material into a cell.
  • Decomposition: Breakdown of dead organic material into simpler substances.
  • Biodegradation: Breakdown of substances by living organisms, especially microorganisms.
  • Pasteurisation: Controlled heating of food or drink to reduce harmful microorganisms.
  • Sterilisation: Destruction or removal of all viable microorganisms.
  • Colony: A visible group of microorganisms growing together on a suitable solid medium.

89. 📝 Common O/L Mistakes

❌ Mistake: Saying all microorganisms cause disease.

✅ Correct: Many microorganisms are harmless or useful; only some are pathogens.

❌ Mistake: Saying antibiotics kill viruses.

✅ Correct: Antibiotics act against bacteria and do not kill viruses.

❌ Mistake: Saying yeast produces oxygen during fermentation.

✅ Correct: Yeast fermentation produces ethanol and carbon dioxide.

❌ Mistake: Confusing a bacterium with a virus.

✅ Correct: Bacteria are cellular organisms; viruses are acellular infectious agents that reproduce inside host cells.

❌ Mistake: Saying genetic engineering and biotechnology are exactly the same thing.

✅ Correct: Genetic engineering is one application within the broader field of biotechnology.

91. 🧠 Master Summary

Microorganisms are extremely small organisms or infectious agents found in almost every environment. Important groups include bacteria, fungi, protozoa and viruses.

Microorganisms can be useful. Yeast is used in bread making and fermentation, bacteria are used in yoghurt production, microorganisms decompose dead material, and some microorganisms are used to produce medicines and enzymes.

Some microorganisms are harmful because they cause disease or spoil food. Pathogens can spread through air, food, water, contact, body fluids or vectors.

Biotechnology uses living organisms, cells, enzymes or biological processes to produce useful products. Modern biotechnology can also involve genetic engineering, where genes are deliberately transferred or altered.

Microorganisms are therefore important in medicine, agriculture, food production, environmental management and industry.
