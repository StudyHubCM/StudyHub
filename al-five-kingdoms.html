🦠 The Five Kingdoms

A/L 0710 Biology — Kingdom Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae & Animalia

1. 🌍 Introduction to the Five-Kingdom System

The enormous diversity of living organisms makes it necessary to classify organisms into groups according to their similarities, differences and evolutionary relationships.

One important traditional classification system divides organisms into five kingdoms:

🧬 The Five Kingdoms

  1. 🦠 Monera — mainly prokaryotic organisms
  2. 🧫 Protista / Protoctista — mainly simple eukaryotic organisms
  3. 🍄 Fungi — absorptive heterotrophs
  4. 🌿 Plantae — mainly photosynthetic multicellular organisms
  5. 🦁 Animalia — multicellular ingestive heterotrophs

🔬 Important Note About Modern Classification

The five-kingdom system is extremely useful for studying biological diversity and remains important in many educational classification systems. However, modern classification also uses the three-domain system based heavily on molecular and genetic evidence.

Therefore, the five kingdoms should be understood as a major traditional framework rather than the only classification system used by modern biologists.

3. 🧠 Basis for Comparing Kingdoms

Organisms can be compared using several important biological characteristics.

  • 🔬 Cell type — prokaryotic or eukaryotic
  • 🧫 Number of cells — unicellular or multicellular
  • 🧱 Presence and composition of cell wall
  • 🍽️ Method of nutrition
  • 🧬 Organisation and complexity
  • 🔁 Method of reproduction
  • 🌍 Habitat
  • 🧬 Evolutionary relationships

4. 🦠 Kingdom Monera / Prokaryotae

Kingdom Monera traditionally includes prokaryotic organisms, especially bacteria and cyanobacteria.

📌 General Characteristics

  • They are prokaryotic.
  • They usually consist of a single cell.
  • They lack a membrane-bound nucleus.
  • DNA is located in a nucleoid region.
  • They lack membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria.
  • Most possess a cell wall.
  • They reproduce mainly by binary fission.
  • They occur in almost every environment.

4.1 🔬 Prokaryotic Cells

A prokaryotic cell is a cell whose genetic material is not enclosed within a membrane-bound nucleus.

4.2 🍽️ Nutrition in Bacteria

Bacteria show considerable nutritional diversity.

  • 🌱 Photoautotrophic nutrition
  • ⚗️ Chemoautotrophic nutrition
  • 🍂 Saprotrophic nutrition
  • 🦠 Parasitic nutrition
  • 🤝 Symbiotic nutrition

4.3 🔁 Reproduction

Most bacteria reproduce asexually by binary fission.

  1. The bacterial DNA replicates.
  2. The cell grows.
  3. The duplicated DNA molecules separate.
  4. The cell membrane and wall divide.
  5. Two genetically similar daughter cells are formed.

4.4 💨 Respiration

Bacteria may be:

  • 🌬️ Aerobic — require oxygen for respiration.
  • 🚫 Anaerobic — respire without oxygen.
  • 🔄 Facultative anaerobes — can use oxygen when available but can survive without it.

4.6 🌱 Cyanobacteria

Cyanobacteria are photosynthetic prokaryotes. They contain photosynthetic pigments and can manufacture organic substances using light energy.

Some cyanobacteria can also fix atmospheric nitrogen, making them important in nutrient cycling.

4.7 ✅ Useful Bacteria

  • 🥛 Production of yoghurt and fermented foods
  • 🌱 Nitrogen fixation
  • ♻️ Decomposition of organic matter
  • 🧪 Production of useful chemicals and enzymes
  • 💊 Production of some medicines and biotechnology products
  • 🧬 Genetic engineering and research

4.8 ⚠️ Harmful Bacteria

Some bacteria cause disease in humans, animals and plants, while others spoil food or damage crops.

5. 🧫 Kingdom Protista / Protoctista

Protists are eukaryotic organisms that do not fit naturally into the traditional definitions of plants, animals or fungi.

📌 General Characteristics

  • They are eukaryotic.
  • Many are unicellular.
  • Some forms are multicellular or colonial.
  • They may be autotrophic or heterotrophic.
  • Many live in aquatic or moist environments.
  • Reproduction may be sexual or asexual.
  • Some possess structures for locomotion.

5.1 🦠 Protozoans

Protozoans are animal-like protists that are generally heterotrophic and often unicellular.

5.3 🌿 Algae

Algae are photosynthetic organisms traditionally placed among the protists in many five-kingdom treatments. They range from unicellular forms to large multicellular seaweeds.

They are important primary producers in aquatic ecosystems and contribute significantly to global oxygen production.

5.4 🍽️ Nutrition

  • 🌱 Autotrophic nutrition through photosynthesis
  • 🍽️ Heterotrophic nutrition in some protists
  • 🔄 Mixotrophic nutrition in organisms such as Euglena

5.5 🔁 Reproduction

Protists may reproduce through:

  • Asexual cell division
  • Fragmentation
  • Spore formation in some groups
  • Sexual reproduction

5.6 🌍 Ecological Importance

  • Primary producers in aquatic ecosystems
  • Important components of food chains
  • Contributors to oxygen production
  • Participants in nutrient cycling
  • Important habitats and food sources for aquatic organisms

5.7 ⚠️ Harmful Protists

Some protists cause diseases or produce harmful effects.

6. 🍄 Kingdom Fungi

Fungi are eukaryotic organisms that obtain nutrients mainly by secreting digestive enzymes onto food and absorbing the soluble products.

📌 General Characteristics

  • Eukaryotic
  • Mostly multicellular
  • Yeast is unicellular
  • Usually have cell walls containing chitin
  • Do not contain chlorophyll
  • Heterotrophic
  • Digest food externally and absorb nutrients
  • Reproduce sexually and/or asexually
  • Many produce spores

6.1 🧵 Hyphae and Mycelium

A hypha is a thread-like structure characteristic of many multicellular fungi.

A network of hyphae forms a mycelium.

6.2 🍂 Saprophytic Nutrition

Saprophytic fungi obtain nutrients from dead or decaying organic matter.

  1. Digestive enzymes are secreted onto the food.
  2. Large molecules are broken into smaller soluble molecules.
  3. The soluble products are absorbed.

6.3 🦠 Parasitic Fungi

Some fungi obtain nutrients from living hosts and may cause diseases in plants, animals or humans.

6.4 🔁 Reproduction

Fungi reproduce by several methods, including:

  • 🍞 Budding in yeast
  • 🌬️ Spore formation
  • 🧩 Fragmentation of hyphae
  • 🧬 Sexual reproduction

6.5 🍞 Yeast

Yeast is a unicellular fungus. It reproduces commonly by budding and is economically important in baking and fermentation.

6.7 🤝 Lichens

A lichen is a close association between a fungus and a photosynthetic partner, usually an alga or cyanobacterium.

The photosynthetic partner produces organic nutrients, while the fungal partner provides structure, protection and access to water and minerals.

6.8 💰 Economic Importance

  • 🍄 Human food
  • 🧪 Biotechnology and research

  • Crop diseases
  • Human fungal infections
  • Production of some toxins

7. 🌿 Kingdom Plantae

Kingdom Plantae consists mainly of multicellular, eukaryotic organisms that possess chloroplasts and obtain energy primarily through photosynthesis.

📌 General Characteristics

  • Eukaryotic
  • Multicellular
  • Usually photosynthetic
  • Cell walls mainly contain cellulose
  • Store carbohydrates mainly as starch
  • Generally non-motile
  • Reproduce sexually and/or asexually

7.2 🌱 Bryophytes

Bryophytes include mosses and related plants. They lack true vascular tissue and are generally small.

  • No true xylem and phloem
  • Do not produce seeds
  • Reproduce using spores
  • Require water for sexual reproduction
  • Often found in moist habitats

7.5 🌸 Angiosperms

Angiosperms are flowering plants. Their ovules are enclosed within an ovary and their seeds develop inside fruits.

7.6 🌽 Monocotyledons

  • One cotyledon
  • Usually parallel leaf venation
  • Usually fibrous root systems
  • Vascular bundles are scattered in the stem
  • Flower parts commonly occur in multiples of three

7.7 🌳 Dicots / Eudicots

  • Usually two cotyledons
  • Usually net-like leaf venation
  • Often have a taproot system
  • Vascular bundles are commonly arranged in a ring
  • Flower parts commonly occur in multiples of four or five

8. 🦁 Kingdom Animalia

Kingdom Animalia contains multicellular eukaryotic organisms that obtain nutrients mainly by ingesting food. Animals generally lack cell walls and possess specialised tissues and organs.

📌 General Characteristics

  • Multicellular
  • Eukaryotic
  • No cellulose cell wall
  • Heterotrophic
  • Usually capable of movement at some stage of life
  • Usually reproduce sexually
  • Have specialised cells and tissues
  • Most possess nervous and sensory systems

⚠️ 11. Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying bacteria have a true nucleus.
    ✅ Bacteria are prokaryotic and lack a membrane-bound nucleus.
  • ❌ Saying fungi are plants.
    ✅ Fungi form a separate kingdom.
  • ❌ Saying fungi photosynthesise.
    ✅ Fungi obtain nutrients heterotrophically by absorption.
  • ❌ Confusing hypha with mycelium.
    ✅ A hypha is a filament; a mycelium is a network of hyphae.
  • ❌ Saying all protists are animals.
    ✅ Protists include diverse organisms with different nutritional strategies.
  • ❌ Saying all plants produce seeds.
    ✅ Bryophytes and pteridophytes reproduce without seeds.
  • ❌ Saying all arthropods have six legs.
    ✅ Six legs is characteristic of insects; arthropods include several other groups.
  • ❌ Saying adult echinoderms are bilateral.
    ✅ Adult echinoderms generally have radial symmetry, while larvae are bilateral.
  • ❌ Treating the five-kingdom system as the only modern classification system.
    ✅ Modern classification also uses domains and molecular/phylogenetic evidence.

⚡ 12. Quick Revision

🦠 Monera

Prokaryotic organisms; mainly unicellular; bacteria and cyanobacteria.

🧫 Protista

Mainly simple eukaryotic organisms; many unicellular; includes protozoans and many algae.

🍄 Fungi

Eukaryotic heterotrophs that absorb nutrients; hyphae, mycelium and spores are common features.

🌿 Plantae

Mainly multicellular photosynthetic organisms; major groups include bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms and angiosperms.

🦁 Animalia

Multicellular eukaryotic ingestive heterotrophs; major groups include Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Mollusca, Arthropoda, Echinodermata and Chordata.
