🦠 UNIT 7 — PATHOLOGY & DISEASE

🎯 Unit Overview

Pathology is the study of disease, including the causes of disease, the changes produced in cells and tissues, and the effects of disease on the structure and function of an organism.

At A/L level, pathology goes beyond simply naming diseases. You should understand cause → pathogen or risk factor → transmission → tissue effects → signs and symptoms → diagnosis → prevention → treatment/control.

1. 🩺 Meaning of Pathology

Pathology is the branch of Biology and medical science concerned with the study of disease and the structural and functional changes associated with disease.

Pathology investigates:

  • 🦠 Causes of disease
  • 🔬 Changes in cells and tissues
  • ⚙️ Effects on normal body functions
  • 🤒 Signs and symptoms
  • 🧪 Laboratory evidence of disease
  • 📊 Patterns of disease in populations
  • 🛡️ Prevention and control

2. 🧬 Disease

A disease is a condition that disrupts the normal structure or function of an organism and produces characteristic changes or effects.

Disease may result from infectious agents, genetic abnormalities, nutritional deficiencies, environmental factors, lifestyle factors, immune disorders, injuries or combinations of these.

3. 🦠 Infectious vs Non-Infectious Disease

Feature Infectious disease Non-infectious disease
Cause Caused by infectious agents such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa or helminths. Not caused by an infectious pathogen.
Transmission May spread between hosts directly or indirectly. Does not spread as an infection from host to host.
Examples Malaria, tuberculosis, cholera and measles. Diabetes, hypertension and many genetic disorders.
Control May involve vaccination, hygiene, vector control or antimicrobial treatment. May involve lifestyle changes, medical treatment or reducing risk factors.

4. 🦠 Pathogens

A pathogen is an infectious agent capable of causing disease in a host.

Pathogen group Examples Diseases associated with them
Bacteria Mycobacterium tuberculosis, Vibrio cholerae Tuberculosis, cholera
Viruses Measles virus, hepatitis B virus Measles, hepatitis B
Protozoa Plasmodium species Malaria
Fungi Pathogenic fungal species Various fungal infections
Helminths Schistosoma species Schistosomiasis

5. 🌍 Major Causes of Disease

Disease can result from many different causes.

  • 🦠 Infectious organisms
  • 🧬 Genetic abnormalities
  • 🥗 Nutritional deficiencies or excesses
  • ☣️ Toxic substances
  • 🌡️ Environmental conditions
  • 🧠 Psychological and physiological factors
  • 🩹 Physical injury
  • 🫀 Disorders of organs or body systems
  • 🛡️ Abnormal immune responses
  • 🧬 Abnormal cell growth

6. 🔬 How Disease Affects Cells and Tissues

Disease may interfere with normal cellular processes and therefore alter tissue and organ function.

Possible changes include:

  • Changes in cell size or shape
  • Cell death
  • Inflammation
  • Loss of normal tissue organisation
  • Abnormal cell growth
  • Reduced oxygen or nutrient supply
  • Changes in organ function

7. 🧩 Tissue Damage

Tissue damage occurs when cells or their surrounding structures are harmed sufficiently to interfere with normal function.

Infectious agents may damage tissues directly, produce harmful substances, trigger inflammation or interfere with normal cellular processes.

🧠 Structure → Function

Damage to tissue can change its structure, and changes in structure can reduce its ability to perform its normal function.

8. 🤒 Signs and Symptoms

Term Meaning Example
Sign An objective finding that can be observed or measured. Raised body temperature.
Symptom A subjective experience reported by the patient. Headache or feeling tired.

A disease may produce several signs and symptoms, but signs and symptoms alone do not always establish a diagnosis.

9. 🔍 Diagnosis

Diagnosis is the process of determining the cause or nature of a disease or disorder.

Diagnosis may involve:

  • 📋 Medical history
  • 👀 Physical examination
  • 🧪 Laboratory tests
  • 🔬 Microscopic examination
  • 🧬 Molecular tests
  • 🩻 Imaging techniques
  • 🩸 Blood or other appropriate samples

10. 🧪 Laboratory Diagnosis

Laboratory methods can help detect a pathogen, its genetic material, antibodies produced by the host or changes associated with disease.

Method What it may detect or investigate
Microscopy Cells, parasites, bacteria or other structures that can be visualised.
Culture Growth of suitable microorganisms under appropriate laboratory conditions.
Antigen testing Specific components of an infectious agent.
Antibody testing Host immune responses to an infectious agent.
Molecular testing Specific nucleic acid sequences associated with an infectious agent.

11. 🧠 Diagnosis vs Treatment

Diagnosis Treatment
Determines what disease or condition is present. Aims to manage, cure or reduce the effects of the condition.
Uses observations, history and investigations. Uses appropriate medical or supportive interventions.

12. 💊 Principles of Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause of disease and the condition of the patient.

General principles include:

  • 🎯 Treating the underlying cause when possible.
  • 🦠 Using appropriate antimicrobial medicines when indicated.
  • 💧 Providing supportive care.
  • 🛡️ Preventing complications.
  • ❤️ Maintaining essential body functions.
  • 📋 Following qualified medical guidance.

13. 🧫 Antimicrobial Treatment

Antimicrobial drugs are medicines used against particular groups of infectious agents.

Medicine type General target
Antibiotics Suitable bacterial infections.
Antifungals Fungal infections.
Antiprotozoals Some protozoan infections.
Anthelmintics Helminth infections.
Antivirals Specific viral infections.

⚠️ Important

Antibiotics do not treat viral infections. Antimicrobial treatment should be based on the suspected or confirmed cause and appropriate professional guidance.

14. 🧬 Antimicrobial Resistance

Antimicrobial resistance occurs when microorganisms develop the ability to survive exposure to medicines that would previously have inhibited or killed them.

Resistance can make infections more difficult to treat and can increase the risk of complications and transmission.

Important contributors include:

  • Unnecessary use of antimicrobial medicines
  • Inappropriate antimicrobial selection
  • Failure to follow prescribed treatment correctly
  • Spread of resistant microorganisms

15. 🛡️ Prevention of Disease

Disease prevention can operate at several levels.

  • 💉 Vaccination
  • 🧼 Personal hygiene
  • 🚰 Safe water
  • 🍽️ Food hygiene
  • 🦟 Vector control
  • 🧹 Environmental sanitation
  • 🛏️ Appropriate infection-control measures
  • 🏥 Early diagnosis and treatment
  • 📢 Health education

16. 🦠 Modes of Disease Transmission

Mode Explanation Example
Direct contact Transmission through direct physical contact. Some skin infections.
Droplets / respiratory secretions Infectious particles released from an infected person's respiratory tract. Influenza and some respiratory infections.
Contaminated food or water Infectious agents are acquired through contaminated food or water. Cholera and some intestinal infections.
Vector transmission A vector transfers an infectious agent between hosts. Malaria transmitted by infected Anopheles mosquitoes.
Blood or body-fluid exposure Transmission through exposure to infected blood or certain body fluids. HIV and hepatitis B.
Soil or environmental exposure Infection acquired from an environmental source. Some helminth infections.

17. 🦟 Vectors

A vector is an organism that transmits an infectious agent between hosts.

The most important vector examples in human disease include mosquitoes, ticks, fleas and some other arthropods.

🦟 Malaria

Malaria parasites of the genus Plasmodium are transmitted to humans by infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.

18. 🔄 Chain of Infection

Transmission of an infectious disease can be understood as a chain involving several stages.

🦠 Infectious agent → 🏠 Reservoir → 🚪 Portal of exit → 🚶 Mode of transmission → 🚪 Portal of entry → 👤 Susceptible host

Breaking any suitable link in this chain can reduce transmission.

19. 📊 Epidemiology

Epidemiology is the study of the distribution and determinants of health-related conditions in populations and the application of this knowledge to control health problems.

It considers questions such as:

  • Who is affected?
  • Where is the disease occurring?
  • When is it occurring?
  • How is it being transmitted?
  • Which factors increase risk?
  • Which control measures reduce transmission?

20. 📈 Incidence and Prevalence

Term Meaning
Incidence Number of new cases occurring in a population during a specified period.
Prevalence Total number of existing cases in a population at a specified time or during a specified period.
Mortality Death in a population, often expressed using a rate.
Morbidity Illness or disease within a population.

21. 🌍 Endemic, Epidemic and Pandemic

Term Meaning
Endemic A disease is consistently present at an expected level in a particular population or geographical area.
Epidemic Cases occur above the expected level in a population or area.
Pandemic An epidemic that spreads across multiple countries or continents and affects large populations.

22. 🏥 Public Health

Public health focuses on protecting and improving the health of populations rather than only treating individual patients.

Public-health measures include:

  • 💉 Immunisation programmes
  • 🚰 Water and sanitation systems
  • 🦟 Vector-control programmes
  • 🧪 Disease surveillance
  • 📢 Health education
  • 🏥 Screening and early diagnosis
  • 📊 Outbreak investigation

23. 🌿 PLANT PATHOLOGY

🌱 Meaning

Plant pathology is the study of plant diseases, their causes, development, effects and methods of prevention and control.

24. 🌱 Plant Disease

A plant disease is a condition in which normal plant structure or function is disturbed by an infectious agent or other harmful factor.

Plant diseases can reduce growth, reproduction, crop quality and yield.

25. 🦠 Causes of Plant Diseases

Cause Examples
Bacteria Bacterial leaf spots, wilts and blights.
Viruses Cassava mosaic disease, maize streak disease and banana bunchy top disease.
Fungi Leaf spots, rusts, wilts and rots.
Oomycetes Some water mould diseases affecting crops.
Nematodes Root-knot and other plant-parasitic nematode diseases.
Environmental factors Drought, flooding, temperature extremes or mineral deficiencies.

26. 🌿 Symptoms of Plant Disease

Common symptoms include:

  • 🟡 Chlorosis — yellowing of plant tissues
  • 🟤 Necrosis — death of plant tissue
  • 🍂 Leaf spots
  • 🌿 Wilting
  • 🌱 Stunted growth
  • 🍃 Leaf curling or distortion
  • 🪴 Root damage
  • 🌳 Cankers
  • 🍎 Fruit rots
  • 🌸 Reduced flowering or fruit production

27. 🔍 Plant Disease Diagnosis

Diagnosis involves determining the cause of abnormal plant growth or symptoms.

Investigation may include:

  • 👀 Observing symptoms and distribution.
  • 🌱 Examining affected plant parts.
  • 🔬 Microscopic examination.
  • 🧪 Laboratory testing.
  • 🧬 Molecular tests where available.
  • 🌍 Investigating environmental conditions.
  • 🦠 Testing for infectious agents.

28. 🦠 Bacterial Plant Diseases

Plant-pathogenic bacteria can enter plants through natural openings, wounds or other routes and may multiply in plant tissues.

Bacterial diseases can produce symptoms such as leaf spots, blights, wilting, soft rots and abnormal growth.

Transmission may occur through contaminated tools, water, soil, insects, infected planting material or contact between plants.

29. 🧬 Viral Plant Diseases

Plant viruses depend on living host cells for replication.

Common symptoms include:

  • Mosaic patterns
  • Leaf distortion
  • Chlorosis
  • Stunting
  • Reduced crop yield

Plant viruses can be transmitted by insect vectors, infected planting material, mechanical contact and other routes depending on the virus.

30. 🍄 Fungal and Oomycete Plant Diseases

Fungi and fungus-like oomycetes can cause major crop diseases.

They may spread through spores or other propagules and can enter plants through natural openings, wounds or directly through tissues depending on the pathogen.

Common disease types include:

  • 🍃 Leaf spots
  • 🌿 Wilts
  • 🌾 Rusts
  • 🌱 Rots
  • 🌳 Cankers
  • 🍂 Blights

31. 🪱 Plant-Parasitic Nematodes

Plant-parasitic nematodes are microscopic worms that feed on plant tissues, particularly roots in many important crop diseases.

Root damage can interfere with water and mineral uptake and may produce stunted growth, wilting and reduced yield.

32. 🦠 Cassava Mosaic Disease

Cassava mosaic disease is an important viral disease of cassava in many parts of sub-Saharan Africa.

Important features include:

  • 🦠 Caused by cassava mosaic begomoviruses.
  • 🍃 Characteristic mosaic patterns may develop on leaves.
  • 🌱 Plants may become stunted.
  • 🦟 Whiteflies can transmit important cassava mosaic viruses.
  • 🌿 Infected planting material can also contribute to spread.
  • 🌾 Severe disease can reduce cassava production.

33. 🌽 Maize Streak Disease

Maize streak disease is a viral disease affecting maize in sub-Saharan Africa.

Important points:

  • 🦠 Caused by maize streak viruses.
  • 🍃 Produces characteristic streaking on leaves.
  • 🦗 Leafhoppers are important vectors.
  • 🌽 Severe infection can reduce plant growth and yield.

34. 🍌 Banana Bunchy Top Disease

Banana bunchy top disease is a serious viral disease of banana and plantain.

Important features include:

  • 🦠 Caused by banana bunchy top virus.
  • 🍃 Leaves may become narrow and stand more upright.
  • 🌱 New leaves may become crowded, producing a bunchy appearance.
  • 🪲 Aphids are important vectors.
  • 🌿 Infected planting material can spread the disease.

35. 🍄 Black Sigatoka

Black Sigatoka is an important fungal disease of banana and plantain.

It primarily affects leaves, reducing the photosynthetic capacity of the plant and potentially reducing fruit production.

Disease management may involve healthy planting material, removal or management of infected leaves and appropriate integrated disease-control practices.

36. 🌾 Effects of Plant Diseases on Agriculture

Effect Consequence
Reduced photosynthesis Reduced plant growth and productivity.
Root damage Reduced water and mineral uptake.
Premature leaf loss Reduced photosynthetic area.
Fruit damage Reduced market value and food supply.
Reduced yield Lower agricultural production.
Plant death Loss of crops and economic resources.

37. 🛡️ Prevention of Plant Diseases

Important preventive measures include:

  • 🌱 Use healthy planting material.
  • 🧹 Remove or appropriately manage infected plant material.
  • 🧼 Clean tools and equipment.
  • 🌾 Practise appropriate crop rotation.
  • 🦟 Control important insect vectors.
  • 🌱 Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available.
  • 🚰 Manage irrigation and drainage appropriately.
  • 🌍 Maintain suitable field sanitation.

38. 🧩 Integrated Plant Disease Management

Integrated disease management combines several compatible methods to reduce disease rather than relying on one control method alone.

Method Example
Cultural control Crop rotation and field sanitation.
Biological control Using beneficial organisms or natural enemies where appropriate.
Host resistance Growing resistant or tolerant crop varieties.
Chemical control Appropriate approved pesticides or fungicides when necessary.
Vector control Reducing populations of vectors that transmit plant pathogens.

39. 🐘 ANIMAL & HUMAN PATHOLOGY

🧠 Core Idea

Animal and human pathology investigates how disease affects tissues, organs and physiological processes.

40. 🦠 Important Infectious Diseases

Disease Cause Main transmission route
Malaria Plasmodium parasites Infected female Anopheles mosquitoes
Tuberculosis Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex bacteria Airborne respiratory particles
Cholera Vibrio cholerae bacteria Contaminated food or water
HIV infection Human immunodeficiency virus Exposure to infected blood or certain body fluids
Hepatitis B Hepatitis B virus Infected blood and certain body fluids
Schistosomiasis Schistosoma parasites Contact with freshwater containing infective stages
Yellow fever Yellow fever virus Infected mosquitoes
Measles Measles virus Respiratory transmission

41. 🦟 Malaria

Malaria is a parasitic disease caused by species of the genus Plasmodium.

In humans, infection is transmitted by the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito.

Important biological stages include:

  • 🦟 Mosquito transmission introduces parasites into a human host.
  • 🧬 Parasites undergo important stages in the human host.
  • 🩸 Red blood cells are involved in the blood stage of infection.
  • 🦟 Another mosquito can acquire parasites when feeding on an infected person.

🎯 Control

Malaria control can involve insecticide-treated mosquito nets, appropriate vector-control measures, environmental management, prompt diagnosis and effective treatment.

42. 🧬 Malaria: Pathological Effects

Malaria parasites infect red blood cells during an important stage of their life cycle. Destruction and altered function of infected cells contribute to disease manifestations.

Severe malaria can affect major organs and requires urgent medical attention.

43. 🫁 Tuberculosis

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease caused mainly by bacteria in the Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex.

TB most commonly affects the lungs, although other organs can also be affected.

Important points:

  • 🫁 Pulmonary TB affects lung tissue.
  • 🌬️ Transmission can occur through airborne particles released by people with infectious pulmonary TB.
  • 🧪 Diagnosis may involve microbiological or molecular testing.
  • 💊 Treatment requires appropriate combinations of anti-TB medicines.

44. 🚰 Cholera

Cholera is an acute intestinal infection caused by toxigenic strains of Vibrio cholerae.

Transmission is strongly associated with contaminated water and food and inadequate sanitation.

Prevention includes:

  • 🚰 Safe drinking water
  • 🧼 Hand hygiene
  • 🚽 Proper sanitation
  • 🍽️ Safe food handling
  • 📢 Rapid outbreak response

45. 🧬 HIV Infection

HIV is a virus that targets important cells of the immune system. Progressive untreated infection can weaken immune function.

HIV can be transmitted through infected blood and certain body fluids, including through sexual transmission, contaminated injecting equipment and mother-to-child transmission.

HIV is not transmitted through ordinary casual contact such as shaking hands, sharing ordinary social spaces or sitting beside someone.

🛡️ Prevention

Prevention includes appropriate testing and treatment services, prevention of blood exposure, prevention of mother-to-child transmission and other evidence-based public-health measures.

46. 🧬 Hepatitis B

Hepatitis B is a viral infection that primarily affects the liver.

Infection may be acute or become chronic. Chronic infection can contribute to serious liver disease.

Prevention includes:

  • 💉 Hepatitis B vaccination
  • 🩸 Safe blood practices
  • 🧪 Appropriate testing
  • 🛡️ Preventing exposure to infected blood and body fluids

47. 💧 Schistosomiasis

Schistosomiasis is a parasitic disease caused by blood flukes of the genus Schistosoma.

Humans can become infected when they come into contact with freshwater containing infective parasite stages released during the parasite's life cycle.

Freshwater snails act as important intermediate hosts for human schistosome species.

🌍 Control

Control can include diagnosis and treatment, improved sanitation, access to safe water and measures that reduce transmission in affected communities.

49. 🦟 Yellow Fever

Yellow fever is a viral disease transmitted by infected mosquitoes.

It can occur in tropical regions of Africa and South America.

Vaccination is an important method of prevention, supported by appropriate mosquito-control and public-health measures.

50. 🫁 Respiratory Infectious Diseases

Respiratory infections can spread through respiratory particles or other routes depending on the pathogen.

Prevention depends on the particular disease and may include vaccination, ventilation, hygiene, staying away from others when infectious and appropriate public-health measures.

51. 🧬 Non-Infectious Diseases

Non-infectious diseases are not caused by transmissible pathogens.

Category Examples
Cardiovascular disease Hypertension, coronary artery disease and stroke.
Metabolic disease Diabetes mellitus.
Genetic disorders Conditions resulting from inherited or genetic changes.
Deficiency diseases Diseases caused by inadequate essential nutrients.
Some cancers Abnormal uncontrolled cell growth and spread.

52. 🫀 Hypertension

Hypertension is persistently elevated blood pressure.

Long-term high blood pressure can increase the risk of cardiovascular, kidney and other organ damage.

Risk reduction may involve healthy diet, regular physical activity, appropriate body-weight management, avoiding tobacco and following medical advice.

53. 🍬 Diabetes Mellitus

Diabetes mellitus is a group of metabolic disorders characterised by persistently elevated blood glucose levels.

It can result from inadequate insulin production, reduced response to insulin, or a combination of factors depending on the type.

Long-term poorly controlled diabetes can damage blood vessels and nerves and affect organs such as the kidneys and eyes.

54. 🧬 Cancer

Cancer is a group of diseases involving abnormal cells that grow uncontrollably and may invade surrounding tissues or spread to other parts of the body.

Cancer can develop through the accumulation of genetic changes that disturb normal control of cell division, survival and tissue organisation.

55. 🥗 Nutritional Disease

Inadequate, excessive or unbalanced nutrition can contribute to disease.

Condition General cause
Iron-deficiency anaemia Insufficient iron available for normal haemoglobin production.
Vitamin A deficiency Insufficient vitamin A intake or availability.
Iodine deficiency disorders Insufficient iodine intake.
Protein-energy malnutrition Severe deficiency of energy and/or protein.

56. 🧬 Genetic Disease

Genetic diseases result from changes in genetic material that affect normal biological processes.

Some are inherited from parents, while others result from genetic changes that arise during an individual's development.

Genetic disorders are generally classified as non-infectious because they are not caused by transmissible pathogens.

57. 🛡️ Host Defence and Disease

The body has several lines of defence against infectious agents.

Defence Example
Physical barrier Skin.
Chemical barrier Acidic conditions in the stomach.
Mucus and cilia Trap and help remove particles from respiratory passages.
Innate immune response Inflammation and phagocytosis.
Adaptive immune response Specific lymphocyte responses and antibody production.

58. 🔥 Inflammation

Inflammation is a protective response to tissue injury or infection involving changes in blood vessels and immune-cell activity.

It can help contain harmful agents and begin tissue repair, although excessive or prolonged inflammation can contribute to tissue damage.

59. 🧬 Immunity and Vaccination

Vaccination exposes the immune system to appropriate antigenic material so that an immune response and immunological memory can develop without causing the disease in the way that natural infection does.

If the vaccinated person later encounters the relevant pathogen, the immune response can be faster and more effective.

60. 💉 Types of Prevention

Level / method Example
Primary prevention Preventing disease before it occurs, such as vaccination.
Secondary prevention Early detection and treatment of disease.
Tertiary prevention Reducing complications, disability or long-term effects.

61. 🌍 Important Diseases Relevant to Cameroon and Africa

The disease burden varies between countries and regions. For Cameroon and many parts of sub-Saharan Africa, important diseases studied in Biology include both infectious and non-infectious conditions.

62. 🧫 Animal Diseases

Domestic and wild animals can also suffer from infectious and non-infectious diseases.

Animal diseases are important because they can affect animal health, agriculture, food production, biodiversity and, in some cases, human health.

Diseases that can naturally pass between animals and humans are called zoonotic diseases.

63. 🔗 Zoonotic Disease

A zoonosis is an infectious disease that can be transmitted naturally between animals and humans.

Prevention may require cooperation between veterinary medicine, human medicine, environmental science and public health.

🌍 One Health

The One Health approach recognises that human health, animal health and environmental health are interconnected.

64. 🧪 Outbreak Investigation

When an unusual increase in disease occurs, public-health workers may investigate the outbreak systematically.

  1. Confirm that an outbreak exists.
  2. Establish a working case definition.
  3. Identify and record cases.
  4. Describe cases by person, place and time.
  5. Investigate possible sources and transmission routes.
  6. Collect appropriate laboratory evidence.
  7. Apply control measures.
  8. Continue surveillance to determine whether transmission is decreasing.

65. 📊 Person, Place and Time

Dimension Questions asked
Person Who is affected? Which age groups or populations are involved?
Place Where are cases concentrated?
Time When did cases occur and how did case numbers change over time?

66. 📈 Epidemic Curves

An epidemic curve is a graph showing the number of disease cases according to time of onset or another relevant time measure.

It can help investigators understand the timing and possible pattern of transmission during an outbreak.

67. 🧠 Risk Factors

A risk factor is a characteristic, exposure or condition associated with an increased likelihood of developing a disease or health outcome.

Examples may include:

  • Age
  • Environmental exposure
  • Genetic factors
  • Diet
  • Physical inactivity
  • Tobacco exposure
  • Occupational exposure
  • Contact with infectious sources or vectors

68. 🔗 Risk Factor vs Cause

An association between a factor and a disease does not automatically prove that the factor directly causes the disease.

Epidemiological studies must consider alternative explanations such as confounding, bias and chance.

69. 🌍 Environmental Factors and Disease

Environmental conditions can influence disease transmission and severity.

  • 🚰 Water quality
  • 🚽 Sanitation
  • 🌡️ Temperature
  • 💧 Humidity
  • 🦟 Vector habitats
  • 🏠 Housing conditions
  • 🌳 Land-use changes
  • 🌍 Climate and seasonal conditions

70. 🧠 Pathogenesis

Pathogenesis describes the development and progression of a disease from its initial cause through the biological changes that produce disease manifestations.

A simplified infectious-disease sequence may be:

🦠 Exposure → Entry → Infection → Multiplication / development → Tissue effects → Signs and symptoms → Immune response → Outcome

71. 🧬 Virulence

Virulence refers to the degree of damage or severity of disease caused by a pathogen in a host.

Virulence is influenced by properties of the pathogen, characteristics of the host and environmental conditions.

72. 🧬 Host Factors

The outcome of infection is influenced not only by the pathogen but also by the host.

  • Age
  • Immune status
  • Genetic factors
  • Nutritional status
  • Previous exposure or vaccination
  • Underlying health conditions
  • Access to diagnosis and treatment

73. 🦠 Reservoirs

A reservoir is a place where an infectious agent normally lives and may multiply or persist.

Reservoirs can include humans, animals or environmental sources depending on the pathogen.

74. 🚪 Portals of Entry and Exit

Term Meaning
Portal of entry Route through which an infectious agent enters a new host.
Portal of exit Route through which an infectious agent leaves its reservoir or infected host.

75. 🧪 Carrier State

A person or animal may sometimes carry an infectious agent without obvious disease symptoms while still being capable of contributing to transmission, depending on the pathogen.

This makes disease control more difficult because infection may not always be obvious from appearance alone.

76. 🧬 Incubation Period

The incubation period is the time between infection or exposure to an infectious agent and the appearance of the first symptoms or signs of disease.

The duration varies between diseases and can also vary between individuals.

77. 🛡️ Herd Immunity

When a sufficiently large proportion of a population is immune to an infectious disease, transmission can be reduced because infectious agents encounter fewer susceptible hosts.

The level of population immunity required varies between diseases.

78. 🧠 Disease Control Strategy

Target Possible control measure
Pathogen Appropriate antimicrobial treatment.
Vector Vector-control measures.
Reservoir Diagnosis, treatment and appropriate management of infected hosts.
Transmission route Hygiene, sanitation, safe water or other route-specific measures.
Susceptible host Vaccination and other appropriate protective measures.

79. 🌿 Plant vs Animal Pathology

Feature Plant pathology Animal/human pathology
Main subject Diseases affecting plants. Diseases affecting animals and humans.
Common pathogens Viruses, bacteria, fungi, oomycetes and nematodes. Viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa and helminths.
Important symptoms Wilting, chlorosis, necrosis, spots and stunting. Varies according to disease and organ system.
Control Healthy planting material, resistant varieties, sanitation, vector control and other integrated methods. Vaccination, hygiene, diagnosis, treatment, vector control and public-health measures.

80. 🔬 Diagnosis: Plant vs Animal

Stage Plant Animal / human
Observation Observe symptoms and affected plant parts. Observe signs and record symptoms.
Sample Collect appropriate plant material. Collect appropriate clinical specimen when required.
Investigation Microscopy, culture, molecular tests and environmental assessment. Laboratory tests, imaging and clinical investigations.
Conclusion Determine likely cause of plant disorder. Determine likely disease or condition.

81. 🧪 Practical Pathology Investigation

A practical investigation of disease should follow a logical scientific sequence.

  1. Define the problem.
  2. Observe the affected organism.
  3. Record symptoms or signs.
  4. Form a suitable hypothesis.
  5. Collect appropriate evidence.
  6. Carry out suitable tests.
  7. Analyse the results.
  8. Identify the most likely cause.
  9. Recommend appropriate prevention or control measures.

82. 📊 Comparing Disease Data

Disease data may be presented using tables, bar charts, line graphs, pie charts or maps depending on the type of information.

Students should examine:

  • 📈 Trends over time
  • 📍 Geographic distribution
  • 👥 Differences between populations
  • 🔢 Number or rate of cases
  • 📊 Changes following an intervention

83. 🧠 Correlation Does Not Prove Causation

If two variables change together, this does not automatically demonstrate that one causes the other.

Other explanations, including confounding variables, bias or chance, must be considered.

84. 🦠 Disease Transmission and Prevention Summary

Transmission route Useful prevention principle
Vector Reduce contact with vectors and control vector populations.
Food / water Improve water safety, sanitation and food hygiene.
Respiratory Use disease-specific respiratory and public-health measures.
Blood / body fluids Prevent exposure and use safe healthcare practices.
Direct contact Appropriate hygiene and infection-control measures.

86. 🧠 Common Pathology Mistakes

  • ❌ Calling every disease infectious.
  • ❌ Confusing a pathogen with a vector.
  • ❌ Confusing a sign with a symptom.
  • ❌ Saying antibiotics treat viruses.
  • ❌ Assuming correlation automatically proves causation.
  • ❌ Giving treatment without identifying the disease cause.
  • ❌ Confusing incidence with prevalence.
  • ❌ Confusing a reservoir with a vector.
  • ❌ Treating plant symptoms as proof of a particular pathogen without diagnosis.
  • ❌ Forgetting the role of sanitation and public health.

90. ⚡ Quick Revision

🩺 Pathology → study of disease and disease-related changes.

🦠 Pathogen → infectious agent capable of causing disease.

🌿 Plant pathology → study of plant diseases.

🤒 Sign → objective, observable or measurable finding.

💭 Symptom → subjective experience reported by a patient.

🔍 Diagnosis → determining the nature or cause of disease.

🦟 Vector → organism that transmits an infectious agent.

🏠 Reservoir → place where an infectious agent normally lives or persists.

📈 Incidence → new cases during a specified period.

📊 Prevalence → existing cases in a population.

💉 Vaccination → stimulates protective immune memory.

🧼 Prevention → reduces exposure, transmission or susceptibility.

🌾 Plant disease → can reduce crop growth, quality and yield.

🦠 Malaria → Plasmodium + Anopheles mosquito.

🚰 Cholera → Vibrio cholerae + contaminated water/food.

🫁 TB → Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex + airborne transmission.

92. 🏁 Final Takeaway

🩺 Think Like a Pathologist

When studying a disease, do not memorise only its name. Ask:

What causes it? → How is it transmitted? → What tissues or organs are affected? → What changes occur? → What signs and symptoms result? → How is it diagnosed? → How can it be prevented? → How can it be treated or controlled?

For plant diseases, also ask how the disease affects growth, reproduction, crop yield and agriculture.

For human and animal diseases, connect pathology with physiology, immunity, epidemiology and public health.
