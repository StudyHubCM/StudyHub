🦴 Movement & Support

Understand support, the human skeleton, joints, muscles, movement and how living organisms maintain their shape and position.

🦴 1. What is Support?

Support is the ability of an organism to maintain its shape, position and structure.

Support is important because living organisms need to maintain their form while carrying out activities such as movement, feeding, growth and reproduction.

📌 Key Point

Support prevents the body from collapsing and provides a framework against which muscles can act.

🏃 2. What is Movement?

Movement is a change in the position of the whole organism or part of an organism.

Movement allows organisms to:

  • 🏃 Move from one place to another.
  • 🍽️ Obtain food.
  • 🛡️ Escape from predators.
  • 💨 Move away from harmful conditions.
  • ❤️ Find suitable conditions for survival and reproduction.

🧍 3. Support and Movement in Humans

In humans, support and movement depend mainly on the interaction between:

  • 🦴 Bones
  • 🦿 Joints
  • 💪 Muscles
  • 🧵 Tendons
  • 🔗 Ligaments
  • 🧊 Cartilage

🧠 Remember

Bones provide support, joints allow movement between bones, and muscles provide the force needed for movement.

🦴 4. The Human Skeleton

The skeleton is the internal framework of bones that supports and protects the body.

The adult human skeleton contains approximately 206 bones.

The skeleton performs several important functions:

  • 🧍 Supports the body and gives it shape.
  • 🛡️ Protects delicate internal organs.
  • 🏃 Provides attachment points for muscles.
  • ⚙️ Works with muscles to produce movement.
  • 🩸 Bone marrow produces blood cells.
  • 🧂 Some bones store mineral salts such as calcium and phosphate.

🦴 8. Structure of a Long Bone

A long bone has several important structures that allow it to provide strength, support and movement.

  • Compact bone: dense, hard bone providing strength.
  • Spongy bone: lighter bone containing spaces.
  • Bone marrow: found inside some bones; red marrow is involved in blood-cell production.
  • Cartilage: covers the ends of bones at joints and reduces friction.
  • Periosteum: tough outer covering of bone containing blood vessels and nerves.

🧱 9. Why are Bones Strong?

Bones are strong because they contain mineral salts, especially calcium compounds, within a framework of tough protein fibres.

This combination gives bone both strength and some flexibility.

🎯 Exam Focus

Do not say that bones are made only of calcium. Bone contains living cells, protein fibres and mineral salts.

🦿 10. What is a Joint?

A joint is a place where two or more bones meet.

Joints allow movement between bones while also helping to hold the skeleton together.

Different joints allow different types and ranges of movement.

🔄 11. Types of Joints

The major movable joints studied at O/L include:

  • ⚽ Ball-and-socket joints
  • 🚪 Hinge joints

⚽ 12. Ball-and-Socket Joint

A ball-and-socket joint allows movement in many directions, including rotation.

Examples include:

  • 💪 Shoulder joint
  • 🦵 Hip joint

One bone has a rounded end called the ball, which fits into a cup-shaped depression called the socket in another bone.

🚪 13. Hinge Joint

A hinge joint mainly allows movement in one plane, similar to the opening and closing of a door.

Examples include:

  • 💪 Elbow
  • 🦵 Knee

🧊 15. Cartilage

Cartilage is a smooth, firm and slightly flexible tissue found at the ends of bones at many joints.

Its main functions include:

  • 🛡️ Protecting the ends of bones.
  • 🔄 Reducing friction between bones.
  • 🧩 Helping joints move smoothly.
  • 💥 Absorbing some shock.

💧 16. Synovial Fluid

Synovial fluid is a slippery fluid found inside synovial joints.

It acts as a lubricant, reducing friction between the moving surfaces of bones.

🧠 Remember

Cartilage covers the bone ends, while synovial fluid lubricates the joint.

🔗 17. Ligaments

Ligaments are strong bands of connective tissue that join one bone to another bone.

They help:

  • 🔗 Hold bones together.
  • 🛡️ Stabilise joints.
  • 🚫 Prevent excessive movement that could damage the joint.

🧵 18. Tendons

Tendons are strong connective tissues that attach muscles to bones.

When a muscle contracts, the tendon transmits the pulling force to the bone, causing movement.

⚠️ Important Difference

Tendon = muscle to bone.
Ligament = bone to bone.

🧩 19. Parts of a Synovial Joint

Structure Function
Cartilage Reduces friction and protects bone ends.
Synovial fluid Lubricates the joint.
Ligament Connects bone to bone and stabilises the joint.
Joint capsule Surrounds and supports the joint.
Bone Provides the rigid framework for movement.

💪 20. What are Muscles?

Muscles are tissues that can contract and produce force.

Muscles are attached to bones by tendons. When a muscle contracts, it becomes shorter and pulls on the bone.

Muscles can therefore work with bones and joints to produce movement.

⚙️ 21. How Muscles Produce Movement

A muscle can contract and relax:

  • Contraction: the muscle becomes shorter and thicker and pulls on a bone.
  • Relaxation: the muscle returns to its resting state and no longer produces the same pulling force.

Muscles can pull on bones but cannot push them.

🧠 Key Idea

Because muscles can only pull, many movements around joints require muscles to work in opposing pairs.

🔄 22. Antagonistic Muscle Pairs

An antagonistic pair is a pair of muscles that work together to produce opposite movements.

In an antagonistic pair, when one muscle contracts, the other relaxes.

A major example is the biceps and triceps at the elbow.

💪 23. Biceps and Triceps

The biceps is found at the front of the upper arm, while the triceps is found at the back of the upper arm.

They work antagonistically to move the forearm.

🖐️ 24. Bending the Elbow

When the elbow is bent:

  1. 💪 The biceps contracts.
  2. 💪 The biceps becomes shorter and thicker.
  3. 🦴 The biceps pulls on the forearm bones through its tendon.
  4. 🔄 The forearm moves upward.
  5. 😌 The triceps relaxes.

✋ 25. Straightening the Elbow

When the elbow is straightened:

  1. 💪 The triceps contracts.
  2. 💪 The triceps becomes shorter and thicker.
  3. 🦴 The triceps pulls on the forearm bones.
  4. 🔄 The forearm moves downward and the elbow straightens.
  5. 😌 The biceps relaxes.

🧠 27. Why Muscles Work in Pairs

A single muscle can only pull a bone in one direction.

Therefore, opposite movements require another muscle to pull the bone in the opposite direction.

🎯 Exam Answer

Muscles work in antagonistic pairs because muscles can only pull when they contract; they cannot push.

🦵 28. Movement at the Knee

The knee is mainly a hinge joint.

Muscles around the thigh act across the knee joint to move the lower leg.

The muscles are arranged so that one muscle group can produce one movement while another produces the opposite movement.

🏃 29. Movement Requires Energy

Muscle contraction requires energy.

The energy used by muscles is released during respiration.

During physical activity, muscles therefore need:

  • 🍬 Glucose as a respiratory substrate.
  • 💨 Oxygen for aerobic respiration.
  • 🩸 A good blood supply to transport oxygen and nutrients.

🔗 Link with Respiration

Respiration releases energy, and muscles use this energy for contraction and movement.

🏋️ 30. Exercise and Muscles

Regular physical activity can improve muscular strength, endurance and fitness.

During exercise:

  • ❤️ Heart rate increases.
  • 💨 Breathing rate increases.
  • 🩸 More blood is supplied to active muscles.
  • ⚡ Muscles require more energy.
  • 🍬 More glucose is used in respiration.

⚡ 31. Vigorous Exercise and Anaerobic Respiration

During very vigorous exercise, the muscles may not receive enough oxygen to meet their energy demands through aerobic respiration alone.

Muscles can then obtain some energy through anaerobic respiration.

In human muscles:

Glucose → Lactic acid + energy

Lactic acid accumulation is associated with muscle fatigue and discomfort during strenuous activity.

🧪 32. Muscle Fatigue

Muscle fatigue occurs when muscles become less able to contract effectively during prolonged or strenuous activity.

One factor contributing to fatigue during vigorous exercise is the accumulation of lactic acid produced during anaerobic respiration.

After exercise, continued breathing provides oxygen that helps the body deal with the effects of anaerobic respiration.

🧍 33. Maintaining Good Posture

The skeleton and muscles work together to maintain posture.

Posture is the position in which the body is held.

Muscles continually make small adjustments to keep the body balanced and upright.

🌿 34. Support in Plants

Plants also require support to maintain their shape and keep their leaves and stems in suitable positions for photosynthesis.

Plant support can come from:

  • 💧 Water inside cells.
  • 🧱 Cell walls.
  • 🌿 Supporting tissues.
  • 🪵 Woody tissues in older plants.

💧 35. Turgor and Plant Support

Water entering plant cells creates turgor pressure.

When cells contain plenty of water, they become firm and help support the plant.

This is particularly important in young, non-woody plants.

🌿 Remember

Water-filled cells are firm and help support the plant. Loss of water causes cells to lose turgor and the plant may wilt.

🥀 36. Wilting

Wilting occurs when a plant loses water faster than it can replace it.

As water is lost:

  1. 💧 Cells lose water.
  2. 📉 Turgor pressure decreases.
  3. 🌿 Cells become less firm.
  4. 🥀 Leaves and stems may droop.

🧱 37. Cell Walls and Plant Support

Plant cells have strong cellulose cell walls.

Cell walls:

  • 🧱 Maintain cell shape.
  • 🛡️ Prevent the cell from bursting when water enters.
  • 🌿 Contribute to the overall support of the plant.

🌳 38. Woody Support

In many mature plants, woody tissues provide additional mechanical support.

The woody parts of stems are strong and help plants remain upright.

This allows some plants to grow tall and support branches and leaves.

🌱 39. Roots and Support

Roots help anchor plants firmly in the soil.

They:

  • ⚓ Anchor the plant.
  • 💧 Absorb water.
  • 🧂 Absorb mineral ions.
  • 🌿 Help prevent the plant from being easily blown over.

🌿 40. Plant Structures Involved in Support

Structure Contribution to Support
Cell wall Provides strength and maintains cell shape.
Water Produces turgor pressure inside cells.
Roots Anchor the plant in the soil.
Woody tissues Provide strong mechanical support.
Supporting tissues Strengthen stems and other plant parts.

🔄 41. Support vs Movement

Support Movement
Maintains shape and position. Changes position of the body or part of the body.
Provided by bones, cell walls, water and supporting tissues. Produced by muscles, joints and other structures.
Provides a stable framework. Allows organisms or body parts to change position.

🦴 42. Skeleton, Joints and Muscles Working Together

Movement of a limb involves several structures working together.

  1. 🧠 A signal from the nervous system stimulates a muscle.
  2. 💪 The muscle contracts.
  3. 🧵 The tendon transmits the force to the bone.
  4. 🦴 The bone moves around a joint.
  5. 🧊 Cartilage and synovial fluid reduce friction.
  6. 🔗 Ligaments help keep the joint stable.

🛡️ 43. Why are Joints Stable?

Joints must allow movement while preventing excessive or damaging movement.

Stability is provided by structures such as:

  • 🔗 Ligaments
  • 🧊 Cartilage
  • 💧 Synovial fluid
  • 🧩 Joint capsule
  • 💪 Muscles and tendons surrounding the joint

⚽ 44. Ball-and-Socket vs Hinge Joints

Feature Ball-and-Socket Hinge
Movement Many directions and rotation Mainly one plane
Example Shoulder, hip Elbow, knee
Function Allows a wide range of movement Allows bending and straightening

💪 45. Tendon vs Ligament

Tendon Ligament
Connects muscle to bone. Connects bone to bone.
Transmits the force of muscle contraction. Stabilises a joint.
Helps produce movement. Helps prevent excessive movement.

🦴 46. Bone vs Cartilage

Bone Cartilage
Hard and rigid. Firm but more flexible.
Provides major support. Covers and protects bone ends at joints.
Contains mineral salts. Provides smooth surfaces for movement.

🧠 47. Important Definitions to Know

Support: The ability to maintain the shape and position of an organism or body part.

Movement: A change in the position of the whole organism or part of it.

Skeleton: The framework of bones that supports and protects the body.

Joint: A place where two or more bones meet.

Muscle: Tissue that contracts to produce force and movement.

Tendon: Strong tissue connecting muscle to bone.

Ligament: Strong tissue connecting bone to bone.

Cartilage: Smooth, firm tissue that covers the ends of bones at many joints.

Synovial fluid: Lubricating fluid found in synovial joints.

Antagonistic pair: Two muscles that produce opposite movements.

Turgor pressure: Pressure produced by the contents of a water-filled plant cell against its cell wall.

Wilting: Drooping of plant parts caused by loss of water and reduced turgor.

❌ 49. Common O/L Mistakes

⚠️ Avoid These Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying a tendon connects bone to bone.
    ✅ A tendon connects muscle to bone.
  • ❌ Saying a ligament connects muscle to bone.
    ✅ A ligament connects bone to bone.
  • ❌ Saying muscles push bones.
    ✅ Muscles pull bones when they contract.
  • ❌ Saying the biceps contracts when the arm is straightened.
    ✅ During straightening, the triceps contracts and the biceps relaxes.
  • ❌ Saying the knee is a ball-and-socket joint.
    ✅ The knee is mainly a hinge joint.
  • ❌ Saying the shoulder is a hinge joint.
    ✅ The shoulder is a ball-and-socket joint.
  • ❌ Saying cartilage produces movement.
    ✅ Cartilage reduces friction and protects bone ends.
  • ❌ Saying plants are supported only by their roots.
    ✅ Roots anchor the plant, while cell walls, turgor and supporting tissues also contribute to support.

🔗 51. Complete Movement & Support Summary

The human skeleton forms a strong internal framework that gives the body shape and protects important organs.

Bones meet at joints. Different joints allow different types of movement. Ball-and-socket joints allow movement in many directions, while hinge joints mainly allow bending and straightening.

Cartilage protects the ends of bones and reduces friction. Synovial fluid lubricates joints, while ligaments hold bones together and help stabilise joints.

Muscles are attached to bones by tendons. When muscles contract, they pull on bones and cause movement.

Because muscles can only pull, muscles often work in antagonistic pairs. The biceps and triceps are a major example.

Plants are supported by strong cell walls, turgor pressure, roots and supporting tissues. Water is particularly important in maintaining turgor in non-woody plants.
