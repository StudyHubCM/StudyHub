💧 Water

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Complete notes on the properties, sources, purification, treatment, uses and chemical reactions of water.

1. 🌊 Introduction to Water

Water is one of the most important substances on Earth. It is essential for living organisms and is widely used in homes, agriculture, industries and laboratories.

💡 Remember: The chemical formula of water is H₂O. Each water molecule contains two hydrogen atoms chemically bonded to one oxygen atom.

2. 🧪 Chemical Formula of Water

The formula of water is H₂O.

  • H = hydrogen
  • O = oxygen
  • ₂ means there are two hydrogen atoms.

Water molecule: H₂O

Elements present: Hydrogen and oxygen

Ratio of hydrogen : oxygen atoms: 2 : 1

3. ⚛️ Structure of a Water Molecule

A water molecule contains two hydrogen atoms covalently bonded to one oxygen atom.

Diagram space: Draw the structure of an H₂O molecule showing oxygen bonded to two hydrogen atoms.

Oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen. Therefore, water is a polar molecule.

4. 🔗 Bonding in Water

The hydrogen and oxygen atoms in a water molecule are joined by covalent bonds.

A covalent bond is formed when atoms share electrons.

Key point: The O–H bonds inside a water molecule are covalent bonds.

5. 💧 Physical States of Water

State Example Particle arrangement
Solid Ice 🧊 Particles closely arranged in a fixed structure
Liquid Water 💧 Particles close together but able to move
Gas Water vapour ☁️ Particles far apart and moving freely

7. 🔥 Melting and Freezing

Melting is the change of a substance from solid to liquid.

Freezing is the change from liquid to solid.

Pure water freezes at approximately 0°C at standard atmospheric pressure.

8. ♨️ Boiling and Condensation

Boiling is the rapid change of a liquid into a gas throughout the liquid.

Condensation is the change of a gas into a liquid.

Pure water boils at approximately 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure.

9. 🌡️ Effect of Pressure on Boiling Point

The boiling point of water changes with pressure.

  • At higher pressure, water boils at a higher temperature.
  • At lower pressure, water boils at a lower temperature.

This is why water can boil at a temperature below 100°C at high altitudes where atmospheric pressure is lower.

10. 🌍 Sources of Water

Water occurs naturally in many places.

  • 🌧️ Rainwater
  • 🏞️ Rivers
  • 🌊 Lakes
  • 🌊 Seas and oceans
  • 🕳️ Wells
  • 💧 Springs
  • 🌱 Groundwater

11. 🌧️ Rainwater

Rainwater is formed when water vapour in the atmosphere condenses into droplets that fall to Earth.

Rainwater is relatively soft because it contains fewer dissolved calcium and magnesium compounds than many groundwater sources.

⚠️ Rainwater is not automatically safe to drink. It can become contaminated by microorganisms, dust, gases and materials collected from roofs or storage containers.

12. 🪨 Surface Water

Surface water includes water found in rivers, lakes, streams and reservoirs.

It may contain suspended particles, microorganisms, dissolved substances and organic matter.

13. 🕳️ Groundwater

Groundwater is water stored below the Earth's surface.

As water passes through rocks and soil, it may dissolve mineral salts such as compounds containing calcium and magnesium.

14. 🧂 Hard Water

Hard water is water that contains dissolved calcium ions and/or magnesium ions that make soap form less lather.

Common dissolved ions responsible for hardness include:

  • Ca²⁺ — calcium ions
  • Mg²⁺ — magnesium ions

15. 🧼 Soft Water

Soft water contains little or no dissolved calcium and magnesium ions and forms lather readily with soap.

Hard Water Soft Water
Contains significant Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ ions Contains little Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺
Soap forms scum more readily Soap forms lather readily
May form scale in pipes and boilers Produces less scale

16. 🧼 Soap Scum in Hard Water

Calcium and magnesium ions react with soap to form insoluble substances called scum.

This reduces the amount of soap available to form lather.

Practical effect: More soap may be required when washing with hard water.

17. 🪨 Temporary Hardness

Temporary hardness is mainly associated with dissolved calcium and magnesium hydrogencarbonates.

It can be reduced by boiling.

18. ♨️ Removal of Temporary Hardness by Boiling

Word equation:

Calcium hydrogencarbonate → calcium carbonate + carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation:

Ca(HCO₃)₂ → CaCO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O

The calcium carbonate is insoluble and can form a solid deposit.

19. 🪨 Permanent Hardness

Permanent hardness is mainly caused by dissolved calcium and magnesium salts such as sulfates and chlorides.

It is not removed simply by boiling.

20. 🧪 Removing Hardness Using Washing Soda

Washing soda, sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃), can be used to remove some calcium and magnesium ions by forming insoluble carbonates.

Word equation:

Calcium chloride + sodium carbonate → calcium carbonate + sodium chloride

Symbol equation:

CaCl₂ + Na₂CO₃ → CaCO₃ + 2NaCl

21. ⚙️ Ion Exchange

Ion-exchange materials can remove calcium and magnesium ions from water by exchanging them with other ions.

Ion-exchange processes are widely used in water-softening systems.

22. ⚠️ Problems Caused by Hard Water

  • 🧼 Wastes soap by producing scum.
  • 🔥 Produces scale in boilers and heating systems.
  • 🚰 Can cause deposits inside pipes.
  • ⚙️ Reduces efficiency of some heating equipment.

23. 🏠 Advantages of Hard Water

Hard water is not simply “bad” water.

  • It provides calcium and magnesium ions.
  • Some people prefer its taste.
  • Calcium-rich water can contribute to dietary mineral intake.

24. 🧪 Test for Water

A common chemical test for water uses anhydrous copper(II) sulfate.

Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate is white. In the presence of water, it becomes hydrated copper(II) sulfate, which is blue.

Word equation:

Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate + water → hydrated copper(II) sulfate

Symbol equation:

CuSO₄ + 5H₂O → CuSO₄·5H₂O

🔍 Observation: White anhydrous copper(II) sulfate turns blue when water is present.

25. 🔬 Another Test for Water

Cobalt(II) chloride can also be used as a test for water.

Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride is blue and becomes pink when hydrated.

Word equation:

Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride + water → hydrated cobalt(II) chloride

Symbol equation:

CoCl₂ + 6H₂O → CoCl₂·6H₂O

26. 🧪 Pure Water

Pure water contains only H₂O molecules, apart from extremely small amounts of ions produced by its natural self-ionisation.

In laboratory work, water can be purified using methods such as distillation.

27. 🧂 Impurities in Natural Water

Natural water usually contains dissolved and suspended substances.

Impurity Possible source
Suspended soil particles Runoff and erosion
Microorganisms Contamination by living organisms
Dissolved mineral salts Rocks and soil
Organic matter Plants, animals and waste
Industrial pollutants Industrial activities

28. 🚰 Water Treatment

Water treatment is the process of making water suitable for a particular use, especially for public supply.

Treatment usually involves several stages to remove suspended materials, microorganisms and other unwanted substances.

29. 🪨 Screening

Large objects such as sticks, leaves and pieces of debris can be removed by passing water through screens.

30. 🧪 Sedimentation

During sedimentation, water is allowed to stand so that heavier suspended particles settle to the bottom.

The settled material is called sludge.

31. 🧪 Coagulation

Coagulation helps small suspended particles clump together into larger particles that can settle more easily.

Aluminium sulfate may be used as a coagulant in some water-treatment systems.

32. 🪨 Filtration

Filtration removes many suspended particles by passing water through a porous material such as sand.

Important: Filtration removes suspended particles, but ordinary filtration does not necessarily remove all dissolved substances or microorganisms.

33. 🦠 Disinfection

Disinfection is used to destroy or inactivate harmful microorganisms in treated water.

Chlorine is commonly used for disinfecting public water supplies.

34. 🧪 Chlorination

Chlorination is the addition of chlorine or chlorine-containing compounds to water to control microorganisms.

Chlorine reacts with water and produces substances that contribute to its disinfecting action.

Word equation:

Chlorine + water ⇌ hydrochloric acid + hypochlorous acid

Symbol equation:

Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO

35. ☀️ Boiling as a Water Treatment Method

Boiling water can kill or inactivate many disease-causing microorganisms.

However, boiling does not remove most dissolved salts or other non-volatile dissolved substances.

36. 💧 Distillation

Distillation can be used to obtain relatively pure water from water containing dissolved substances.

The water is heated to form water vapour. The vapour is then cooled and condensed back into liquid water.

Diagram space: Draw and label a simple distillation apparatus showing the flask, thermometer, condenser, water inlet, water outlet and receiving container.

37. ♨️ Distillation Process

  1. Water is heated.
  2. Water changes into vapour.
  3. The vapour enters a condenser.
  4. The vapour is cooled.
  5. The vapour condenses into liquid water.
  6. The purified water is collected.

38. 🧪 Distillation and Dissolved Salts

Most dissolved non-volatile salts do not evaporate with water during ordinary distillation.

They remain in the original container while the water vapour is condensed and collected separately.

39. 🔬 Distilled Water vs Tap Water

Distilled Water Tap Water
Contains very few dissolved substances Contains dissolved substances
Prepared by distillation Prepared by water-treatment processes
Useful in many laboratory applications Usually treated for domestic use

40. ⚗️ Chemical Reaction of Water with Sodium

Sodium reacts vigorously with water to produce sodium hydroxide and hydrogen gas.

Word equation:

Sodium + water → sodium hydroxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation:

2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂

41. ⚗️ Chemical Reaction of Water with Calcium

Calcium reacts with water to produce calcium hydroxide and hydrogen.

Word equation:

Calcium + water → calcium hydroxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation:

Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂

42. 🔥 Reaction of Water with Magnesium

Magnesium reacts very slowly with cold water, but reacts more readily with hot water or steam.

Word equation with steam:

Magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation:

Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂

43. ⚙️ Water and Iron

Iron does not react significantly with cold water, but hot iron can react with steam to form iron oxide and hydrogen.

Word equation:

Iron + steam → iron(II,III) oxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation:

3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂

44. 🧪 Water as a Solvent

Water is an important solvent and dissolves many ionic and polar substances.

This is why water is sometimes called a universal solvent, although this does not mean that it dissolves every substance.

45. 🧂 Soluble and Insoluble Substances

Type Meaning Example
Soluble Dissolves in a particular solvent Many salts in water
Insoluble Does not dissolve significantly Sand in water

46. 🌡️ Factors Affecting Solubility

For many solid substances, increasing temperature increases solubility, although the effect depends on the substance.

For gases dissolved in water, increasing temperature generally decreases their solubility.

47. 🌬️ Dissolved Gases in Water

Natural water contains dissolved gases from the atmosphere.

Oxygen dissolved in water is important for aquatic organisms.

48. 🐟 Importance of Dissolved Oxygen

Fish and many other aquatic organisms depend on dissolved oxygen for respiration.

Pollution by biodegradable organic matter can reduce dissolved oxygen because microorganisms use oxygen while decomposing the organic material.

49. 🌱 Water Pollution

Water pollution occurs when harmful substances enter water and reduce its quality or make it unsuitable for particular uses.

  • 🏭 Industrial waste
  • 🌾 Agricultural chemicals
  • 🚽 Domestic sewage
  • 🛢️ Oil and petroleum products
  • 🗑️ Solid waste
  • 🦠 Disease-causing microorganisms

50. 🦠 Biological Water Pollution

Water contaminated with pathogenic microorganisms can transmit water-borne diseases.

Examples include cholera and typhoid.

💡 Safe water supplies require effective treatment, protection from contamination and proper storage.

51. 🌱 Agricultural Water Pollution

Fertilisers and pesticides can enter rivers and lakes through runoff.

Excess nutrients, especially nitrogen and phosphorus compounds, can contribute to eutrophication.

52. 🌿 Eutrophication

Eutrophication is the enrichment of a body of water with nutrients, often leading to excessive growth of algae and aquatic plants.

  1. Excess nutrients enter the water.
  2. Algae and aquatic plants grow rapidly.
  3. Light penetration may decrease.
  4. Plants and algae die and are decomposed by microorganisms.
  5. Decomposition increases oxygen consumption.
  6. Dissolved oxygen levels may fall.
  7. Aquatic organisms may be harmed or die.

53. 🏭 Industrial Pollution

Industrial activities may release chemicals, heavy metals and other pollutants into water if waste is not properly controlled.

54. 🚽 Sewage Pollution

Untreated sewage can introduce microorganisms, organic matter and nutrients into water.

Proper sewage treatment helps reduce these pollutants before water is discharged into the environment.

55. 🌍 Importance of Water to Living Organisms

  • 💧 It is a major component of living cells.
  • 🧪 It acts as a solvent for many biological substances.
  • 🚚 It helps transport dissolved substances.
  • 🌡️ It helps regulate temperature.
  • 🌱 It is required for photosynthesis.
  • 🧬 It provides a medium for many biochemical reactions.

56. 🌱 Water in Photosynthesis

Plants use water and carbon dioxide to produce glucose during photosynthesis.

Word equation:

Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Symbol equation:

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Light energy and chlorophyll are required for photosynthesis.

57. ⚡ Electrolysis of Water

Water can be decomposed into hydrogen and oxygen by electrolysis when suitable conditions are provided.

Word equation:

Water → hydrogen + oxygen

Symbol equation:

2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂

🔋 During electrolysis, hydrogen is produced at the cathode and oxygen at the anode under the usual arrangement for acidified water.

58. ⚡ Products of Electrolysis

Electrode Product
Cathode (−) Hydrogen, H₂
Anode (+) Oxygen, O₂

59. 🔥 Test for Hydrogen

Hydrogen can be tested using a lighted splint.

Hydrogen produces a characteristic squeaky pop when tested with a lighted splint.

60. 🔥 Test for Oxygen

Oxygen supports combustion.

A glowing splint is relit in oxygen.

61. 🧪 Water and Copper(II) Oxide

Water can be formed when hydrogen reacts with oxygen.

Word equation:

Hydrogen + oxygen → water

Symbol equation:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

⚠️ This reaction releases a large amount of energy. Hydrogen and oxygen mixtures can be dangerous, so this reaction is studied under controlled laboratory conditions.

62. 🔄 Water Cycle

The water cycle describes the continuous movement of water between the Earth's surface and atmosphere.

Diagram space: Draw the water cycle showing evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection, infiltration and transpiration.

63. ☀️ Evaporation

Evaporation is the change of liquid water into water vapour from the surface of the liquid.

It can occur at temperatures below the boiling point.

64. ☁️ Condensation in the Water Cycle

Water vapour rises and may cool. It condenses into tiny water droplets that contribute to cloud formation.

65. 🌧️ Precipitation

Precipitation is water that falls from clouds to Earth's surface.

It may occur as rain, and in colder conditions as snow, sleet or hail.

66. 🌱 Transpiration

Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants, mainly through stomata in leaves.

67. 🌍 Importance of the Water Cycle

  • ♻️ Recycles water in the environment.
  • 🌱 Supplies water to plants and ecosystems.
  • 🌧️ Helps maintain rainfall.
  • 🏞️ Replenishes rivers, lakes and groundwater.
  • 🌊 Connects atmospheric, surface and underground water.

68. 🧪 Water as a Reactant

Water participates in many chemical reactions.

Examples include reactions with reactive metals, electrolysis and photosynthesis.

69. 🧪 Water as a Product

Water can also be produced during chemical reactions.

Word equation:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

70. 🧪 Water in Neutralisation

Acids react with bases to form salt and water.

Word equation:

Hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water

Symbol equation:

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

The formation of water is an important feature of neutralisation between an acid and a hydroxide.

71. ⚗️ Water and Metal Oxides

Some metal oxides react with water to form alkaline solutions.

Word equation:

Calcium oxide + water → calcium hydroxide

Symbol equation:

CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂

73. ⚡ Water in Hydroelectric Power

Moving water can turn turbines connected to generators.

This allows the energy of moving or falling water to be converted into electrical energy.

74. 🧪 Water in the Laboratory

Water is commonly used in laboratories as:

  • 🧪 A solvent
  • 💧 A reagent
  • ⚗️ A medium for reactions
  • 🧴 A cleaning agent for laboratory apparatus
  • 🌡️ A cooling medium in some experiments

77. ⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying filtration removes all dissolved salts.
    ✅ Filtration mainly removes suspended particles.
  • ❌ Saying all hard water is unsafe to drink.
    ✅ Hardness is mainly related to dissolved calcium and magnesium ions; it is different from microbial contamination.
  • ❌ Saying boiling removes every impurity.
    ✅ Boiling can destroy many microorganisms but does not remove most dissolved salts.
  • ❌ Confusing evaporation with boiling.
    ✅ Evaporation occurs at the surface and can occur below the boiling point; boiling occurs throughout the liquid.
  • ❌ Forgetting that H₂O contains two hydrogen atoms.
    ✅ The subscript ₂ applies to hydrogen.
  • ❌ Writing unbalanced water reactions.
    ✅ Check that every element has the same number of atoms on both sides.
