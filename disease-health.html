BIOLOGY • O/L 0510

🦠 Disease & Health

Understand health, diseases, pathogens, transmission, prevention, immunity, vaccination, antibiotics and the effects of disease on the body.

1. ❤️ What is Health?

Health is a state of physical, mental and social well-being, rather than simply the absence of disease.

Good health allows a person to carry out normal activities effectively and maintain a good quality of life.

📌 Factors that affect health

  • 🍎 Diet and nutrition.
  • 🏃 Physical activity.
  • 😴 Adequate rest and sleep.
  • 🧼 Personal hygiene.
  • 💧 Clean water.
  • 🏠 Good living conditions.
  • 🧠 Mental and social well-being.
  • 🦠 Exposure to pathogens.
  • 🧬 Genetic factors.

2. 🦠 What is Disease?

A disease is a condition that interferes with the normal functioning of the body or causes abnormal changes in the body.

Diseases can be divided into two major groups:

  1. 🦠 Infectious diseases.
  2. 🫀 Non-infectious diseases.

3. 🦠 Infectious Diseases

An infectious disease is a disease caused by a pathogen that can be transmitted from one host to another, either directly or indirectly.

Infectious diseases are caused by disease-causing organisms or agents called pathogens.

4. 🧫 Pathogens

A pathogen is a microorganism or infectious agent that causes disease.

The main groups of pathogens are:

  • 🧫 Bacteria.
  • 🦠 Viruses.
  • 🍄 Fungi.
  • 🧬 Protozoa.
  • 🪱 Helminths or parasitic worms.

5. 🧫 Bacteria

Bacteria are microscopic single-celled organisms. Some bacteria are harmless or useful, while others cause disease.

Disease-causing bacteria may reproduce rapidly inside a host and may produce substances that damage body tissues.

Examples of bacterial diseases include:

  • Tuberculosis (TB).
  • Cholera.
  • Typhoid.
  • Some forms of food poisoning.

6. 🦠 Viruses

A virus is a very small infectious agent that can reproduce only inside living cells.

Viruses enter host cells and use the host cell's machinery to make more virus particles.

Examples of viral diseases include:

  • Influenza.
  • Measles.
  • Dengue fever.
  • HIV/AIDS.
  • Polio.

9. 🍄 Fungi

Some fungi are pathogenic and can cause infections in humans.

Examples include:

  • Ringworm.
  • Athlete's foot.
  • Some fungal skin infections.

10. 🧬 Protozoa

Protozoa are microscopic single-celled organisms. Some protozoa are parasites and cause disease.

An important example is Plasmodium, the organism that causes malaria.

11. 🪱 Parasitic Worms

Some parasitic worms live inside or on the bodies of other organisms and obtain nutrients from their hosts.

Examples include:

  • Roundworms.
  • Tapeworms.
  • Hookworms.

13. 🫁 Tuberculosis (TB)

Tuberculosis is an infectious disease caused by bacteria, commonly affecting the lungs.

It can spread through the air when an infected person releases tiny infectious droplets, particularly through coughing or sneezing.

Possible symptoms include:

  • Persistent cough.
  • Fever.
  • Night sweats.
  • Weight loss.
  • Tiredness.

🛡️ Prevention

  • Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment.
  • Good ventilation.
  • Good respiratory hygiene.
  • Vaccination where recommended.

14. 💧 Cholera

Cholera is a bacterial disease that can spread through contaminated water or food.

The disease can cause severe diarrhoea and rapid loss of water and salts.

⚠️ Major danger: Dehydration

Severe diarrhoea can cause the body to lose large amounts of water and mineral ions.

Safe drinking water, sanitation, proper sewage disposal and good hygiene help prevent cholera.

15. 🍚 Typhoid

Typhoid is a bacterial disease that is mainly spread by consuming food or water contaminated with the bacterium.

Possible symptoms include:

  • High fever.
  • Headache.
  • Abdominal pain.
  • Weakness.

Good sanitation, safe water, food hygiene and appropriate vaccination help reduce the risk.

16. 🦟 Malaria

Malaria is caused by parasites of the genus Plasmodium.

It is transmitted by the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito.

The mosquito acts as a vector.

17. 🦟 Malaria Transmission

The basic transmission cycle is:

🦟 Infected mosquito bites a person

↓

🧬 Plasmodium enters the person's bloodstream

↓

🩸 Parasites reproduce in the body

↓

🦟 Another mosquito feeds on the infected person

↓

🦟 The mosquito becomes infected

↓

🦟 The infected mosquito can transmit the parasite to another person

18. 🛡️ Preventing Malaria

  • Use insecticide-treated mosquito nets.
  • Reduce mosquito breeding sites.
  • Remove stagnant water where mosquitoes can breed.
  • Use appropriate mosquito-control measures.
  • Seek appropriate medical treatment when malaria is suspected.

19. 🤒 Influenza

Influenza (flu) is a viral infection that mainly affects the respiratory system.

Common symptoms include:

  • Fever.
  • Cough.
  • Sore throat.
  • Muscle aches.
  • Tiredness.

It can spread through respiratory droplets and contaminated hands or surfaces.

20. 🔴 Measles

Measles is a highly infectious viral disease.

Symptoms may include:

  • Fever.
  • Cough.
  • Runny nose.
  • Red eyes.
  • Skin rash.

Vaccination is an important way of preventing measles.

21. 🧬 HIV and AIDS

HIV is a virus that attacks cells involved in the immune system.

If HIV infection is not appropriately treated, it can progressively weaken the immune system and may lead to AIDS.

Modern treatment can greatly reduce the amount of HIV in the body and helps people with HIV live much longer and healthier lives.

22. 🛡️ HIV Transmission and Prevention

HIV can be transmitted through certain infected body fluids, including blood, and through sexual transmission or from mother to child during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding.

It is not spread through ordinary activities such as shaking hands, hugging, sharing normal classroom space or eating food together.

🛡️ Prevention

  • Use appropriate barrier protection during sexual activity.
  • Do not share needles or other equipment that can transfer blood.
  • Use screened blood for transfusions.
  • Testing helps people know their HIV status.
  • Effective treatment greatly reduces the risk of transmission.

23. 🧫 How Pathogens Cause Disease

Pathogens can cause disease in several ways.

  • They may damage host cells.
  • They may reproduce inside the host.
  • Some bacteria produce toxins.
  • They may interfere with normal body functions.
  • The body's immune response may also contribute to symptoms.

24. 🚶 Direct Transmission

Direct transmission occurs when a pathogen passes directly from an infected person or animal to another person.

Examples include:

  • Direct skin contact.
  • Contact with infected body fluids.
  • Respiratory droplets at close range.

25. 💨 Airborne Transmission

Some pathogens can spread through the air in respiratory droplets or smaller airborne particles.

Examples include some respiratory infections such as:

  • Influenza.
  • Measles.
  • Tuberculosis.

26. 💧 Water-Borne Transmission

Water-borne diseases are spread when people consume water contaminated with pathogens.

Examples include cholera and some forms of typhoid.

27. 🍽️ Food-Borne Transmission

Food can become contaminated by pathogens through unsafe handling, contaminated water, poor hygiene or inadequate cooking.

Eating contaminated food can result in food-borne disease.

28. 🦟 Vectors

A vector is an organism that carries a pathogen from one host to another.

Mosquitoes are important vectors of several diseases.

🦟 Important O/L example: The female Anopheles mosquito transmits the Plasmodium parasite that causes malaria.

29. 🧼 Personal Hygiene

Good personal hygiene reduces the chance of pathogens entering or spreading through the body.

  • Wash hands regularly with soap and clean water.
  • Keep the body clean.
  • Keep teeth and mouth clean.
  • Cover coughs and sneezes appropriately.
  • Keep cuts and wounds clean.
  • Avoid sharing personal items that may spread infection.

30. 💧 Safe Drinking Water

Drinking water should be protected from contamination.

Water can be made safer by appropriate treatment such as boiling, filtration or chlorination.

Clean water is particularly important in preventing water-borne diseases such as cholera.

31. 🍎 Food Hygiene

  • Wash hands before preparing or eating food.
  • Wash fruits and vegetables appropriately.
  • Cook food thoroughly.
  • Protect food from flies and other pests.
  • Keep raw and cooked food separated.
  • Store food at suitable temperatures.

32. 🚽 Sanitation

Sanitation involves maintaining conditions that prevent human waste and other sources of contamination from spreading disease.

Good sanitation includes:

  • Proper disposal of human waste.
  • Safe sewage systems.
  • Clean toilets.
  • Clean surroundings.
  • Safe drinking water.

33. 🗑️ Proper Waste Disposal

Poor waste disposal can encourage the growth and spread of pathogens and attract disease vectors such as flies and rats.

Waste should therefore be collected, contained and disposed of safely.

34. 💉 Vaccination

Vaccination involves introducing a harmless form or part of a pathogen, or another preparation containing pathogen antigens, into the body to stimulate an immune response.

Vaccines help the immune system develop protection against specific diseases.

35. 🧬 Antigens

An antigen is a substance recognised by the immune system that can stimulate an immune response.

Vaccines contain antigens that stimulate the body to develop protection without causing the disease in the usual way.

36. 🛡️ Antibodies

Antibodies are proteins produced by certain white blood cells called lymphocytes.

Each antibody is specific to a particular antigen.

Antibodies can help destroy or neutralise pathogens and their toxins.

37. 🧠 Immune Response

When a pathogen enters the body, the immune system recognises foreign antigens.

The response may involve:

  1. Recognition of foreign antigens.
  2. Activation of lymphocytes.
  3. Production of specific antibodies.
  4. Destruction or neutralisation of the pathogen.
  5. Formation of memory cells.

38. 🧠 Memory Cells

After an immune response, some lymphocytes remain as memory cells.

If the same pathogen enters the body again, memory cells allow a faster and stronger immune response.

📌 Why is the second response faster?

Memory cells already recognise the pathogen's antigen, so specific lymphocytes can respond rapidly and produce antibodies more quickly.

39. 💉 How Vaccination Produces Protection

  1. A vaccine introduces specific antigens.
  2. The immune system recognises the antigens.
  3. Lymphocytes produce specific antibodies.
  4. Memory cells are formed.
  5. Later exposure to the pathogen produces a rapid immune response.

40. 👥 Herd Immunity

When a large proportion of a population is immune to an infectious disease, the spread of that disease can be reduced.

This can provide indirect protection to some people who cannot be vaccinated or who do not develop strong immunity.

41. 🛡️ Natural and Artificial Immunity

Natural Immunity Artificial Immunity
Develops through natural exposure or natural transfer of antibodies. Develops through medical intervention such as vaccination.
Can occur after infection. Vaccination stimulates the immune system without causing the disease in the usual way.

42. 💊 Antibiotics

Antibiotics are medicines used to treat infections caused by bacteria.

They do not work against viruses such as influenza because viruses do not have the bacterial structures or processes targeted by antibiotics.

43. 💊 Antibiotic Resistance

Antibiotic resistance occurs when bacteria become less sensitive or resistant to antibiotics that previously killed them or stopped their growth.

Resistant bacteria can survive treatment and reproduce.

⚠️ How resistance increases

  • Random mutations can produce resistant bacteria.
  • Antibiotics kill susceptible bacteria.
  • Resistant bacteria survive.
  • The resistant bacteria reproduce.
  • The population becomes more resistant.

44. 🚫 Preventing Antibiotic Resistance

  • Use antibiotics only when appropriate.
  • Use antibiotics as directed by a qualified healthcare professional.
  • Do not use antibiotics to treat viral infections.
  • Do not share antibiotics with other people.
  • Use the correct antibiotic when one is needed.

45. 🫀 Non-Infectious Diseases

Non-infectious diseases are diseases that are not caused by pathogens and cannot normally be transmitted from one person to another as an infection.

They may result from:

  • 🧬 Genetic factors.
  • 🍎 Poor diet.
  • 🚬 Harmful lifestyle factors.
  • 🏭 Environmental factors.
  • ☢️ Radiation or harmful substances.
  • ⚠️ Accidents and injuries.

46. 🍬 Diabetes

Diabetes is a condition in which blood glucose levels become abnormally high because the body does not produce enough effective insulin or does not respond properly to insulin.

Insulin normally helps cells take up glucose and helps regulate blood glucose concentration.

47. 🩸 Hypertension

Hypertension means persistently high blood pressure.

Long-term high blood pressure can increase the risk of damage to organs such as the heart, brain and kidneys.

48. 🫀 Coronary Heart Disease

Coronary heart disease occurs when the coronary arteries supplying the heart muscle become narrowed or blocked.

Reduced blood flow means less oxygen reaches the heart muscle.

⚠️ Risk factors

  • Smoking.
  • Unhealthy diet.
  • Lack of physical activity.
  • Obesity.
  • High blood pressure.
  • High blood cholesterol.
  • Family history and genetic factors.

49. ⚖️ Obesity

Obesity is excessive accumulation of body fat that can increase the risk of several health problems.

Long-term obesity is associated with an increased risk of conditions such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension and coronary heart disease.

50. 🩸 Anaemia

Anaemia occurs when the blood has a reduced ability to transport oxygen, often because there is insufficient haemoglobin or too few red blood cells.

Iron deficiency is an important cause of anaemia.

Possible symptoms include:

  • Tiredness.
  • Weakness.
  • Shortness of breath.
  • Pale appearance.

51. 🧬 Genetic Diseases

Some diseases are caused partly or completely by inherited genetic changes.

Examples include:

  • Haemophilia.
  • Sickle cell disease.
  • Some inherited forms of blindness.

52. 🥗 Diet and Disease

A balanced diet provides the nutrients required for normal growth, repair and body functions.

An unhealthy diet can increase the risk of several non-infectious diseases.

🍎 Healthy eating involves:

  • A variety of foods.
  • Appropriate amounts of carbohydrates.
  • Protein for growth and repair.
  • Healthy sources of fats.
  • Fruit and vegetables.
  • Enough water.
  • Limited excessive salt, sugar and unhealthy fats.

53. 🏃 Exercise and Health

Regular physical activity helps maintain physical fitness and can reduce the risk of several non-infectious diseases.

Exercise can help maintain a healthy body mass, improve cardiovascular fitness and support mental well-being.

54. 🚭 Smoking and Health

Smoking damages the respiratory system and increases the risk of several diseases.

Smoking is associated with:

  • Lung cancer.
  • Chronic respiratory disease.
  • Coronary heart disease.
  • Reduced lung function.

Tobacco smoke contains harmful substances including nicotine and carbon monoxide, as well as many chemicals that can damage body tissues.

55. 🫁 Smoking and the Lungs

Harmful substances in cigarette smoke can damage the cilia lining the airways.

This reduces the ability of the respiratory system to remove mucus and trapped particles effectively.

Smoking also damages lung tissue and reduces the efficiency of gaseous exchange.

56. 🧠 Mental Health

Mental health is an important part of overall health.

Good mental health is supported by healthy relationships, adequate sleep, physical activity, appropriate management of stress and seeking support when needed.

57. 🏥 Prevention of Disease

Disease prevention involves reducing exposure to causes of disease and reducing factors that increase the risk of illness.

🛡️ Major methods of prevention

  • 🧼 Good personal hygiene.
  • 💧 Safe water.
  • 🍽️ Safe food preparation.
  • 🚽 Good sanitation.
  • 💉 Vaccination.
  • 🦟 Vector control.
  • 🗑️ Proper waste disposal.
  • 🏃 Healthy lifestyle.
  • 🏥 Early diagnosis and treatment.

58. 🧼 Chain of Infection

The spread of an infectious disease can be understood as a chain involving several stages.

🦠 Pathogen

↓

🧍 Source / reservoir

↓

🚪 Exit from source

↓

↓

↓

🧍 Susceptible host

Breaking any part of this chain can help reduce disease transmission.

59. 🧪 Control of Infectious Diseases

Control Method How it Helps
Vaccination Produces immunity and reduces the number of susceptible people.
Hand washing Removes pathogens and reduces transmission.
Clean water Reduces water-borne disease.
Sanitation Prevents contamination by human waste.
Vector control Reduces transmission by vectors.
Isolation where appropriate Reduces contact between infectious people and others.
Effective treatment Can reduce disease severity and, for some infections, transmission.

60. 🧪 Investigation: Effect of Hand Washing

The effectiveness of hand washing can be investigated using harmless methods that demonstrate how cleaning reduces the transfer of microbes.

🔬 Important Experimental Ideas

  • Use a suitable control for comparison.
  • Keep other conditions the same.
  • Use safe, approved materials and procedures.
  • Compare results before and after cleaning.

📌 In school practical work, cultures of unknown environmental microorganisms should not be handled without appropriate laboratory safety procedures.

61. 🧪 Investigating the Effect of Temperature on Microbial Growth

Microorganisms generally require suitable environmental conditions for growth.

A school investigation may compare microbial growth under different controlled conditions using safe, approved organisms and procedures.

📌 Experimental terms

  • Independent variable: the factor deliberately changed.
  • Dependent variable: the factor measured.
  • Control variables: factors kept constant.

62. 🧬 Infectious vs Non-Infectious Diseases

Infectious Disease Non-Infectious Disease
Caused by pathogens. Not caused by pathogens.
Can be transmitted between hosts. Does not spread as an infection from person to person.
Examples: malaria, cholera, TB. Examples: diabetes, hypertension, coronary heart disease.
Can often be controlled using hygiene, vaccination and other infection-control methods. Risk can often be reduced through healthy lifestyle choices and other preventive measures.

63. 🦠 Virus vs Bacterium

Virus Bacterium
Not a complete cell. Single-celled organism.
Much smaller than most bacteria. Larger than viruses.
Contains genetic material inside a protein coat. Contains DNA in a cell containing cytoplasm and a cell membrane.
Can reproduce only inside living cells. Can reproduce independently when suitable conditions are available.
Antibiotics do not work against viruses. Some bacterial infections can be treated with antibiotics.

64. 🧠 Disease and the Immune System

The body has several lines of defence against pathogens.

  • 🧴 Skin: forms a physical barrier.
  • 👁️ Tears: help wash microorganisms from the eyes and contain antimicrobial substances.
  • 🫁 Mucus: traps particles and microorganisms.
  • ⚡ Cilia: move mucus and trapped particles away from the lungs.
  • 🧪 Stomach acid: kills many microorganisms swallowed with food.
  • 🩸 White blood cells: defend the body against pathogens.

65. 🩸 White Blood Cells

White blood cells are involved in defending the body against pathogens.

Different white blood cells have different roles.

  • Phagocytes engulf and digest pathogens.
  • Lymphocytes produce specific antibodies and are involved in immune memory.

66. 🧫 Phagocytosis

Phagocytosis is the process by which a phagocyte surrounds, engulfs and digests a pathogen.

  1. The phagocyte moves towards the pathogen.
  2. It surrounds and engulfs the pathogen.
  3. The pathogen is enclosed inside the cell.
  4. Digestive enzymes break down the pathogen.

67. 🧬 Specific Immunity

Specific immunity means that the immune response targets particular foreign antigens.

Antibodies have specific shapes that allow them to bind to their matching antigens.

68. 🛡️ Why Vaccines Are Important

Vaccination protects individuals and helps reduce the spread of infectious diseases through populations.

Vaccines have been important in controlling diseases such as measles, polio and other serious infections.

69. 🌍 Public Health

Public health involves actions taken to protect and improve the health of communities and populations.

Examples include:

  • Vaccination programmes.
  • Clean water supplies.
  • Sewage treatment.
  • Waste management.
  • Disease surveillance.
  • Health education.
  • Vector-control programmes.

70. 📊 Risk Factors for Disease

A risk factor is something that increases the likelihood of developing a disease.

Risk Factor Possible Health Effect
Smoking Increases risk of lung disease, cancer and heart disease.
Unhealthy diet Can increase risk of obesity, diabetes and cardiovascular disease.
Lack of physical activity Can increase risk of obesity and cardiovascular disease.
Unsafe water Increases risk of water-borne infections.
Poor sanitation Increases spread of infectious diseases.
Exposure to pathogens Can result in infectious disease.

71. 🧠 Disease Symptoms vs Causes

A cause is what produces a disease, while a symptom is a sign or effect experienced because of the disease.

🦠 Example: In malaria, the Plasmodium parasite is the cause of the disease, while fever and chills are symptoms.

72. 🧪 Disease Diagnosis

Diagnosis involves identifying the disease or condition affecting a person.

Depending on the disease, diagnosis may involve physical examination, medical history, blood tests, imaging or laboratory tests.

Early diagnosis can allow appropriate treatment to begin sooner.

73. 💊 Treatment vs Prevention

Treatment Prevention
Deals with disease after it occurs. Attempts to stop disease from occurring or spreading.
Examples include appropriate medicines and medical care. Examples include vaccination, hygiene and sanitation.

74. 📚 Important Definitions

Health: A state of physical, mental and social well-being.

Disease: A condition that interferes with normal body functioning.

Pathogen: A disease-causing microorganism or infectious agent.

Infectious disease: A disease caused by a pathogen that can be transmitted between hosts.

Non-infectious disease: A disease not caused by a pathogen and not transmitted as an infection.

Vector: An organism that carries a pathogen from one host to another.

Immunity: The ability of the body to resist a particular infection.

Antigen: A substance recognised by the immune system that can stimulate an immune response.

Antibody: A specific protein produced by lymphocytes in response to an antigen.

Vaccine: A preparation containing antigens that stimulates protective immunity.

Antibiotic: A medicine used to treat bacterial infections.

Sanitation: Practices that help maintain clean and hygienic conditions and prevent disease transmission.

Phagocytosis: The engulfing and digestion of pathogens by phagocytes.

76. 📝 O/L Disease Question Strategy

🧠 When asked about a disease, remember:

  1. Name the disease.
  2. Identify the pathogen or cause.
  3. State how it is transmitted.
  4. Give important symptoms.
  5. Explain how it can be prevented or controlled.

📌 For example, for malaria, remember:

Plasmodium → female Anopheles mosquito → vector → fever/chills → mosquito control and prevention.

77. ❌ Common O/L Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying that all microorganisms are harmful.
  • ❌ Saying that antibiotics kill viruses.
  • ❌ Calling a mosquito the cause of malaria.
  • ❌ Forgetting that Plasmodium is the pathogen causing malaria.
  • ❌ Confusing a pathogen with a vector.
  • ❌ Saying that vaccination directly kills every pathogen.
  • ❌ Saying that antibodies are pathogens.
  • ❌ Confusing antigens with antibodies.
  • ❌ Saying that infectious diseases are always caused by bacteria.
  • ❌ Saying that HIV is spread by ordinary casual contact.
  • ❌ Confusing symptoms with the cause of a disease.
  • ❌ Forgetting the importance of sanitation in preventing water-borne diseases.
