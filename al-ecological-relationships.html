🌍 Ecological Relationships Between Organisms

Advanced Biology notes on interactions between organisms, competition, predation, parasitism, mutualism, ecological niches, coexistence and relationships within ecosystems.

1. 🌍 Introduction to Ecological Relationships

📖 Definition

An ecological relationship is an interaction between organisms of the same species or different species within an ecosystem.

Organisms do not live independently. They interact with other organisms while obtaining food, space, shelter, mates and other resources necessary for survival and reproduction.

🔗 Why Organisms Interact

  • 🍽️ To obtain food.
  • 💧 To obtain water.
  • 🏠 To obtain shelter or living space.
  • 🌱 To obtain nutrients and other resources.
  • ❤️ To reproduce.
  • 🛡️ To obtain protection.
  • ⚔️ To compete for limited resources.
  • 🐾 To feed on other organisms.
  • 🦠 To live in association with other organisms.

⭐ Key A/L Idea

An interaction may benefit one organism, benefit both organisms, harm one or both organisms, or have little measurable effect on one of the organisms.

2. 🔗 Major Types of Ecological Relationships

Relationship Species A Species B General Effect
Competition − − Both organisms are negatively affected by competition for limited resources.
Predation + − Predator benefits; prey is harmed.
Herbivory + − Herbivore obtains food; plant is damaged.
Parasitism + − Parasite benefits; host is harmed.
Mutualism + + Both organisms benefit.
Commensalism + 0 One benefits while the other is not significantly affected.
Antagonism − − / 0 An interaction involving inhibition, harm or suppression.

🧠 Remember the Symbols

+ = benefit    − = harm or negative effect    0 = little or no measurable effect

3. 🐾 Intraspecific and Interspecific Relationships

🧬 Intraspecific Relationships

Intraspecific means occurring between organisms belonging to the same species.

Example: Two male antelopes competing for access to mates.

🌍 Interspecific Relationships

Interspecific means occurring between organisms belonging to different species.

Example: A hawk feeding on a rat.

Feature Intraspecific Interspecific
Meaning Between members of the same species. Between members of different species.
Example Two lions competing for territory. Lion feeding on an antelope.
Competition Common when resources are limited. Occurs when different species require similar resources.

4. ⚔️ Competition

📖 Definition

Competition occurs when organisms require the same limited resource.

The resource may include food, water, light, minerals, territory, shelter or mates.

🌱 Types of Competition

4.1 🐾 Intraspecific Competition

Competition between members of the same species.

Example: Young plants of the same species competing for light, water and mineral ions.

4.2 🌍 Interspecific Competition

Competition between members of different species.

Example: Different plant species competing for sunlight and mineral ions.

📌 Resources That May Be Limited

  • ☀️ Light
  • 💧 Water
  • 🍃 Food
  • 🧂 Mineral ions
  • 🏠 Space
  • 🌳 Shelter
  • ❤️ Mates
  • 🗺️ Territory

5. 🚫 Competitive Exclusion

The competitive exclusion principle states that two species with essentially the same ecological requirements cannot indefinitely occupy exactly the same niche in a stable environment if they compete for the same limiting resources.

🌿 Possible Outcomes

  • One species may outcompete the other.
  • One species may decline or disappear locally.
  • Both species may survive by using different resources.
  • Species may occupy different parts of the habitat.
  • Species may feed at different times.

⭐ A/L Connection

Competition is strongly related to the concept of the ecological niche.

6. 🦁 Predation

📖 Definition

Predation is an interaction in which one organism, the predator, captures and consumes another organism, the prey.

🐆 Examples

  • 🦅 Hawk feeding on a rodent.
  • 🦁 Lion feeding on an antelope.
  • 🐸 Frog feeding on insects.
  • 🐍 Snake feeding on a frog.
  • 🐟 Fish feeding on smaller aquatic organisms.

🎯 Importance of Predation

  • Controls prey populations.
  • Transfers energy through food chains.
  • Removes weak or vulnerable individuals.
  • Can influence natural selection.
  • Maintains interactions between populations.
  • Can prevent some prey species from becoming excessively abundant.

7. 📈 Predator–Prey Relationships

Predator and prey populations often influence each other.

🔄 Typical Pattern

  1. 🐇 Prey population increases.
  2. 🦊 More food becomes available to predators.
  3. 🦊 Predator population increases.
  4. 🐇 Increased predation reduces prey population.
  5. 🦊 Reduced prey availability causes predator numbers to decrease.
  6. 🐇 Prey population may then recover.

📊 Diagram Space

⏱️ Time Lag

Predator numbers usually do not increase immediately after prey numbers rise because reproduction and population growth require time.

8. 🌿 Herbivory

Herbivory is an interaction in which an animal feeds on plant material such as leaves, stems, roots, seeds, fruits or flowers.

🐛 Examples

  • 🐛 Caterpillars feeding on leaves.
  • 🐐 Goats feeding on grasses and shrubs.
  • 🐘 Elephants feeding on vegetation.
  • 🦗 Grasshoppers feeding on crops.

🌱 Effects on Plants

Herbivory may reduce the plant's photosynthetic surface, damage tissues, reduce growth or reduce reproductive success.

🛡️ Plant Defences Against Herbivory

  • 🌵 Thorns and spines.
  • ☠️ Toxic chemicals.
  • 😖 Bitter or unpleasant compounds.
  • 🪴 Tough leaves.
  • 🧪 Chemical deterrents.
  • 🌿 Rapid replacement of damaged tissues.

9. 🦠 Parasitism

📖 Definition

Parasitism is a relationship in which one organism, the parasite, obtains nutrients or other benefits from another organism, the host, while harming the host.

🦠 Examples

  • 🪱 Tapeworm living in the intestine of a human.
  • 🦟 Plasmodium living in humans and mosquitoes during its life cycle.
  • 🌱 Parasitic plants obtaining resources from host plants.
  • 🐄 Internal parasites living in livestock.

🧬 Adaptations of Parasites

  • Strong attachment structures.
  • High reproductive capacity.
  • Protective body coverings.
  • Reduced structures that are unnecessary in the host environment.
  • Complex life cycles in some species.
  • Ability to survive host immune responses.

10. 🏠 Host–Parasite Relationships

The host provides the parasite with resources and a suitable environment.

Term Meaning Example
Parasite Organism obtaining resources from a host. Tapeworm.
Host Organism that supports the parasite. Human.
Endoparasite Parasite living inside the host. Tapeworm.
Ectoparasite Parasite living on the outside of the host. Tick or louse.

⚠️ Effects of Parasites on Hosts

  • Reduced nutrient availability.
  • Tissue damage.
  • Reduced growth.
  • Reduced reproductive success.
  • Weakness or illness.
  • Increased susceptibility to other stresses.

11. 🤝 Mutualism

📖 Definition

Mutualism is a relationship in which both interacting organisms gain a benefit.

🌿 Examples

  • 🐝 Bees obtaining nectar while pollinating flowering plants.
  • 🌱 Legumes and nitrogen-fixing bacteria.
  • 🍄 Lichen association between a fungus and a photosynthetic partner.
  • 🪸 Coral and photosynthetic microorganisms in coral tissues.

🌱 Nitrogen-Fixing Bacteria and Legumes

Certain bacteria associated with legume roots convert atmospheric nitrogen into nitrogen-containing compounds that can contribute to plant nitrogen nutrition.

The bacteria receive carbohydrates and a suitable habitat from the plant.

12. 🏠 Commensalism

Commensalism is an interaction in which one organism benefits while the other is not significantly affected.

Examples

  • 🐦 Some birds may use trees for nesting without significantly affecting the tree.
  • 🐟 Some organisms may use another organism or its structure as shelter without substantially affecting it.

⚠️ Exam Note

It can be difficult to prove that an organism experiences exactly zero effect. Therefore, commensalism is often described as a relationship where one benefits while the other is not significantly affected.

13. 🧬 Symbiosis

📖 Meaning

Symbiosis generally refers to a close and prolonged biological association between organisms of different species.

In many school contexts, symbiosis is used broadly to include mutualism, commensalism and parasitism.

Relationship Effect on First Organism Effect on Second Organism
Mutualism + +
Commensalism + 0
Parasitism + −

🧠 Important Distinction

Symbiosis is not automatically the same as mutualism. Symbiotic associations can have different effects on the organisms involved.

14. ⚔️ Antagonism

📖 Definition

Antagonism refers broadly to interactions in which one organism inhibits, suppresses or harms another organism.

🦠 Examples

Some microorganisms produce chemicals that inhibit the growth of other microorganisms.

This is one basis of antibiosis.

🍄 Antibiotic Production

Certain fungi and bacteria produce substances that can inhibit the growth of other microorganisms.

⭐ A/L Connection

Antagonistic interactions can influence which microorganisms are able to survive in a particular habitat.

15. 🏡 Ecological Niche

📖 Definition

An ecological niche describes the role and position of a species within its ecosystem, including how it uses resources, interacts with other organisms and responds to environmental conditions.

🌍 A Niche Includes

  • 🍽️ Food resources used.
  • 🏠 Habitat occupied.
  • ⏰ Time of activity.
  • 🌡️ Environmental conditions tolerated.
  • 🐾 Predators and prey.
  • 🤝 Other species with which it interacts.
  • 🌱 Its role in energy and nutrient flow.
  • 🧬 Its reproductive requirements.

🏠 Habitat vs Niche

Term Meaning Easy Way to Remember
Habitat The physical place where an organism lives. "Where it lives."
Niche The organism's role and resource use within the ecosystem. "How it lives."

16. 🎯 Fundamental and Realized Niches

🌐 Fundamental Niche

The fundamental niche is the range of conditions and resources under which a species could potentially survive and reproduce in the absence of limiting biological interactions such as competition and predation.

🎯 Realized Niche

The realized niche is the portion of the potential niche actually occupied by a species when interactions such as competition, predation and parasitism are taken into account.

🧠 Simple Relationship

Fundamental niche → potential range

Realized niche → actual range

17. 🌿 Niche Differentiation / Resource Partitioning

Species that compete for similar resources may reduce competition by using resources differently.

Examples

  • 🍃 Feeding on different parts of the same plant.
  • ⏰ Feeding at different times of the day.
  • 🌳 Living at different heights in vegetation.
  • 💧 Using different parts of an aquatic habitat.
  • 🍽️ Feeding on different food resources.

⭐ Result

Resource partitioning can reduce direct competition and allow different species to coexist.

18. 🤝 Coexistence

Coexistence occurs when different species are able to survive in the same ecosystem over time.

🌿 How Species Can Coexist

  • Different species may use different resources.
  • They may occupy different microhabitats.
  • They may feed at different times.
  • They may have different predators.
  • They may have different environmental tolerances.
  • Competition may be reduced through niche differentiation.

19. 📊 Ecological Importance of Predation

Predation does more than provide food for predators. It can influence the structure of entire communities.

  • 🦁 Controls prey abundance.
  • 🌱 Can indirectly influence plant abundance through herbivores.
  • 🧬 Can favour prey with effective defensive adaptations.
  • 🐾 Can influence community composition.
  • ⚖️ Helps maintain population balance.

🔗 Example of a Trophic Effect

If a predator reduces the abundance of a herbivore, pressure on plants may decrease, potentially allowing plant abundance to increase.

20. 🦅 Predator Adaptations

Predators possess adaptations that increase their ability to locate, capture and consume prey.

Adaptation Advantage
Sharp claws Capture and hold prey.
Sharp teeth Seize and process food.
Good eyesight Detect prey.
Speed Catch moving prey.
Camouflage Approach prey without being detected.
Sensitive hearing or smell Locate prey.

21. 🛡️ Prey Defences

Structural Defences

  • 🐢 Shells.
  • 🦔 Spines.
  • 🌵 Thorns.
  • 🛡️ Protective body coverings.

Behavioural Defences

  • 🏃 Running away.
  • 🫥 Hiding.
  • 🌙 Being active at safer times.
  • 👥 Living in groups.

🎭 Camouflage

Camouflage makes an organism less conspicuous in its environment.

⚠️ Warning Colouration

Some organisms have conspicuous colours that signal chemical defence, unpleasant taste or other forms of protection.

22. 🧬 Host–Parasite Coevolution

Hosts and parasites can exert strong selection pressures on each other.

A parasite that becomes better adapted to exploiting its host may increase its success, while hosts with traits that reduce infection may have a survival or reproductive advantage.

Over generations, this can produce continuing evolutionary changes in both organisms.

🔄 Examples of Adaptation

  • Parasites evolving better attachment mechanisms.
  • Hosts evolving stronger defensive responses.
  • Parasites evolving ways to avoid host defences.
  • Hosts evolving behaviours that reduce exposure.

23. 🌱 Important Mutualistic Associations

Association Organism A Gains Organism B Gains

26. 📈 Effects on Population Size

Interaction Possible Effect on Population
Competition Can reduce growth, survival or reproduction.
Predation Can reduce prey population.
Herbivory Can reduce plant biomass or reproductive success.
Parasitism Can reduce host health, survival or reproduction.
Mutualism Can improve survival or reproduction of both partners.

27. 📊 Density-Dependent Interactions

Many ecological interactions become stronger or weaker depending on population density.

Competition, predation and parasitism are commonly density-dependent factors.

As population density increases, individuals may encounter each other more frequently, increasing competition, disease transmission or predation opportunities.

28. 🌱 Competition in Plants

Plants compete even though they do not move from place to place.

Resources

  • ☀️ Light
  • 💧 Water
  • 🧂 Mineral ions
  • 🌱 Space
  • 🌍 Rooting area

🌳 Effects of Competition on Plants

  • Reduced growth.
  • Reduced access to light.
  • Reduced mineral uptake.
  • Reduced reproductive success.
  • Death of weaker individuals under severe competition.

29. 🐾 Competition in Animals

Animals may compete for:

  • 🍖 Food.
  • 💧 Water.
  • 🏠 Shelter.
  • 🗺️ Territory.
  • ❤️ Mates.

🦁 Intraspecific Competition

Competition between members of the same species may be particularly intense because they usually require very similar resources.

🦌 Territorial Competition

Some animals defend areas containing resources needed for survival and reproduction.

31. 🌎 Interactions Within a Biological Community

A biological community contains populations of different species. These populations interact continuously.

The distribution and abundance of a species are influenced by both abiotic factors and biotic interactions.

🌡️ Abiotic Factors

  • Temperature
  • Light intensity
  • Water availability
  • pH
  • Mineral availability
  • Salinity

🐾 Biotic Factors

  • Competition
  • Predation
  • Herbivory
  • Parasitism
  • Disease
  • Mutualism

32. 🌿 Coexistence and Biodiversity

Species coexistence can contribute to high biodiversity when species occupy different niches and use resources in different ways.

Greater habitat complexity can provide more opportunities for different species to occupy different niches.

🌳 Example

In a forest, different organisms may use different levels of the vegetation, feed on different resources and be active at different times. This reduces direct competition and permits coexistence.

33. 🧬 Ecological Interactions and Evolution

Ecological interactions can act as selection pressures.

Examples

  • 🦊 Predation can favour prey with effective defensive adaptations.
  • 🐾 Competition can favour organisms that use resources efficiently.
  • 🦠 Parasitism can favour host resistance.
  • 🧬 Host resistance can create selection for parasite adaptations.
  • 🌱 Herbivory can favour plants with defensive characteristics.

⭐ A/L Connection

Ecological interactions are therefore important not only in determining population sizes but also in influencing evolutionary change.

34. ⚖️ Important Differences to Know

Concept 1 Concept 2 Difference
Predation Parasitism Predators generally capture and consume prey; parasites usually live on or in a host and obtain resources over a longer period.
Herbivory Predation Herbivory specifically involves feeding on plants or plant material.
Mutualism Commensalism Both benefit in mutualism; only one benefits significantly in commensalism.
Intraspecific Interspecific Intraspecific interactions occur within one species; interspecific interactions occur between different species.
Habitat Niche Habitat is where an organism lives; niche describes its role and resource use.
Competition Predation Competition involves competing for resources; predation involves one organism consuming another.

35. 📝 Exam-Style Question 1

Question: Define competition and distinguish between intraspecific and interspecific competition.

Answer:

Competition is an interaction in which organisms require the same limited resource. Intraspecific competition occurs between members of the same species, whereas interspecific competition occurs between members of different species.

36. 📝 Exam-Style Question 2

Question: Explain why predator numbers may increase after an increase in prey population.

Answer points:

  • More prey means greater food availability.
  • Predators have improved access to food.
  • Survival may increase.
  • Reproductive success may increase.
  • Predator numbers therefore increase after a time lag.

37. 📝 Exam-Style Question 3

Question: Explain how two species can coexist despite competing for similar resources.

Answer points:

  • They may use different parts of the available resources.
  • They may occupy different microhabitats.
  • They may feed at different times.
  • They may have different environmental requirements.
  • This reduces direct competition.
  • Their realized niches therefore differ.

38. 📝 Exam-Style Question 4

Question: Distinguish between mutualism, commensalism and parasitism.

Answer:

Relationship Effect
Mutualism Both organisms benefit.
Commensalism One benefits while the other is not significantly affected.
Parasitism Parasite benefits while the host is harmed.

39. 📝 Exam-Style Question 5

Question: Explain the difference between an organism's habitat and its ecological niche.

Answer:

The habitat is the physical place where an organism lives, whereas its ecological niche describes its role, resource use, interactions and position within the ecosystem.

40. 🧠 Application Question

A farmer notices that weeds growing around a crop become more abundant when the crop plants are small. As the crop grows, some weeds die.

Question: Explain the likely ecological relationship.

Answer:

  • The crop and weeds require similar resources.
  • They compete for light, water, mineral ions and space.
  • The relationship is mainly interspecific competition.
  • As crop plants grow, they may capture more light and other resources.
  • Some weeds receive insufficient resources and may die.

41. ❌ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying that all symbiosis is mutualism.
  • ❌ Confusing habitat with niche.
  • ❌ Calling herbivory the same as parasitism.
  • ❌ Forgetting that competition can occur within the same species.
  • ❌ Saying predators and prey increase at exactly the same time.
  • ❌ Assuming that commensalism means the second organism is always completely unaffected.
  • ❌ Confusing predator–prey relationships with host–parasite relationships.
  • ❌ Giving examples without explaining the benefit or harm to each organism.

46. 🌍 Final Takeaway

Ecosystems are networks of interacting organisms. Competition, predation, herbivory, parasitism, mutualism and commensalism influence the survival, distribution and abundance of populations.

At A/L level, it is important not only to know the definitions but also to understand how these interactions affect populations, ecological niches, coexistence, biodiversity and evolution.

🌱 Organisms interact → populations change → communities are shaped → ecosystems function.
