BIOLOGY • O/L 0510

🌸 Reproduction

Understand reproduction, reproductive systems, gametes, puberty, fertilisation, pregnancy, birth, contraception, reproductive health and reproduction in flowering plants.

1. 🌱 What is Reproduction?

Reproduction is the process by which living organisms produce new individuals of the same species.

Reproduction is essential for the survival of a species because it replaces organisms that die.

📌 Important Point

An individual organism does not need to reproduce in order to survive, but reproduction is necessary for the continuation of the species.

2. 🔄 Types of Reproduction

There are two main types of reproduction:

  • 🌱 Asexual reproduction.
  • 🧬 Sexual reproduction.

3. 🌱 Asexual Reproduction

Asexual reproduction is reproduction involving only one parent and producing offspring without the fusion of gametes.

The offspring are usually genetically very similar to the parent.

Examples

  • 🍞 Budding in yeast.
  • 🌿 Vegetative reproduction in plants.
  • 🦠 Binary fission in some microorganisms.

4. 🧬 Sexual Reproduction

Sexual reproduction involves the fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote.

The offspring receive genetic material from two parents and therefore show genetic variation.

5. ⚖️ Asexual vs Sexual Reproduction

Asexual Reproduction Sexual Reproduction
Usually involves one parent. Usually involves two parents.
No fusion of gametes. Involves fusion of gametes.
Produces genetically similar offspring. Produces genetically varied offspring.
Usually faster. Usually slower.
Useful when conditions are stable. Variation may help populations survive environmental changes.

6. 🧬 Gametes

A gamete is a reproductive cell involved in sexual reproduction.

In humans, the two types of gametes are:

  • ♂️ Sperm cell — male gamete.
  • ♀️ Egg cell — female gamete.

Gametes contain half the normal number of chromosomes found in body cells.

7. 🧬 Sperm and Egg Cells

Sperm Cell Egg Cell
Male gamete. Female gamete.
Small cell. Much larger cell.
Has a flagellum for movement. Does not have a flagellum.
Contains a nucleus with genetic material. Contains a nucleus with genetic material.
Produced in the testes. Produced in the ovaries.

9. ⚙️ Sperm Cell Adaptations

The sperm cell is specialized for reaching and fertilising the egg cell.

  • 🏊 Tail: helps the sperm swim.
  • ⚡ Mitochondria: release energy needed for movement.
  • 🧬 Nucleus: contains the male genetic material.
  • 🧪 Acrosome: contains enzymes involved in penetrating the egg cell.
  • 📏 Small size: allows it to move efficiently.

10. 👦 Puberty

Puberty is the period during which a child's body develops into an adult body capable of sexual reproduction.

Puberty is controlled by hormones.

11. 👦 Changes in Boys During Puberty

  • Testes begin producing sperm.
  • The reproductive organs mature.
  • The voice becomes deeper.
  • Facial and body hair develops.
  • Muscle development increases.
  • Growth in height occurs.

12. 👧 Changes in Girls During Puberty

  • Ovaries begin releasing mature eggs.
  • Menstrual cycles begin.
  • Breasts develop.
  • Body hair develops.
  • The reproductive organs mature.
  • Growth in height occurs.

13. 🧪 Reproductive Hormones

Hormones are chemical messengers produced by endocrine glands and carried in the blood to target organs.

Important reproductive hormones include:

  • Testosterone.
  • Oestrogen.
  • Progesterone.
  • FSH.
  • LH.

15. 🧬 Testes

The testes are the male reproductive organs.

The testes:

  • Produce sperm cells.
  • Produce the hormone testosterone.

16. 🌡️ Scrotum

The scrotum is the sac containing the testes.

It helps maintain the testes at a temperature slightly below normal body temperature, which is important for sperm production.

17. 🧬 Epididymis

The epididymis is a coiled tube associated with each testis where sperm mature and are stored temporarily.

18. 🛣️ Sperm Duct

The sperm duct, or vas deferens, transports sperm from the epididymis towards the urethra.

19. 🧪 Seminal Vesicles and Prostate Gland

The seminal vesicles and prostate gland contribute fluids to semen.

These fluids provide substances that help support sperm and allow them to move.

20. 🚻 Urethra

The urethra is a tube through which urine leaves the body and through which semen can leave the male reproductive system.

22. 🥚 Ovaries

The ovaries are the female reproductive organs.

The ovaries:

  • Produce and release egg cells.
  • Produce hormones including oestrogen and progesterone.

23. 🛣️ Oviducts

The oviducts, also called fallopian tubes, transport the egg from the ovary towards the uterus.

Fertilisation normally occurs in an oviduct.

24. 🏠 Uterus

The uterus is a muscular organ in which an embryo and fetus develop during pregnancy.

25. 🩸 Uterine Lining

The inner lining of the uterus is called the endometrium.

It becomes thicker during the menstrual cycle and is suitable for implantation of an embryo.

26. 🚪 Cervix

The cervix is the narrow lower part of the uterus that opens into the vagina.

27. 🌸 Vagina

The vagina is a muscular canal leading from the cervix to the outside of the body.

It receives sperm during sexual intercourse and also forms part of the birth canal.

28. 🔄 Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle is a repeating series of changes in the female reproductive system.

The average cycle is often described as about 28 days, although actual cycle lengths vary between individuals and from cycle to cycle.

The cycle prepares the body for possible pregnancy.

29. 🩸 Menstruation

Menstruation occurs when the thickened uterine lining is broken down and leaves the body through the vagina.

This occurs when pregnancy has not occurred and hormone levels fall.

30. 🥚 Ovulation

Ovulation is the release of a mature egg cell from an ovary.

In a typical 28-day cycle, ovulation occurs around the middle of the cycle, but the exact timing varies.

31. 🧪 Oestrogen

Oestrogen is an important female reproductive hormone.

Its functions include:

  • Stimulating development of female secondary sexual characteristics.
  • Helping the uterine lining develop.
  • Playing a role in controlling the menstrual cycle.

32. 🧪 Progesterone

Progesterone is produced mainly by the corpus luteum after ovulation and is important in maintaining the uterine lining.

During pregnancy, progesterone levels remain high to help maintain conditions suitable for the developing embryo.

33. 🧪 FSH

Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) helps stimulate the development of ovarian follicles and supports egg maturation.

34. 🧪 LH

Luteinising hormone (LH) is involved in triggering ovulation and the formation of the corpus luteum.

35. 🔄 Menstrual Cycle Summary

🩸 Menstruation: uterine lining breaks down.

↓

🥚 Follicle develops: an egg matures in the ovary.

↓

🥚 Ovulation: mature egg is released.

↓

🏠 Uterine lining remains thick: prepares for possible implantation.

↓

❌ No pregnancy: hormone levels fall and menstruation begins again.

36. 🧬 Fertilisation

Fertilisation is the fusion of the nucleus of a male gamete with the nucleus of a female gamete.

In humans, fertilisation normally occurs in the oviduct.

The fertilised egg is called a zygote.

37. 🧬 Formation of a Zygote

The sperm nucleus and egg nucleus fuse during fertilisation.

The zygote therefore contains genetic material from both parents.

🧬 Gamete + Gamete → Zygote

38. 🚶 Journey of the Zygote

After fertilisation, the zygote undergoes repeated cell division as it travels along the oviduct towards the uterus.

The developing group of cells eventually reaches the uterus.

39. 🏠 Implantation

Implantation is the attachment of the developing embryo to the lining of the uterus.

Successful implantation allows the embryo to develop in the uterus and establish a connection with the mother's blood supply through the placenta.

40. 👶 Embryo and Fetus

The developing human is called an embryo during the early stages of development.

Later in development, it is called a fetus.

41. 🩸 Placenta

The placenta is an organ that develops during pregnancy and forms a connection between the mother and developing fetus.

The placenta allows:

  • Oxygen to pass from the mother's blood to the fetus.
  • Glucose and other nutrients to reach the fetus.
  • Carbon dioxide to pass from the fetus to the mother.
  • Urea and other waste substances to pass to the mother.

42. 🧬 Umbilical Cord

The umbilical cord connects the fetus to the placenta.

It contains blood vessels that carry substances between the fetus and placenta.

43. 🛡️ Placenta as a Barrier

Maternal and fetal blood normally do not mix directly.

Substances pass between the two blood supplies across the placenta.

The placenta also provides some protection by preventing many harmful substances and pathogens from reaching the fetus, although it is not a complete barrier.

44. 🫁 Gas Exchange Between Mother and Fetus

Oxygen passes from the mother's blood across the placenta into the fetal blood.

Carbon dioxide passes from fetal blood into the mother's blood and is removed by the mother's lungs.

45. 🍎 Nutrient Supply to the Fetus

Nutrients such as glucose, amino acids, vitamins, mineral ions and water can pass from the mother's blood to the fetus through the placenta.

These substances are needed for growth and development.

46. 🗑️ Removal of Fetal Waste

Waste products produced by the fetus, such as carbon dioxide and urea, pass into the mother's blood through the placenta.

The mother's excretory organs then remove these wastes.

47. 🚫 Harmful Substances During Pregnancy

Some substances can cross the placenta and affect fetal development.

Pregnant people should therefore avoid harmful substances and follow appropriate medical advice.

⚠️ Examples

  • Alcohol can affect fetal development.
  • Some medicines can affect the fetus.
  • Smoking exposes the fetus to harmful substances.
  • Some infections can pass from mother to fetus.

48. 👶 Birth

Birth is the process by which the fetus leaves the uterus at the end of pregnancy.

Hormonal changes and contractions of the uterus are involved in labour.

49. 💪 Labour

During labour, the muscular wall of the uterus contracts repeatedly.

The cervix dilates, allowing the baby to pass through the birth canal.

50. 🍼 Breastfeeding

Breast milk provides nutrients and water to a newborn baby.

It also contains antibodies and other substances that help protect the baby against infections.

51. 🧪 Amniotic Fluid

The fetus develops inside a fluid-filled amniotic sac.

Amniotic fluid helps protect the fetus from physical shock and allows movement.

52. 🧬 Asexual Reproduction in Plants

Many plants can reproduce asexually using vegetative structures.

Examples include:

  • 🌱 Runners.
  • 🥔 Tubers.
  • 🧅 Bulbs.
  • 🌿 Cuttings.

53. 🌿 Vegetative Propagation

Vegetative propagation is a form of asexual reproduction in plants in which new plants develop from vegetative parts such as stems, roots or leaves.

📌 Advantages

  • Rapid production of new plants.
  • Only one parent is required.
  • Useful characteristics can be maintained.
  • Plants can be produced without seeds.

54. 🌸 Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

Sexual reproduction in flowering plants involves the production of male and female gametes and their fusion to form a zygote.

The flower contains the reproductive organs of the plant.

55. 🌼 Structure of a Flower

The main parts of a typical flower include:

  • Petals.
  • Sepals.
  • Stamens.
  • Carpels.
  • Anthers.
  • Filaments.
  • Stigma.
  • Style.
  • Ovary.

56. 🌾 Stamen

The stamen is the male reproductive structure of a flower.

It consists mainly of:

  • Anther: produces pollen grains.
  • Filament: supports the anther.

57. 🌸 Carpel

The carpel is the female reproductive structure of a flower.

It consists of:

  • Stigma: receives pollen.
  • Style: connects stigma to ovary.
  • Ovary: contains ovules.

58. 🌾 Pollen Grains

Pollen grains contain the male reproductive cells of a flowering plant.

They are transferred from the anther to the stigma during pollination.

59. 🔄 Pollination

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma of a flower of the same species.

60. 🌬️ Types of Pollination

There are two important types:

  • Self-pollination: pollen is transferred to the stigma of the same flower or another flower on the same plant.
  • Cross-pollination: pollen is transferred to a flower on a different plant of the same species.

61. 🐝 Insect-Pollinated Flowers

Flowers pollinated by insects have adaptations that help attract insects and transfer pollen.

  • Brightly coloured petals.
  • Strong scent.
  • Nectar.
  • Sticky pollen.
  • Anthers positioned so insects brush against them.
  • Sticky stigma.

62. 🌬️ Wind-Pollinated Flowers

Wind-pollinated flowers release pollen into the air.

  • Small or inconspicuous petals.
  • No need for strong scent or nectar.
  • Large amounts of light pollen.
  • Exposed anthers.
  • Large or feathery stigmas.

63. 🌾 Fertilisation in Flowering Plants

After pollen lands on a suitable stigma, a pollen tube grows down through the style towards an ovule.

The male nucleus travels down the pollen tube and fuses with the female nucleus in the ovule.

This forms a zygote.

64. 🌱 Seed Formation

After fertilisation, the fertilised ovule develops into a seed.

The ovary develops into a fruit.

🌸 Flowering plant changes after fertilisation:

  • Ovule → Seed.
  • Ovary → Fruit.
  • Zygote → Embryo.

65. 🌱 Seed Germination

Germination is the process by which a seed begins to grow into a new plant.

A seed requires suitable conditions before germination can occur.

66. 💧 Conditions Needed for Germination

  • 💧 Water.
  • 💨 Oxygen.
  • 🌡️ Suitable temperature.

📌 Light is not required for germination of all seeds. The essential conditions are water, oxygen and a suitable temperature.

67. 🌱 Seed Germination Process

  1. The seed absorbs water.
  2. The seed swells.
  3. The seed coat breaks.
  4. Enzymes become active.
  5. Stored food is broken down and used for respiration and growth.
  6. The root emerges first.
  7. The shoot grows upwards.

68. 🌱 Seed Dispersal

Seed dispersal moves seeds away from the parent plant.

This reduces competition between the parent plant and its offspring.

Seeds may be dispersed by:

  • 🌬️ Wind.
  • 💧 Water.
  • 🐦 Animals.
  • 💥 Explosive mechanisms.

69. 🧬 Variation in Sexual Reproduction

Sexual reproduction produces genetic variation because offspring receive genetic material from two parents.

Variation means individuals of the same species are not genetically identical.

70. 🌱 Advantages of Sexual Reproduction

  • Produces genetic variation.
  • Variation may help populations adapt to changing environments.
  • Some offspring may have characteristics that increase survival.

71. 🌱 Advantages of Asexual Reproduction

  • Only one parent is needed.
  • It can occur rapidly.
  • Large numbers of offspring can be produced.
  • Successful characteristics are retained.

72. ⚠️ Disadvantages of Asexual Reproduction

  • Produces little genetic variation.
  • A disease or environmental change may affect many offspring similarly.

73. ⚠️ Disadvantages of Sexual Reproduction

  • Usually requires two parents.
  • Finding a mate can take time and energy.
  • Reproduction is generally slower than asexual reproduction.

74. 🦠 Sexually Transmitted Infections

Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are infections that can be transmitted through sexual contact.

They may be caused by bacteria, viruses or other pathogens.

Examples include:

  • HIV infection.
  • Gonorrhoea.
  • Syphilis.
  • Chlamydia.
  • Genital herpes.

75. 🛡️ Preventing STIs

  • Use appropriate barrier protection.
  • Testing helps identify infections.
  • Seek medical treatment for suspected infections.
  • Avoid sharing needles or equipment that can transfer blood.
  • Follow public-health advice about vaccination where available.

76. 🧫 Bacterial vs Viral STIs

Bacterial STI Viral STI
Caused by bacteria. Caused by viruses.
Some can be treated with appropriate antibiotics. Antibiotics do not treat viral infections.
Examples include gonorrhoea and syphilis. Examples include HIV and genital herpes.

77. 💊 Treatment of Reproductive Infections

Treatment depends on the cause of the infection.

Bacterial infections may be treated with appropriate antibiotics, while viral infections are managed using treatments appropriate for the particular virus.

Medical advice should be sought when an STI is suspected.

78. 🛡️ Contraception

Contraception refers to methods used to prevent pregnancy.

Different contraceptive methods work in different ways.

79. 💊 Methods of Contraception

Method Basic Principle
Condom Forms a barrier that prevents sperm from entering the female reproductive tract.
Oral contraceptive pill Uses hormones to prevent ovulation and/or alter conditions needed for fertilisation.
Hormonal implant Releases hormones that mainly prevent ovulation and alter cervical conditions.
Hormonal injection Releases hormones that mainly prevent ovulation.
Intrauterine device (IUD) Placed inside the uterus and prevents pregnancy by mechanisms depending on the type.
Abstinence Not having sexual intercourse prevents pregnancy from intercourse.

80. 🛡️ Condoms and Disease Prevention

Condoms can help reduce the risk of pregnancy and also reduce the risk of transmission of many sexually transmitted infections when used correctly.

📌 Unlike most contraceptive methods, condoms can provide protection against both pregnancy and many STIs.

81. 🧬 Infertility

Infertility is difficulty in achieving pregnancy despite regular unprotected sexual intercourse over a period of time.

Infertility can have many possible causes affecting either partner.

Medical professionals can investigate possible causes and provide appropriate advice and treatment.

82. 🔬 Artificial Reproductive Technologies

Medical techniques can sometimes help people experiencing infertility.

One example is in vitro fertilisation (IVF), in which fertilisation occurs outside the body and an embryo may later be transferred to the uterus.

83. 🧬 Reproductive Health

Reproductive health involves maintaining the physical, mental and social well-being of the reproductive system.

Important aspects include:

  • Good personal hygiene.
  • Prevention and treatment of reproductive infections.
  • Access to accurate reproductive-health information.
  • Appropriate medical care.
  • Healthy lifestyle choices.

86. 📖 Important Definitions

Reproduction: The process by which organisms produce new individuals of the same species.

Asexual reproduction: Reproduction involving one parent and no fusion of gametes.

Sexual reproduction: Reproduction involving the fusion of male and female gametes.

Gamete: A reproductive cell involved in sexual reproduction.

Fertilisation: Fusion of the nuclei of male and female gametes.

Zygote: The cell formed after fertilisation.

Ovulation: Release of a mature egg from an ovary.

Menstruation: Breakdown and loss of the uterine lining.

Implantation: Attachment of the developing embryo to the uterine lining.

Embryo: The developing human during the early stages of development.

Fetus: The developing human after the early embryonic stage.

Placenta: Organ through which substances are exchanged between mother and fetus.

Puberty: Period during which the body develops reproductive maturity.

Pollination: Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma.

Vegetative propagation: Asexual reproduction in plants using vegetative structures.

Contraception: Methods used to prevent pregnancy.

STI: An infection that can be transmitted through sexual contact.

88. 📝 O/L Exam Question Strategy

🧠 When asked to explain fertilisation:

  1. Sperm reaches the egg.
  2. The male and female nuclei fuse.
  3. A zygote is formed.
  4. The zygote undergoes repeated cell division.

🧠 When asked about the placenta:

Always mention exchange of substances.

  • Oxygen → fetus.
  • Glucose/nutrients → fetus.
  • Carbon dioxide → mother.
  • Urea → mother.

🧠 When asked about pollination:

Remember: anther → stigma.

89. ❌ Common O/L Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying pollination and fertilisation are the same process.
  • ❌ Saying the ovary produces sperm.
  • ❌ Saying fertilisation normally occurs in the uterus.
  • ❌ Confusing the oviduct with the uterus.
  • ❌ Saying the placenta directly mixes the mother's blood with the fetus's blood.
  • ❌ Saying the umbilical cord is the same thing as the placenta.
  • ❌ Forgetting that the testes produce testosterone as well as sperm.
  • ❌ Saying menstruation is the same as ovulation.
  • ❌ Saying all flowers are insect-pollinated.
  • ❌ Forgetting that wind-pollinated flowers usually produce large quantities of light pollen.
  • ❌ Saying antibiotics cure viral STIs.
  • ❌ Confusing sexual reproduction with asexual reproduction.
