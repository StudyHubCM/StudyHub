🔥 Fuels & Energy

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Learn about fuels, combustion, energy changes, fossil fuels, renewable energy sources, fuel efficiency and environmental effects.

1. 🔥 Introduction to Fuels

A fuel is a substance that burns in oxygen and releases useful energy, mainly in the form of heat.

Fuels are important because energy released from combustion can be used for cooking, heating, transportation, electricity generation and industrial processes.

Examples of fuels

  • Wood
  • Charcoal
  • Coal
  • Petrol
  • Kerosene
  • Diesel
  • Natural gas
  • Biogas
  • Ethanol
  • Hydrogen

📌 Key Idea

Most common fuels release energy through combustion. In combustion, the fuel reacts with oxygen.

2. 🔥 Combustion

Combustion is a chemical reaction in which a substance reacts with oxygen and releases energy, usually as heat and often light.

Complete combustion

Complete combustion occurs when a fuel burns in a sufficient supply of oxygen.

For a hydrocarbon, the main products of complete combustion are carbon dioxide and water.

Word equation:

Methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation:

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

Complete combustion of a general hydrocarbon

Hydrocarbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Incomplete combustion

Incomplete combustion occurs when the supply of oxygen is insufficient.

It can produce carbon monoxide and/or carbon.

Word equation:

Methane + oxygen → carbon monoxide + water

Symbol equation:

2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O

Formation of carbon

Very limited oxygen can cause the formation of carbon particles, commonly called soot.

Word equation:

Methane + oxygen → carbon + water

Symbol equation:

CH₄ + O₂ → C + 2H₂O

⚠️ Safety

Carbon monoxide is poisonous. In poorly ventilated areas, incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels can produce dangerous concentrations of carbon monoxide.

3. ⚡ Energy Changes in Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions involve changes in energy.

During combustion, chemical energy stored in the fuel is converted mainly into thermal energy and sometimes light energy.

Exothermic reactions

An exothermic reaction is a reaction that releases energy to the surroundings.

Combustion is an important example of an exothermic reaction.

Example:

Methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy

Observing an exothermic reaction

The temperature of the surroundings or reaction mixture may increase because energy is released.

Endothermic reactions

An endothermic reaction is a reaction that absorbs energy from the surroundings.

Endothermic processes require energy to be supplied.

Reaction type Energy change Example
Exothermic Releases energy Combustion
Endothermic Absorbs energy Some decomposition reactions

4. ⭐ Properties of a Good Fuel

A useful fuel should have properties that make it efficient, economical and safe to use.

Important properties

  • It should release a large amount of energy when burned.
  • It should burn at a suitable rate.
  • It should be readily available.
  • It should be reasonably cheap.
  • It should be easy to transport and store.
  • It should be easy to ignite when required.
  • It should produce little harmful pollution.
  • It should produce little smoke or soot.
  • It should be safe to handle.

Why high energy output is important

A fuel that releases more useful energy for a given mass can be more efficient because less fuel may be needed to perform the same task.

5. 🔥 Calorific Value

The calorific value of a fuel is the amount of heat energy released when a given mass of the fuel undergoes complete combustion.

It is commonly expressed in:

kJ/g or MJ/kg.

Comparing fuels

A fuel with a higher calorific value releases more energy per unit mass when completely burned.

Fuel General energy consideration
Wood Useful solid fuel but varies in composition and moisture.
Charcoal High carbon content and useful for cooking.
Petrol High-energy liquid fuel used mainly in spark-ignition engines.
Diesel High-energy liquid fuel widely used in diesel engines.
Natural gas Useful gaseous fuel with relatively clean combustion when properly burned.

6. 🪨 Fossil Fuels

Fossil fuels are fuels formed from the remains of organisms over very long periods of geological time.

The major fossil fuels are:

  • Coal
  • Petroleum
  • Natural gas

Coal

Coal is a solid fossil fuel containing a high proportion of carbon. It is used for energy production and in some industrial processes.

Petroleum

Petroleum, also called crude oil, is a mixture of many hydrocarbons.

It is separated into useful fractions by fractional distillation.

Natural gas

Natural gas is a gaseous fossil fuel consisting mainly of methane, although its exact composition varies.

Word equation for methane combustion:

Methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation:

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

Fossil fuel State Main use
Coal Solid Energy generation and industry
Petroleum Liquid Transport fuels and chemical manufacture
Natural gas Gas Cooking, heating and electricity generation

⚠️ Important

Fossil fuels are non-renewable because they take extremely long periods of time to form and cannot be replaced quickly on a human timescale.

7. 🛢️ Petroleum and Its Products

Crude petroleum is a complex mixture of hydrocarbons with different chain lengths and boiling points.

The components are separated by fractional distillation according to differences in boiling point.

Important petroleum fractions

Fraction Typical use
Refinery gases Cooking and heating
Petrol Fuel for many spark-ignition vehicles
Kerosene Jet fuel and heating
Diesel oil Fuel for diesel engines
Fuel oil Ships, industrial heating and power generation
Bitumen Road surfacing and roofing

8. 🌱 Renewable Energy Sources

Renewable energy sources are energy sources that are naturally replenished on a timescale useful to humans.

Solar energy

Solar energy comes from the Sun and can be converted into electrical energy using solar cells or used directly as heat.

Wind energy

Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy of moving air into electrical energy.

Hydroelectric energy

Moving or falling water can turn turbines connected to generators.

Biomass

Biomass consists of biological material that can be used as a fuel or converted into other useful forms of energy.

Biogas

Biogas is produced when microorganisms break down organic matter in the absence of oxygen.

It commonly contains a large proportion of methane and can be burned as a fuel.

Geothermal energy

Geothermal energy comes from heat within the Earth.

Comparison of energy sources

Biomass Generally renewable when sustainably managed Biological material

Natural gas No Stored chemical energy

9. 🌾 Biofuels

Biofuels are fuels produced from biological material.

Ethanol as a biofuel

Ethanol can be produced by fermentation of sugars using microorganisms such as yeast.

Word equation:

Glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation:

C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂

Fermentation occurs under suitable conditions, including an appropriate temperature and limited oxygen availability.

Advantages of biofuels

  • They can be produced from renewable biological materials.
  • They can reduce dependence on fossil fuels.
  • Some can be produced from agricultural products or wastes.

Limitations of biofuels

  • Large-scale production can require land and water.
  • Growing fuel crops can compete with food production.
  • The overall environmental benefit depends on how the fuel is produced.

10. 💧 Hydrogen as a Fuel

Hydrogen can react with oxygen and release energy.

Word equation:

Hydrogen + oxygen → water

Symbol equation:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

The reaction releases energy.

Advantages of hydrogen

  • Its combustion produces water as the main chemical product.
  • It can release a large amount of energy per unit mass.
  • It can be used in fuel cells to generate electrical energy.

Limitations of hydrogen

  • Hydrogen must be produced from another source of energy or material.
  • Storage and transportation can be challenging.
  • The environmental impact depends on how the hydrogen is produced.

11. 🌍 Environmental Effects of Burning Fuels

Burning fuels can release substances that affect air quality and the environment.

Carbon dioxide

Complete combustion of carbon-containing fuels produces carbon dioxide.

Increased atmospheric carbon dioxide contributes to the enhanced greenhouse effect and climate change.

Example:

Carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide

C + O₂ → CO₂

Carbon monoxide

Incomplete combustion can produce carbon monoxide.

Carbon monoxide is poisonous and can be particularly dangerous in enclosed or poorly ventilated spaces.

Sulfur dioxide

Fuels containing sulfur can produce sulfur dioxide during combustion.

Word equation:

Sulfur + oxygen → sulfur dioxide

Symbol equation:

S + O₂ → SO₂

Sulfur dioxide can contribute to acid deposition and respiratory problems.

Nitrogen oxides

At high temperatures, nitrogen and oxygen from air can react to form nitrogen oxides.

Nitrogen oxides contribute to air pollution and can contribute to acid deposition and photochemical pollution.

Particulates and soot

Incomplete combustion can produce solid carbon particles and other fine particles.

These particles can reduce air quality and cause respiratory problems.

Pollutant Main source Environmental/health effect
CO₂ Combustion of carbon-containing fuels Enhanced greenhouse effect
CO Incomplete combustion Poisonous
SO₂ Burning sulfur-containing fuels Acid deposition and respiratory effects
NOₓ High-temperature combustion Air pollution and acid deposition
Particulates Incomplete combustion and other processes Respiratory and air-quality problems

12. 🌱 Reducing Pollution from Fuels

Pollution from fuel use can be reduced through improved technology, cleaner fuels and more efficient energy use.

Complete combustion

Supplying enough oxygen helps reduce the formation of carbon monoxide and soot from carbon-containing fuels.

Cleaner fuels

Fuels containing less sulfur generally produce less sulfur dioxide during combustion.

Catalytic converters

Catalytic converters are used in many petrol-powered vehicles to reduce certain harmful gases in exhaust emissions.

They help convert carbon monoxide and nitrogen oxides into less harmful substances, while also helping oxidise unburned hydrocarbons.

Energy efficiency

Using energy efficiently reduces the amount of fuel needed to perform a particular task.

Renewable energy

Increasing the use of renewable energy can reduce dependence on fossil fuels.

13. 💡 Fuel Conservation and Efficient Energy Use

Fuel conservation means using fuels carefully and reducing unnecessary fuel consumption.

Ways of conserving fuel

  • Use fuel-efficient appliances.
  • Maintain engines properly.
  • Avoid unnecessary idling of vehicles.
  • Use public transport where practical.
  • Reduce unnecessary electricity consumption.
  • Use renewable energy where appropriate.
  • Improve insulation in buildings.
  • Use efficient cooking methods.

Why conservation matters

  • It reduces fuel costs.
  • It conserves non-renewable resources.
  • It reduces pollution.
  • It can reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

🧠 Important Definitions

  • Fuel: A substance that releases useful energy when it undergoes combustion.
  • Combustion: A chemical reaction in which a substance reacts with oxygen and releases energy.
  • Complete combustion: Combustion occurring with a sufficient supply of oxygen.
  • Incomplete combustion: Combustion occurring when the supply of oxygen is insufficient.
  • Exothermic reaction: A reaction that releases energy to the surroundings.
  • Endothermic reaction: A reaction that absorbs energy from the surroundings.
  • Calorific value: The heat energy released by complete combustion of a given mass of fuel.
  • Fossil fuel: A fuel formed from ancient biological material over geological periods of time.
  • Renewable energy source: An energy source that is naturally replenished on a useful human timescale.
  • Non-renewable energy source: An energy source that is not replaced quickly enough for continued use on a human timescale.
  • Biofuel: A fuel produced from biological material.
  • Biogas: A fuel gas produced by microbial decomposition of organic matter, commonly containing a high proportion of methane.
  • Petroleum: A naturally occurring mixture of hydrocarbons found underground.
  • Fractional distillation: A method used to separate a mixture of liquids based mainly on differences in boiling points.
  • Pollutant: A substance released into the environment that can cause harmful effects.
  • Carbon monoxide: A poisonous gas produced during incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels.
  • Greenhouse effect: The warming effect caused when certain atmospheric gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation.
  • Fuel conservation: The careful and efficient use of fuels to reduce unnecessary consumption.

17. 🧪 Practical and Exam Skills

Heating water using a fuel

A simple experiment can compare fuels by measuring the temperature rise of a known mass of water when a known mass of fuel is burned.

Basic method

  1. Measure a known mass or volume of water.
  2. Measure the starting temperature of the water.
  3. Burn a known mass of fuel below the container.
  4. Heat the water for a controlled period.
  5. Measure the final temperature.
  6. Determine the temperature change.
  7. Compare the energy released by different fuels.

Variables

  • Independent variable: the fuel being tested.
  • Dependent variable: the temperature rise or energy transferred to the water.
  • Control variables: mass of water, apparatus, heating distance and other relevant conditions.

Sources of heat loss

  • Heat escaping to the surroundings.
  • Heat absorbed by the container.
  • Incomplete combustion.
  • Heat carried away by hot gases.

19. ⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Confusing complete and incomplete combustion: Complete combustion requires sufficient oxygen.
  • Forgetting carbon monoxide: Incomplete combustion can produce poisonous carbon monoxide.
  • Calling fossil fuels renewable: Coal, petroleum and natural gas are non-renewable.
  • Confusing fuel with energy source: A fuel is a substance that can release useful energy, while an energy source is a broader term.
  • Forgetting oxygen in combustion equations: Combustion involves reaction with oxygen.
  • Not balancing combustion equations: The number of atoms of each element must be equal on both sides.
  • Assuming renewable means pollution-free: Some renewable technologies or fuels can still have environmental impacts.
  • Confusing calorific value with total energy: Calorific value refers to energy released per specified amount of fuel.
  • Ignoring heat loss in experiments: Not all energy released by a fuel reaches the water or useful system.
