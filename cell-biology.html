🧫 8. Cell Ultrastructure

Cell ultrastructure refers to the detailed internal structure of cells as revealed by high-resolution microscopy such as electron microscopy.

🔬 Important Cell Structures

  • Nucleus: Contains DNA and controls cell activities.
  • Nucleolus: Region inside the nucleus where components of ribosomes are produced.
  • Ribosomes: Sites of protein synthesis.
  • Rough endoplasmic reticulum: Membrane system associated with ribosomes and involved in the synthesis and transport of proteins.
  • Smooth endoplasmic reticulum: Involved in lipid synthesis and other metabolic functions.
  • Golgi apparatus: Modifies, sorts and packages proteins and other substances for transport.
  • Mitochondrion: Site of aerobic respiration and ATP production.
  • Lysosome: Contains hydrolytic enzymes used to digest materials inside the cell.
  • Chloroplast: Site of photosynthesis in photosynthetic eukaryotic cells.
  • Vacuole: Contains cell sap and contributes to turgor in plant cells.

🧱 9. Cell Membranes

The cell membrane controls the movement of substances between the cell and its surroundings. It is selectively permeable, meaning that different substances cross it at different rates or by different mechanisms.

🧬 Fluid Mosaic Model

The cell surface membrane consists mainly of a phospholipid bilayer with proteins embedded within it.

  • Phospholipids form the basic bilayer.
  • Proteins may act as channels, carriers or receptors.
  • Some membrane proteins are involved in cell recognition.
  • The membrane is flexible and its components can move within the membrane.

💧 10. Movement of Substances Across Membranes

➡️ Diffusion

Diffusion: The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, down a concentration gradient, as a result of random movement.

Diffusion does not require energy from ATP.

💧 Osmosis

Osmosis: The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane.

⚡ Active Transport

Active transport: The movement of substances against their concentration gradient using energy released by respiration and specific membrane transport proteins.

  • Requires energy.
  • Can move substances from low concentration to high concentration.
  • Uses carrier proteins in the membrane.

📥 Endocytosis

Endocytosis is the process by which a cell takes substances into the cell by forming a vesicle from the cell membrane.

  • Phagocytosis: Engulfment of relatively large solid particles.
  • Pinocytosis: Uptake of extracellular fluid and dissolved substances.

📤 Exocytosis

Exocytosis is the movement of substances out of a cell when vesicles fuse with the cell surface membrane and release their contents outside the cell.

📈 11. Factors Affecting Diffusion

  • Concentration gradient: A greater difference in concentration generally increases the rate of diffusion.
  • Temperature: Higher temperature increases particle kinetic energy.
  • Surface area: A larger surface area allows more particles to cross at the same time.
  • Diffusion distance: A shorter distance allows faster diffusion.
  • Size of particles: Smaller particles generally diffuse more easily.

🔋 12. ATP and Cellular Energy

ATP is the main immediate energy-transfer molecule used by cells.

ATP: A molecule that transfers energy for cellular processes.

  • Active transport
  • Muscle contraction
  • Protein synthesis
  • Cell division
  • Movement of substances using vesicles

ATP is continuously produced and used by cells rather than being stored in large quantities.

🔄 13. Cell Cycle

The cell cycle is the sequence of events through which a cell grows, prepares for division and divides.

Major stages

  • Interphase: The cell grows, carries out normal activities, replicates its DNA and prepares for division.
  • Mitosis: The nucleus divides.
  • Cytokinesis: The cytoplasm divides to form separate daughter cells.

🧬 14. Mitosis

Mitosis produces genetically identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number as the parent cell.

Importance of mitosis

  • Growth
  • Replacement of damaged or worn-out cells
  • Repair of tissues
  • Asexual reproduction in some organisms

🔬 Main Stages of Mitosis

  • Prophase: Chromosomes become visible and the nuclear envelope breaks down.
  • Metaphase: Chromosomes arrange at the equator of the cell.
  • Anaphase: Sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.
  • Telophase: New nuclear envelopes form around the chromosomes.
  • Cytokinesis: The cytoplasm divides.

🧬 15. Meiosis

Meiosis is a type of cell division that produces genetically different haploid cells.

Why meiosis is important

  • Produces gametes.
  • Reduces the chromosome number by half.
  • Creates genetic variation.

📊 Mitosis vs Meiosis

Feature Mitosis Meiosis
Number of divisions One Two
Daughter cells Usually 2 Usually 4
Chromosome number Maintained Halved
Genetic similarity Usually genetically identical Genetically different
Main role Growth and repair Production of gametes

🔬 16. Light Microscope vs Electron Microscope

Feature Light Microscope Electron Microscope
Radiation used Visible light Beam of electrons
Resolution Lower Much higher
Magnification Lower Much higher
Living specimens Can be observed Generally cannot be observed alive

📐 17. Resolution

Resolution: The ability to distinguish two objects that are very close together as separate objects.

Important: Magnification makes an image larger, while resolution determines how much detail can be distinguished.

🎯 18. GCE Cell Biology Skills

For examination questions, do not only memorize names. You should be able to define, identify, label, compare, explain, calculate and interpret.

  • Define diffusion and osmosis.
  • Explain active transport.
  • Compare diffusion, osmosis and active transport.
  • Explain phagocytosis and pinocytosis.
  • Identify cell organelles from diagrams.
  • State functions of organelles.
  • Explain adaptations of specialized cells.
  • Interpret microscope images.
  • Calculate magnification and actual size.
  • Distinguish magnification from resolution.
  • Identify stages of mitosis.
  • Compare mitosis and meiosis.
  • Interpret experimental results involving movement of substances.

🧪 19. Practical Cell Biology

Experiments you should understand

  • Osmosis investigations using plant tissue.
  • Diffusion investigations.
  • Microscope investigations.
  • Preparation of temporary slides.
  • Biological drawings.
  • Measurement and magnification calculations.
  • Identification of experimental variables.
  • Recording observations in tables.
  • Drawing and interpreting graphs.

📝 20. Common GCE Question Patterns

  • Define: Give the precise biological meaning.
  • State: Give the required fact without unnecessary explanation.
  • Describe: Give what happens or what is observed.
  • Explain: Give the reason or biological mechanism.
  • Compare: Give similarities and/or differences.
  • Calculate: Show the formula, substitution, working and unit where appropriate.
  • Label: Identify the correct structures on a diagram.
