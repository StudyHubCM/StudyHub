🔬 Cells & Microscopy

Cell theory, cell structure, organelles, specialized cells, microscopy, magnification and biological drawings.

🧬 1. Cell Theory

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made up of cells.

🧠 The Three Main Principles of Cell Theory

  1. All living organisms are made up of one or more cells.
  2. The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
  3. All new cells arise from pre-existing cells.

🦠 Unicellular Organisms

A unicellular organism consists of only one cell. The single cell carries out all the functions necessary for life.

Examples include bacteria and organisms such as Amoeba and Paramecium.

👥 Multicellular Organisms

A multicellular organism consists of many cells. The cells may become specialized and perform different functions.

Examples include humans, maize plants and other plants and animals.

🔬 2. Cell Structure

Although cells differ in size and shape, many cells contain structures that perform important functions.

Plant and animal cells are both eukaryotic cells. They contain a nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane and contain membrane-bound organelles.

🐾 Animal Cell

Animal cells contain many structures found in plant cells, but they do not have a cellulose cell wall or chloroplasts.

⚙️ 3. Cell Organelles and Their Functions

An organelle is a specialized structure within a cell that carries out a particular function.

🧠 Important Organelles

  • Nucleus: Contains genetic material and controls the activities of the cell.
  • Cell membrane: Controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
  • Cytoplasm: Contains organelles and is the site of many chemical reactions.
  • Mitochondria: Site of aerobic respiration and release of energy for cellular activities.
  • Ribosomes: Sites of protein synthesis.
  • Chloroplasts: Contain chlorophyll and are the main site of photosynthesis in plant cells.
  • Cell wall: Provides support and helps maintain the shape of plant cells.
  • Large permanent vacuole: Contains cell sap and helps maintain pressure inside plant cells.

🧬 Nucleus

The nucleus contains chromosomes made of DNA. It controls many activities of the cell by regulating gene expression.

⚡ Mitochondria

Mitochondria are the main sites of aerobic respiration. Cells that require large amounts of energy often contain many mitochondria.

For example, muscle cells contain many mitochondria because muscle contraction requires energy.

🌿 Chloroplasts

Chloroplasts contain the green pigment chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs light energy needed for photosynthesis.

🧱 Cell Wall

The plant cell wall is mainly made of cellulose. It strengthens the cell and helps prevent the cell from bursting when water enters.

💧 Vacuole

The large permanent vacuole in a typical plant cell contains cell sap. It helps maintain turgor and therefore contributes to the firmness of plant tissues.

🧠 Important Exam Point

Plant and animal cells are both eukaryotic. Therefore, they share several structures, but plant cells have additional structures that are important for photosynthesis and support.

🧩 5. Specialized Cells

In multicellular organisms, cells become specialized to perform particular functions.

🎯 Structure → Adaptation → Function

When answering questions about specialized cells, always connect the special feature of the cell to the function it performs.

🌱 Root Hair Cell

Root hair cells absorb water and mineral ions from the soil.

🔬 Adaptations

  • Long root hair: Provides a large surface area for absorption.
  • Thin cell wall: Provides a short diffusion distance for water.
  • Large vacuole: Helps maintain conditions that favour water uptake.
  • Many mitochondria: Provide energy for active transport of mineral ions.

🌿 Palisade Cell

Palisade cells are found near the upper surface of leaves and are specialized for photosynthesis.

🔬 Adaptations

  • Many chloroplasts absorb light.
  • Long shape helps cells become closely packed.
  • Large vacuole helps position chloroplasts near the cell edge.

🍃 Guard Cells

Guard cells occur in pairs around stomata. They control the opening and closing of the stomatal pore.

🔬 Important Features

  • Their shape changes as their water content changes.
  • They contain chloroplasts.
  • They regulate gas exchange and water loss through stomata.

🩸 Red Blood Cell

Red blood cells transport oxygen around the body.

🔬 Adaptations

  • Biconcave shape: Gives a large surface area and short diffusion distance.
  • No nucleus in mature mammalian cells: Provides more space for haemoglobin.
  • Haemoglobin: Combines with oxygen.
  • Flexible membrane: Allows the cell to pass through narrow blood vessels.

🧬 Sperm Cell

The sperm cell is the male gamete and is adapted for reaching and fertilizing the female gamete.

🔬 Adaptations

  • Flagellum: Allows movement through fluid.
  • Many mitochondria: Supply energy for movement.
  • Acrosome: Contains enzymes that help the sperm penetrate the egg.
  • Streamlined shape: Helps efficient movement.

🔍 6. Microscopy

A microscope is an instrument used to produce a magnified image of objects that are too small to be seen clearly with the naked eye.

🔬 Light Microscope

Learn the names and functions of the labelled parts.

⚙️ Important Microscope Parts

  • Eyepiece lens: The lens through which the specimen is viewed.
  • Objective lenses: Magnify the specimen.
  • Stage: Supports the slide.
  • Stage clips: Hold the slide in position.
  • Light source: Provides light for viewing the specimen.
  • Diaphragm: Controls the amount of light passing through the specimen.
  • Coarse adjustment: Makes large focusing adjustments.
  • Fine adjustment: Makes small adjustments to obtain a sharp image.

🧫 Preparing a Temporary Slide

  1. Place a thin specimen on a clean microscope slide.
  2. Add a suitable drop of water or mounting liquid if needed.
  3. Lower a coverslip carefully over the specimen.
  4. Remove excess liquid if necessary.

🔎 Using a Microscope

  • Start with the lowest-power objective lens.
  • Position the specimen over the light.
  • Use the coarse adjustment when appropriate to obtain approximate focus.
  • Use the fine adjustment to obtain a sharp image.
  • Handle slides and coverslips carefully.

📏 7. Magnification

Magnification is the number of times larger an image appears compared with the actual object.

🧮 Main Formulae

Magnification = Image size ÷ Actual size

Image size = Magnification × Actual size

Actual size = Image size ÷ Magnification

📐 Unit Conversions

  • 1 mm = 1,000 μm
  • 1 μm = 1,000 nm
  • 1 mm = 1,000,000 nm

🧠 Example

A cell has an actual size of 50 μm and an image size of 10 mm. Calculate its magnification.

First convert:

10 mm = 10,000 μm

Then:

Magnification = 10,000 ÷ 50

= ×200

⚠️ Exam Tip

Before calculating magnification, make sure the image size and actual size are expressed in the same unit.

✏️ 8. Biological Drawings

A biological drawing is a clear scientific representation of a specimen or biological structure as observed under a microscope or with the naked eye.

🎯 Rules for Biological Drawings

  • Use clear, single lines.
  • Do not shade.
  • Draw only structures that are actually observed.
  • Make the drawing large enough to show important features.
  • Use straight label lines where possible.
  • Do not allow label lines to cross.
  • Include a suitable title.
  • Include magnification or scale information when required.

🧠 Drawing vs Artistic Sketch

A biological drawing is not an artistic picture. Accuracy, clarity, correct proportions and appropriate labels are more important than making the drawing look attractive.
