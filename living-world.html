🌱 The Living World

Characteristics of life, classification, biodiversity and conservation.

🧬 1. Characteristics of Living Organisms

Living organisms carry out a number of processes that distinguish them from non-living things.

🧠 MRS GREN

A useful way to remember the main characteristics of living organisms is MRS GREN.

  • M — Movement: An action by which an organism or part of an organism changes position or place.
  • R — Respiration: The chemical reactions in cells that release energy from food.
  • S — Sensitivity: The ability to detect and respond to changes in the environment.
  • G — Growth: A permanent increase in size and dry mass.
  • R — Reproduction: The process by which organisms produce new individuals of their own kind.
  • E — Excretion: The removal of metabolic waste products and substances present in excess from an organism.
  • N — Nutrition: The taking in of substances needed for energy, growth and repair.

🧩 2. Levels of Organization

Multicellular organisms are organized into different levels. Cells work together to form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs work together in organ systems.

➡️ Organization Pathway

Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism

  • Cell: The basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • Tissue: A group of similar cells working together to perform a particular function.
  • Organ: A structure made up of different tissues working together.
  • Organ system: A group of organs working together to perform a major function.
  • Organism: A complete living individual.

🧠 Example

Muscle cells form muscle tissue. Different tissues can form an organ such as the heart. The heart works with other organs as part of the circulatory system.

🔬 3. Classification

Classification is the process of grouping organisms according to their similarities and differences.

Scientists classify organisms so that they can be identified, studied and compared more easily.

🧠 Why Classify Organisms?

  • Makes identification easier.
  • Makes it easier to study the huge variety of organisms.
  • Shows relationships between organisms.
  • Provides a common system for naming organisms.

🏷️ 4. Binomial Nomenclature

Binomial nomenclature is a system of giving each species a scientific name consisting of two parts.

🧠 The Two Parts

Genus + species

The genus name begins with a capital letter, while the specific name begins with a lowercase letter.

Scientific names are normally written in italics when typed.

🎯 Why Use Scientific Names?

Common names can differ between countries and languages. A scientific naming system allows scientists around the world to communicate about the same organism.

🌍 5. Major Groups of Organisms

Organisms can be grouped according to important characteristics such as their cell structure, nutrition and method of reproduction.

🦠 Bacteria

  • Usually unicellular.
  • Prokaryotic cells.
  • Do not have a membrane-bound nucleus.

🧫 Fungi

  • Eukaryotic organisms.
  • Usually obtain nutrients by absorption.
  • Examples include mushrooms and moulds.

🌿 Plants

  • Multicellular eukaryotic organisms.
  • Most plants contain chlorophyll.
  • Many make food by photosynthesis.

🐾 Animals

  • Multicellular eukaryotic organisms.
  • Obtain nutrients by feeding on other organisms.
  • Usually capable of movement from place to place.

🦠 Protoctists

  • Mostly microscopic eukaryotic organisms.
  • Some are unicellular while others are multicellular.
  • Examples include Amoeba and some algae.

🌳 6. Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms found in a particular area or on Earth as a whole.

🧠 Importance of Biodiversity

  • Provides food and other useful resources.
  • Provides medicinal resources.
  • Helps maintain functioning ecosystems.
  • Provides genetic resources that may be useful in agriculture and medicine.
  • Has educational, cultural and recreational value.

🌿 7. Local and Medicinal Plants

Plants are important resources in many communities. Different plants may be used as food, medicine, building materials, fibres or sources of other useful products.

🌱 Examples of Uses

  • Some plants provide food.
  • Some are used traditionally for medicinal purposes.
  • Some provide timber and building materials.
  • Some provide fibres used to make materials.

When discussing medicinal plants, remember that traditional use does not automatically prove that a plant is safe or medically effective.

🌍 8. Conservation

Conservation is the protection and responsible management of natural resources and biodiversity.

⚠️ Threats to Biodiversity

  • Deforestation.
  • Habitat destruction.
  • Pollution.
  • Overhunting and overfishing.
  • Climate and environmental changes.
  • Introduction of invasive species.

🛡️ Ways of Conserving Biodiversity

  • Establishing protected areas and reserves.
  • Reforestation and afforestation.
  • Controlling hunting and fishing.
  • Reducing pollution.
  • Protecting endangered species.
  • Using natural resources sustainably.
  • Educating communities about conservation.
