🌍 The Living World

Biology Notes | O/L GCE Cameroon

1. What is Biology?

Biology is the scientific study of living organisms. The word Biology comes from two Greek words:

  • Bio – life
  • Logos – study

Therefore, Biology means the study of life.

2. Characteristics of Living Organisms

Living organisms carry out several life processes. These characteristics help us distinguish living things from non-living things.

The main characteristics are:

  • Movement – the ability to change position or move parts of the body.
  • Respiration – the chemical process that releases energy from food.
  • Sensitivity – the ability to detect and respond to changes in the environment.
  • Growth – a permanent increase in size and dry mass.
  • Reproduction – the production of new individuals of the same species.
  • Excretion – the removal of metabolic waste products from the body.
  • Nutrition – obtaining or making food needed for energy, growth and repair.
  • Homeostasis – maintaining a stable internal environment.

3. Nutrition

Nutrition is the process by which organisms obtain or manufacture the substances they need for energy, growth and repair.

Types of Nutrition

There are two major types of nutrition:

  • Autotrophic nutrition
  • Heterotrophic nutrition

Autotrophic Nutrition

Green plants make their own food through photosynthesis. They use carbon dioxide and water in the presence of light energy and chlorophyll.

Heterotrophic Nutrition

Animals and other organisms that cannot manufacture their own food obtain nutrients by feeding on other organisms or organic materials.

4. Respiration

Respiration is a chemical process in living cells that releases energy from food.

Aerobic Respiration

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen to release energy from glucose.

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy

Anaerobic Respiration

Anaerobic respiration releases energy without using oxygen.

5. Growth

Growth is a permanent increase in size and dry mass of an organism.

Growth occurs because cells divide and increase in size.

Importance of Growth

  • Allows organisms to become larger.
  • Allows damaged tissues to be repaired.
  • Allows organisms to develop and mature.

6. Reproduction

Reproduction is the process by which organisms produce new individuals of their own species.

Types of Reproduction

  • Sexual reproduction – involves the fusion of male and female gametes.
  • Asexual reproduction – involves one parent and does not involve fusion of gametes.

7. Excretion

Excretion is the removal of metabolic waste products from an organism.

Examples of Excretory Products

  • Carbon dioxide
  • Urea
  • Excess water
  • Excess mineral salts

8. Sensitivity

Sensitivity is the ability of an organism to detect and respond to changes in its internal or external environment.

Examples

  • A plant growing towards light.
  • Pulling the hand away from a hot object.
  • Pupils becoming smaller in bright light.

9. Movement

Movement is the action by which an organism or part of an organism changes position.

Animals can move from one place to another, while plants may show movements such as the growth of shoots towards light.

10. Habitat

A habitat is the place where an organism normally lives and obtains the conditions and resources necessary for survival.

Examples of Habitats

  • Forest
  • Grassland
  • Freshwater
  • Marine environment
  • Desert

11. Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a community of organisms interacting with one another and with the non-living components of their environment.

Components of an Ecosystem

  • Biotic components – living organisms such as plants, animals and microorganisms.
  • Abiotic components – non-living factors such as light, temperature, water, soil and mineral salts.

12. Water Cycle

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between the atmosphere, land and bodies of water.

Main Processes

  • Evaporation – liquid water changes into water vapour due to heat.
  • Transpiration – water vapour is released from plant leaves.
  • Condensation – water vapour cools and changes into tiny water droplets.
  • Precipitation – water falls from clouds as rain, snow or hail.
  • Collection – water collects in rivers, lakes, oceans and underground.

13. Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms found in a particular area or on Earth as a whole.

Importance of Biodiversity

  • Provides food and raw materials.
  • Maintains stable ecosystems.
  • Provides sources of medicines.
  • Supports agriculture.
  • Has scientific and educational value.

14. Conservation

Conservation is the protection and careful management of natural resources and living organisms.

Ways of Conserving the Environment

  • Planting trees.
  • Preventing unnecessary cutting of forests.
  • Protecting endangered species.
  • Reducing pollution.
  • Recycling materials.
  • Creating protected areas and national parks.
