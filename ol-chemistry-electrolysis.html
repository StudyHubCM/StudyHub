⚡ Electrolysis

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Learn how electricity causes chemical changes in molten and aqueous substances.

1. ⚡ Introduction to Electrolysis

Electrolysis is the chemical decomposition of an ionic compound when an electric current is passed through the molten compound or its aqueous solution.

⭐ Key idea: Electrolysis converts electrical energy into chemical change.

2. 🧪 Electrolytes

An electrolyte is a substance that conducts electricity when molten or dissolved in water and is chemically decomposed during the process.

Substance Electrolyte? Reason
Molten sodium chloride Yes Contains mobile Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions.
Sodium chloride solution Yes Contains mobile ions in water.
Solid sodium chloride No Its ions are fixed in the crystal lattice.
Sugar solution No Sugar does not form ions in solution.

3. 🔋 Non-electrolytes

A non-electrolyte does not conduct electricity through mobile ions when dissolved in water or in its pure liquid state.

4. 🧲 Ions in Electrolysis

Electrolysis depends on the movement of charged particles called ions. Positive ions are called cations, while negative ions are called anions.

5. ➕ Cations

Cations are positively charged ions. They are attracted to the negative electrode.

Example: Na⁺, Cu²⁺, H⁺

➡️ Cations move towards the cathode.

6. ➖ Anions

Anions are negatively charged ions. They are attracted to the positive electrode.

Example: Cl⁻, Br⁻, OH⁻

➡️ Anions move towards the anode.

7. 🔌 Electrodes

Electrodes are conducting materials through which electric current enters or leaves the electrolyte.

Electrode Charge during electrolysis Ions attracted
Cathode Negative Cations
Anode Positive Anions

8. 🧠 Important Definitions

Electrolysis: Chemical decomposition caused by passing an electric current through a molten or aqueous electrolyte.

Electrolyte: A substance that conducts electricity through mobile ions and is chemically decomposed during electrolysis.

Electrode: A conductor through which electric current enters or leaves an electrolyte.

Cathode: The negative electrode in an electrolytic cell.

Anode: The positive electrode in an electrolytic cell.

Cation: A positively charged ion.

Anion: A negatively charged ion.

Inert electrode: An electrode that does not readily take part in the chemical reaction, such as platinum or graphite.

9. 🧭 Movement of Ions

During electrolysis, cations move towards the cathode and anions move towards the anode.

🧠 Easy memory trick: CATions → CAThode

Anions → ANode

11. 🧪 Molten Ionic Compounds

An ionic compound can conduct electricity when molten because its ions are free to move.

In the solid state, the ions are held in fixed positions and cannot move freely.

12. 🧂 Electrolysis of Molten Sodium Chloride

Molten sodium chloride contains Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions. The sodium ions move to the cathode and chloride ions move to the anode.

At the cathode:

Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na

At the anode:

2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻

Word equation: Sodium chloride → sodium + chlorine

Symbol equation: 2NaCl(l) → 2Na(l) + Cl₂(g)

14. 🔋 What Happens at the Cathode?

Positive ions gain electrons at the cathode. Gaining electrons is reduction.

⭐ Cathode = reduction

Cations gain electrons at the cathode.

15. ⚡ What Happens at the Anode?

Negative ions lose electrons at the anode. Loss of electrons is oxidation.

⭐ Anode = oxidation

Anions lose electrons at the anode.

16. 🧠 Oxidation and Reduction

Oxidation: Loss of electrons.

Reduction: Gain of electrons.

A useful memory aid is: OIL RIG — Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain.

17. 💧 Electrolysis of Aqueous Solutions

When an ionic compound is dissolved in water, the solution may contain ions from both the compound and water.

Therefore, predicting the products of electrolysis of an aqueous solution requires considering the possible ions present.

18. 💧 Ions Present in Water

Water can be represented as containing very small concentrations of H⁺ and OH⁻ ions. In an aqueous solution, ions from the dissolved substance are also present.

19. 🧂 Electrolysis of Aqueous Sodium Chloride

Aqueous sodium chloride contains Na⁺, Cl⁻, H⁺ and OH⁻ ions. Under typical laboratory electrolysis conditions with inert electrodes, hydrogen is produced at the cathode and chlorine at the anode.

At the cathode:

2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻

At the anode:

2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻

Overall word equation: Sodium chloride solution → hydrogen + chlorine + sodium hydroxide solution

Overall symbol equation: 2NaCl + 2H₂O → H₂ + Cl₂ + 2NaOH

21. 🧪 Electrolysis of Dilute Sulfuric Acid

Dilute sulfuric acid provides ions in solution. Using inert electrodes, hydrogen is produced at the cathode and oxygen at the anode.

At the cathode:

2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂

At the anode:

2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻

Word equation: Water → hydrogen + oxygen

Symbol equation: 2H₂O(l) → 2H₂(g) + O₂(g)

23. 💧 Electrolysis of Water

Water can be decomposed by electrolysis when it contains enough ions to conduct electricity.

Word equation: Water → hydrogen + oxygen

Symbol equation: 2H₂O(l) → 2H₂(g) + O₂(g)

Electrolysis Cathode product Anode product

25. 🪨 Inert Electrodes

Inert electrodes conduct electricity without being significantly consumed during the electrolysis.

Common inert electrode materials include graphite and platinum.

26. 🧪 Copper Electrodes

Copper electrodes can participate in electrolysis because copper can be oxidised at the anode and copper ions can be reduced at the cathode.

27. 🟤 Electrolysis of Copper(II) Sulfate Using Copper Electrodes

During electrolysis of copper(II) sulfate solution using copper electrodes, copper is deposited at the cathode and copper dissolves from the anode.

At the cathode:

Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu

At the anode:

Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻

Word equation: Copper at the anode → copper at the cathode

Overall symbol representation: Cu(anode) → Cu(cathode)

29. 🟠 Electrolysis of Copper(II) Sulfate Using Inert Electrodes

When copper(II) sulfate solution is electrolysed using inert electrodes, copper ions are reduced at the cathode while water is oxidised at the anode.

At the cathode:

Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu

At the anode:

2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻

Word equation: Copper(II) sulfate + water → copper + oxygen + sulfuric acid

Symbol equation: 2CuSO₄ + 2H₂O → 2Cu + O₂ + 2H₂SO₄

31. 🏭 Electroplating

Electroplating is the process of coating an object with a thin layer of another metal using electrolysis.

32. 🪙 Purpose of Electroplating

  • ✨ Improve appearance.
  • 🛡️ Protect the object from corrosion.
  • 💰 Use a thin layer of an expensive metal over a cheaper material.
  • ⚙️ Give the surface particular properties.

33. 🔌 Electroplating Setup

The object being coated is normally connected to the negative terminal and acts as the cathode. The plating metal is connected to the positive terminal and acts as the anode when a soluble metal anode is used.

Cathode: Object being plated.

Anode: Plating metal.

Electrolyte: Solution containing ions of the plating metal.

35. 🧪 Example: Silver Plating

In silver plating, the object is connected to the negative terminal and silver ions in the electrolyte are reduced and deposited on the object.

At the cathode:

Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag

36. 🧪 Extraction of Aluminium

Aluminium is extracted from aluminium oxide by electrolysis because aluminium is too reactive to be extracted economically by ordinary carbon reduction.

37. 🏭 Aluminium Ore

The main ore of aluminium is bauxite. Aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃, is obtained from bauxite before being electrolysed.

38. 🔥 Why Aluminium Oxide Is Molten

Aluminium oxide must be molten so that its ions are free to move. Industrially, aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite to lower the operating temperature and improve electrical conductivity.

40. ⚡ Aluminium Ions at the Cathode

Aluminium ions gain electrons at the cathode to form aluminium metal.

Electron equation: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al

41. ⚡ Oxide Ions at the Anode

Oxide ions lose electrons at the anode. With carbon anodes, the oxygen formed reacts with carbon, producing carbon dioxide.

Ion equation: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻

Word equation: Carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: C + O₂ → CO₂

42. 🏭 Overall Aluminium Extraction

The overall process converts aluminium oxide into aluminium while carbon anodes are gradually consumed.

Word equation: Aluminium oxide + carbon → aluminium + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: 2Al₂O₃ + 3C → 4Al + 3CO₂

43. 🧠 Why Aluminium Extraction Uses Electrolysis

Aluminium is high in the reactivity series. Its oxide is very stable, so carbon cannot readily remove the oxygen from aluminium oxide under ordinary extraction conditions.

44. ⚙️ Factors Affecting Electrolysis

  • Type of electrolyte.
  • Concentration of ions in solution.
  • Nature of the electrodes.
  • Temperature.
  • Current and voltage supplied.

45. 🔥 Effect of Temperature

Increasing temperature can increase ion mobility and may improve the conductivity of an electrolyte. Industrial electrolysis often uses carefully controlled temperatures.

46. 💡 Electrical Conductivity

An electrolyte conducts electricity because charged ions move through it. Electrons travel through the external circuit.

⭐ Inside the electrolyte: ions carry charge.

⭐ In the external circuit: electrons carry charge.

47. 🔄 Direction of Ion Movement

Particle Charge Moves towards
Cation Positive Cathode
Anion Negative Anode
Electron in external circuit Negative Through the external circuit

48. 🧪 Predicting Products at the Cathode

In aqueous solutions, several positive ions may be present. The ion that is preferentially discharged at the cathode depends on the conditions and relative ease of reduction.

At O/L level, a common pattern is that hydrogen is produced instead of very reactive metal ions such as Na⁺ in aqueous solutions.

49. 🧪 Predicting Products at the Anode

At the anode, negative ions can lose electrons. In aqueous solutions, halide ions such as chloride may be discharged, while oxygen can be produced from hydroxide/water depending on the conditions.

50. 🧪 Chloride vs Hydroxide at the Anode

In concentrated chloride solutions, chlorine is commonly produced at the anode. In many dilute solutions, oxygen can be produced instead.

⚠️ Always pay attention to the electrolyte and the conditions given in the question.

51. 📏 Measuring Products

The amount of a substance produced during electrolysis depends on the quantity of electricity passed through the electrolyte.

52. 🔢 Charge and Electrolysis

Electric charge can be calculated using:

Q = It

Q = charge in coulombs (C)

I = current in amperes (A)

t = time in seconds (s)

53. 🧮 Simple Electrolysis Calculation

If a current of 2 A flows for 60 seconds:

Q = It

Q = 2 × 60

Q = 120 C

54. 🧠 Faraday's Idea

The quantity of chemical change during electrolysis is related to the quantity of electricity passed through the electrolyte.

55. 🔋 Electrolysis and Energy

Electrolysis is an example of an endothermic chemical process in the sense that electrical energy must be supplied to drive the non-spontaneous chemical change.

56. 🏭 Uses of Electrolysis

  • ⚙️ Extraction of reactive metals.
  • ✨ Electroplating.
  • 🧪 Manufacture of chemicals.
  • 🔬 Purification of some metals.
  • 🏭 Production of hydrogen and chlorine.

57. 🏭 Industrial Uses

Process Use of electrolysis
Aluminium extraction Extracts aluminium from molten aluminium oxide.
Chlorine production Produces chlorine from chloride ions.
Hydrogen production Water can be electrolysed to produce hydrogen.
Electroplating Deposits a metal coating on an object.
Copper purification Electrolysis can produce high-purity copper.

58. 🟤 Electrorefining of Copper

Electrorefining is used to purify copper. Impure copper is used as the anode and pure copper is used as the cathode.

Copper atoms from the impure anode become Cu²⁺ ions, and Cu²⁺ ions are deposited as pure copper at the cathode.

61. 🧠 Memory Rules

➕ Cations → Cathode

➖ Anions → Anode

⬇️ Cathode → Reduction

⬆️ Anode → Oxidation

⚡ Electrolyte → mobile ions carry charge.

62. 🧪 Word Equations and Symbol Equations

When writing reactions involving electrolysis, give the word equation where appropriate and the corresponding balanced symbol equation.

Example:

Word equation: Water → hydrogen + oxygen

Symbol equation: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂

63. ⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying the cathode is positive in an electrolytic cell.
  • ❌ Saying the anode is negative in an electrolytic cell.
  • ❌ Mixing up cations and anions.
  • ❌ Forgetting that cations move to the cathode.
  • ❌ Forgetting that anions move to the anode.
  • ❌ Confusing oxidation with reduction.
  • ❌ Saying solid ionic compounds conduct because their ions can move.
  • ❌ Forgetting that aqueous solutions contain ions from water as well.
  • ❌ Forgetting to balance electrode equations.
  • ❌ Writing only a symbol equation when a word equation is required.
  • ❌ Confusing electrolysis with electroplating.

69. 🏁 Final Takeaway

Electrolysis uses electrical energy to drive chemical change. The key to solving electrolysis questions is to identify the ions present, know which electrode attracts each ion, determine whether oxidation or reduction occurs, and then write the correct electrode and overall equations.

⭐ Remember the four essentials

➕ Cations → Cathode

➖ Anions → Anode

⬇️ Reduction → Cathode

⬆️ Oxidation → Anode
