BIOLOGY • O/L 0510

🌍 Ecology & Environment

Understand ecosystems, habitats, populations, food chains, food webs, energy flow, nutrient cycles, biodiversity, pollution, conservation and human impacts.

1. 🌍 What is Ecology?

Ecology is the study of how living organisms interact with one another and with their physical environment.

Ecology helps us understand how plants, animals, microorganisms and non-living factors are connected in an ecosystem.

📌 Key Definition

Ecology: The study of the relationships between organisms and between organisms and their environment.

2. 🌱 What is the Environment?

The environment includes all the living and non-living conditions surrounding an organism.

It includes:

  • 🌿 Plants
  • 🐘 Animals
  • 🦠 Microorganisms
  • 💧 Water
  • 🌬️ Air
  • 🌡️ Temperature
  • ☀️ Light
  • 🪨 Soil and minerals

3. 🧬 Biotic and Abiotic Factors

Environmental factors can be divided into biotic and abiotic factors.

Biotic Factors Abiotic Factors
Living components of an environment Non-living components of an environment
Predators Temperature
Competitors Light intensity
Food availability Water availability
Diseases and parasites pH and mineral content

4. 🏞️ Habitat

A habitat is the place where an organism normally lives.

Examples include:

  • 🌳 Forest
  • 🌊 Pond
  • 🌿 Grassland
  • 🏜️ Desert
  • 🌊 Ocean
  • 💧 River

🎯 Exam Definition

Habitat: The place where an organism lives.

5. 🐾 Population

A population is all the organisms of one species living in a particular area at the same time.

Example: All the frogs of one species living in a particular pond form a population.

6. 🌿 Community

A community consists of all the populations of different species living and interacting in a particular area.

For example, a pond community may contain fish, frogs, algae, insects, bacteria and aquatic plants.

7. 🌎 Ecosystem

An ecosystem consists of a community of organisms together with the non-living parts of their environment.

🧠 Remember

Ecosystem = Living organisms + Non-living environment

8. 🔗 Levels of Organisation

Ecological organisation can be remembered as:

Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem

  • 🐟 Organism: One individual living thing.
  • 🐟🐟 Population: Members of the same species.
  • 🌿🐟🦠 Community: Different populations together.
  • 🌍 Ecosystem: Community + physical environment.

9. 🌱 Producers

Producers are organisms that make their own organic food, usually through photosynthesis.

Green plants and algae are important producers.

Producers form the first trophic level in most food chains.

10. 🐇 Consumers

Consumers are organisms that obtain energy by feeding on other organisms.

Consumers include:

  • 🌿 Herbivores: Eat plants.
  • 🥩 Carnivores: Eat animals.
  • 🍽️ Omnivores: Eat both plants and animals.

11. 🦁 Primary, Secondary and Tertiary Consumers

  • Primary consumers feed directly on producers.
  • Secondary consumers feed on primary consumers.
  • Tertiary consumers feed on secondary consumers.

Example:

🌿 Grass → 🐇 Rabbit → 🐍 Snake → 🦅 Eagle

Grass = Producer
Rabbit = Primary consumer
Snake = Secondary consumer
Eagle = Tertiary consumer

12. 🦠 Decomposers

Decomposers are organisms that break down dead organisms and waste materials into simpler substances.

Important decomposers include bacteria and fungi.

Decomposition returns mineral ions and other substances to the environment.

13. 🍂 Importance of Decomposers

Decomposers:

  • Break down dead organisms.
  • Break down waste materials.
  • Release mineral ions into the soil.
  • Recycle nutrients.
  • Prevent dead material from accumulating.

14. 🍽️ Food Chains

A food chain shows the transfer of energy and food from one organism to another.

🌿 Example Food Chain

☀️ → 🌿 Grass → 🦗 Grasshopper → 🐸 Frog → 🐍 Snake → 🦅 Eagle

The arrows show the direction of energy transfer.

⚠️ Important

The arrow in a food chain points from the organism being eaten to the organism that eats it.

15. 🔢 How to Construct a Food Chain

When constructing a food chain:

  1. Start with a producer.
  2. Add the organism that eats the producer.
  3. Continue with the next consumer.
  4. Use arrows to show the direction of energy transfer.

Example:

🌿 Leaves → 🐛 Caterpillar → 🐦 Bird → 🦅 Hawk

16. 🏆 Trophic Levels

A trophic level is the position an organism occupies in a food chain.

Trophic Level Organism
1st Producer
2nd Primary consumer
3rd Secondary consumer
4th Tertiary consumer

17. 🕸️ Food Webs

A food web is a network of interconnected food chains.

Food webs are more realistic than simple food chains because most organisms eat more than one type of food and may be eaten by several different organisms.

🌿 Example Food Web

Grass may be eaten by rabbits, grasshoppers and other herbivores.

Grasshoppers may be eaten by frogs and birds.

Rabbits may be eaten by snakes and foxes.

Snakes may be eaten by eagles.

18. 🔍 Reading a Food Web

When answering food-web questions:

  • Follow the arrows carefully.
  • Identify producers first.
  • Identify primary consumers next.
  • Trace what each organism eats.
  • Trace which organisms feed on it.

🎯 Exam Tip

If asked "What does organism X eat?", follow the arrow into X from the food source.

19. ☀️ Energy Entry into Ecosystems

The main source of energy for most ecosystems is the Sun.

Plants absorb light energy and convert it into chemical energy during photosynthesis.

This chemical energy is stored in organic substances such as glucose.

20. 🔥 Energy Flow Through an Ecosystem

Energy flows through an ecosystem from the Sun to producers and then through consumers.

Energy is eventually released as heat during respiration.

☀️ Energy Pathway

Sun → Producer → Primary Consumer → Secondary Consumer → Tertiary Consumer

21. 📉 Why Energy Decreases Along a Food Chain

Less energy is available at each successive trophic level.

Energy is lost because:

  • 🔥 Respiration releases energy as heat.
  • 💩 Not all food is digested or absorbed.
  • 🚽 Some energy leaves the body in waste.
  • 🌿 Some parts of organisms are not eaten.
  • 🏃 Movement uses energy.

22. 📊 Pyramids of Number

A pyramid of numbers represents the number of organisms at each trophic level.

The width of each level represents the number of organisms at that level.

📌 Important

A pyramid of numbers can sometimes be inverted. For example, one large tree may support many insects.

23. ⚖️ Pyramids of Biomass

A pyramid of biomass represents the total mass of living material at each trophic level.

Biomass is usually measured as dry mass because water content varies.

24. ⚡ Pyramids of Energy

A pyramid of energy shows the amount of energy available at each trophic level.

Energy pyramids normally become narrower towards the top because energy is lost at each trophic level.

🎯 Exam Focus

Energy pyramids are always upright because available energy decreases along the food chain.

25. 🌿 Population Size

The size of a population depends on factors such as:

  • Food availability
  • Water availability
  • Predation
  • Competition
  • Disease
  • Availability of space
  • Temperature

26. 📈 Population Growth

A population may increase when the birth rate is greater than the death rate.

Immigration can also increase population size, while emigration can decrease it.

🧠 Population Change

Population increases due to: Births + Immigration

Population decreases due to: Deaths + Emigration

27. 🐺 Predation

Predation occurs when one organism kills and eats another organism.

The organism that hunts is the predator, while the organism being eaten is the prey.

Example: A lion is a predator and a zebra is prey.

28. 🌿 Competition

Competition occurs when organisms require the same limited resources.

Organisms may compete for:

  • Food
  • Water
  • Light
  • Mineral ions
  • Space
  • Mates

29. 🐇 Intraspecific and Interspecific Competition

Type Meaning Example
Intraspecific Competition between members of the same species Two rabbits competing for food
Interspecific Competition between different species Rabbits and grasshoppers competing for plants

30. 🌡️ Environmental Factors Affecting Organisms

Organisms are affected by abiotic factors such as:

  • 🌡️ Temperature
  • ☀️ Light intensity
  • 💧 Water availability
  • 🧪 pH
  • 🪨 Mineral content
  • 🌬️ Wind speed
  • 💨 Oxygen concentration
  • 🌧️ Rainfall

31. 🌡️ Temperature

Temperature affects the activity and survival of organisms.

Enzyme-controlled reactions are particularly affected by temperature.

Very high or very low temperatures may reduce survival and reproduction.

32. ☀️ Light Intensity

Light intensity affects photosynthesis in plants.

It also affects the behaviour and distribution of some animals.

Plants growing in shaded areas may receive less light for photosynthesis.

33. 💧 Water Availability

Water is essential for life.

Plants require water for photosynthesis and mineral transport. Animals also require water for metabolic processes.

Organisms living in dry environments have adaptations that help reduce water loss.

34. 🧪 pH

pH describes how acidic or alkaline an environment is.

Different organisms have different pH ranges in which they can survive and reproduce.

35. 📏 Measuring Population Size

Scientists use sampling techniques to estimate the number and distribution of organisms.

Common methods include quadrats and transects.

36. ⬜ Quadrat Sampling

A quadrat is a square frame used to sample organisms, especially plants and slow-moving organisms.

Basic method:

  1. Place the quadrat randomly in the study area.
  2. Count the organisms inside it.
  3. Repeat several times.
  4. Calculate the mean number per quadrat.
  5. Use the result to estimate the population.

37. 📐 Estimating Population Size Using Quadrats

A simple estimate can be calculated using:

Estimated population = Mean number per quadrat × Total area ÷ Area of one quadrat

Repeating the sampling increases reliability.

38. 📏 Transects

A transect is a line placed across an area to study how organisms are distributed along an environmental gradient.

For example, a transect can be used to investigate how plant distribution changes from a shaded area to an open area.

39. 📊 Abiotic Measurements in Field Studies

During ecological investigations, abiotic factors may be measured using:

  • 🌡️ Thermometer — temperature
  • 💧 Moisture meter — soil moisture
  • 💡 Light meter — light intensity
  • 🧪 pH meter — pH
  • 🌬️ Anemometer — wind speed

40. 🧪 Reliability in Ecological Investigations

To improve reliability:

  • Take many samples.
  • Use random sampling where appropriate.
  • Calculate a mean.
  • Repeat measurements.
  • Use the same method throughout.

41. ♻️ Nutrient Recycling

Unlike energy, nutrients are recycled through ecosystems.

Important nutrients include carbon, nitrogen and mineral ions.

Decomposers play an important role in returning nutrients to the environment.

42. 🌳 Carbon Cycle

Carbon is constantly transferred between organisms and the environment.

Important processes include:

  • 🌿 Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.
  • 🫁 Respiration releases carbon dioxide.
  • 🍂 Decomposition releases carbon dioxide.
  • 🔥 Combustion releases carbon dioxide.

🔄 Simple Carbon Cycle

Carbon dioxide → Photosynthesis → Plants → Animals → Respiration → Carbon dioxide

43. 🌱 Nitrogen and Mineral Recycling

Plants need mineral ions such as nitrate ions to make proteins.

When plants and animals die, decomposers break down their remains. Mineral substances are returned to the soil.

44. 🦠 Role of Decomposers in Nutrient Cycling

Decomposers:

  • Digest dead organic matter.
  • Release mineral ions into the soil.
  • Return carbon compounds to the environment.
  • Make nutrients available for producers again.

45. 🌳 Adaptations to the Environment

An adaptation is a feature that helps an organism survive and reproduce successfully in its environment.

Adaptations may be:

  • Structural
  • Behavioural
  • Physiological

46. 🏜️ Adaptations of Plants to Dry Conditions

Examples include:

  • Thick waxy cuticle.
  • Reduced leaf area.
  • Sunken or fewer stomata.
  • Leaves modified into spines.
  • Water storage tissues.
  • Extensive root systems.

47. 🐪 Adaptations of Animals

Animals are adapted to the conditions in which they live.

Examples include:

  • Camouflage to avoid predators.
  • Thick fur in cold environments.
  • Large ears in some hot-climate animals to increase heat loss.
  • Nocturnal behaviour to avoid daytime heat.

48. 🦋 Biodiversity

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms in an area or on Earth.

High biodiversity generally means that many different species are present.

49. 🌳 Importance of Biodiversity

Biodiversity is important because ecosystems provide:

  • Food
  • Medicines
  • Raw materials
  • Pollination
  • Soil formation
  • Clean water
  • Oxygen production
  • Climate regulation

50. ⚠️ Threats to Biodiversity

Major threats include:

  • Deforestation
  • Habitat destruction
  • Pollution
  • Climate change
  • Overhunting
  • Overfishing
  • Invasive species
  • Illegal wildlife trade

51. 🌳 Deforestation

Deforestation is the large-scale removal of trees from forests.

Effects include:

  • Loss of habitats.
  • Loss of biodiversity.
  • Soil erosion.
  • Reduced carbon dioxide removal by plants.
  • Increased carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.
  • Disruption of the water cycle.

52. 🏭 Pollution

Pollution is the introduction of harmful substances or energy into the environment.

Major types include:

  • Air pollution
  • Water pollution
  • Soil pollution
  • Noise pollution

53. 💨 Air Pollution

Air pollution occurs when harmful substances are released into the atmosphere.

Sources include:

  • Vehicle exhaust
  • Factories
  • Burning fossil fuels
  • Burning forests
  • Burning waste

Air pollution can damage human health, plants and ecosystems.

54. 🌧️ Acid Rain

Burning fossil fuels can release sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides. These gases can react with water in the atmosphere and contribute to acid rain.

Acid rain can:

  • Damage leaves.
  • Lower the pH of lakes and rivers.
  • Harm aquatic organisms.
  • Damage buildings and limestone.

55. 💧 Water Pollution

Water can become polluted by:

  • Sewage
  • Fertilizers
  • Pesticides
  • Industrial waste
  • Oil
  • Plastic waste

56. 🌿 Eutrophication

Eutrophication occurs when excessive mineral ions, especially nitrates and phosphates, enter a body of water.

The process can occur as follows:

  1. Fertilizers enter water.
  2. Nitrate and phosphate concentrations increase.
  3. Algae grow rapidly.
  4. An algal bloom forms.
  5. Light is blocked from reaching submerged plants.
  6. Plants die.
  7. Decomposers increase as dead material is broken down.
  8. Respiration by microorganisms uses oxygen.
  9. Dissolved oxygen concentration decreases.
  10. Aquatic animals may die from lack of oxygen.

57. 🧴 Plastic Pollution

Plastic can enter rivers, oceans and land environments.

Problems include:

  • Animals may become trapped in plastic.
  • Animals may mistake plastic for food.
  • Plastic can persist in the environment for long periods.
  • Microplastics can enter food chains.

58. 🌡️ Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term changes in climate patterns.

Increased concentrations of greenhouse gases contribute to global warming.

Possible effects include:

  • Rising temperatures.
  • Changes in rainfall.
  • Melting of ice.
  • Rising sea levels.
  • Changes in habitats.
  • Changes in species distribution.

59. 🌫️ Greenhouse Effect

The greenhouse effect is the warming of Earth's surface and lower atmosphere because greenhouse gases absorb and re-radiate infrared radiation.

Important greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour.

60. 🔥 Human Activities Increasing Greenhouse Gases

  • Burning fossil fuels.
  • Deforestation.
  • Industrial activities.
  • Transport.
  • Agriculture.
  • Waste decomposition.

61. 🌍 Conservation

Conservation is the protection and careful management of natural resources and living organisms.

Conservation methods include:

  • Creating national parks.
  • Protecting endangered species.
  • Reforestation.
  • Controlling hunting and fishing.
  • Reducing pollution.
  • Recycling resources.

62. 🌱 Reforestation

Reforestation is the replanting of trees in areas where forests have been removed or damaged.

Benefits include:

  • Restores habitats.
  • Reduces soil erosion.
  • Absorbs carbon dioxide.
  • Provides resources.
  • Supports biodiversity.

63. ♻️ Recycling

Recycling involves processing used materials so that they can be used again.

It can:

  • Reduce waste.
  • Reduce the use of raw materials.
  • Save energy.
  • Reduce pollution.

64. 💧 Sustainable Use of Resources

Sustainable use means using resources in a way that allows them to remain available for future generations.

Examples include:

  • Sustainable fishing.
  • Responsible forestry.
  • Using renewable energy.
  • Reducing unnecessary water use.

65. 🐘 Endangered Species

An endangered species is a species whose population has fallen to a level where it is at significant risk of extinction.

Causes include habitat destruction, hunting, pollution and climate change.

66. 🏞️ Conservation Methods for Endangered Species

  • Protecting natural habitats.
  • Creating protected areas.
  • Controlling hunting.
  • Breeding programmes.
  • Seed banks.
  • Education and public awareness.
  • International protection agreements.

67. 🧬 Extinction

A species becomes extinct when no living individuals of that species remain.

Extinction can reduce biodiversity and disrupt food webs.

68. 🔗 What Happens When One Species Disappears?

Removing one species can affect many other organisms in a food web.

For example:

If a predator decreases → prey may increase → plants may decrease → competition for plants may increase.

This shows that organisms in an ecosystem are interconnected.

69. 🐝 Pollination and Ecosystems

Many flowering plants depend on animals such as insects for pollination.

A reduction in pollinator populations can reduce the reproduction of some plants and affect food production.

70. 🌾 Agriculture and the Environment

Agriculture can affect ecosystems through:

  • Deforestation.
  • Fertilizer use.
  • Pesticides.
  • Soil erosion.
  • Water consumption.
  • Habitat loss.

71. 🧪 Fertilizers and the Environment

Fertilizers provide mineral ions needed for plant growth. However, excessive fertilizer use can cause nutrient pollution and eutrophication.

72. 🐛 Pesticides

Pesticides are substances used to control organisms considered harmful to crops.

Excessive or inappropriate pesticide use can harm non-target organisms and contaminate soil and water.

73. 🐟 Bioaccumulation

Bioaccumulation is the gradual build-up of a substance inside an organism when the rate of intake is greater than the rate of removal.

Some pollutants can accumulate in organisms over time.

74. 🔺 Biomagnification

Biomagnification occurs when concentrations of certain persistent pollutants become greater at higher trophic levels in a food chain.

Example

Water → Small organisms → Fish → Large fish → Fish-eating bird

If a persistent pollutant enters the food chain, organisms at higher trophic levels may receive higher concentrations.

75. 🌊 Marine Ecosystems

Marine ecosystems include oceans, coral reefs, mangroves and coastal habitats.

They provide food, habitats and other resources and help regulate Earth's climate.

76. 🌳 Forest Ecosystems

Forest ecosystems contain many interacting organisms and often have high biodiversity.

Forests provide habitats, absorb carbon dioxide, protect soil and influence the water cycle.

77. 🐸 Freshwater Ecosystems

Freshwater ecosystems include rivers, lakes, ponds and streams.

They contain organisms adapted to freshwater conditions.

78. 🔄 Interdependence

Interdependence means that organisms depend on one another for resources or other benefits.

Examples include:

  • Plants provide food for herbivores.
  • Predators control prey populations.
  • Pollinators help plants reproduce.
  • Decomposers recycle nutrients.

79. 🧠 Ecosystem Balance

Ecosystems are dynamic systems in which populations change over time.

Changes to one population can cause changes in other populations because organisms are connected through food webs and competition.

80. 📈 Graphs in Ecology

O/L Biology questions may provide graphs showing changes in population size, environmental factors or species distribution.

When interpreting a graph:

  1. Read the axes.
  2. Check the units.
  3. Identify the independent variable.
  4. Identify the dependent variable.
  5. Describe the trend.
  6. Use data from the graph to support your answer.

81. 📊 Population Growth Curves

A population may initially increase rapidly when resources are plentiful. As resources become limited, growth may slow.

Factors such as food shortage, disease, competition and predation can limit population growth.

82. 🧮 Percentage Change in Ecological Data

Percentage change may be calculated using:

Percentage change = (Change ÷ Original value) × 100

Always identify the original value before calculating percentage change.

83. 🧪 Planning an Ecological Investigation

A good investigation should include:

  • A clear aim.
  • A suitable method.
  • Independent variable.
  • Dependent variable.
  • Control variables.
  • Repeats.
  • Suitable equipment.
  • Safe working procedures.

84. 🔬 Sampling and Randomisation

Random sampling reduces the chance of choosing only organisms from a particular part of the habitat.

Several samples should normally be taken so that a mean can be calculated.

86. 📚 Important Ecology Definitions

  • Ecology: Study of organisms and their environment.
  • Environment: Living and non-living surroundings.

  • Population: Organisms of one species in an area.
  • Community: Different populations living together.
  • Ecosystem: Community plus its physical environment.
  • Producer: Organism that makes its own organic food.
  • Consumer: Organism that obtains food from other organisms.
  • Decomposer: Organism that breaks down dead material.
  • Food chain: Sequence showing feeding relationships.
  • Food web: Network of interconnected food chains.

  • Extinction: Complete disappearance of a species.

88. 📝 Common O/L Mistakes

❌ Mistake: Saying the arrow in a food chain points toward the food.

✅ Correct: The arrow points in the direction of energy transfer.

❌ Mistake: Saying decomposers are not important.

✅ Correct: Decomposers recycle nutrients from dead material.

❌ Mistake: Confusing habitat with ecosystem.

✅ Correct: A habitat is where an organism lives; an ecosystem includes organisms and their physical environment.

❌ Mistake: Saying energy is recycled.

✅ Correct: Energy flows through ecosystems and is eventually dissipated as heat; nutrients are recycled.

❌ Mistake: Calling every animal a secondary consumer.

✅ Correct: Its trophic level depends on what it eats.

90. 🧠 Master Ecology Summary

Ecosystems contain living organisms and non-living environmental factors. Organisms interact through feeding relationships, competition, predation and other forms of interdependence.

Producers capture energy through photosynthesis. Consumers obtain energy by feeding on other organisms, while decomposers break down dead material and recycle nutrients.

Food chains show individual feeding pathways, while food webs show the many interconnected feeding relationships within an ecosystem.

Energy is transferred between trophic levels but much energy is lost, mainly through respiration and heat loss. Nutrients, however, are recycled.

Human activities such as deforestation, pollution, overexploitation and greenhouse-gas emissions can damage ecosystems and reduce biodiversity. Conservation helps protect species, habitats and natural resources.
