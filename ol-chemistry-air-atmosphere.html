🌍 Air & Atmospheric Chemistry

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Complete notes on the composition of air, properties of atmospheric gases, combustion, air pollution, greenhouse gases, ozone and atmospheric chemistry.

1. 🌬️ Introduction to Air

Air is a mixture of several gases surrounding the Earth. It is essential for life and supports processes such as respiration, combustion and photosynthesis.

💡 Key idea: Air is a mixture, not a compound, because its gases are not chemically bonded together and can be separated by physical methods.

2. 🧪 Main Components of Air

Dry air is mainly composed of nitrogen and oxygen, with smaller amounts of argon, carbon dioxide and other gases.

Gas Approximate percentage by volume Importance
Nitrogen 78% Important in proteins and plant nutrition
Oxygen 21% Supports respiration and combustion
Argon About 0.93% Inert gas used in some industrial applications
Carbon dioxide About 0.04% Used by plants in photosynthesis
Other gases Small amounts Includes noble gases and variable trace gases

🌧️ Water vapour is also present in the atmosphere, but its concentration varies considerably with location and weather.

3. ⚛️ Nitrogen in Air

Nitrogen is the most abundant gas in the atmosphere, making up about 78% of dry air.

Nitrogen gas exists mainly as diatomic molecules, N₂.

Formula: N₂

Each nitrogen molecule contains two nitrogen atoms joined by a strong covalent bond.

4. 🌱 Importance of Nitrogen

  • 🌿 Plants need nitrogen compounds to make proteins.
  • 🧬 Nitrogen is present in nucleic acids.
  • 🌱 Nitrogen compounds are important for plant growth.
  • 🏭 Nitrogen is used in several industrial processes.

5. 🔥 Nitrogen and Combustion

Nitrogen is relatively unreactive under ordinary conditions. Therefore, it does not normally support combustion.

Its relative inertness makes it useful where an atmosphere that does not readily react is required.

6. 🫁 Oxygen in Air

Oxygen makes up approximately 21% of dry air.

Oxygen is a reactive gas and is essential for aerobic respiration. It also supports combustion.

🔥 Oxygen supports combustion, but oxygen itself is not normally described as a fuel.

7. 🧪 Test for Oxygen

Oxygen can be tested using a glowing splint.

🔥 Observation: A glowing splint relights in oxygen.

8. 🫁 Oxygen and Respiration

Oxygen is used by aerobic organisms during respiration to release energy from food.

Word equation:

Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O

9. 🔥 Oxygen and Combustion

Combustion is a chemical reaction in which a substance reacts with oxygen, usually releasing energy as heat and often light.

Word equation:

Carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide

Symbol equation:

C + O₂ → CO₂

10. 🧪 Carbon Dioxide in Air

Carbon dioxide is a minor component of the atmosphere. Its concentration is much lower than those of nitrogen and oxygen.

Carbon dioxide is produced naturally and by human activities.

11. 🌱 Importance of Carbon Dioxide

Green plants use carbon dioxide during photosynthesis.

Word equation:

Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Symbol equation:

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Light energy and chlorophyll are required.

12. 🧪 Test for Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide is commonly tested using limewater.

When carbon dioxide is bubbled through limewater, the limewater turns milky because insoluble calcium carbonate forms.

Word equation:

Calcium hydroxide + carbon dioxide → calcium carbonate + water

Symbol equation:

Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O

🔍 Observation: Limewater turns milky/cloudy.

13. 🌫️ Sources of Carbon Dioxide

  • 🫁 Respiration
  • 🔥 Combustion of fuels
  • 🌋 Volcanic activity
  • 🦠 Decomposition of organic matter
  • 🏭 Industrial processes

14. 🌱 Removal of Carbon Dioxide

Green plants remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere during photosynthesis.

Oceans also absorb significant amounts of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.

15. 💨 Argon

Argon is a noble gas and makes up about 0.93% of dry air.

It is chemically unreactive because it has a stable outer electron arrangement.

16. 💡 Uses of Argon

  • 💡 Used in some electric and lighting applications.
  • 🔧 Used as an inert shielding gas in welding.
  • 🧪 Used where an unreactive atmosphere is required.

17. 💧 Water Vapour in the Atmosphere

Water vapour is an important atmospheric gas. Unlike nitrogen and oxygen, its concentration varies greatly.

Water vapour is involved in cloud formation, rainfall and the water cycle.

18. 🌍 Atmosphere

The atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding the Earth.

🌎 The atmosphere provides gases needed by living organisms and helps regulate Earth's temperature.

19. 🛰️ Layers of the Atmosphere

Layer Main feature
Troposphere Lowest layer; most weather occurs here
Stratosphere Contains the main concentration of atmospheric ozone
Mesosphere Higher atmospheric layer above the stratosphere
Thermosphere Very thin air and high temperatures
Exosphere Outermost region gradually merging with space

20. 🌦️ Weather and the Atmosphere

Weather occurs mainly in the troposphere.

Processes such as evaporation, condensation, cloud formation, precipitation and wind occur within this region.

21. 🔥 Combustion

Combustion is the reaction of a substance with oxygen, usually producing energy.

Many fuels contain carbon and hydrogen and are called hydrocarbons when they contain only carbon and hydrogen.

22. 🔥 Complete Combustion

Complete combustion occurs when a hydrocarbon burns in a sufficient supply of oxygen.

The main products are carbon dioxide and water.

Word equation:

Methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation:

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

23. ⚠️ Incomplete Combustion

Incomplete combustion occurs when there is insufficient oxygen.

Carbon monoxide and/or carbon can be produced instead of only carbon dioxide.

Word equation:

Methane + oxygen → carbon monoxide + water

Symbol equation:

2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O

24. ☠️ Carbon Monoxide

Carbon monoxide, CO, is a poisonous gas produced when carbon compounds undergo incomplete combustion.

It is dangerous because it reduces the blood's ability to transport oxygen effectively.

⚠️ Carbon monoxide is colourless and odourless, making it difficult to detect without a suitable detector.

25. 🏠 Preventing Carbon Monoxide Poisoning

  • 🔥 Ensure fuel-burning appliances receive adequate air.
  • 🏠 Maintain proper ventilation.
  • 🔧 Service fuel-burning appliances properly.
  • 🚨 Use appropriate carbon monoxide detectors where required.

26. 🌫️ Smoke and Soot

Incomplete combustion can produce tiny carbon particles called soot.

These particles can contribute to air pollution and respiratory problems when inhaled.

27. 🏭 Air Pollution

Air pollution occurs when harmful substances are present in the atmosphere at concentrations that can cause environmental or health problems.

Pollutants may come from natural sources or human activities.

28. 🚗 Major Sources of Air Pollution

  • 🚗 Vehicle exhausts
  • 🏭 Industrial emissions
  • 🔥 Burning fossil fuels
  • 🗑️ Burning waste
  • 🌾 Agricultural activities
  • 🌋 Volcanic eruptions
  • 🔥 Wildfires

29. 🧪 Major Air Pollutants

Pollutant Common source Possible effect
Carbon monoxide, CO Incomplete combustion Poisonous; interferes with oxygen transport
Sulfur dioxide, SO₂ Burning sulfur-containing fuels Respiratory irritation and acid rain
Nitrogen oxides, NOₓ High-temperature combustion Smog, respiratory effects and acid deposition
Particulates Combustion and dust Respiratory and environmental problems
Volatile organic compounds Fuel evaporation and combustion Can contribute to photochemical smog

30. 🌋 Sulfur Dioxide

Sulfur dioxide is produced when sulfur-containing fuels are burned.

Word equation:

Sulfur + oxygen → sulfur dioxide

Symbol equation:

S + O₂ → SO₂

31. 🏭 Sulfur Dioxide from Sulfur-Containing Fuels

Some fossil fuels contain sulfur compounds. Burning such fuels can release sulfur dioxide into the atmosphere.

Sulfur dioxide can contribute to the formation of acid deposition.

32. 🌧️ Acid Rain

Acid rain refers to precipitation that has become more acidic than normal because of acidic substances formed in the atmosphere.

Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides are important contributors.

33. 🧪 Formation of Sulfur Dioxide

Word equation:

Sulfur + oxygen → sulfur dioxide

Symbol equation:

S + O₂ → SO₂

34. 🧪 Further Oxidation of Sulfur Dioxide

Sulfur dioxide can be oxidised to sulfur trioxide in the atmosphere under suitable conditions.

Word equation:

Sulfur dioxide + oxygen → sulfur trioxide

Symbol equation:

2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃

35. 🌧️ Formation of Sulfuric Acid

Sulfur trioxide reacts with water to form sulfuric acid.

Word equation:

Sulfur trioxide + water → sulfuric acid

Symbol equation:

SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄

36. 🌧️ Effects of Acid Deposition

  • 🌳 Can damage vegetation.
  • 🐟 Can acidify lakes and streams.
  • 🏛️ Can damage limestone and marble structures.
  • 🪨 Can accelerate weathering of some materials.
  • 🌱 Can affect soil chemistry.

37. 🏭 Reducing Sulfur Dioxide Emissions

  • ⛽ Use fuels with lower sulfur content.
  • 🏭 Remove sulfur compounds during fuel processing.
  • 🧪 Use flue-gas desulfurisation where appropriate.
  • ⚡ Use alternative energy sources that produce less sulfur dioxide.

38. 🚗 Nitrogen Oxides

Nitrogen oxides, commonly represented as NOₓ, include nitrogen monoxide, NO, and nitrogen dioxide, NO₂.

They can form during high-temperature combustion, especially in vehicle engines and industrial processes.

39. 🔥 Formation of Nitrogen Monoxide

At very high temperatures, nitrogen and oxygen from air can react.

Word equation:

Nitrogen + oxygen → nitrogen monoxide

Symbol equation:

N₂ + O₂ → 2NO

40. 🌫️ Formation of Nitrogen Dioxide

Nitrogen monoxide can react further with oxygen to form nitrogen dioxide.

Word equation:

Nitrogen monoxide + oxygen → nitrogen dioxide

Symbol equation:

2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂

41. 🌧️ Nitrogen Oxides and Acid Rain

Nitrogen oxides can contribute to the formation of nitric acid in atmospheric moisture.

This contributes to acid deposition.

42. 🚗 Reducing Nitrogen Oxides

Modern vehicles can use catalytic converters to reduce certain harmful exhaust gases.

Catalytic converters promote reactions that convert some pollutants into less harmful substances.

43. 🚘 Catalytic Converter

A catalytic converter is fitted to the exhaust system of many vehicles to help reduce emissions of pollutants such as carbon monoxide and nitrogen oxides.

Word equation:

Carbon monoxide + nitrogen monoxide → carbon dioxide + nitrogen

Symbol equation:

2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂

44. 🌫️ Particulate Pollution

Particulate matter consists of tiny solid particles or liquid droplets suspended in air.

Examples include soot, dust and particles produced during combustion.

45. 🫁 Effects of Particulates

  • 🫁 Can irritate the respiratory system.
  • 🌫️ Can reduce visibility.
  • 🏙️ Can contribute to urban air pollution.
  • 🌱 Can settle on plants and surfaces.

46. 🌫️ Photochemical Smog

Photochemical smog forms when sunlight drives chemical reactions involving nitrogen oxides and other atmospheric pollutants.

It can contain harmful oxidising substances, including ozone near ground level.

47. ☀️ Role of Sunlight in Photochemical Smog

Sunlight provides energy for photochemical reactions involving pollutants released into the atmosphere.

☀️ Remember: Ground-level ozone is a pollutant, even though ozone in the stratosphere is important for protecting life from ultraviolet radiation.

48. 🌍 Greenhouse Effect

The greenhouse effect is the natural warming of Earth's surface and lower atmosphere caused by gases that absorb and re-emit infrared radiation.

It is essential for maintaining temperatures suitable for life.

49. 🌡️ Greenhouse Gases

Greenhouse gas Examples of sources
Carbon dioxide Fossil-fuel combustion, deforestation and industry
Methane Agriculture, waste and fossil-fuel activities
Nitrous oxide Agricultural soils and combustion
Water vapour Evaporation and the water cycle

50. 🌡️ Enhanced Greenhouse Effect

Human activities can increase the atmospheric concentration of certain greenhouse gases.

This can strengthen the greenhouse effect and contribute to long-term global climate change.

51. 🏭 Human Activities Increasing Carbon Dioxide

  • 🔥 Burning coal, oil and natural gas
  • 🚗 Transport
  • 🏭 Industrial processes
  • 🌳 Deforestation and land-use change
  • ⚡ Fossil-fuel electricity generation

52. 🌱 Reducing Carbon Dioxide Emissions

  • 🌞 Increase the use of renewable energy.
  • ⚡ Improve energy efficiency.
  • 🌳 Protect and restore forests.
  • 🚲 Encourage low-emission transport.
  • 🏭 Improve industrial efficiency.
  • ♻️ Reduce unnecessary energy consumption.

53. 🛡️ The Ozone Layer

Ozone is a form of oxygen containing three oxygen atoms.

Its formula is O₃.

Most atmospheric ozone is concentrated in the stratosphere, where it forms the ozone layer.

54. ☀️ Importance of Stratospheric Ozone

Stratospheric ozone absorbs a large proportion of harmful ultraviolet radiation from the Sun.

This helps protect living organisms from excessive ultraviolet exposure.

55. 🧪 Formation of Ozone

Ultraviolet radiation can split oxygen molecules into oxygen atoms. These atoms can then combine with oxygen molecules to form ozone.

Word equation:

Oxygen → oxygen atoms

Symbol equation:

O₂ → 2O

Word equation:

Oxygen atom + oxygen → ozone

Symbol equation:

O + O₂ → O₃

56. ⚠️ Ozone Depletion

Ozone depletion refers to the reduction of stratospheric ozone concentration in particular regions or over time.

Certain chemicals, including chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), can release reactive chlorine species in the stratosphere that participate in ozone-destroying reactions.

57. 🧪 CFCs

Chlorofluorocarbons are compounds containing carbon, chlorine and fluorine.

They were historically used in refrigeration, aerosol propellants and other applications.

Many ozone-depleting CFCs have been restricted internationally because of their effects on the ozone layer.

58. 🛡️ Protecting the Ozone Layer

  • 🚫 Reduce the release of ozone-depleting substances.
  • ♻️ Recover refrigerants during equipment disposal.
  • 🧪 Use suitable alternatives where possible.
  • 🌍 Follow international controls on ozone-depleting substances.

59. 🌫️ Ozone: Good and Bad

Stratospheric ozone Ground-level ozone
Helps absorb harmful UV radiation Air pollutant
Found mainly in the stratosphere Can form near Earth's surface
Protective role Can irritate the respiratory system

60. 🌍 Air as a Mixture

Air is a mixture because:

  • Its components are not chemically bonded to each other.
  • The gases retain their individual properties.
  • The composition can vary slightly depending on location.
  • The components can be separated by physical methods.

61. ❄️ Separation of Air

The main gases in air can be separated industrially by fractional distillation of liquid air.

The process depends mainly on the different boiling points of the gases.

62. 🧊 Making Air Liquid

Air can be converted into a liquid by cooling it to very low temperatures and applying pressure as appropriate.

Before fractional distillation, unwanted water vapour and carbon dioxide are removed because they would freeze at the low temperatures involved.

63. ♨️ Fractional Distillation of Liquid Air

Liquid air is warmed gradually. The gases boil off at different temperatures because they have different boiling points.

65. 🏭 Uses of Nitrogen

  • 🧪 Manufacture of ammonia and nitrogen compounds.
  • ❄️ Liquid nitrogen is used for cooling.
  • 📦 Used to provide an inert atmosphere in some processes.
  • 🏭 Used in food packaging to reduce oxidation.

66. 🏭 Uses of Oxygen

  • 🏥 Medical oxygen supplies.
  • 🔥 Oxy-fuel welding and cutting.
  • 🏭 Steel manufacture and other industrial processes.
  • 🚀 Used as an oxidiser in some rocket systems.

67. 🏭 Uses of Carbon Dioxide

  • 🥤 Carbonation of beverages.
  • 🧯 Fire extinguishers in suitable applications.
  • ❄️ Solid carbon dioxide, dry ice, is used for cooling.
  • 🌱 Used in controlled plant-growing environments.

68. 🏭 Uses of Argon

  • 💡 Lighting applications.
  • 🔧 Welding.
  • 🧪 Inert atmospheres in laboratories and industries.

69. 🧪 Air and Chemical Reactions

Oxygen and nitrogen from air participate in several chemical processes.

Reaction Word equation Symbol equation
Carbon combustion Carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide C + O₂ → CO₂
Sulfur combustion Sulfur + oxygen → sulfur dioxide S + O₂ → SO₂
Nitrogen oxide formation Nitrogen + oxygen → nitrogen monoxide N₂ + O₂ → 2NO
Carbon monoxide removal Carbon monoxide + nitrogen monoxide → carbon dioxide + nitrogen 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂

70. 🔥 Metals Reacting with Oxygen

Many metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides.

Word equation:

Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation:

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

71. 🔥 Non-metals Reacting with Oxygen

Several non-metals also react with oxygen.

Word equation:

Carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide

Symbol equation:

C + O₂ → CO₂

72. 🧪 Hydrogen Reacting with Oxygen

Hydrogen burns in oxygen to form water.

Word equation:

Hydrogen + oxygen → water

Symbol equation:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

73. 🌧️ Atmospheric Water and Humidity

Humidity describes the amount of water vapour present in the air.

Warm air can generally contain more water vapour than cold air before becoming saturated.

74. ☁️ Condensation in the Atmosphere

When moist air cools sufficiently, water vapour can condense into liquid droplets.

This process contributes to cloud formation and precipitation.

75. 🌍 Natural Balance of Atmospheric Gases

The concentrations of atmospheric gases are influenced by natural processes such as respiration, photosynthesis, decomposition, volcanic activity and the oceans.

Human activities can alter these natural balances, especially for greenhouse gases and pollutants.

76. 🌱 Photosynthesis and Respiration Balance

Process Effect on gases
Photosynthesis Uses CO₂ and releases O₂
Aerobic respiration Uses O₂ and releases CO₂
Combustion Generally consumes O₂ and can release CO₂
Decomposition Can release CO₂ and, under anaerobic conditions, methane

77. 🏭 Industrial Effects on the Atmosphere

Industrial activities can affect atmospheric composition by releasing gases and particles.

Good environmental management aims to reduce harmful emissions and improve air quality.

78. 🚗 Transport and Atmospheric Chemistry

Internal combustion engines can release carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, hydrocarbons and particulate matter.

Vehicle emission-control technologies help reduce some of these pollutants.

79. 🧪 Acidic Oxides

Some non-metal oxides react with water to form acids.

Examples include sulfur oxides and nitrogen oxides involved in atmospheric acid deposition.

Word equation:

Sulfur trioxide + water → sulfuric acid

Symbol equation:

SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄

80. 🪨 Acid Rain and Limestone

Acidic water can react with calcium carbonate in limestone.

Word equation:

Calcium carbonate + sulfuric acid → calcium sulfate + carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation:

CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + CO₂ + H₂O

Acid deposition can therefore contribute to the deterioration of limestone structures.

81. 🧪 Air Quality

Air quality describes how clean or polluted the air is.

Air quality can be affected by pollutant concentrations, weather conditions, geography and human activities.

82. 🌳 Ways to Improve Air Quality

  • 🌱 Plant and protect vegetation.
  • 🚍 Encourage public transport.
  • 🚲 Encourage walking and cycling where suitable.
  • 🏭 Control industrial emissions.
  • 🔥 Avoid unnecessary burning of waste.
  • ⚡ Improve energy efficiency.
  • 🌞 Increase cleaner energy sources.

83. 🔬 Practical: Testing Air for Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide in a gas sample can be tested by passing the sample through limewater.

Observation: Limewater becomes milky if sufficient carbon dioxide is present.

84. 🔬 Practical: Testing Oxygen

A gas suspected to contain oxygen can be tested using a glowing splint.

Observation: The glowing splint relights in oxygen.

87. ⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying air is a compound.
    ✅ Air is a mixture of gases.
  • ❌ Saying oxygen is a fuel.
    ✅ Oxygen supports combustion and acts as an oxidising gas.
  • ❌ Saying carbon dioxide supports combustion.
    ✅ Carbon dioxide generally does not support ordinary combustion.
  • ❌ Confusing carbon monoxide with carbon dioxide.
    ✅ CO is carbon monoxide; CO₂ is carbon dioxide.
  • ❌ Saying ozone is always harmful.
    ✅ Stratospheric ozone is protective, while ground-level ozone is a pollutant.
  • ❌ Saying complete combustion occurs with insufficient oxygen.
    ✅ Complete combustion requires a sufficient supply of oxygen.
  • ❌ Forgetting to balance atmospheric reaction equations.
    ✅ Check the number of atoms of every element on both sides.
