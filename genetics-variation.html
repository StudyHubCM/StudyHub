🧬 Genetics & Variation

Understand chromosomes, genes, DNA, alleles, genotype, phenotype, inheritance, genetic crosses, variation, mutation, natural selection and selective breeding.

1. 🧬 What is Genetics?

Genetics is the branch of biology that studies inheritance and variation.

It explains how characteristics are passed from parents to offspring and why offspring are not always genetically identical to their parents.

2. 👨‍👩‍👧 What is Inheritance?

Inheritance is the transmission of genetic information from parents to their offspring.

Genetic information is carried in DNA, which is organised into chromosomes.

3. 🧬 What is DNA?

DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid.

DNA is the molecule that carries genetic information in cells.

The information in DNA helps determine the characteristics of an organism.

5. 🧬 DNA Bases

DNA contains four different bases:

  • A — Adenine.
  • T — Thymine.
  • C — Cytosine.
  • G — Guanine.

Adenine pairs with thymine, while cytosine pairs with guanine.

🧬 Base pairing:

A ↔ T

C ↔ G

6. 🧬 What is a Gene?

A gene is a section of DNA that contains information for a particular characteristic or biological function.

Genes are found on chromosomes.

7. 🧬 What is an Allele?

An allele is an alternative form of a gene.

For example, a gene may control a characteristic such as flower colour, while different alleles may determine different colours.

8. 🧬 Chromosomes

Chromosomes are structures made mainly of DNA and associated proteins.

They are found in the nucleus of eukaryotic cells.

Chromosomes carry genes.

10. 🧬 Chromosome Number in Humans

Normal human body cells contain 46 chromosomes, arranged in 23 pairs.

One chromosome of each pair is inherited from the mother and the other from the father.

Human gametes contain 23 chromosomes.

11. 🧬 Diploid and Haploid Cells

Diploid cells contain pairs of chromosomes.

Most human body cells are diploid and contain 46 chromosomes.

Haploid cells contain one chromosome from each pair.

Human sperm and egg cells are haploid and contain 23 chromosomes.

12. 🧬 Genes and Chromosomes

Genes are sections of DNA located on chromosomes.

A chromosome therefore contains many genes.

🧬 DNA → Genes → Chromosomes → Genetic information

13. 🧬 What is a Characteristic?

A characteristic is a feature or trait of an organism that can be influenced by genes and, in many cases, the environment.

Examples include:

  • Eye colour.
  • Blood group.
  • Height.
  • Hair characteristics.
  • Ability to taste certain substances.

14. 🧬 Genotype

The genotype is the combination of alleles an organism has for a particular gene or characteristic.

Genotypes are commonly represented using letters.

For example:

  • BB
  • Bb
  • bb

15. 👀 Phenotype

The phenotype is the observable characteristic of an organism.

A phenotype results from the interaction between genetic information and environmental factors.

Examples include the appearance of a plant, blood group or measured height.

16. ⚖️ Genotype vs Phenotype

Genotype Phenotype
The genetic makeup for a characteristic. The observable characteristic.
Represented using alleles such as BB, Bb or bb. Described using words such as tall or short.
Cannot normally be observed directly. Can usually be observed or measured.

17. 🧬 Homozygous

An organism is homozygous for a gene when it has two identical alleles.

Examples:

  • BB — homozygous dominant.
  • bb — homozygous recessive.

18. 🧬 Heterozygous

An organism is heterozygous when it has two different alleles for a gene.

Example:

Bb

19. 🧬 Dominant Allele

A dominant allele is an allele whose effect is expressed in the phenotype when at least one copy is present, in the simple Mendelian inheritance model.

It is usually represented by a capital letter.

Example:

B

20. 🧬 Recessive Allele

A recessive allele is an allele whose effect is expressed in the phenotype only when two copies are present, in a simple Mendelian inheritance model.

It is usually represented by a lowercase letter.

Example:

b

21. 🧬 Dominant vs Recessive

Dominant Recessive
Usually represented by a capital letter. Usually represented by a lowercase letter.
Can be expressed in a heterozygous genotype. Usually expressed only when homozygous.
Example: B. Example: b.

22. 🧬 Genetic Terminology Example

Suppose B represents an allele for black fur and b represents an allele for white fur.

BB = homozygous black genotype.

Bb = heterozygous black genotype.

bb = homozygous white genotype.

23. 🧬 Example 1 — Genotype and Phenotype

In pea plants, suppose T represents tall and t represents short, with T dominant.

TT → Tall phenotype.

Tt → Tall phenotype.

tt → Short phenotype.

24. 🧬 Example 2 — Black and White Fur

Suppose B represents black fur and b represents white fur, with black dominant.

BB → Black fur phenotype.

Bb → Black fur phenotype.

bb → White fur phenotype.

25. 🧬 Example 3 — Flower Colour

Suppose R represents red flowers and r represents white flowers, with red dominant.

RR → Red flowers.

Rr → Red flowers.

rr → White flowers.

26. 🧬 Example 4 — Seed Shape

Suppose S represents smooth seeds and s represents wrinkled seeds, with smooth dominant.

SS → Smooth seeds.

Ss → Smooth seeds.

ss → Wrinkled seeds.

27. 🧬 Example 5 — Plant Height

Suppose H represents tall plants and h represents short plants.

HH → Tall.

Hh → Tall.

hh → Short.

28. 🧬 Example 6 — Coat Colour

Suppose C represents brown coat colour and c represents cream coat colour.

CC → Brown.

Cc → Brown.

cc → Cream.

29. 🧬 Example 7 — Leaf Colour

Suppose G represents green leaves and g represents yellow leaves, with green dominant.

GG → Green leaves.

Gg → Green leaves.

gg → Yellow leaves.

30. 🧬 Example 8 — Genetic Carrier

A person with one dominant allele and one recessive allele has a heterozygous genotype.

If the recessive allele is associated with a genetic condition, the heterozygous person may be described as a carrier if they do not show the condition but can pass the recessive allele to their children.

Example: Aa → carrier of recessive allele a in a simple inheritance example.

31. 🧬 Why Can Different Genotypes Have the Same Phenotype?

When one allele is dominant over another, a homozygous dominant genotype and a heterozygous genotype can produce the same phenotype.

For example:

BB → black

Bb → black

The genotypes are different, but the phenotype is the same.

32. 🧬 Genetic Crosses

A genetic cross is used to predict possible genotypes and phenotypes of offspring produced by particular parents.

A Punnett square is commonly used to show possible combinations of alleles.

33. 🧬 Steps for Solving a Genetic Cross

  1. Identify the alleles involved.
  2. State which allele is dominant and which is recessive.
  3. Write the genotype of each parent.
  4. Determine the gametes produced by each parent.
  5. Draw the genetic cross or Punnett square.
  6. Determine the possible offspring genotypes.
  7. Determine the possible offspring phenotypes.
  8. Give the genotype and phenotype ratios if required.

34. 🧬 Example 9 — Homozygous Dominant × Homozygous Recessive

Let T = tall and t = short.

Cross:

TT × tt

T T
t Tt Tt
t Tt Tt

Genotype ratio: 100% Tt.

Phenotype ratio: 100% tall.

35. 🧬 Example 10 — Heterozygous × Heterozygous

Let T = tall and t = short.

Cross:

Tt × Tt

T t
T TT Tt
t Tt tt

Genotype ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt.

Phenotype ratio: 3 tall : 1 short.

36. 🧬 Example 11 — Heterozygous × Recessive

Let B = black and b = white.

Cross:

Bb × bb

B b
b Bb bb
b Bb bb

Genotype ratio: 1 Bb : 1 bb.

Phenotype ratio: 1 black : 1 white.

37. 🧬 Example 12 — Homozygous Dominant × Heterozygous

Let R = red and r = white.

Cross:

RR × Rr

R R
R RR RR
r Rr Rr

Genotype ratio: 1 RR : 1 Rr.

Phenotype: 100% red.

38. 🧬 Example 13 — Identifying an Unknown Genotype

Suppose a plant has a dominant phenotype but its genotype is unknown.

It could be either:

  • Homozygous dominant: TT.
  • Heterozygous: Tt.

A test cross can be used by crossing the individual with a homozygous recessive individual.

If the unknown plant is TT, all offspring will be tall.

If it is Tt, some offspring may be short.

40. 🧬 Probability in Genetic Crosses

A genetic cross can be used to calculate the probability of an offspring having a particular genotype or phenotype.

In a Tt × Tt cross:

  • Probability of TT = 1/4.
  • Probability of Tt = 2/4 = 1/2.
  • Probability of tt = 1/4.

Therefore, the probability of the recessive phenotype is 1/4 or 25%.

41. 🧬 Sex Determination in Humans

Humans have a pair of sex chromosomes.

Females normally have XX chromosomes.

Males normally have XY chromosomes.

44. 🧬 What is Variation?

Variation refers to differences in characteristics between individuals of the same species.

Variation means that members of the same species are not necessarily genetically identical or phenotypically identical.

45. 🌍 Causes of Variation

Variation can be caused by:

  • 🧬 Genetic differences.
  • 🌱 Environmental differences.
  • 🔄 A combination of genetic and environmental factors.

46. 🧬 Genetic Variation

Genetic variation results from differences in genetic information between individuals.

Important sources include:

  • Mutation.
  • Meiosis and independent assortment.
  • Crossing over during meiosis.
  • Random fertilisation.

47. 🌱 Environmental Variation

Environmental variation results from differences in the conditions in which organisms live.

Examples include:

  • Amount of food available.
  • Temperature.
  • Amount of sunlight.
  • Exercise.
  • Mineral availability in soil.

48. 🧬 Genetic vs Environmental Variation

Genetic Variation Environmental Variation
Caused by differences in genetic information. Caused by differences in environmental conditions.
Can be inherited if the genetic change is present in reproductive cells. Usually cannot be inherited.
Examples include blood group differences. Examples include differences caused by diet or exercise.

49. 🧬 Continuous Variation

Continuous variation occurs when characteristics show a range of values with many possible intermediate forms.

Examples include:

  • Height.
  • Body mass.
  • Hand span.

Continuous variation is often influenced by many genes and the environment.

50. 🧬 Discontinuous Variation

Discontinuous variation produces distinct categories rather than a continuous range.

Examples include:

  • ABO blood group.
  • Some inherited genetic conditions.

Discontinuous variation is often mainly determined by genes.

51. 📊 Continuous vs Discontinuous Variation

Continuous Discontinuous
Shows a range of values. Shows distinct categories.
Often influenced by many genes and environment. Often mainly controlled by genes.
Examples: height and body mass. Example: ABO blood group.

53. 🧬 What is a Mutation?

A mutation is a change in the genetic material of an organism.

Mutations can occur spontaneously or can be caused by mutagenic agents.

54. ⚡ Causes of Mutation

Factors that can increase mutation rates include:

  • ☢️ Ionising radiation.
  • ☀️ Ultraviolet radiation.
  • 🧪 Certain chemicals.

55. 🧬 Effects of Mutations

A mutation may have:

  • Little or no noticeable effect.
  • A harmful effect.
  • A beneficial effect.

The effect depends on the gene involved and the environment.

56. 🧬 Mutations and Genetic Variation

Mutations create new versions of genes and therefore are an important source of genetic variation.

If a mutation occurs in a reproductive cell or its precursor, it may be passed to offspring.

57. 🧬 Mutation and Cancer

Some mutations can cause cells to divide uncontrollably.

This can contribute to the development of cancer.

58. 🧬 Meiosis and Variation

Meiosis is a type of cell division that produces haploid gametes.

Meiosis contributes to genetic variation through the production of genetically different gametes.

59. 🧬 Meiosis Diagram

🧬 Meiosis Diagram — PLACE DIAGRAM HERE

Know that meiosis produces cells with half the chromosome number and contributes to genetic variation.

60. 🧬 Random Fertilisation

During sexual reproduction, any one of many sperm cells may fertilise an egg cell.

This random combination of genetic material contributes to variation among offspring.

61. 🧬 Why Sexual Reproduction Produces Variation

  • Different gametes contain different combinations of alleles.
  • Meiosis produces genetically varied gametes.
  • Fertilisation combines genetic material from two parents.
  • Mutations can introduce new alleles.

62. 🌳 Natural Selection

Natural selection is the process by which organisms with advantageous inherited characteristics are more likely to survive and reproduce.

Over many generations, advantageous alleles may become more common in a population.

63. 🌱 Steps of Natural Selection

  1. Individuals in a population show variation.
  2. Some variation is caused by genetic differences.
  3. Organisms compete for resources and face environmental pressures.
  4. Individuals with advantageous inherited characteristics are more likely to survive and reproduce.
  5. These individuals pass their alleles to their offspring.
  6. Over many generations, the advantageous alleles may become more common in the population.

64. 🦠 Example of Natural Selection — Antibiotic Resistance

A bacterial population may contain genetic variation.

If some bacteria have a mutation that makes them resistant to an antibiotic, those bacteria may survive treatment while susceptible bacteria die.

The resistant bacteria reproduce and pass the resistance allele to their offspring.

Over generations, antibiotic-resistant bacteria can become more common.

65. 🦋 Example of Natural Selection — Camouflage

Suppose a population of insects contains variation in body colour.

Birds may find brightly coloured insects more easily than insects that blend into their surroundings.

Better-camouflaged insects may survive and reproduce more successfully.

Over generations, the allele associated with better camouflage may become more common.

66. 🌍 Adaptation

An adaptation is an inherited feature that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment.

Adaptations may be structural, physiological or behavioural.

67. 🧬 Adaptation and Natural Selection

Natural selection can cause advantageous inherited characteristics to become more common in a population over many generations.

This process can result in populations becoming better adapted to their environments.

68. 🌾 Selective Breeding

Selective breeding, also called artificial selection, is the process of choosing organisms with desired characteristics and breeding them to produce offspring with those characteristics.

69. 🐄 Selective Breeding in Animals

Farmers may select animals with desirable characteristics such as:

  • High milk production.
  • Fast growth.
  • Large meat yield.
  • Disease resistance.

70. 🌽 Selective Breeding in Plants

Plant breeders may select plants with characteristics such as:

  • High crop yield.
  • Large fruits.
  • Disease resistance.
  • Drought tolerance.
  • Good nutritional value.

71. ⚖️ Natural Selection vs Selective Breeding

Natural Selection Selective Breeding
Environment determines which characteristics give an advantage. Humans choose organisms with desired characteristics.
Occurs naturally in populations. Controlled by humans.
Can lead to adaptation. Used to improve crops and livestock.

72. 🧬 Advantages of Selective Breeding

  • Increased crop yield.
  • Improved food quality.
  • Greater milk or meat production.
  • Improved disease resistance.
  • Improved resistance to environmental conditions.

73. ⚠️ Disadvantages of Selective Breeding

  • Reduces genetic variation in a population.
  • Closely related organisms may be repeatedly bred.
  • Inbreeding can increase the chance of harmful recessive alleles being expressed.
  • Populations with low genetic variation may be less able to respond to environmental change or new diseases.

74. 🧬 Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering involves deliberately changing the genetic material of an organism.

A gene with a desired characteristic can be introduced into another organism using biotechnology.

75. 🧫 Genetically Modified Organisms

A genetically modified organism (GMO) is an organism whose genetic material has been deliberately altered using genetic engineering.

Examples of possible applications include crops with improved pest resistance or nutritional characteristics.

76. 🧬 Genetic Engineering vs Selective Breeding

Genetic Engineering Selective Breeding
Genetic material is deliberately altered. Organisms with desired characteristics are selected for breeding.
Can introduce a specific gene or modify genetic material directly. Relies on existing variation and reproduction.
Can be relatively precise for a particular gene. Usually takes several generations.

77. 🧬 Inherited Conditions

Some conditions are caused by particular alleles inherited from parents.

Examples include some forms of:

  • Sickle-cell disease.
  • Cystic fibrosis.
  • Haemophilia.

The inheritance pattern depends on the particular condition.

78. 🩸 Sickle-Cell Disease

Sickle-cell disease is an inherited condition involving haemoglobin.

Affected red blood cells can have an abnormal shape, which can interfere with normal blood flow and oxygen transport.

The condition is caused by genetic changes affecting haemoglobin.

79. 🧬 Carriers and Recessive Conditions

For a simple recessive inherited condition, a person with one normal allele and one recessive disease allele may be a carrier.

Two carriers can have an affected child if the child inherits the recessive allele from both parents.

80. 🧬 Example 14 — Recessive Inheritance

Let A represent a normal allele and a represent a recessive disease allele.

Two carriers:

Aa × Aa

A a
A AA Aa
a Aa aa

Genotype ratio: 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa.

Carrier genotype: Aa.

Recessive affected genotype: aa.

In this simplified model, the probability of an affected child is 1/4 or 25%.

A pedigree diagram is used to show the inheritance of a characteristic through several generations of a family.

Common symbols include:

  • □ Male.
  • ○ Female.
  • Shaded symbol — individual showing the characteristic being studied.

83. 🧬 Family Inheritance

A genetic characteristic can be passed from parents to offspring through alleles carried in gametes.

Each child receives one allele for a gene from the mother and one allele from the father.

84. 🧬 Why Offspring Are Not Genetically Identical

In sexual reproduction, offspring inherit combinations of alleles from both parents.

Meiosis creates genetically different gametes and fertilisation combines gametes randomly.

Therefore, siblings can have different genotypes and phenotypes.

85. 🧬 Genetic vs Acquired Characteristics

Genetic Characteristic Acquired / Environmental Characteristic
Influenced by inherited genetic information. Develops mainly because of environmental conditions or experiences.
Can be inherited if controlled by genes. Usually cannot be passed genetically to offspring.
Example: ABO blood group. Example: muscle development from exercise.

86. 🧬 Phenotype Can Be Affected by Environment

Some characteristics depend on both genotype and environment.

For example, a person's height is influenced by genes but also by nutrition and health during development.

🧬 Phenotype = genetic information + environmental influence

This is a simplified way of remembering that both factors can contribute to an observed characteristic.

88. 🧠 How to Read a Genetics Question

  1. Find the characteristic being inherited.
  2. Identify the dominant and recessive alleles.
  3. Choose suitable symbols for the alleles.
  4. Write the parents' genotypes.
  5. Determine their gametes.
  6. Complete the genetic cross.
  7. State the offspring genotypes.
  8. State the offspring phenotypes.
  9. Give ratios or probabilities where required.

89. ❌ Common Genetics Mistakes

  • ❌ Confusing genotype with phenotype.
  • ❌ Calling Bb homozygous.
  • ❌ Calling BB heterozygous.
  • ❌ Forgetting that a recessive phenotype normally requires two recessive alleles in a simple Mendelian example.
  • ❌ Writing two alleles into a gamete. A gamete carries one allele of each gene.
  • ❌ Mixing up genotype ratio and phenotype ratio.
  • ❌ Saying dominant means "stronger" or "better".
  • ❌ Saying a dominant allele is always more common in a population.
  • ❌ Saying mutations are always harmful.
  • ❌ Saying all variation is inherited.
  • ❌ Saying natural selection occurs because organisms deliberately change themselves.
  • ❌ Confusing natural selection with selective breeding.
  • ❌ Forgetting that human gametes contain 23 chromosomes.
  • ❌ Saying the mother determines whether the child is XX or XY.

91. 📝 O/L Genetics Question Strategy

🧬 If asked for a genotype:

Give the allele combination, for example: Tt.

👀 If asked for a phenotype:

Give the observable characteristic, for example: tall.

🧬 If asked for a genetic cross:

  1. Write the parental genotypes.
  2. Write the gametes.
  3. Draw the Punnett square.
  4. Write offspring genotypes.
  5. Convert them into phenotypes.
  6. Give the ratio or probability.

🌳 If asked about natural selection:
