BIOLOGY • O/L 0510

🧠 Coordination & Response

Learn how organisms detect changes, coordinate responses and respond to their environment.

1. 🧠 What is Coordination?

Coordination is the process by which different parts of an organism work together so that the organism can respond appropriately to changes inside and outside the body.

📌 Key Definition

Coordination allows an organism to detect a change, process the information and produce an appropriate response.

2. 🌍 What is a Stimulus?

A stimulus is a change in the internal or external environment that can be detected by an organism.

Examples include:

  • 💡 Change in light intensity
  • 🌡️ Change in temperature
  • 🔊 Sound
  • 👃 Chemicals producing smells
  • 🤚 Touch or pressure
  • ⚠️ Pain

3. 🎯 Response

A response is the reaction of an organism to a stimulus.

🔄 Stimulus → Response

A stimulus is detected by a receptor. Information is passed through a coordination system, which produces a response by an effector.

4. 🔗 The Coordination Pathway

A typical response follows this sequence:

  1. 🌍 A stimulus occurs.
  2. 👁️ A receptor detects the stimulus.
  3. 🧠 Information is transmitted to a coordination centre.
  4. 📡 Information is sent to an effector.
  5. 💪 The effector produces a response.

🧠 Remember

Stimulus → Receptor → Coordinator → Effector → Response

5. 👁️ Receptors

Receptors are specialised cells or groups of cells that detect particular stimuli.

Receptor Stimulus Detected Example
👁️ Photoreceptors Light Cells in the retina
👂 Receptors Sound/vibrations Cells in the inner ear
👃 Chemoreceptors Chemicals Nose and tongue
🤚 Touch receptors Pressure/touch Skin
🌡️ Temperature receptors Temperature changes Skin

6. ⚡ Nervous Coordination

The nervous system provides rapid coordination in animals. It uses electrical impulses travelling along specialised cells called neurones.

⚡ Main Features

  • Very fast
  • Produces precise responses
  • Usually has a short-lasting effect
  • Uses electrical impulses along neurones

7. 🧬 Neurones

A neurone is a specialised nerve cell that carries electrical impulses around the nervous system.

Three important types are:

  • 🔵 Sensory neurones
  • 🟢 Relay neurones
  • 🔴 Motor neurones

8. 🔵 Sensory Neurones

Sensory neurones carry electrical impulses from receptors to the central nervous system.

📌 Exam Point

Sensory neurone: receptor → central nervous system.

9. 🟢 Relay Neurones

Relay neurones are found mainly in the central nervous system. They connect sensory neurones to motor neurones.

🔗 Pathway

Sensory neurone → Relay neurone → Motor neurone

10. 🔴 Motor Neurones

Motor neurones carry electrical impulses from the central nervous system to effectors such as muscles and glands.

📌 Exam Point

Motor neurone: central nervous system → effector.

11. 🧩 Structure of a Neurone

Important parts of a neurone include:

  • Cell body — contains the nucleus.
  • Dendrites — receive signals from other cells.
  • Axon — carries the electrical impulse away from the cell body.
  • Myelin sheath — insulating layer around some axons.
  • Axon terminals — form connections with other cells.

12. 🧠 Central Nervous System

The central nervous system (CNS) consists of:

  • 🧠 Brain
  • 🦴 Spinal cord

The CNS receives information, processes it and coordinates appropriate responses.

13. 🕸️ Peripheral Nervous System

The peripheral nervous system consists mainly of nerves that connect the central nervous system to receptors and effectors throughout the body.

🧠 Remember

CNS = brain + spinal cord.

14. 🧠 The Brain

The brain is the main coordination centre of the nervous system. It receives information, interprets it and coordinates responses.

Major regions include:

  • 🧠 Cerebrum
  • ⚖️ Cerebellum
  • ❤️ Medulla oblongata

15. 🧠 Cerebrum

The cerebrum is involved in conscious activities such as thinking, learning, memory, intelligence, interpreting sensory information and voluntary movement.

📌 Exam Focus

The cerebrum is responsible for many conscious and voluntary activities.

16. ⚖️ Cerebellum

The cerebellum coordinates muscular activity and helps maintain balance and posture.

  • ⚖️ Balance
  • 🤸 Coordination of movement
  • 🧍 Posture

17. ❤️ Medulla Oblongata

The medulla oblongata controls many involuntary activities, including breathing and heart rate.

📌 Important

Involuntary means the activity does not normally require conscious control.

18. 🦴 Spinal Cord

The spinal cord carries impulses between the brain and the rest of the body. It also acts as a coordination centre for many reflex actions.

19. ⚡ Electrical Impulses

Nerve impulses are electrical signals that travel along neurones. They allow information to be transmitted rapidly through the nervous system.

⚡ Key Point

Electrical impulses allow nervous responses to occur quickly.

20. 🔗 Synapses

A synapse is the junction between two neurones or between a neurone and another cell.

When an electrical impulse reaches the end of a neurone, chemicals called neurotransmitters are released. These chemicals cross the small gap and stimulate the next cell.

21. 🚨 Reflex Actions

A reflex action is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus.

Examples include:

  • 🔥 Pulling your hand away from a hot object
  • 💡 Changes in pupil size
  • 👁️ Blinking
  • 🦵 Knee-jerk response

22. ⚡ Reflex Arc

The pathway followed by an impulse during a reflex action is called a reflex arc.

🔄 Reflex Arc Pathway

Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → Relay neurone → Motor neurone → Effector → Response

23. 🛡️ Importance of Reflex Actions

Reflex actions help protect the body from potentially harmful stimuli.

  • ⚡ They are rapid.
  • 🛡️ They reduce the chance of injury.
  • 🧠 They are automatic.
  • 🔥 They allow rapid responses to danger.

24. 🧠 Voluntary and Involuntary Actions

Voluntary Action Involuntary Action
Under conscious control Not normally under conscious control
Example: walking Example: heartbeat
Example: writing Example: breathing rhythm

25. 🧠 Reflex vs Voluntary Response

Reflex Response Voluntary Response
Rapid Usually slower
Automatic Consciously controlled
Protective in many cases Often involves conscious decision-making

26. 👁️ The Eye

The eye is a sense organ containing receptors that detect light. It allows animals to detect the direction, intensity and colour of light.

Important parts include:

  • 👁️ Cornea
  • 🟤 Iris
  • ⚫ Pupil
  • 🔍 Lens
  • 🧠 Retina
  • 📡 Optic nerve
  • 💧 Aqueous and vitreous humour

27. 👁️ Cornea

The cornea is the transparent front surface of the eye. It refracts incoming light and helps focus it towards the retina.

28. 🟤 Iris and ⚫ Pupil

The iris is the coloured part of the eye surrounding the pupil. It controls the amount of light entering the eye.

The pupil is the opening through which light enters the eye.

📌 Important Difference

Iris = controls the pupil.
Pupil = opening through which light enters.

29. 🔍 Lens

The lens refracts light and changes shape to focus light onto the retina.

30. 🧠 Retina

The retina contains light-sensitive receptor cells. Light is converted into nerve impulses that are transmitted to the brain through the optic nerve.

31. 📡 Optic Nerve

The optic nerve carries electrical impulses from the retina to the brain.

32. ☀️ Response of the Pupil to Light

The pupil changes size according to the intensity of light.

  • ☀️ In bright light, the pupil becomes smaller.
  • 🌙 In dim light, the pupil becomes larger.

🛡️ Why?

The pupil becomes smaller in bright light to reduce the amount of light entering the eye and protect the retina.

33. 🌞 Bright-Light Reflex

In bright light, receptors in the retina detect the increase in light intensity. Nerve impulses are sent to the brain, which coordinates a response causing the circular muscles of the iris to contract and the pupil to become smaller.

34. 🌙 Dim-Light Response

In dim light, the pupil becomes larger so that more light can enter the eye. This allows more light to reach the retina.

35. 🔍 Accommodation

Accommodation is the change in the shape of the lens to focus objects at different distances on the retina.

📌 Exam Definition

Accommodation is the process by which the lens changes shape to focus light from objects at different distances onto the retina.

36. 👀 Viewing a Near Object

When focusing on a near object:

  1. The ciliary muscles contract.
  2. The suspensory ligaments become less tight.
  3. The lens becomes thicker and more rounded.
  4. The lens refracts light more strongly.

37. 🌄 Viewing a Distant Object

When focusing on a distant object:

  1. The ciliary muscles relax.
  2. The suspensory ligaments become tighter.
  3. The lens becomes thinner.
  4. The lens refracts light less strongly.

38. 👁️ Accommodation Summary

Near Object Distant Object
Ciliary muscles contract Ciliary muscles relax
Suspensory ligaments loosen Suspensory ligaments tighten
Lens becomes thicker Lens becomes thinner
Greater refraction Less refraction

39. 👂 The Ear

The ear is involved in hearing and maintaining balance.

Important structures include:

  • 👂 Pinna
  • 🌀 Ear canal
  • 🥁 Eardrum
  • 🔗 Ossicles
  • 🐚 Cochlea
  • ⚖️ Semicircular canals
  • 📡 Auditory nerve

40. 🔊 Hearing

The basic pathway of sound is:

🔊 Sound Pathway

Pinna → Ear canal → Eardrum → Ossicles → Cochlea → Auditory nerve → Brain

41. 🥁 Eardrum and Ossicles

The eardrum vibrates when sound waves reach it.

The three small bones called the ossicles transmit and amplify vibrations towards the inner ear.

42. 🐚 Cochlea

The cochlea contains receptor cells that detect vibrations and convert them into electrical nerve impulses.

43. ⚖️ Balance

Structures in the inner ear help detect movement and maintain balance. The semicircular canals detect changes associated with movement of the head.

44. 🌱 Coordination in Plants

Plants do not have a nervous system like animals. However, they respond to environmental stimuli using growth responses and plant hormones.

Examples include responses to:

  • 💡 Light
  • 🌍 Gravity
  • 💧 Water
  • 🤝 Contact or touch

45. 🌱 Tropisms

A tropism is a growth response of a plant in which the direction of growth is determined by the direction of the stimulus.

📌 Important

Growth towards a stimulus is called a positive tropism. Growth away from a stimulus is called a negative tropism.

46. 💡 Phototropism

Phototropism is the growth response of a plant to light.

Shoots usually show positive phototropism because they grow towards light.

Roots usually show negative phototropism because they tend to grow away from light.

47. 🌍 Geotropism

Geotropism, also called gravitropism, is the growth response of a plant to gravity.

  • 🌱 Roots usually show positive geotropism.
  • 🌿 Shoots usually show negative geotropism.

48. 🧪 Auxin

Auxin is a plant hormone involved in controlling growth, particularly the growth of shoots.

Auxin is produced mainly in growing regions such as the shoot tip.

49. 🌿 How Shoots Bend Towards Light

When light comes mainly from one side, auxin becomes distributed more towards the shaded side of the shoot.

In shoots, auxin stimulates cell elongation. Cells on the shaded side therefore elongate more, causing the shoot to bend towards the light.

💡 Sequence to Remember

One-sided light → auxin becomes unevenly distributed → greater elongation on shaded side → shoot bends towards light.

50. 🌱 Importance of Tropisms

Tropisms help plants obtain the resources they need for survival.

  • 💡 Shoots grow towards light for photosynthesis.
  • 💧 Roots grow towards sources of water.
  • 🧂 Roots grow through soil where mineral ions can be obtained.
  • ⚓ Roots growing downwards help anchor the plant.

51. 🧪 Investigating Phototropism

Phototropism can be investigated by growing young seedlings under controlled conditions where light comes from different directions.

🔬 Experimental Variables

  • Independent variable: direction/source of light.
  • Dependent variable: direction or amount of shoot growth.
  • Control variables: water, temperature, plant type, soil and time.

52. 🧠 Nervous vs Hormonal Coordination

Nervous Coordination Hormonal Coordination
Uses electrical impulses and neurotransmitters Uses chemical hormones
Usually very fast Usually slower
Effects often short-lived Effects can last longer
Usually acts on specific effectors Hormones are transported to target tissues

53. 🔄 Complete Coordination Pathway

🧠 Master Summary

Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → CNS → Motor neurone → Effector → Response

In a reflex action, a relay neurone in the CNS commonly connects the sensory neurone to the motor neurone.

54. 📖 Important Definitions to Know

  • Stimulus: a change in the environment detected by a receptor.
  • Response: a reaction to a stimulus.
  • Receptor: a specialised cell or group of cells that detects a stimulus.
  • Effector: a muscle or gland that produces a response.
  • Neurone: a specialised nerve cell that carries electrical impulses.
  • CNS: the brain and spinal cord.
  • Reflex action: a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus.
  • Reflex arc: the pathway followed by impulses during a reflex action.
  • Synapse: a junction between neurones or between a neurone and another cell.
  • Accommodation: change in lens shape to focus objects at different distances.
  • Tropism: a directional growth response of a plant to a stimulus.
  • Phototropism: growth response to light.
  • Geotropism: growth response to gravity.
  • Auxin: a plant hormone involved in growth responses.

56. ❌ Common O/L Mistakes

⚠️ Avoid These Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying the pupil controls the amount of light instead of the iris.
  • ❌ Saying the lens is the light-sensitive part of the eye.
  • ❌ Confusing sensory neurones with motor neurones.
  • ❌ Saying the spinal cord is part of the peripheral nervous system.
  • ❌ Confusing a stimulus with a response.
  • ❌ Saying reflex actions are slow because they do not involve the brain.
  • ❌ Forgetting that reflex actions can involve the spinal cord.
  • ❌ Confusing phototropism with geotropism.
  • ❌ Forgetting that shoot growth towards light is positive phototropism.
  • ❌ Saying roots grow towards light.
  • ❌ Mixing up ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments during accommodation.
