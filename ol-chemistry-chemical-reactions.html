⚗️ Chemical Reactions

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Complete notes on chemical reactions, reaction types, energy changes, rates of reaction, catalysts, reversible reactions, oxidation and reduction, and how to represent chemical changes using equations.

1. ⚗️ Introduction to Chemical Reactions

A chemical reaction is a process in which one or more substances are changed into new substances with different chemical properties.

The substances present before a reaction are called reactants, while the new substances formed are called products.

💡 Example

When magnesium burns in oxygen, magnesium oxide is formed.

Word equation: Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation: 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)

2. 🔬 Physical Change vs Chemical Change

Not every change in matter is a chemical reaction.

Physical change Chemical change
No new substance is formed. New substance or substances are formed.
Usually changes state, shape or appearance. Changes chemical composition.
Often reversible. Often difficult to reverse by physical means.
Example: melting ice. Example: iron rusting.

3. 👀 Signs of a Chemical Reaction

Evidence that a chemical reaction may have occurred includes:

  • 🌡️ A temperature change.
  • 💡 Production of light.
  • 🫧 Formation of a gas.
  • 🎨 A permanent colour change.
  • ⚪ Formation of a precipitate.
  • 👃 Formation of a new substance with a different smell.

These observations provide evidence of chemical change, but individual observations should be interpreted carefully.

4. 🧪 Reactants and Products

In a chemical equation, reactants are written on the left and products are written on the right.

Word equation: Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

The arrow means that the reactants are changed into the products under the stated conditions.

5. 📝 Word Equations

A word equation represents a chemical reaction using the names of the substances involved.

Word equation: Zinc + hydrochloric acid → zinc chloride + hydrogen

Word equations are useful for understanding which substances are reacting and which substances are produced.

6. 🔣 Symbol Equations

A symbol equation represents a chemical reaction using chemical symbols and formulae.

Word equation: Zinc + hydrochloric acid → zinc chloride + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)

A correct symbol equation must have the correct formulae and must be balanced.

7. ⚖️ Conservation of Mass

The law of conservation of mass states that matter is not created or destroyed during a chemical reaction.

Therefore, the total number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides of a balanced equation.

8. ⚖️ Balancing Chemical Equations

A chemical equation is balanced by placing coefficients in front of formulae.

Never change the subscripts inside a chemical formula when balancing an equation because that would change the substance itself.

Unbalanced starting equation: Mg + O₂ → MgO

Balanced word equation: Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Balanced symbol equation: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

9. 🧮 How to Balance an Equation

  1. Write the correct formula for every reactant and product.
  2. Count the atoms of each element on both sides.
  3. Add coefficients in front of formulae.
  4. Do not change subscripts.
  5. Check every element again.
  6. Use the smallest whole-number coefficients possible.

10. 🔥 Combination Reactions

A combination reaction occurs when two or more substances combine to form one main product.

Word equation: Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation: 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)

This is also a combustion reaction because magnesium reacts with oxygen.

11. 🔥 Combustion

Combustion is a chemical reaction in which a substance reacts with oxygen, usually releasing energy as heat and often light.

Word equation: Carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)

12. 🔥 Complete Combustion of Hydrocarbons

A hydrocarbon contains only carbon and hydrogen. Complete combustion occurs when there is sufficient oxygen.

The products are carbon dioxide and water.

Word equation: Methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation: CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l)

13. 🌫️ Incomplete Combustion

When a hydrocarbon burns with insufficient oxygen, incomplete combustion may occur.

Carbon monoxide and/or carbon may be formed instead of only carbon dioxide.

Word equation: Methane + oxygen → carbon monoxide + water

Symbol equation: 2CH₄(g) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO(g) + 4H₂O(l)

⚠️ Safety

Carbon monoxide is a poisonous gas. Incomplete combustion should therefore be taken seriously in enclosed spaces.

14. 🔥 Combustion of Propane

Word equation: Propane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation: C₃H₈(g) + 5O₂(g) → 3CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(l)

15. 🧪 Displacement Reactions

A displacement reaction occurs when a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.

Word equation: Zinc + copper(II) sulfate → zinc sulfate + copper

Symbol equation: Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)

16. 🪙 Metal Reactivity and Displacement

A metal can displace another metal from its compound if it is more reactive than the metal being displaced.

For example, zinc is more reactive than copper, so zinc can displace copper from copper(II) sulfate.

Word equation: Zinc + copper(II) sulfate → zinc sulfate + copper

Symbol equation: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

17. 🧪 Metal + Acid Reactions

Reactive metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series can react with dilute acids to produce a salt and hydrogen gas.

Word equation: Magnesium + hydrochloric acid → magnesium chloride + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)

18. 🧪 Acid + Metal Oxide

Metal oxides are generally basic oxides and can react with acids.

Word equation: Copper(II) oxide + sulfuric acid → copper(II) sulfate + water

Symbol equation: CuO(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → CuSO₄(aq) + H₂O(l)

19. 🧪 Acid + Metal Hydroxide

Acids react with metal hydroxides in neutralisation reactions.

Word equation: Hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water

Symbol equation: HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

20. 🫧 Acid + Carbonate

Acids react with carbonates to produce a salt, water and carbon dioxide.

Word equation: Hydrochloric acid + calcium carbonate → calcium chloride + water + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: 2HCl(aq) + CaCO₃(s) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

21. 🧪 Thermal Decomposition

Thermal decomposition is the breakdown of a compound into simpler substances when it is heated.

Word equation: Calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g)

22. 🔥 Thermal Decomposition of Copper(II) Carbonate

Word equation: Copper(II) carbonate → copper(II) oxide + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: CuCO₃(s) → CuO(s) + CO₂(g)

The reaction produces a black solid, copper(II) oxide, and carbon dioxide gas.

23. 🧪 Decomposition Reactions

A decomposition reaction occurs when one compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances.

Word equation: Hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen

Symbol equation: 2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g)

This decomposition can be accelerated by a catalyst such as manganese(IV) oxide.

24. 🔄 Double Displacement Reactions

In a double displacement reaction, ions from two compounds exchange partners to form new compounds.

A common example is precipitation.

Word equation: Silver nitrate + sodium chloride → silver chloride + sodium nitrate

Symbol equation: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq)

25. ⚪ Precipitation Reactions

A precipitation reaction occurs when an insoluble solid forms from solutions.

Word equation: Barium nitrate + sodium sulfate → barium sulfate + sodium nitrate

Symbol equation: Ba(NO₃)₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaNO₃(aq)

The insoluble barium sulfate forms a white precipitate.

26. 🧪 Neutralisation Reactions

Neutralisation is the reaction between an acid and a base to produce salt and water.

Word equation: Nitric acid + potassium hydroxide → potassium nitrate + water

Symbol equation: HNO₃(aq) + KOH(aq) → KNO₃(aq) + H₂O(l)

27. ⚡ Ionic Equation for Neutralisation

The essential chemical change during neutralisation can be represented using an ionic equation.

Word representation: Hydrogen ions + hydroxide ions → water

Symbol representation: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)

28. 🧪 Oxidation

At O/L level, oxidation can commonly be described as:

  • Combination with oxygen.
  • Gain of oxygen.

Word equation: Copper + oxygen → copper(II) oxide

Symbol equation: 2Cu(s) + O₂(g) → 2CuO(s)

29. 🔥 Oxidation of Magnesium

Magnesium is oxidised when it reacts with oxygen.

Word equation: Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation: 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)

30. 🧪 Reduction

At O/L level, reduction can commonly be described as:

  • Removal of oxygen from a substance.

Oxidation and reduction occur together in a redox reaction.

Word equation: Copper(II) oxide + hydrogen → copper + water

Symbol equation: CuO(s) + H₂(g) → Cu(s) + H₂O(g)

Copper(II) oxide loses oxygen and is therefore reduced.

31. 🔴 Redox Reactions

A redox reaction is a reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur at the same time.

In the reaction between copper(II) oxide and hydrogen:

Word equation: Copper(II) oxide + hydrogen → copper + water

Symbol equation: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O

  • CuO is reduced because it loses oxygen.
  • Hydrogen is oxidised because it combines with oxygen to form water.

32. 🔋 Redox in Terms of Electrons

At a more advanced level, oxidation can also be described as loss of electrons, while reduction is gain of electrons.

Oxidation: Loss of electrons

Reduction: Gain of electrons

A useful memory aid is: OIL RIG — Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain.

33. 🌡️ Exothermic Reactions

An exothermic reaction releases energy to the surroundings, usually as heat.

The temperature of the surroundings may increase during the reaction.

Word equation: Methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

Symbol equation: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

Combustion reactions are commonly exothermic.

34. ❄️ Endothermic Reactions

An endothermic reaction absorbs energy from the surroundings.

The surroundings may become cooler during the reaction.

Word equation: Calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g)

Thermal decomposition requires energy supplied as heat.

35. 🌡️ Exothermic vs Endothermic

Exothermic Endothermic
Releases energy. Absorbs energy.
Surroundings may warm. Surroundings may cool.
Combustion is commonly exothermic. Many thermal decomposition reactions are endothermic.

36. ⚡ Activation Energy

Activation energy is the minimum energy that reacting particles must have for a successful reaction to occur.

Even an exothermic reaction needs activation energy to get started.

37. ⚡ Energy Profile

An energy profile shows how the energy of a reacting system changes as a reaction proceeds.

📈 Important Features

  • Reactants have an initial energy.
  • The curve rises to an activation-energy peak.
  • The curve then falls toward the products.
  • The energy difference between reactants and products relates to the overall energy change.

38. ⚗️ Catalysts

A catalyst is a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction without being permanently consumed.

A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy.

39. 🧪 Example of a Catalyst

Manganese(IV) oxide can catalyse the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide.

Word equation: Hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen

Symbol equation: 2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g)

Manganese(IV) oxide speeds up the reaction but is not included as a reactant in the overall equation.

40. 🧪 Enzymes as Biological Catalysts

Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions in living organisms.

They are usually proteins and are highly specific to particular substrates or reactions.

41. ⏱️ Rate of Reaction

The rate of reaction describes how quickly reactants are converted into products.

A fast reaction produces observable change in a short time. A slow reaction takes longer.

42. 📈 Measuring Reaction Rate

Reaction rate can be determined by measuring a change in a quantity over time.

For example, if a gas is produced:

Average rate = amount of gas produced ÷ time taken

Suitable measurements depend on the reaction being investigated.

43. 🌡️ Effect of Temperature on Rate

Increasing temperature generally increases the rate of a chemical reaction.

Particles move faster and collide more frequently. A greater proportion of collisions also have enough energy to overcome the activation energy.

44. 🧪 Effect of Concentration

Increasing the concentration of a reactant in solution generally increases reaction rate.

There are more reactant particles in a given volume, increasing the frequency of successful collisions.

45. 💨 Effect of Pressure on Gases

For reactions involving gases, increasing pressure can increase the reaction rate by bringing gas particles closer together.

This increases the frequency of collisions between reacting particles.

46. 🪨 Effect of Surface Area

Increasing the surface area of a solid reactant generally increases the rate of reaction.

A powdered solid has a greater surface area available for collisions than the same mass of a large lump.

47. ⚗️ Effect of a Catalyst on Rate

A catalyst increases the rate of reaction by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.

The catalyst does not change the overall chemical equation or the final products.

48. 🔄 Reversible Reactions

A reversible reaction can proceed in both the forward and backward directions under suitable conditions.

Word equation: Nitrogen + hydrogen ⇌ ammonia

Symbol equation: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)

The double arrow indicates that both directions are possible.

49. ⚖️ Dynamic Equilibrium

In a closed system at dynamic equilibrium, the forward and reverse reactions continue to occur, but at equal rates.

The concentrations of reactants and products remain constant, although the reactions have not stopped.

50. 🧪 Conditions for Chemical Reactions

Some reactions require particular conditions before they occur or occur at a useful rate.

  • 🔥 Heat
  • ⚡ Electricity
  • ☀️ Light
  • 🧪 A catalyst
  • 💧 Water or another solvent
  • 💨 Particular pressure

51. 🔥 Heating as a Reaction Condition

Some chemical reactions require heating.

Word equation: Calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g)

The heat supplies energy needed for thermal decomposition.

52. ⚡ Electrolysis as a Chemical Change

Electrolysis uses electrical energy to cause chemical decomposition.

Word equation: Water → hydrogen + oxygen

Symbol equation: 2H₂O(l) → 2H₂(g) + O₂(g)

The reaction requires electrical energy.

53. 💡 Photochemical Reactions

Some chemical reactions require light energy.

Light can provide the energy required to initiate or drive particular chemical changes.

A familiar example is the decomposition of silver chloride in light.

Word equation: Silver chloride → silver + chlorine

Symbol equation: 2AgCl(s) → 2Ag(s) + Cl₂(g)

54. 🧪 Reactions of Metals with Oxygen

Many metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides.

Word equation: Calcium + oxygen → calcium oxide

Symbol equation: 2Ca(s) + O₂(g) → 2CaO(s)

Word equation: Copper + oxygen → copper(II) oxide

Symbol equation: 2Cu(s) + O₂(g) → 2CuO(s)

55. 💧 Metals with Water

Some reactive metals react with water to form hydroxides and hydrogen.

Word equation: Sodium + water → sodium hydroxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation: 2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g)

Word equation: Calcium + water → calcium hydroxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Ca(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + H₂(g)

56. 🧪 Non-metals with Oxygen

Many non-metals react with oxygen to form non-metal oxides.

Word equation: Sulfur + oxygen → sulfur dioxide

Symbol equation: S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g)

Many non-metal oxides are acidic oxides.

57. 🧪 Reactions with Acids

Reactants Products
Acid + metal Salt + hydrogen
Acid + metal oxide Salt + water
Acid + metal hydroxide Salt + water
Acid + carbonate Salt + water + carbon dioxide

58. 🧪 Reaction of an Acid with Ammonia

Ammonia reacts with acids to form ammonium salts.

Word equation: Hydrochloric acid + ammonia → ammonium chloride

Symbol equation: HCl(aq) + NH₃(aq) → NH₄Cl(aq)

59. 🧪 Reaction of Acids with Carbonates

The carbon dioxide produced can be identified using limewater.

Word equation: Calcium hydroxide + carbon dioxide → calcium carbonate + water

Symbol equation: Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)

The limewater turns milky because calcium carbonate precipitates.

60. 🧪 Reaction Rate Practical: Acid + Carbonate

The rate of reaction between an acid and a carbonate can be investigated by measuring the volume of carbon dioxide produced over time.

Word equation: Hydrochloric acid + calcium carbonate → calcium chloride + water + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: 2HCl(aq) + CaCO₃(s) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

🔬 Variables

  • Independent variable: The factor being changed.
  • Dependent variable: The reaction measurement.
  • Control variables: Conditions kept constant.

61. 📊 Reaction Rate Graphs

A graph of product formed against time can be used to study reaction rate.

  • A steep gradient indicates a faster reaction.
  • A smaller gradient indicates a slower reaction.
  • A horizontal section indicates that the measured change has stopped.

📈 Key idea

The gradient of a quantity-time graph represents the rate of change of that quantity.

62. 🧪 Reaction Rate and Particle Collisions

The collision theory explains chemical reactions in terms of particles.

For a reaction to occur, reacting particles must collide with sufficient energy and with a suitable orientation.

63. 💥 Effective Collisions

A collision is successful when the reacting particles have enough energy to overcome the activation energy and the collision allows bonds to break and/or form appropriately.

64. 🧪 Factors Affecting Reaction Rate

Factor General effect on rate
Temperature Increasing temperature usually increases rate.
Concentration Increasing concentration usually increases rate.
Pressure of gases Increasing pressure can increase rate.
Surface area Increasing surface area usually increases rate.
Catalyst Increases rate by providing a lower-energy pathway.

65. ⚖️ Reversible vs Irreversible Reactions

Irreversible reaction Reversible reaction
Products do not readily react to reform the original reactants under the same conditions. Products can react to reform reactants.
Usually represented by →. Usually represented by ⇌.
Example: combustion. Example: ammonia formation.

66. 🧪 Reaction Conditions in Equations

Conditions such as heat, light, catalysts or pressure can be written above the reaction arrow.

For example, thermal decomposition requires heating.

Word equation: Calcium carbonate —heat→ calcium oxide + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: CaCO₃(s) —heat→ CaO(s) + CO₂(g)

67. 🧪 State Symbols

Symbol Meaning
(s) Solid
(l) Liquid
(g) Gas
(aq) Aqueous solution

68. 🫧 Gas Formation

Gas formation is often observed as bubbling or effervescence.

For example, an acid reacting with a carbonate produces carbon dioxide.

Word equation: Hydrochloric acid + sodium carbonate → sodium chloride + water + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: 2HCl(aq) + Na₂CO₃(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

69. ⚪ Formation of a Precipitate

A precipitate is an insoluble solid formed during a reaction in solution.

Word equation: Silver nitrate + sodium chloride → silver chloride + sodium nitrate

Symbol equation: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq)

70. 🎨 Colour Changes

A colour change can provide evidence that a new substance has formed.

For example, copper metal reacts with oxygen to form black copper(II) oxide.

Word equation: Copper + oxygen → copper(II) oxide

Symbol equation: 2Cu(s) + O₂(g) → 2CuO(s)

71. 🌡️ Temperature Changes

Temperature changes can provide evidence of energy transfer during a chemical reaction.

A temperature increase may indicate an exothermic process, while a temperature decrease may indicate an endothermic process.

72. 🧪 Reaction Between Iron and Copper(II) Sulfate

Iron is more reactive than copper and can displace copper from copper(II) sulfate solution.

Word equation: Iron + copper(II) sulfate → iron(II) sulfate + copper

Symbol equation: Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)

The solution changes because copper ions are removed and iron(II) ions enter the solution.

73. 🧪 Reaction Between Chlorine and Sodium Bromide

A more reactive halogen can displace a less reactive halogen from its halide solution.

Word equation: Chlorine + sodium bromide → sodium chloride + bromine

Symbol equation: Cl₂(aq) + 2NaBr(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + Br₂(aq)

74. 🔥 Energy Changes and Bond Breaking

Chemical reactions involve breaking existing bonds and forming new bonds.

Breaking bonds requires energy, while forming bonds releases energy. The overall energy change depends on the balance between these processes.

75. 🧠 How to Predict Products

Use the reaction pattern when possible.

Reactants Likely products

Non-metal + oxygen Non-metal oxide
Reactive metal + acid Salt + hydrogen
Acid + metal oxide Salt + water
Acid + hydroxide Salt + water
Acid + carbonate Salt + water + carbon dioxide
More reactive metal + salt New salt + displaced metal

76. 🧪 Writing a Chemical Equation from Words

  1. Identify the reactants.
  2. Identify the products.
  3. Write the correct chemical formula for each substance.
  4. Write the unbalanced symbol equation.
  5. Balance the equation using coefficients.
  6. Add state symbols when required.
  7. Add reaction conditions where necessary.
  8. Check that every element is balanced.

77. 🧮 Worked Equation Example

Aluminium reacts with oxygen to form aluminium oxide.

Word equation: Aluminium + oxygen → aluminium oxide

Unbalanced symbol equation: Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃

Balanced symbol equation: 4Al(s) + 3O₂(g) → 2Al₂O₃(s)

There are four aluminium atoms and six oxygen atoms on both sides.

78. 🧮 Another Worked Equation

Calcium reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide and hydrogen.

Word equation: Calcium + water → calcium hydroxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Ca(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + H₂(g)

The number of calcium, hydrogen and oxygen atoms is balanced on both sides.

79. 🧪 Complete vs Ionic Equations

A complete chemical equation shows all substances involved. An ionic equation focuses only on the particles that undergo chemical change.

Word equation: Hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water

Symbol equation: HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

Word ionic representation: Hydrogen ions + hydroxide ions → water

Symbol ionic representation: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)

80. 🧪 Reaction Types Summary

81. 🧠 Important Definitions

Term Definition
Chemical reaction A process in which substances are converted into new substances.
Reactant A substance present at the start of a chemical reaction.
Product A substance formed during a chemical reaction.
Catalyst A substance that increases reaction rate without being permanently consumed.
Oxidation At O/L level, commonly gain of oxygen or combination with oxygen.
Reduction At O/L level, commonly loss or removal of oxygen.
Redox A reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur together.
Precipitate An insoluble solid formed in a reaction in solution.
Activation energy The minimum energy needed for a successful reaction to occur.
Exothermic A reaction that releases energy to the surroundings.
Endothermic A reaction that absorbs energy from the surroundings.

82. ⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Changing subscripts when balancing an equation.
  • ❌ Forgetting to balance oxygen and hydrogen atoms.
  • ❌ Using incorrect chemical formulae.
  • ❌ Forgetting state symbols when they are required.
  • ❌ Confusing reactants with products.
  • ❌ Writing salt + hydrogen for every acid reaction.
  • ❌ Forgetting carbon dioxide in acid-carbonate reactions.
  • ❌ Confusing oxidation with only the presence of oxygen without considering the actual reaction.
  • ❌ Saying a catalyst changes the products of a reaction.
  • ❌ Saying that equilibrium means the reaction has stopped.
  • ❌ Confusing a physical change with a chemical reaction.
