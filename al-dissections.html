🧤 UNIT 6 — DISSECTION & BIOLOGICAL PRACTICALS

🎯 Unit Overview

Dissection is an important practical technique in Biology used to investigate the structure and organisation of organisms. It allows students to observe structures directly, understand their positions and relationships, and connect structure with function.

At A/L level, the emphasis is not simply on cutting open a specimen. Students should be able to observe, identify, describe, draw, label, compare and explain biological structures accurately.

1. 🔬 Meaning of Dissection

Dissection is the careful separation or opening of parts of a biological specimen so that its internal structures can be observed and studied.

Dissection may be used with suitable plant or animal specimens and is normally carried out under the supervision of a teacher or trained laboratory personnel.

📌 Key Point

Dissection is primarily a method of investigating biological structure and structure–function relationships.

2. 🎯 Purpose of Dissection

Dissection may be carried out to:

  • 🔍 Observe internal structures directly.
  • 🧠 Understand the organisation of organs.
  • 🫀 Identify organs and tissues.
  • 🔗 Study relationships between organs.
  • ⚙️ Relate structure to function.
  • 🧬 Compare anatomical structures between organisms.
  • 📚 Confirm information learned from diagrams and textbooks.
  • ✏️ Produce accurate biological drawings.
  • 🧪 Develop practical laboratory skills.

3. 🧰 Common Dissection Equipment

Equipment Use
🧤 Gloves Protect the hands and reduce contamination.
🧥 Laboratory coat Protects clothing and skin from laboratory materials.
🧷 Dissecting pins Hold structures or parts of a specimen in position.
✂️ Scissors Make controlled cuts through suitable tissues.
🔪 Scalpel Make precise cuts when appropriate and under supervision.
🔍 Hand lens Magnifies structures that are difficult to see clearly.
🧫 Petri dish / dissecting tray Provides a suitable surface for examination.
📏 Ruler Measures structures and specimens.
💧 Dropping pipette Adds small quantities of liquids where required.
🧻 Paper towels Absorb excess liquid and help keep the work area clean.

4. 🛡️ Dissection Safety

Safe practical work is essential.

  • 🧤 Wear appropriate protective equipment.
  • 🧼 Wash hands before and after practical work.
  • 🔪 Handle sharp instruments carefully.
  • 👨‍🏫 Follow the teacher's instructions at all times.
  • 🚫 Never use equipment for purposes for which it was not designed.
  • 👀 Keep sharp instruments directed away from yourself and others.
  • 🧹 Keep the work surface clean and organised.
  • 🗑️ Dispose of biological material according to laboratory instructions.
  • 🧴 Clean and disinfect reusable equipment as instructed.
  • 🚿 Report spills, injuries or broken equipment immediately.

⚠️ Exam Focus

Safety questions often test the reason for a precaution. Do not simply state "wear gloves"; explain that gloves help protect the student and reduce contamination.

5. 🌱 Ethical Considerations

Biological practical work should be conducted responsibly and respectfully. Specimens should only be obtained and used when appropriate for educational purposes.

  • Use suitable specimens approved for educational work.
  • Avoid unnecessary harm or waste.
  • Use the minimum specimen material required.
  • Follow institutional and local laboratory rules.
  • Treat living organisms and biological specimens responsibly.
  • Use alternatives such as models, preserved specimens or images when appropriate.

6. 📦 Specimen Handling

Good specimen handling preserves structures and improves the quality of observations.

  1. Identify the specimen before examination.
  2. Observe the external features first.
  3. Handle the specimen gently.
  4. Keep important structures intact where possible.
  5. Use instruments carefully and deliberately.
  6. Record observations as they are made.
  7. Do not rely on memory after the practical.

7. 👀 External Examination

External examination should normally take place before internal examination.

Record observable features such as:

  • 📏 Size and approximate dimensions
  • 🎨 Colour
  • 🔲 Shape
  • 🧩 Segmentation
  • 🦵 Appendages
  • 👁️ Position of visible openings
  • 🌿 Leaves, roots, stems or reproductive structures in plants
  • 🐟 Body regions and external structures in animals

🧠 Observation Rule

Record what you can actually see or measure. Avoid writing an explanation as an observation.

8. 🧭 Anatomical Terminology

Anatomical terms provide a precise way of describing the position of structures.

Term Meaning
Anterior Towards the front end of an organism.
Posterior Towards the rear end.
Dorsal Towards the back surface.
Ventral Towards the underside or belly surface.
Medial Towards the midline.
Lateral Away from the midline.
Proximal Closer to the point of attachment.
Distal Further from the point of attachment.
Internal Located inside the organism.
External Located on or near the outside.

9. ✂️ Internal Examination

Internal examination involves exposing structures carefully so that their location, shape, size and relationships can be studied.

Important observations include:

  • Position of organs
  • Relative size
  • Shape
  • Colour
  • Texture, where appropriate and safe to observe
  • Connections between structures
  • Protective coverings
  • Openings and ducts
  • Arrangement of organs within body cavities

10. 🫁 Body Cavities and Organ Arrangement

In many animals, organs are organised within body cavities. These spaces protect organs and provide room for movement and functioning.

During practical work, students should be able to identify the position of major organs and describe their relationships without confusing position with function.

11. 🧠 Structure and Function

A major purpose of practical Biology is to connect what is observed with biological function.

Structure Structural feature Functional significance
Heart Muscular walls and chambers Allows blood to be pumped through the circulation.
Lung Large internal surface supplied with blood vessels Provides a surface for gas exchange.
Small intestine Long region with specialised absorptive surface Allows efficient absorption of digested nutrients.
Kidney Highly organised internal tissue containing nephrons Involved in filtration, reabsorption and regulation of body fluids.
Leaf Broad surface with photosynthetic tissues Provides a large area for light capture and gas exchange.

12. ✏️ Biological Drawings

Biological drawings are scientific representations of specimens or structures. They are not artistic drawings.

A good biological drawing should:

  • Be large enough to show important features clearly.
  • Use clear, continuous lines.
  • Show only structures that are actually observed.
  • Avoid unnecessary shading.
  • Use accurate proportions as far as possible.
  • Have a clear title.
  • Include appropriate labels.
  • Include magnification or scale information when required.

13. 🖊️ Biological Drawing Rules

  • Use a sharp pencil.
  • Draw clear single lines.
  • Do not use sketchy repeated strokes.
  • Do not shade unless specifically required.
  • Keep labels horizontal where possible.
  • Use straight label lines.
  • Label lines should touch the structure being identified.
  • Avoid crossing label lines.
  • Do not use arrowheads unless instructed.
  • Do not include structures that were not observed.

14. 🏷️ Labelling

Labels identify important structures in a biological drawing.

Good labels should be:

  • Accurate
  • Clearly written
  • Placed neatly
  • Connected to the correct structure
  • Specific rather than vague

❌ Avoid

Labels such as "tube", "part", or "thing" when a more precise biological term is known.

15. 📏 Magnification and Scale

When an observed structure is drawn larger or smaller than its actual size, magnification can be calculated.

Magnification = image size ÷ actual size

Units must be consistent before calculation.

🧮 Example

If a structure is 40 mm on a drawing and its actual size is 10 mm:

Magnification = 40 ÷ 10 = ×4

16. 🔍 Observation vs Inference

Observation Inference
A structure has several chambers. The chambers may allow different regions of circulation to be separated.
A leaf has many visible veins. The veins may transport water, minerals and manufactured food.
A specimen has a broad surface. The broad surface may increase the area available for exchange or absorption.

Observation is what is directly seen or measured. Inference is an interpretation based on observations and biological knowledge.

17. 🌱 Plant Dissections

Plant dissections are used to investigate the organisation of roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds.

Depending on the specimen, students may examine:

  • 🌱 Roots
  • 🌿 Stems
  • 🍃 Leaves
  • 🌸 Flowers
  • 🍎 Fruits
  • 🌰 Seeds

18. 🌱 Root Examination

Roots can be examined externally and, where appropriate, by making transverse sections.

Features may include:

  • Root cap
  • Region of cell division
  • Region of elongation
  • Region of maturation
  • Root hairs
  • Vascular tissues

🧠 Structure–Function

Root hairs greatly increase the surface area available for absorption of water and mineral ions.

19. 🌿 Stem Examination

A transverse section of a stem can reveal the arrangement of vascular tissues and supporting tissues.

Students may identify:

  • Epidermis
  • Cortex
  • Vascular bundles
  • Xylem
  • Phloem
  • Pith, where present

20. 🍃 Leaf Examination

A leaf can be examined externally or sectioned to investigate its internal organisation.

Important structures include:

  • Upper epidermis
  • Lower epidermis
  • Mesophyll
  • Vascular bundles
  • Xylem
  • Phloem
  • Stomata
  • Guard cells

21. 🌸 Flower Dissection

Flower dissection is used to investigate the reproductive structures of flowering plants.

Major floral parts include:

Part Function
Sepal Protects the developing flower bud.
Petal Often attracts pollinators through colour, scent or other features.
Anther Produces pollen grains.
Filament Supports the anther.
Stigma Receives pollen.
Style Connects stigma with ovary.
Ovary Contains ovules.
Ovule Contains the female gametophyte and develops into a seed after fertilisation.

22. 🌸 Flower Dissection Procedure

A simplified practical sequence is:

  1. Observe the flower externally.
  2. Identify the sepals and petals.
  3. Carefully expose the reproductive structures.
  4. Identify stamens and carpels.
  5. Observe the anthers and stigma.
  6. Make a suitable longitudinal section of the ovary when required.
  7. Observe and identify the ovules.
  8. Record observations.
  9. Make a labelled biological drawing.

23. 🌸 Floral Formula and Floral Diagram

A floral diagram is a symbolic representation of the arrangement of floral parts.

A floral formula uses symbols and abbreviations to represent features such as sepals, petals, stamens and carpels.

🧠 Exam Focus

A student should be able to identify floral structures from a specimen or diagram and relate them to their reproductive roles.

28. 🫁 Respiratory Structures

Respiratory structures can be examined to understand how their anatomy supports gas exchange.

Structure Important feature Function
Fish gill Filaments and lamellae Gas exchange with water.
Amphibian lung Internal respiratory surface Gas exchange with air.
Mammalian lung Numerous alveoli Large surface for gas exchange.
Plant leaf Stomata and internal air spaces Allows movement of respiratory and photosynthetic gases.

29. 🫁 Fish Gills

Fish gills are specialised for efficient gas exchange in water.

Important structures include:

  • Gill arch
  • Gill filaments
  • Gill lamellae
  • Blood vessels

Their large exchange surface, thin diffusion distance and blood supply help maintain efficient gas exchange.

🖼️ Diagram Space

Insert a labelled fish gill diagram showing the gill arch, filaments, lamellae and blood supply.

30. 🫁 Mammalian Respiratory Structures

In a mammal, the respiratory system includes structures such as the trachea, bronchi, bronchioles and lungs.

Within the lungs, alveoli provide a large surface for gas exchange between air and blood.

During practical examination, students should be able to identify the major structures and explain how their features support ventilation and gas exchange.

31. 🍽️ Digestive Structures

The digestive system can be studied by identifying the organs involved in ingestion, digestion, absorption and egestion.

Structure Main role
Mouth Ingestion and mechanical processing of food.
Oesophagus Moves food towards the stomach.
Stomach Stores and mechanically mixes food and contributes to digestion.
Small intestine Major site of digestion and absorption.
Large intestine Absorbs water and forms faeces.
Liver Produces bile and performs many metabolic functions.
Pancreas Produces digestive enzymes and bicarbonate-rich secretion.
Rectum Stores faeces before elimination.

32. 🔬 Digestive Tract Examination

When examining a digestive system, identify structures in relation to one another rather than memorising isolated names.

A useful sequence is:

Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus

🧠 Practical Tip

Accessory organs such as the liver and pancreas are associated with the digestive tract but food does not pass through them.

33. ❤️ Circulatory Structures

The circulatory system can be examined by identifying the heart and major blood vessels.

Important structures include:

  • Right and left atria
  • Right and left ventricles
  • Septum
  • Major arteries
  • Major veins
  • Valves

34. ❤️ Heart Structure

The mammalian heart has four chambers arranged into right and left sides.

Structure Role
Right atrium Receives deoxygenated blood from the body.
Right ventricle Pumps blood towards the lungs.
Left atrium Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs.
Left ventricle Pumps oxygenated blood around the body.
Septum Separates the right and left sides of the heart.
Valves Help prevent backflow of blood.

35. 🫀 Comparing Heart Chambers

The ventricles have thicker muscular walls than the atria because they generate greater pressure to pump blood out of the heart.

The left ventricle generally has the thickest wall because it pumps blood through the systemic circulation.

36. 🧬 Reproductive Structures

Reproductive structures may be examined in appropriate specimens to understand the organisation of the reproductive system.

In flowering plants, reproductive structures include stamens and carpels. In animals, structures include organs such as testes, ovaries and associated reproductive ducts.

⚠️ Practical Note

Reproductive anatomy should be studied scientifically and respectfully. Practical work should follow the specimen, laboratory and teacher's instructions.

Structure General function

39. 🔍 Comparing Organs

Practical questions may require students to compare organs according to observable features and function.

Comparison Important differences
Right vs left ventricle The left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall and pumps into systemic circulation.
Small vs large intestine The small intestine is specialised for digestion and absorption; the large intestine is especially important in water absorption and faeces formation.
Xylem vs phloem Xylem mainly transports water and mineral ions; phloem transports assimilates.
Root vs stem Roots are mainly associated with anchorage and absorption; stems provide support and transport and bear leaves and reproductive structures.

40. 🧩 Organ Systems

An organ system consists of organs working together to perform major functions.

System Major function Representative structures
Digestive Processing and absorption of nutrients Stomach, intestine, liver, pancreas
Respiratory Gas exchange Lungs, trachea, bronchi
Circulatory Transport of substances Heart, blood vessels, blood
Excretory Removal of metabolic wastes and regulation of internal conditions Kidneys, ureters, bladder
Reproductive Production of gametes and reproduction Gonads and associated structures

41. 🔗 Relationships Between Organ Systems

Organ systems do not work independently. Their activities are coordinated to maintain the functioning of the whole organism.

  • 🫁 The respiratory system supplies oxygen to the blood.
  • ❤️ The circulatory system transports oxygen and nutrients.
  • 🍽️ The digestive system supplies absorbed nutrients.
  • 🚽 The excretory system removes certain metabolic wastes.
  • 🧠 The nervous and endocrine systems coordinate activities.

42. 🧪 Sectioning Specimens

Sectioning involves cutting a specimen to expose structures for observation.

Common section orientations include:

Section Description
Transverse section A section made approximately across the long axis.
Longitudinal section A section made along the long axis.
Oblique section A section made at an angle to the main axis.

43. 🔬 Dissection and Microscopy

Dissection and microscopy can complement each other.

Dissection reveals the organisation of organs and larger structures, while microscopy reveals tissues and cells at much smaller scales.

Technique Main scale observed
Dissection Organs and larger structures
Histology Tissues
Light microscopy Cells and tissues
Electron microscopy Ultrastructure and very small cellular structures

46. 🧠 Structure–Function Questions

A common A/L practical question asks students to explain how a visible feature is related to function.

Example

Question: Why is the wall of the left ventricle thicker than the wall of the right ventricle?

Answer: The left ventricle pumps blood through the systemic circulation, which requires a higher pressure than pumping blood to the lungs.

48. 🧠 Practical Identification Strategy

When given an unknown specimen or structure:

  1. Observe its overall shape.
  2. Look for distinctive features.
  3. Determine its position relative to nearby structures.
  4. Compare it with known diagrams or reference information.
  5. Use appropriate biological terminology.
  6. Confirm the identification using more than one feature where possible.

🔑 Important

Do not identify a structure from colour alone. Use its shape, position, relationships and other observable characteristics.

49. ❌ Common Dissection Mistakes

  • ❌ Cutting structures unnecessarily.
  • ❌ Failing to examine the specimen externally first.
  • ❌ Confusing organs with tissues.
  • ❌ Guessing structures without checking their position.
  • ❌ Drawing structures that were not observed.
  • ❌ Producing artistic rather than scientific drawings.
  • ❌ Using vague labels.
  • ❌ Forgetting units when recording measurements.
  • ❌ Confusing observation with inference.
  • ❌ Giving a function without identifying the structure correctly.
  • ❌ Ignoring safety instructions.

52. 🧮 Practical Calculation Example

Magnification

A biological structure has an actual length of 5 mm and is represented by a drawing 25 mm long.

Magnification = image size ÷ actual size

Magnification = 25 ÷ 5

Magnification = ×5

53. 🔬 Dissection vs Biological Drawing

Dissection and drawing are connected practical skills, but they are not the same activity.

Dissection Biological drawing
Reveals structures in a specimen. Records observed structures visually.
Uses practical equipment. Uses drawing equipment.
Requires careful specimen handling. Requires accurate observation and representation.
Can reveal relationships between organs. Can communicate those relationships clearly.

54. 🧠 From Specimen to Scientific Conclusion

Practical Biology follows a logical sequence:

👀 Observe → 📏 Measure → 📝 Record → ✏️ Draw → 🔍 Identify → 🧠 Interpret → 🎯 Conclude

Keeping these stages separate helps prevent unsupported conclusions.

55. 🧪 Practical Investigation Example

Suppose students examine a plant stem to determine the arrangement of vascular tissues.

  1. Observe the stem externally.
  2. Prepare an appropriate section under supervision.
  3. Observe the section.
  4. Identify the vascular tissues.
  5. Record their arrangement.
  6. Make a labelled drawing.
  7. Compare the arrangement with another plant type if required.
  8. Relate the observed arrangement to the plant's structure and function.

56. 🔍 Micrographs and Dissection

A/L students may be given photographs, diagrams or micrographs instead of a physical specimen.

The same principles apply:

  • Observe carefully.
  • Identify distinctive features.
  • Use scale information where provided.
  • Use correct biological terminology.
  • Distinguish observations from interpretations.

57. 📐 Using Scale Bars

A scale bar provides a reference for determining the size of structures in an image.

If the image size of a structure and the image size of the scale bar are measured using the same units:

Actual size of structure = measured structure length ÷ measured scale-bar length × scale-bar value

⚠️ Remember

Always keep the units consistent throughout the calculation.

58. 🧠 Higher-Level Structure–Function Analysis

A/L questions may require several observations to be connected in one explanation.

For example, when studying a respiratory organ, consider:

  • Surface area
  • Thickness of the exchange surface
  • Blood supply
  • Ventilation
  • Concentration gradients
  • Distance for diffusion

This allows students to move beyond simply naming structures and explain why the organ is effective.

59. 🧤 Good Practical Habits

  • 📖 Read the instructions before beginning.
  • 🔍 Observe before manipulating.
  • 🧠 Think before cutting.
  • 📏 Measure rather than estimate whenever possible.
  • 📝 Record observations immediately.
  • ✏️ Draw what you actually see.
  • 🏷️ Label precisely.
  • 🧼 Clean the work area afterwards.
  • 👨‍🏫 Ask the teacher when unsure about a procedure.

60. ⚠️ Common Exam Traps

Trap 1 — Observation vs Function

"The organ is long" is an observation. "The organ is long to increase absorption" is an explanation.

Trap 2 — Naming vs Identifying

If asked to identify a structure, give its specific biological name rather than a general description.

Trap 3 — Drawing vs Art

A biological drawing should communicate observed scientific structure, not artistic appearance.

Trap 4 — Position Matters

Structures with similar appearances may be distinguished by their position and relationship with surrounding structures.

61. 🚨 Safety During Sharp-Instrument Work

Sharp instruments should only be used according to laboratory instructions and under appropriate supervision.

  • Keep instruments controlled and pointed away from people.
  • Use only the amount of force required.
  • Do not leave sharp instruments hidden beneath biological material.
  • Pass instruments according to laboratory procedure.
  • Return instruments safely after use.

🔬 Dissection Is More Than Cutting a Specimen
