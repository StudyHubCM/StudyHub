⚙️ Metals

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Properties, reactivity, extraction, alloys, corrosion and important reactions of metals.

1. 🔩 Introduction to Metals

Metals are elements that generally have good electrical and thermal conductivity, are shiny when freshly cut or polished, and tend to form positive ions by losing electrons.

⭐ Examples of Metals

Sodium (Na), potassium (K), calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), aluminium (Al), zinc (Zn), iron (Fe), copper (Cu), silver (Ag) and gold (Au).

2. ⚙️ General Physical Properties of Metals

Property Description
Lustre Most metals have a shiny surface when freshly cut or polished.
Electrical conductivity Metals generally conduct electricity well.
Thermal conductivity Metals generally conduct heat well.
Malleability They can be hammered into sheets.
Ductility They can be drawn into wires.
Strength Many metals are strong and useful for construction.
Sonority Many metals produce a ringing sound when struck.
High melting point Many metals have relatively high melting points, although there are exceptions.

3. 🧲 Why Do Metals Conduct Electricity?

Metallic bonding contains positive metal ions surrounded by delocalised electrons. These electrons can move through the metal and carry electrical charge.

💡 Key idea: The movement of delocalised electrons allows metals to conduct electricity.

4. 🔨 Malleability

Malleability is the ability of a substance to be hammered or rolled into thin sheets without breaking.

Metals are malleable because layers of metal particles can slide past one another while metallic bonding is maintained.

5. 🧵 Ductility

Ductility is the ability of a substance to be drawn into wires.

Copper and aluminium are commonly used for electrical cables because they are good electrical conductors and can be drawn into wires.

6. 🔥 Thermal Conductivity

Metals are generally good conductors of heat because their mobile electrons transfer energy through the metal.

7. 🧪 Chemical Properties of Metals

Metals can react with oxygen, water, acids and other substances. The reactions depend strongly on the position of the metal in the reactivity series.

8. 📊 The Reactivity Series

The reactivity series arranges metals according to how readily they react.

🔥 Common O/L Reactivity Series

Potassium → Sodium → Calcium → Magnesium → Aluminium → Carbon → Zinc → Iron → Hydrogen → Copper → Silver → Gold

Carbon and hydrogen are included as reference points even though they are not metals.

9. 🧠 Meaning of Reactivity

A highly reactive metal loses electrons easily and therefore reacts readily with other substances.

A less reactive metal does not lose electrons as easily.

10. ⚡ Metals and Electron Loss

Metals usually form positive ions by losing one or more outer-shell electrons.

Example:

Sodium atom → sodium ion + electron

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻

11. 🧲 Formation of Positive Ions

When a metal atom loses electrons, it becomes a positively charged ion called a cation.

Word/electron representation: Magnesium atom → magnesium ion + two electrons

Symbol representation: Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻

12. 🧪 Metals Reacting with Oxygen

Many metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides.

Example: Magnesium

Word equation: Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

13. 🔥 Magnesium Burning in Oxygen

Magnesium burns in oxygen with a bright white flame and forms magnesium oxide.

Word equation: Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

14. 🔥 Metals and Oxygen: General Pattern

For many metals:

Word equation: Metal + oxygen → metal oxide

Symbol pattern: Metal + O₂ → metal oxide

The exact symbol equation depends on the metal and the formula of its oxide.

15. 🪙 Aluminium and Oxygen

Aluminium reacts with oxygen to form aluminium oxide. A thin layer of aluminium oxide forms on the surface and helps protect the metal from further corrosion.

Word equation: Aluminium + oxygen → aluminium oxide

Symbol equation: 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃

16. 🧱 Zinc and Oxygen

Word equation: Zinc + oxygen → zinc oxide

Symbol equation: 2Zn + O₂ → 2ZnO

17. 🔥 Iron and Oxygen

Iron reacts with oxygen under suitable conditions to form iron oxides. In ordinary corrosion, water is also involved.

Word equation: Iron + oxygen → iron(III) oxide

Symbol equation: 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃

18. 💧 Metals and Water

Some metals react with water. The products depend on the metal's reactivity.

Metal Reaction with water
Potassium Very vigorous reaction with cold water
Sodium Very vigorous reaction with cold water
Calcium Reacts with cold water
Magnesium Very slow with cold water; reacts more readily with steam
Zinc Reacts with steam when heated
Iron Reacts with steam when heated
Copper Does not react readily with water

19. 💦 Sodium and Water

Sodium reacts vigorously with cold water to produce sodium hydroxide and hydrogen.

Word equation: Sodium + water → sodium hydroxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂

20. 💧 Calcium and Water

Calcium reacts with cold water less vigorously than sodium.

Word equation: Calcium + water → calcium hydroxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂

21. ♨️ Magnesium and Steam

Magnesium reacts more readily with steam than with cold water.

Word equation: Magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂

22. ♨️ Iron and Steam

Word equation: Iron + steam → iron(II,III) oxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation: 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂

23. 🧪 Metals and Dilute Acids

Metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series can generally react with dilute hydrochloric acid or dilute sulfuric acid to produce a salt and hydrogen gas.

General word equation: Metal + acid → salt + hydrogen

24. 🧪 Magnesium and Hydrochloric Acid

Word equation: Magnesium + hydrochloric acid → magnesium chloride + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂

25. 🧪 Zinc and Hydrochloric Acid

Word equation: Zinc + hydrochloric acid → zinc chloride + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

26. 🧪 Iron and Hydrochloric Acid

Word equation: Iron + hydrochloric acid → iron(II) chloride + hydrogen

Symbol equation: Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂

27. 🚫 Copper and Dilute Hydrochloric Acid

Copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series, so it does not normally displace hydrogen from dilute hydrochloric acid.

💡 Exam point: Do not automatically write a reaction for every metal + acid combination. Check the reactivity series first.

28. 🫧 Test for Hydrogen

Hydrogen can be tested using a lighted splint. Hydrogen burns with a characteristic squeaky pop.

Word equation: Hydrogen + oxygen → water

Symbol equation: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

29. 🧲 Displacement Reactions

A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from a compound.

General word equation: More reactive metal + compound of less reactive metal → compound of more reactive metal + less reactive metal

30. 🧪 Iron and Copper(II) Sulfate

Iron is more reactive than copper, so iron displaces copper from copper(II) sulfate solution.

Word equation: Iron + copper(II) sulfate → iron(II) sulfate + copper

Symbol equation: Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu

31. 🔵 Copper and Iron(II) Sulfate

Copper is less reactive than iron, so copper cannot displace iron from iron(II) sulfate.

No reaction: Cu + FeSO₄ → no reaction

32. 🧪 Zinc and Copper(II) Sulfate

Zinc is more reactive than copper and therefore displaces copper.

Word equation: Zinc + copper(II) sulfate → zinc sulfate + copper

Symbol equation: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

33. 🧪 Magnesium and Copper(II) Sulfate

Word equation: Magnesium + copper(II) sulfate → magnesium sulfate + copper

Symbol equation: Mg + CuSO₄ → MgSO₄ + Cu

34. 🧠 Predicting Displacement

To predict whether displacement occurs:

  1. Identify the metal being added.
  2. Identify the metal in the compound.
  3. Compare their positions in the reactivity series.
  4. If the added metal is more reactive, displacement occurs.
  5. If it is less reactive, there is normally no displacement.

35. 🧪 Metals and Acids: Reaction Speed

Different metals react with acids at different rates. More reactive metals generally react more vigorously under comparable conditions.

Observation Possible meaning
Rapid bubbling Gas is being produced quickly.
Slow bubbling Gas is being produced more slowly.
Temperature rises The reaction may be exothermic.
No visible reaction The metal may be too unreactive under the conditions.

36. 🧱 Metal Oxides

Many metal oxides are basic. They can react with acids to form salts and water.

General word equation: Metal oxide + acid → salt + water

37. 🧪 Copper(II) Oxide and Sulfuric Acid

Word equation: Copper(II) oxide + sulfuric acid → copper(II) sulfate + water

Symbol equation: CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O

38. 🧪 Zinc Oxide and Hydrochloric Acid

Word equation: Zinc oxide + hydrochloric acid → zinc chloride + water

Symbol equation: ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O

39. ⚗️ Amphoteric Metal Oxides

Some metal oxides can react with both acids and strong alkalis. Such oxides are called amphoteric oxides.

Important examples at O/L level include aluminium oxide and zinc oxide.

40. 🧪 Aluminium Oxide with an Acid

Word equation: Aluminium oxide + hydrochloric acid → aluminium chloride + water

Symbol equation: Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O

41. 🧪 Aluminium Oxide with a Strong Alkali

Aluminium oxide can also react with sodium hydroxide solution. The exact form of the product depends on the conditions and representation used.

💡 Remember: Amphoteric means able to react with both acids and bases under suitable conditions.

42. ⛏️ Ores

An ore is a naturally occurring rock or mineral from which a metal can be extracted economically.

Some ores contain compounds of the desired metal mixed with unwanted materials.

43. 🪨 Minerals and Ores

A mineral is a naturally occurring substance containing a useful element or compound. An ore is a mineral or rock from which a metal can be extracted profitably.

44. 🏭 Extraction of Metals

The method used to extract a metal depends mainly on its position in the reactivity series.

Position of metal Typical extraction method
Very unreactive May occur naturally or require relatively simple processing
Below carbon Often extracted by reduction with carbon or carbon monoxide
Above carbon Generally extracted by electrolysis of a suitable molten compound

45. 🔥 Extraction Using Carbon

Metals below carbon in the reactivity series can often be extracted by reducing their oxides using carbon.

General word equation: Metal oxide + carbon → metal + carbon dioxide

46. 🧱 Extraction of Zinc

Zinc can be extracted from zinc oxide by heating it with carbon.

Word equation: Zinc oxide + carbon → zinc + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: 2ZnO + C → 2Zn + CO₂

47. 🏭 Extraction of Iron

Iron is extracted from iron ores in a blast furnace. The main reducing agent is carbon monoxide.

Word equation: Iron(III) oxide + carbon monoxide → iron + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂

48. 🏭 The Blast Furnace

The blast furnace is used to extract iron from iron ore. Important raw materials include iron ore, coke and limestone.

Material Main role
Iron ore Source of iron compounds
Coke Fuel and source of carbon monoxide
Limestone Helps remove impurities as slag
Hot air Provides oxygen for combustion

49. 🔥 Formation of Carbon Dioxide in the Blast Furnace

Word equation: Carbon + oxygen → carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: C + O₂ → CO₂

50. 🔥 Formation of Carbon Monoxide

Word equation: Carbon dioxide + carbon → carbon monoxide

Symbol equation: CO₂ + C → 2CO

51. 🧲 Reduction of Iron Ore

Carbon monoxide removes oxygen from iron(III) oxide to produce iron. This is a reduction reaction because oxygen is removed from the metal oxide.

Word equation: Iron(III) oxide + carbon monoxide → iron + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂

52. 🪨 Role of Limestone

Limestone decomposes on heating to form calcium oxide and carbon dioxide.

Word equation: Calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide

Symbol equation: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

53. 🧱 Formation of Slag

Calcium oxide reacts with acidic impurities such as silicon dioxide to form calcium silicate, which is part of the slag.

Word equation: Calcium oxide + silicon dioxide → calcium silicate

Symbol equation: CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃

54. ⚡ Extraction by Electrolysis

Very reactive metals such as aluminium cannot normally be extracted by reducing their oxides with carbon. They are extracted by electrolysis of molten compounds.

55. ⚡ Aluminium Extraction

Aluminium is extracted from aluminium oxide by electrolysis. The aluminium ions gain electrons at the cathode to form aluminium metal.

Word/electron representation: Aluminium ions + electrons → aluminium

Symbol representation: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al

56. 🧪 Reduction and Oxidation

Reduction can be remembered as removal of oxygen or gain of electrons.

Oxidation can be remembered as addition of oxygen or loss of electrons.

OIL RIG:
Oxidation Is Loss of electrons.
Reduction Is Gain of electrons.

57. 🔋 Metal Ions During Electrolysis

Positive metal ions move towards the negative electrode, where they gain electrons.

Example:

Aluminium ions + electrons → aluminium

Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al

58. 🧪 Corrosion

Corrosion is the gradual destruction of a metal through chemical reactions with substances in its environment.

Rusting is the corrosion of iron or steel.

59. 🧲 Rusting of Iron

Rusting requires oxygen and water. Salt and other substances can increase the rate of corrosion.

Conditions needed for rusting:

  • Oxygen
  • Water or moisture

60. 🧪 Rust

Rust is a hydrated form of iron(III) oxide formed during the corrosion of iron. Its composition is variable rather than represented by one fixed formula.

61. 🛡️ Preventing Rusting

Method How it works
Painting Creates a barrier between iron and air/moisture.
Oiling Prevents contact with water and oxygen.
Greasing Provides a protective barrier.
Plastic coating Separates the metal from the environment.
Galvanising Coats iron or steel with zinc.
Alloying Changes the properties and corrosion resistance of the metal.

62. 🛡️ Galvanising

Galvanising is coating iron or steel with zinc to protect it from corrosion.

Zinc provides a physical barrier and can also provide sacrificial protection because zinc is more reactive than iron.

63. 🔋 Sacrificial Protection

A more reactive metal can be attached to iron so that the more reactive metal corrodes preferentially.

Zinc and magnesium can be used for this purpose.

64. 🪙 Alloys

An alloy is a mixture containing a metal and one or more other elements. Alloys are produced to obtain useful properties.

65. 🔩 Examples of Alloys

Alloy Main components Common use
Steel Iron + carbon Buildings, tools and machines
Stainless steel Iron + chromium + other elements Cutlery and equipment
Brass Copper + zinc Fittings and instruments
Bronze Copper + tin Statues and engineering applications
Duralumin Aluminium-based alloy Lightweight structures

66. 🧱 Why Are Alloys Often Harder?

In a pure metal, layers of particles can slide relatively easily. Different-sized atoms in an alloy can disrupt this regular arrangement, making movement of the layers more difficult.

72. ♻️ Recycling Metals

Metals can often be recycled and reused. Recycling reduces the need to extract new metal ores.

🌍 Advantages of Recycling Metals

  • Reduces mining.
  • Conserves metal ores.
  • Can reduce energy consumption.
  • Reduces waste.
  • Reduces environmental damage associated with extraction.

73. 🌍 Environmental Effects of Metal Extraction

Mining and metal extraction can affect the environment through land disturbance, waste production, dust, water pollution and energy consumption.

81. 📝 Important Definitions

Metal: An element that generally has metallic properties such as good electrical conductivity and a tendency to form positive ions.

Reactivity series: An arrangement of metals according to their reactivity.

Ore: A naturally occurring material from which a metal can be extracted economically.

Alloy: A mixture containing a metal and one or more other elements.

Corrosion: The gradual destruction of a metal through chemical reactions with its environment.

Rusting: The corrosion of iron or steel involving oxygen and water.

Galvanising: Coating iron or steel with zinc to protect it from corrosion.

Malleability: The ability to be hammered or rolled into sheets.

Ductility: The ability to be drawn into wires.

Reduction: Gain of electrons or removal of oxygen.

Oxidation: Loss of electrons or addition of oxygen.

82. ⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Saying every metal reacts with water.
  • ❌ Saying copper reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to release hydrogen.
  • ❌ Forgetting to check the reactivity series before predicting displacement.
  • ❌ Writing an unbalanced symbol equation.
  • ❌ Confusing an ore with a pure metal.
  • ❌ Saying aluminium is extracted using carbon reduction.
  • ❌ Forgetting that rusting requires both oxygen and water.
  • ❌ Confusing malleability with ductility.
  • ❌ Giving only a symbol equation when a word equation is required.
