🧪 Periodic Table

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Understand the arrangement of elements, groups, periods, electronic structure, periodic trends and the properties of important elements.

1. 📖 Introduction to the Periodic Table

The Periodic Table is a systematic arrangement of chemical elements according to their atomic numbers and repeating patterns in their properties.

Elements with similar chemical properties are placed in the same vertical columns called groups. Horizontal rows are called periods.

⭐ Key idea

The periodic table helps us predict the physical and chemical properties of elements from their position in the table.

2. 🔢 Atomic Number

The atomic number of an element is the number of protons in the nucleus of one atom of that element.

It is represented by the symbol Z.

📌 Important

Every element has its own unique atomic number. The atomic number determines the identity of an element.

For a neutral atom:

Number of protons = Number of electrons

🧮 Example

Sodium has atomic number 11.

Therefore, a neutral sodium atom contains:

  • 11 protons
  • 11 electrons

3. ⚖️ Mass Number

The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.

It is represented by A.

Mass number = Number of protons + Number of neutrons

Therefore:

Number of neutrons = Mass number − Atomic number

🧮 Example

An atom has atomic number 17 and mass number 35.

Number of neutrons = 35 − 17 = 18

Each element has a chemical symbol.

Example: chlorine is Cl, not CL.

5. 🧭 Arrangement of the Periodic Table

The modern periodic table is arranged in order of increasing atomic number.

It consists of:

  • 7 periods — horizontal rows
  • 18 groups — vertical columns

🧠 Remember

Periods = horizontal rows

Groups = vertical columns

6. 📊 Periods

A period is a horizontal row in the periodic table.

The period number corresponds to the number of occupied electron shells in the atoms of the elements in that period.

Period Number of Electron Shells Example
1 1 Hydrogen
2 2 Carbon
3 3 Sodium
4 4 Potassium

For example, sodium has the electronic configuration 2,8,1. It has three occupied shells, so sodium is in Period 3.

7. 🏛️ Groups

A group is a vertical column in the periodic table.

Elements in the same main group generally have the same number of electrons in their outermost shell and therefore show similar chemical properties.

Group Common Name Examples
1 Alkali metals Li, Na, K
2 Alkaline earth metals Mg, Ca
17 Halogens F, Cl, Br, I
18 Noble gases He, Ne, Ar

8. ⚡ Valence Electrons

Valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost occupied electron shell of an atom.

For many main-group elements, the number of valence electrons is related to the group in which the element is found.

Group Typical Number of Valence Electrons Example
1 1 Na: 2,8,1
2 2 Mg: 2,8,2
13 3 Al: 2,8,3
14 4 C: 2,4
15 5 N: 2,5
16 6 O: 2,6
17 7 Cl: 2,8,7
18 8* Ne: 2,8

*Helium is an exception: it has two electrons in its only occupied shell.

9. 🔄 Periodicity

The word periodic means something that repeats at regular intervals.

In the periodic table, certain properties of elements show repeating patterns when elements are arranged according to atomic number.

These repeating patterns are called periodic trends.

10. 🧩 The Main Regions of the Periodic Table

The periodic table can broadly be divided into metals, non-metals and a region of elements with properties intermediate between typical metals and non-metals.

Category General Location Examples
Metals Left and centre Na, Mg, Al, Fe
Non-metals Upper right region C, N, O, Cl
Metalloids Near the metal/non-metal boundary B, Si, Ge

Hydrogen is a non-metal even though it is positioned above Group 1.

11. 🔩 Metals

Most elements are metals.

General physical properties of metals include:

  • Good conductors of heat 🔥
  • Good conductors of electricity ⚡
  • Usually shiny when freshly cut or polished ✨
  • Usually malleable
  • Usually ductile
  • Usually solid at room temperature

Mercury is a notable exception because it is liquid at room temperature.

12. 🌬️ Non-metals

Non-metals generally have properties opposite to those of metals.

  • Generally poor conductors of electricity
  • Generally poor conductors of heat
  • Many are gases or low-melting solids
  • Solid non-metals are often brittle

There are exceptions, so these are general trends rather than rules without exceptions.

13. 🧱 Metalloids

Metalloids are elements that have a mixture of metallic and non-metallic properties.

Silicon is an important example.

Silicon is a semiconductor, meaning its electrical conductivity lies between that of a good conductor and a good insulator.

14. 🧂 Group 1 — Alkali Metals

Group 1 contains the alkali metals.

Important examples include:

  • Lithium — Li
  • Sodium — Na
  • Potassium — K

Group 1 metals have one electron in their outer shell and commonly form ions with a +1 charge.

💧 Reaction with water

Word equation:

Sodium + water → sodium hydroxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation:

2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂

The reactivity of Group 1 metals generally increases down the group.

15. 🪨 Group 2 — Alkaline Earth Metals

Group 2 contains the alkaline earth metals.

Examples include:

  • Magnesium — Mg
  • Calcium — Ca
  • Strontium — Sr

Group 2 elements have two outer-shell electrons and commonly form ions with a +2 charge.

🔥 Example: Magnesium and oxygen

Word equation:

Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation:

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

💧 Example: Calcium and water

Word equation:

Calcium + water → calcium hydroxide + hydrogen

Symbol equation:

Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂

Group 2 metals generally become more reactive down the group.

16. 🧪 Group 17 — Halogens

Group 17 elements are called halogens.

Important examples include:

  • Fluorine — F
  • Chlorine — Cl
  • Bromine — Br
  • Iodine — I

Halogens have seven outer-shell electrons and commonly form ions with a −1 charge.

They exist naturally as diatomic molecules:

F₂, Cl₂, Br₂ and I₂

🧂 Example: Chlorine reacting with sodium

Word equation:

Sodium + chlorine → sodium chloride

Symbol equation:

2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl

The reactivity of the halogens generally decreases down the group.

17. 🎈 Group 18 — Noble Gases

Group 18 contains the noble gases.

  • Helium — He
  • Neon — Ne
  • Argon — Ar
  • Krypton — Kr
  • Xenon — Xe
  • Radon — Rn

Noble gases have very stable outer electron arrangements. They are therefore generally very unreactive.

⭐ Important property

Noble gases are monatomic, meaning they exist as individual atoms rather than molecules such as O₂ or Cl₂.

18. 🧠 Electronic Configuration and Position

The electronic configuration of an atom can be used to help determine its position in the periodic table.

Example: Sodium

Sodium has atomic number 11.

Electronic configuration: 2,8,1

It has:

  • 3 occupied electron shells → Period 3
  • 1 outer-shell electron → Group 1

Example: Chlorine

Chlorine has atomic number 17.

Electronic configuration: 2,8,7

It has:

  • 3 occupied shells → Period 3
  • 7 outer-shell electrons → Group 17

20. 📈 Trends Across a Period

As we move from left to right across a period, the atomic number increases.

The number of occupied electron shells remains the same, while the number of outer-shell electrons generally increases across the main-group elements.

Example: Period 3

Element Configuration Valence Electrons
Sodium 2,8,1 1
Magnesium 2,8,2 2
Aluminium 2,8,3 3
Silicon 2,8,4 4
Phosphorus 2,8,5 5
Sulfur 2,8,6 6
Chlorine 2,8,7 7
Argon 2,8,8 8

21. 📉 Trends Down a Group

When moving down a group:

  • The number of occupied electron shells increases.
  • Atomic size generally increases.
  • Outer electrons are generally farther from the nucleus.

These changes help explain the different reactivities of groups such as Group 1 and Group 17.

22. 🔥 Reactivity of Group 1

Group 1 metals become generally more reactive down the group.

The outer electron becomes farther from the nucleus as the number of electron shells increases.

It is therefore easier for the atom to lose its outer electron.

📌 Trend

Lithium → Sodium → Potassium

Reactivity generally increases ↓

23. 🧪 Reactivity of Group 17

Halogens become generally less reactive down the group.

Halogens react by gaining an electron. As the atoms become larger down the group, the outer shell is farther from the nucleus.

The attraction between the nucleus and an incoming electron becomes weaker.

📌 Trend

Fluorine → Chlorine → Bromine → Iodine

Reactivity generally decreases ↓

24. ⚛️ Formation of Ions and Group Position

Many atoms form ions in order to obtain a more stable electron arrangement.

Group 1

Group 1 atoms usually lose one electron.

Electron representation:

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻

Group 2

Group 2 atoms usually lose two electrons.

Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻

Group 17

Group 17 atoms usually gain one electron.

Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

🧠 Important connection

The number of outer-shell electrons helps explain how an element tends to react and what ion it commonly forms.

25. 🧂 Periodic Table and Compound Formation

The position of elements in the periodic table can help us predict the formulae of ionic compounds.

Example: Sodium chloride

Sodium forms Na⁺ while chlorine forms Cl⁻.

The charges balance in a 1:1 ratio.

Word equation:

Sodium + chlorine → sodium chloride

Symbol equation:

2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl

Example: Magnesium chloride

Magnesium forms Mg²⁺ while chlorine forms Cl⁻. Two chloride ions are therefore needed for every magnesium ion.

Formula: MgCl₂

Word equation:

Magnesium + chlorine → magnesium chloride

Symbol equation:

Mg + Cl₂ → MgCl₂

26. 🧪 Metals and Non-metals Across a Period

Across a period, elements generally change from more metallic character on the left to more non-metallic character on the right.

Period 3 provides a useful example:

Na → Mg → Al → Si → P → S → Cl → Ar

Sodium, magnesium and aluminium are metals. Silicon is a metalloid. Phosphorus, sulfur and chlorine are non-metals. Argon is a noble gas.

27. 💧 Oxides Across Period 3

The oxides of Period 3 elements show an important change across the period.

Metallic elements tend to form more basic oxides, while non-metallic elements tend to form more acidic oxides.

Example: Sodium oxide

Word equation:

Sodium + oxygen → sodium oxide

Symbol equation:

4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O

Example: Magnesium oxide

Word equation:

Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Symbol equation:

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Further across the period, oxides become increasingly associated with non-metallic and acidic behaviour.

28. 🧪 Amphoteric Oxides

An amphoteric oxide can react with both acids and bases.

Aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃, is an important example.

Amphoteric behaviour is different from simply being a basic or acidic oxide.

⭐ Remember

Aluminium oxide is commonly described as amphoteric.

29. 🧭 How to Identify an Element's Position

If you are given the electronic configuration of an element, you can often determine its period and group.

Example 1

Electronic configuration = 2,8,1

  • Three occupied shells → Period 3
  • One outer electron → Group 1

The element is sodium.

Example 2

Electronic configuration = 2,8,7

  • Three occupied shells → Period 3
  • Seven outer electrons → Group 17

The element is chlorine.

30. 🧮 Finding an Element from Atomic Number

The atomic number tells us the number of protons. In a neutral atom it also tells us the number of electrons.

Example

An element has atomic number 12.

It has 12 protons and 12 electrons.

Its electronic configuration is: 2,8,2

Therefore:

  • Period = 3
  • Group = 2
  • Element = magnesium
  • Symbol = Mg

31. 🔬 Why Elements in the Same Group Behave Similarly

Elements in the same main group have similar outer-shell electron arrangements.

Since chemical reactions mainly involve outer electrons, elements with similar numbers of outer electrons often show similar chemical behaviour.

Example

Sodium and potassium are both Group 1 elements.

Sodium: 2,8,1

Potassium: 2,8,8,1

Both have one outer-shell electron and commonly form +1 ions.

32. 📜 Development of the Periodic Table

Scientists gradually developed ways of organizing the known elements as more elements were discovered.

Early classifications attempted to group elements with similar properties.

Dmitri Mendeleev played a major role in the development of the periodic table.

Mendeleev arranged elements mainly according to increasing atomic mass while paying attention to their chemical properties.

He also left gaps for elements that had not yet been discovered and predicted some of their properties.

The modern periodic table is arranged by increasing atomic number.

34. 📝 Important Definitions

Term Definition

Atomic number The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom.
Mass number The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus.
Group A vertical column in the periodic table.
Period A horizontal row in the periodic table.
Valence electrons Electrons in the outermost occupied shell.
Alkali metals Group 1 elements, excluding hydrogen when referring specifically to the metal family.
Halogens Group 17 elements.
Noble gases Group 18 elements with very stable electron arrangements.

35. ⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Confusing groups with periods.
    ✅ Groups are vertical; periods are horizontal.
  • ❌ Saying atomic number is the number of neutrons.
    ✅ Atomic number is the number of protons.
  • ❌ Using mass number as the number of electrons.
    ✅ For a neutral atom, electrons = atomic number.
  • ❌ Writing chlorine as CL.
    ✅ The correct symbol is Cl.
  • ❌ Saying all Group 1 elements are gases.
    ✅ Group 1 contains alkali metals, while hydrogen is a non-metal placed above the group.
  • ❌ Saying halogen reactivity increases down the group.
    ✅ Halogen reactivity generally decreases down the group.
  • ❌ Saying Group 18 elements normally react easily.
    ✅ Noble gases are generally very unreactive.

40. 🌟 Final Takeaway

The periodic table is much more than a list of elements. Its arrangement allows chemists to recognize patterns and predict how elements behave.

The most important connections to remember are:

Atomic number → identity of the element

Period → number of occupied electron shells

Group → similar outer-electron arrangement for main-group elements

Outer electrons → chemical behaviour and common ion formation

Position → useful clues about properties and reactivity

Once you understand these relationships, the periodic table becomes a powerful tool for predicting chemical behaviour rather than something you simply memorize. 🧪⚛️📚
