⚛️ Atomic Structure

O/L Chemistry — 0515

Understand the structure of atoms, subatomic particles, atomic number, mass number, isotopes, ions, electron arrangement and how atoms are represented in Chemistry.

1. ⚛️ What Is an Atom?

An atom is the smallest particle of an element that retains the chemical properties of that element.

Everything around us is made from matter, and matter is made from particles. The basic particles that make up elements are called atoms.

🧠 Example

A piece of copper contains enormous numbers of copper atoms. Every copper atom has the same number of protons in its nucleus.

2. 🔬 Structure of the Atom

An atom consists of three main types of subatomic particles:

  • ➕ Protons
  • ⚪ Neutrons
  • ➖ Electrons

Protons and neutrons are found inside the nucleus, while electrons occupy regions around the nucleus called electron shells or energy levels.

📍 Simple Atom Layout

Nucleus: contains protons and neutrons.

Outside the nucleus: contains electrons arranged in shells or energy levels.

3. 🧩 Subatomic Particles

Particle Symbol Relative Charge Relative Mass Location
Proton p⁺ +1 1 Nucleus
Neutron n⁰ 0 1 Nucleus
Electron e⁻ −1 Approximately 1/1840 Outside nucleus

📌 Remember

Almost all of the mass of an atom is concentrated in its nucleus, because protons and neutrons are much heavier than electrons.

4. 🎯 The Nucleus

The nucleus is the small, dense central region of an atom.

It contains:

  • ➕ Protons
  • ⚪ Neutrons

The nucleus has an overall positive charge because it contains positively charged protons and neutral neutrons.

⚠️ Important

Electrons are not found inside the nucleus. They occupy regions around it.

5. ➕ Protons

A proton is a positively charged subatomic particle found in the nucleus.

  • Symbol: p⁺
  • Relative charge: +1
  • Relative mass: 1
  • Location: Nucleus

🧠 Why Are Protons Important?

The number of protons determines the identity of an element.

For example, every atom of hydrogen has 1 proton, while every atom of carbon has 6 protons.

6. ⚪ Neutrons

A neutron is a subatomic particle with no electrical charge.

  • Symbol: n⁰
  • Relative charge: 0
  • Relative mass: 1
  • Location: Nucleus

Atoms of the same element can have different numbers of neutrons. These different forms are called isotopes.

7. ➖ Electrons

An electron is a negatively charged subatomic particle found outside the nucleus.

  • Symbol: e⁻
  • Relative charge: −1
  • Relative mass: approximately 1/1840
  • Location: Electron shells / energy levels

Electrons are involved in chemical bonding and many chemical reactions.

📌 Key Idea

The electrons in the outermost occupied shell are called valence electrons. They are especially important in chemical reactions and bonding.

8. ⚖️ Relative Charge and Mass

Chemists often use relative values instead of the actual masses and charges of subatomic particles.

Particle Relative Charge Relative Mass
Proton +1 1
Neutron 0 1
Electron −1 Approximately 1/1840

🎯 Exam Memory Trick

Proton = Positive ➕

Electron = Negative ➖

Neutron = Neutral ⚪

9. 🔢 Atomic Number

The atomic number, represented by Z, is the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom.

Therefore:

📐 Formula

Atomic number = Number of protons

The atomic number identifies the element.

Element Symbol Number of Protons Atomic Number
Hydrogen H 1 1
Helium He 2 2
Carbon C 6 6
Oxygen O 8 8
Sodium Na 11 11
Chlorine Cl 17 17

10. ⚖️ Mass Number

The mass number, represented by A, is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus.

📐 Formula

Mass number = Number of protons + Number of neutrons

Therefore:

🔢 Finding the Number of Neutrons

Number of neutrons = Mass number − Atomic number

🧪 Example

An atom has atomic number 11 and mass number 23.

Protons = 11

Neutrons = 23 − 11 = 12

11. 📝 Nuclide Notation

An atom can be represented using nuclide notation.

📐 General Form

ᴬZX

where:

  • A = mass number
  • Z = atomic number
  • X = chemical symbol of the element

🧪 Example: Sodium-23

Sodium has atomic number 11 and mass number 23.

²³₁₁Na

Therefore:

  • Protons = 11
  • Neutrons = 23 − 11 = 12
  • Electrons in a neutral atom = 11

12. 🧮 Calculating Subatomic Particles

For a neutral atom:

Number of protons = Atomic number

Number of electrons = Number of protons

Number of neutrons = Mass number − Atomic number

🧪 Example

Consider an atom of chlorine-35.

Chlorine has atomic number 17.

  • Protons = 17
  • Electrons = 17
  • Neutrons = 35 − 17 = 18

So chlorine-35 contains 17 protons, 18 neutrons and 17 electrons.

13. 🧬 Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

📌 Therefore Isotopes Have:

  • ✅ Same atomic number
  • ✅ Same number of protons
  • ❌ Different numbers of neutrons
  • ❌ Different mass numbers

🧪 Example: Carbon Isotopes

Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes of carbon.

Isotope Protons Neutrons Electrons in Neutral Atom
Carbon-12 6 6 6
Carbon-14 6 8 6

Both have 6 protons because both are carbon atoms, but they have different numbers of neutrons.

14. ⚗️ Uses of Isotopes

Some isotopes are radioactive and have useful applications.

Application Use
🏥 Medicine Some radioisotopes are used in diagnosis and treatment.
🏺 Archaeology Carbon-14 can be used to estimate the age of once-living materials.
🏭 Industry Radioisotopes can be used in thickness measurements and tracing processes.
🌾 Agriculture Some isotopes are used in scientific studies of nutrient uptake and other processes.

15. ⚡ Ions

An ion is an atom or group of atoms that has an overall electrical charge because it has gained or lost electrons.

Type What Happens? Charge
➕ Cation Atom loses one or more electrons. Positive
➖ Anion Atom gains one or more electrons. Negative

🧠 Remember

Losing negative electrons leaves an atom positive.

Gaining negative electrons makes an atom negative.

16. ➕ Formation of Positive Ions

A metal atom can lose one or more electrons to form a positive ion.

🧪 Example: Sodium

A sodium atom loses one electron:

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻

The sodium atom becomes a sodium ion, Na⁺.

The number of protons remains 11, but the number of electrons changes from 11 to 10.

17. ➖ Formation of Negative Ions

A non-metal atom can gain one or more electrons to form a negative ion.

🧪 Example: Chlorine

A chlorine atom gains one electron:

Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

The chlorine atom becomes a chloride ion, Cl⁻.

The number of protons remains 17, while the number of electrons increases from 17 to 18.

18. 🧮 Calculating Electrons in Ions

The number of protons does not change when an ordinary ion is formed. Only the number of electrons changes.

Species Protons Electrons Charge
Na 11 11 0
Na⁺ 11 10 +1
Cl 17 17 0
Cl⁻ 17 18 −1

🎯 Exam Rule

Positive ion: electrons = protons − charge

Negative ion: electrons = protons + magnitude of charge

For example, Ca²⁺ has 20 protons and therefore 18 electrons.

19. 🌀 Electron Shells

Electrons occupy different energy levels, commonly represented at O/L level as electron shells.

The shells are numbered starting from the shell closest to the nucleus.

Shell Maximum Electrons at Basic O/L Level
1st shell 2
2nd shell 8
3rd shell 8 for the first 20 elements in the simple O/L arrangement
4th shell Begins to be occupied in elements beyond the first 20

⚠️ Important O/L Note

For the first 20 elements, the commonly used O/L electron arrangements follow the pattern 2, 8, 8, 2.

21. 🧠 How to Write an Electron Arrangement

To determine the electron arrangement of a neutral atom:

  1. 🔢 Find the atomic number.
  2. ⚛️ The atomic number gives the number of electrons in a neutral atom.
  3. 🌀 Place electrons into the shells in order.
  4. 📌 Fill the first shell before moving to the next.

🧪 Example: Oxygen

Oxygen has atomic number 8.

Therefore, a neutral oxygen atom has 8 electrons.

First shell = 2 electrons

Remaining electrons = 8 − 2 = 6

Electron arrangement = 2,6

22. 🧲 Valence Electrons

Valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost occupied electron shell.

They are important because they are involved in chemical bonding and chemical reactions.

Element Electron Arrangement Valence Electrons
Li 2,1 1
C 2,4 4
O 2,6 6
Na 2,8,1 1
Cl 2,8,7 7
Ar 2,8,8 8

23. 🛡️ Stable Electron Arrangements

Atoms tend to become more stable when their outer electron shells achieve a stable arrangement.

The noble gases have particularly stable outer electron arrangements.

🧪 Examples

Neon: 2,8

Argon: 2,8,8

These atoms have full outer shells.

This helps explain why many atoms form ions or share electrons during chemical bonding.

24. 🔄 Atoms, Ions and Electron Loss/Gain

Atoms can become ions by changing their number of electrons. The nucleus remains unchanged during ordinary ion formation.

Species Electron Arrangement Change
Na 2,8,1 Neutral sodium atom
Na⁺ 2,8 Loses 1 electron
Cl 2,8,7 Neutral chlorine atom
Cl⁻ 2,8,8 Gains 1 electron

25. 🧪 Atomic Structure and Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions involve the rearrangement of atoms. Electrons are especially important because changes involving outer electrons can lead to the formation of ions and chemical bonds.

⚗️ Example: Formation of Sodium Chloride

Step 1 — Sodium loses an electron:

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻

Step 2 — Chlorine gains an electron:

Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

Step 3 — The ions combine:

Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl

Word equation:

Sodium + Chlorine → Sodium chloride

Symbol equation:

2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl

📌 Important

The sodium and chlorine atoms do not disappear. Their electrons are rearranged, and the resulting ions form the compound sodium chloride.

26. 🔬 Development of Atomic Models

Scientists developed different models of the atom as new experimental evidence became available.

Scientist / Model Main Idea
John Dalton Proposed that matter was made of atoms and that atoms of each element had characteristic properties.
J. J. Thomson Identified the electron and proposed a model containing electrons within a positively charged region.
Ernest Rutherford Evidence from the alpha-particle scattering experiment led to the idea of a small, dense, positively charged nucleus.
Niels Bohr Proposed that electrons occupy specific energy levels around the nucleus.
Modern atomic model Electrons are described using quantum-mechanical orbitals and probabilities rather than simple fixed circular paths.

27. 🧪 Rutherford's Alpha-Particle Scattering Experiment

Rutherford and his colleagues directed alpha particles at a very thin sheet of gold foil.

Most alpha particles passed through the foil, while a small number were deflected and a very small number were deflected through large angles.

🔎 Conclusions

  • Most of the atom is empty space.
  • Positive charge is concentrated in a very small region.
  • Most of the atom's mass is concentrated in the nucleus.
  • The nucleus is much smaller than the overall atom.

28. 🌟 Why Atomic Structure Matters

Understanding atomic structure is essential because it forms the foundation for many other Chemistry topics.

  • 📊 Periodic Table
  • 🔗 Chemical Bonding
  • ⚡ Electrolysis
  • 🧪 Chemical Reactions
  • ➕ Acids, Bases and Salts
  • 🔋 Electrochemistry
  • 🧬 Organic Chemistry
  • ⚖️ Chemical Calculations

🧠 Big Idea

The arrangement of electrons, especially the outer electrons, helps explain how elements behave chemically.

29. 🧮 Worked Examples

Example 1 — Finding Neutrons

An atom has atomic number 13 and mass number 27. Find the number of neutrons.

Neutrons = Mass number − Atomic number

Neutrons = 27 − 13

Neutrons = 14

Example 2 — Finding Particles

An atom has atomic number 8 and mass number 16. Find the number of protons, neutrons and electrons.

Protons = 8

Neutrons = 16 − 8 = 8

Electrons = 8 because the atom is neutral.

Answer: 8 protons, 8 neutrons and 8 electrons.

Example 3 — Finding Electrons in an Ion

Aluminium has atomic number 13. How many electrons are in Al³⁺?

Neutral aluminium has 13 electrons.

Al³⁺ has lost 3 electrons.

Electrons = 13 − 3

Electrons = 10

Example 4 — Electron Arrangement

An element has atomic number 17. Write its electron arrangement.

A neutral atom has 17 electrons.

First shell = 2

Second shell = 8

Remaining = 17 − 10 = 7

Electron arrangement = 2,8,7

31. 🧠 Key Definitions

Term Definition
Atom The smallest particle of an element that retains the chemical properties of that element.
Proton A positively charged subatomic particle found in the nucleus.
Neutron A neutral subatomic particle found in the nucleus.
Electron A negatively charged subatomic particle found outside the nucleus.
Nucleus The small, dense central region of an atom containing protons and neutrons.
Atomic number The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom.
Mass number The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus.
Isotope An atom of an element having the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons.
Ion An atom or group of atoms with an overall electrical charge due to gaining or losing electrons.
Cation A positively charged ion formed when electrons are lost.
Anion A negatively charged ion formed when electrons are gained.
Electronic configuration The arrangement of electrons in the energy levels or shells of an atom.
Valence electrons Electrons in the outermost occupied shell of an atom.

32. ⚠️ Common Exam Mistakes

  • ❌ Do not confuse atomic number with mass number.
  • ❌ Do not say that atomic number is the number of neutrons.
  • ❌ Do not forget that atomic number equals the number of protons.
  • ❌ Do not change the number of protons when an atom becomes an ion.
  • ❌ Do not say that isotopes have different numbers of protons. They have different numbers of neutrons.
  • ❌ Do not confuse cations and anions.
  • ❌ Do not forget that a neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons.
  • ❌ Do not change chemical symbols incorrectly.
  • ❌ Do not forget the charge when writing an ion.
  • ❌ Do not change subscripts in chemical formulas when writing ordinary chemical equations.

35. 🎓 Final Takeaway

Atomic structure is one of the most important foundations of Chemistry. Every element is identified by its number of protons, while its chemical behaviour is strongly connected to its electrons, especially those in the outermost shell.

Once you understand protons, neutrons, electrons, atomic number, mass number, isotopes, ions and electron arrangements, you will have the foundation needed for the next major topic: the Periodic Table.

🧠 Remember These Four Relationships

Atomic number = Protons

Mass number = Protons + Neutrons

Neutrons = Mass number − Atomic number

Neutral atom: Protons = Electrons
