BIOLOGY • O/L NOTES

💨 Respiration

Understand respiration, breathing, the human respiratory system, gaseous exchange, aerobic and anaerobic respiration, exercise and respiratory health.

💨 1. What is Respiration?

Respiration is the chemical process that takes place in living cells to release energy from food, especially glucose.

⚡ Why is respiration important?

The energy released is used for growth, movement, active transport, repair, reproduction, cell division and maintaining body temperature.

Respiration takes place continuously in living organisms, both during the day and at night.

🫁 2. Respiration vs Breathing

Breathing Respiration
Physical movement of air into and out of the lungs. Chemical process that releases energy from food in cells.
Mainly involves the respiratory system. Takes place inside living cells.
Does not itself release energy from glucose. Releases energy from food.
⚠️ Exam warning:

Never define respiration as simply "breathing". They are two different processes.

⚡ 3. Uses of Energy Released by Respiration

  • 💪 Muscle contraction and movement
  • 🚚 Active transport
  • 🌱 Growth and development
  • 🧬 Cell division
  • 🩹 Repair of tissues
  • 🌡️ Maintaining body temperature
  • 🧪 Making substances needed by cells
  • 🌸 Reproduction

🧪 4. Aerobic Respiration

Aerobic respiration is respiration that uses oxygen to release a relatively large amount of energy from glucose.

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy

Aerobic respiration occurs in the cells of plants, animals and many other organisms.

⚗️ 5. Aerobic Respiration Equation

The balanced chemical equation is:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy
🧠 Remember:

Aerobic respiration requires oxygen and produces carbon dioxide and water.

🚫 6. Anaerobic Respiration

Anaerobic respiration is respiration that releases energy from glucose without using oxygen.

It releases a relatively small amount of energy compared with aerobic respiration.

Anaerobic respiration can occur in microorganisms and temporarily in human muscle cells during vigorous exercise.

💪 7. Anaerobic Respiration in Human Muscles

During vigorous exercise, muscles require energy rapidly. The oxygen supply may not be sufficient to meet this demand.

Glucose → Lactic acid + Energy

Anaerobic respiration allows muscles to continue working for a short period, but lactic acid accumulates.

🦠 8. Fermentation in Yeast

Yeast can obtain energy from glucose without oxygen through a process called fermentation.

Glucose → Ethanol + Carbon dioxide + Energy

Fermentation is useful in food production, including bread-making.

⚖️ 9. Aerobic vs Anaerobic Respiration

Feature Aerobic Anaerobic
Oxygen Required Not required
Energy released Relatively large amount Relatively small amount
Human muscle products Carbon dioxide + water Lactic acid
Yeast products Carbon dioxide + water Ethanol + carbon dioxide

🌿 10. Respiration in Plants

Plants respire continuously, during both daytime and nighttime.

During aerobic respiration, plant cells use oxygen and produce carbon dioxide and water.

🌱 Important:

Plants photosynthesise when suitable conditions are present, but they also respire continuously.

🔬 11. Respiration Takes Place in Living Cells

Respiration is a cellular process. Cells require the released energy to carry out their activities.

Therefore, respiration occurs in living tissues and organs of both plants and animals.

🫁 12. Human Respiratory System

The human respiratory system allows the body to take in oxygen and remove carbon dioxide.

Major structures include:

  • 👃 Nasal cavity
  • 👄 Mouth
  • ➡️ Pharynx
  • 🎤 Larynx
  • 🔵 Trachea
  • 🌿 Bronchi
  • 🌿 Bronchioles
  • 🫁 Lungs
  • 🫧 Alveoli
  • 💪 Intercostal muscles
  • 🫁 Diaphragm
  • 🦴 Ribs

🖼️ 13. Labelled Human Respiratory System

🎯 O/L Exam Tip:

Practise drawing and labelling the respiratory system. You should be able to identify the trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, lungs, alveoli, ribs and diaphragm.

👃 14. Nose and Nasal Cavity

Air normally enters the respiratory system through the nose.

The nasal cavity helps:

  • 🌡️ Warm incoming air
  • 💧 Add moisture to air
  • 🧹 Trap dust particles
  • 🦠 Trap some microorganisms

Hairs and mucus help filter inhaled air.

🗣️ 15. Pharynx and Larynx

The pharynx is a passage through which air travels towards the larynx.

The larynx, also known as the voice box, connects the pharynx to the trachea.

🔵 16. Trachea

The trachea is the main airway leading from the larynx towards the bronchi.

It contains rings of cartilage that help prevent it from collapsing.

🧹 Protection:

Mucus traps particles while cilia move mucus towards the throat.

🧹 17. Cilia and Mucus

Mucus traps dust and microorganisms.

Cilia are tiny hair-like structures that move mucus towards the throat.

This protects deeper parts of the respiratory system.

🌿 18. Bronchi

The trachea divides into two main tubes called the bronchi.

One bronchus enters each lung.

🌿 19. Bronchioles

Bronchioles are smaller branches of the bronchi.

They distribute air throughout the lungs and eventually lead towards the alveoli.

⚠️ Don't confuse them:

Bronchi are the larger branches from the trachea. Bronchioles are the smaller branches inside the lungs.

🫁 20. Lungs

The lungs contain millions of tiny air sacs called alveoli.

They are protected by the rib cage and are the major organs involved in gaseous exchange.

🫧 21. Alveoli

Alveoli are tiny air sacs where gaseous exchange takes place between air and blood.

Oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood, while carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveoli.

⭐ 22. Adaptations of Alveoli

Alveoli are adapted for rapid and efficient gaseous exchange.

  • 📏 Large surface area — millions of alveoli provide a large exchange surface.
  • 🧱 Thin walls — the walls are about one cell thick, giving a short diffusion distance.
  • 🩸 Rich blood supply — many capillaries surround each alveolus.
  • 💧 Moist surface — gases can dissolve before diffusing.
  • 🔄 Continuous ventilation — fresh air continually reaches the alveoli.

🔄 23. Characteristics of a Good Respiratory Surface

A good respiratory surface should have:

  • 📏 Large surface area
  • 🧱 Thin surface
  • 💧 Moist surface
  • 🩸 Good blood supply
  • 🔄 A mechanism for maintaining concentration gradients

↔️ 24. Gaseous Exchange by Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

🫁 At the alveoli:

  • Oxygen moves from alveolar air into the blood.
  • Carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the alveoli.

➡️ 25. Pathway of Oxygen

Nose/Mouth → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli → Blood

Oxygen is then transported by the blood to body tissues, where cells use it during aerobic respiration.

⬅️ 26. Pathway of Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide produced by respiring cells is transported in the blood to the lungs.

Body Cells → Blood → Lung Capillaries → Alveoli → Bronchioles → Bronchi → Trachea → Nose/Mouth

🔄 27. Ventilation

Ventilation is the movement of air into and out of the lungs.

  • 🫁 Inspiration — breathing in.
  • 💨 Expiration — breathing out.

Ventilation continually replaces the air in the alveoli, helping maintain concentration gradients for diffusion.

⬇️ 28. Inspiration

During inspiration:

  1. The diaphragm contracts and flattens.
  2. External intercostal muscles contract.
  3. The ribs move upwards and outwards.
  4. The volume of the thoracic cavity increases.
  5. Pressure inside the lungs decreases.
  6. Air moves into the lungs.

⬆️ 29. Expiration

During normal expiration:

  1. The diaphragm relaxes and becomes dome-shaped.
  2. The intercostal muscles relax.
  3. The ribs move downwards and inwards.
  4. The volume of the thoracic cavity decreases.
  5. Pressure inside the lungs increases.
  6. Air moves out of the lungs.

💪 30. Role of the Diaphragm

Inspiration 🫁 Expiration 💨
Contracts Relaxes
Flattens Becomes dome-shaped
Moves downward Moves upward
Chest volume increases Chest volume decreases

🦴 31. Ribs and Intercostal Muscles

The ribs protect the lungs and move during breathing.

Intercostal muscles are muscles found between the ribs.

Their contraction and relaxation help change the volume of the thoracic cavity.

🏃 32. Breathing Rate and Depth

Breathing rate is the number of breaths taken per minute.

Depth of breathing refers to the amount of air moved during each breath.

Both can increase when the body's demand for oxygen rises.

🏃‍♂️ 33. Effect of Exercise on Breathing

During exercise, muscles respire more rapidly and require more energy.

  • 💨 Breathing rate increases.
  • 🫁 Breathing becomes deeper.
  • ❤️ Heart rate increases.
  • 🩸 More oxygen is delivered to muscles.
  • 🌫️ More carbon dioxide is removed.

🌬️ 34. Inspired and Expired Air

Inspired Air Expired Air
More oxygen Less oxygen
Less carbon dioxide More carbon dioxide
Less water vapour More water vapour
Usually cooler Usually warmer

🧪 35. Why Does Expired Air Contain More CO₂?

Carbon dioxide is produced during aerobic respiration in body cells.

It is transported by the blood to the lungs.

Carbon dioxide then diffuses from the blood into the alveoli and is removed during expiration.

🧪 36. Test for Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide can be tested using limewater.

🧪 When carbon dioxide is bubbled through limewater, the limewater turns milky/cloudy.

This indicates the presence of carbon dioxide.

🔬 37. Experiment: Respiration Produces Carbon Dioxide

Respiring biological material can be used to demonstrate carbon dioxide production.

🧪 Basic procedure:

  1. Place actively respiring material in a suitable container.
  2. Direct the gas produced towards limewater.
  3. Observe the limewater.
👀 Observation:

Limewater turns milky.

🧠 Conclusion:

Carbon dioxide is produced during respiration.

🌡️ 38. Experiment: Respiration Releases Heat

Germinating seeds can be used to demonstrate that respiration releases energy partly as heat.

🧪 Procedure:

  1. Place germinating seeds in an insulated container.
  2. Insert a thermometer.
  3. Record the initial temperature.
  4. Leave the setup for an appropriate period.
  5. Record the final temperature.
👀 Expected observation:

The temperature of the actively respiring material increases.

🧠 Conclusion:

Energy released during respiration is partly transferred as heat.

⚖️ 39. Importance of a Control

A control setup helps show that the temperature change is caused by respiration rather than another factor.

A suitable control can contain non-germinating or inactive material under otherwise similar conditions.

🎯 Exam point:

The experimental and control setups should be kept under the same conditions except for the factor being investigated.

🏃‍♂️ 40. Lactic Acid During Vigorous Exercise

During vigorous exercise, muscles may not receive enough oxygen to meet their energy requirements.

Anaerobic respiration can then occur and produces lactic acid.

The accumulation of lactic acid is associated with muscle fatigue.

🔄 41. Oxygen Debt

After vigorous exercise, breathing and heart rate may remain high for some time.

This helps provide additional oxygen needed by the body after exercise.

The additional oxygen helps the body deal with products of anaerobic respiration and return towards normal conditions.

🧠 Remember:

Oxygen debt is linked to the additional oxygen required after vigorous exercise.

🦠 42. Respiratory Diseases

Respiratory diseases can interfere with breathing or gaseous exchange.

  • 🫁 Asthma
  • 🦠 Bronchitis
  • 🦠 Pneumonia
  • 🦠 Tuberculosis (TB)
  • 🫁 Emphysema

🚭 43. Effects of Smoking

Tobacco smoke contains harmful substances that can damage the respiratory system.

  • 🟤 Tar — can damage lung tissue and contains carcinogenic substances.
  • ☁️ Carbon monoxide — reduces the blood's ability to transport oxygen efficiently.
  • ⚠️ Nicotine — is addictive and affects the cardiovascular system.
🚭 Smoking can:
  • Damage cilia
  • Increase risk of respiratory disease
  • Damage lung tissue
  • Reduce efficiency of gaseous exchange

🌱 44. Respiration vs Photosynthesis

Respiration 💨 Photosynthesis 🌿
Releases energy from glucose. Stores energy in glucose.
Occurs in living cells. Occurs in chlorophyll-containing cells.
Occurs day and night. Requires suitable light conditions.
Uses glucose during aerobic respiration. Produces glucose.
Produces carbon dioxide during aerobic respiration. Uses carbon dioxide.

📚 45. Important Definitions

  • Respiration: Chemical process in cells that releases energy from food.
  • Breathing: Movement of air into and out of the lungs.
  • Aerobic respiration: Respiration using oxygen.
  • Anaerobic respiration: Respiration without oxygen.
  • Fermentation: Anaerobic breakdown of glucose in microorganisms such as yeast.
  • Alveolus: A tiny air sac where gaseous exchange occurs.
  • Diffusion: Net movement of particles from higher concentration to lower concentration.
  • Ventilation: Movement of air into and out of the lungs.
  • Oxygen debt: Additional oxygen required after vigorous exercise to help the body recover.

💪 Anaerobic respiration in humans

⚠️ 48. Common O/L Mistakes

  • Saying respiration means breathing.
  • Saying plants do not respire.
  • Saying aerobic respiration occurs only in animals.
  • Saying anaerobic respiration releases more energy than aerobic respiration.
  • Confusing bronchi with bronchioles.
  • Reversing oxygen and carbon dioxide movement at the alveoli.
  • Forgetting that alveoli have thin walls and a rich blood supply.
  • Reversing the movements of the diaphragm.
  • Forgetting that carbon dioxide is produced during aerobic respiration.
  • Confusing fermentation in yeast with anaerobic respiration in human muscles.
